** Identity & forms
- Acarbose is a prescription oral antidiabetic drug. It is a complex oligosaccharide that acts locally in the gut. Less than 2% of an oral dose is absorbed as active drug. About 35% of a 14C-labelled dose is absorbed, mostly as metabolites, and about 51% is excreted in the feces [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- US labelling covers generic tablets (25, 50 and 100 mg). In the label, the maximum dose is 50 mg three times daily for people weighing 60 kg or less, and 100 mg three times daily for people weighing more than 60 kg. Each dose is taken with the first bite of a main meal [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- A modified-release, low-dose acarbose (40 mg) is being developed in a fixed combination with orlistat (EMP16) for obesity. This is investigational [PMID:40769876].

** Mechanisms
- Acarbose competitively and reversibly inhibits pancreatic alpha-amylase and the alpha-glucoside hydrolases of the intestinal brush border. This delays starch and disaccharide digestion and blunts post-meal rises in glucose. It does not stimulate insulin secretion and does not inhibit lactase [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Its main molecular target is sucrase-isomaltase. A variant in the SI gene changes the glucose response to acarbose (human pharmacodynamic study) [PMID:39805337].
- Animal evidence: more starch reaches the colon and is fermented by gut bacteria. In ITP mice, acarbose caused a bloom of Muribaculaceae and raised fecal propionate. Fecal short-chain fatty acids predicted lifespan [PMID:31195972]. A 2026 mouse study found that acarbose redirected microbial carbohydrate use (Parabacteroides distasonis, succinate) and suppressed anaphylaxis [PMID:42120908].
- Acarbose has been proposed as a calorie-restriction mimetic because it reduces postprandial glucose spikes without lowering food intake. This is a review and hypothesis [PMID:33006707].

** Dosing in trials
- T2D: Cochrane found no additional HbA1c benefit above 50 mg TID, but more GI adverse effects at higher doses [PMID:15846673]. MARCH used 300 mg/day (100 mg TID) [PMID:24622668] [PMID:40319921].
- Prevention: STOP-NIDDM used 100 mg TID for about 3.3 years [PMID:12086760] [PMID:12876091]. ACE used 50 mg TID for a median of 5 years [PMID:28917545].
- Label: start at 25 mg TID (or 25 mg once daily, then titrate), titrate every 4-8 weeks, maximum 100 mg TID (50 mg TID if 60 kg or less) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Mouse ITP doses were 400, 1,000 and 2,500 ppm in chow. All three doses extended lifespan [PMID:30688027].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Gastrointestinal effects dominate. In US placebo-controlled trials (n=1,255), flatulence occurred in 74% vs 29% on placebo, diarrhea in 31% vs 12%, and abdominal pain in 19% vs 9%. Symptoms tended to lessen over time [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36]. In STOP-NIDDM, 31% discontinued on acarbose vs 19% on placebo [PMID:12086760]. In ACE, GI disorders led to discontinuation or dose change in 7% vs 5% [PMID:28917545].
- Hepatotoxicity (FDA label):
  - In US studies with doses up to 300 mg TID, ALT/AST rose above 3x ULN in 3% vs 1% on placebo. The rises were dose-related, more common in females, and reversible. At doses up to 100 mg TID, rates were similar to placebo.
  - Postmarketing, 62 cases of transaminases above 500 IU/L were reported (29 with jaundice). Most were at 100 mg TID or more, and 33 of 45 with a recorded weight were under 60 kg. Fulminant hepatitis with fatal outcome has been reported, with unclear causality.
  - The label advises checking transaminases every 3 months in the first year [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
  - LiverTox describes rare clinically apparent acute liver injury [PMID:31643509]. Case reports include ALT 640 U/L that resolved after stopping (RUCAM "probable") [PMID:16317107] [PMID:9517669].
- Hypoglycemia: acarbose alone should not cause hypoglycemia. It can add to the hypoglycemia risk from insulin or sulfonylureas [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36]. A CGM RCT found less time below range with metformin+acarbose than with metformin+sitagliptin [PMID:40178116].
- Rare events: pneumatosis cystoides intestinalis, ileus/subileus, thrombocytopenia, and skin hypersensitivity (postmarketing) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36]. A PCI case report from 2025 resolved after stopping acarbose [PMID:41000298].
- Overdose causes GI symptoms, not hypoglycemia [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].

** Contraindications
Label contraindications are [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36]:
- hypersensitivity
- diabetic ketoacidosis
- cirrhosis
- inflammatory bowel disease
- colonic ulceration
- partial intestinal obstruction, or predisposition to obstruction
- chronic intestinal disease with marked maldigestion or malabsorption
- conditions that may worsen with increased intestinal gas

Other label cautions [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36]:
- Severe renal impairment (serum creatinine above 2.0 mg/dL): not recommended. With CrCl below 25 mL/min, peak plasma levels are about 5x higher and AUC about 6x higher.
- Nursing: should not be given.
- Pregnancy: use only if clearly needed (Category B).
- Pediatric safety has not been established.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Insulin and sulfonylureas: additive glucose lowering and a higher risk of hypoglycemia. Hypoglycemic shock has been reported very rarely. Treat hypoglycemia with oral glucose (dextrose), not sucrose, because acarbose inhibits sucrose hydrolysis. Severe cases may need IV glucose or glucagon [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Digoxin: the evidence is inconsistent.
  - The label reports lower digoxin bioavailability (Cmax -26%) and says a digoxin dose adjustment may be needed [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
  - In a crossover study in healthy volunteers, digoxin AUC and Cmax fell when it was given after acarbose [PMID:9702852].
  - Another study found no significant pharmacokinetic interaction at 50 mg TID, apart from a higher Cmax [PMID:12389338].
- Digestive enzyme preparations with carbohydrate-splitting enzymes (amylase, pancreatin) and intestinal adsorbents (charcoal) may reduce the effect of acarbose. Do not co-administer [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Metformin: effects are additive. Metformin Cmax falls by about 20% but AUC is unchanged, which the label calls of little clinical significance [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Drugs that raise glucose can cause loss of control: thiazides and other diuretics, corticosteroids, phenothiazines, thyroid products, estrogens/oral contraceptives, phenytoin, nicotinic acid, sympathomimetics, calcium-channel blockers, isoniazid. If these are withdrawn while on sulfonylurea/insulin, watch for hypoglycemia [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- No pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic effect on nifedipine, propranolol or ranitidine [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Orlistat: acarbose added to modified-release orlistat increased weight loss in a phase 2 RCT [PMID:40769876].
- Rapamycin (ANIMAL): the combination extended male mouse lifespan more than rapamycin alone in earlier cohorts [PMID:36179270].
*** Other supplements
- Niacin (nicotinic acid) may raise glucose and weaken glycemic control [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- No cited clinical interaction studies were found for the other supplements in this batch.
*** Conditions
- See Contraindications. Low body weight (60 kg or less) is linked to transaminase elevations [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Postprandial hypotension in older adults: in a general geriatric sample, acarbose attenuated the mean SBP fall but PPH events were more frequent [PMID:40719030].
*** Lab-test interference
- The 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) assay is unreliable for monitoring glycemic control during acarbose treatment [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].
- Small reductions in hematocrit, and low serum calcium and vitamin B6, have been reported. The label considers these spurious or not clinically significant [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=067c0adc-7322-489d-9baa-1d061b37be36].

** Genotype notes
- Sucrase-isomaltase (SI) 43G>T: in 66 Chinese participants, heterozygous carriers had a 50-68% smaller acarbose-induced reduction in glucose pharmacodynamic parameters than wild type. This is a single small study [PMID:39805337].

** Latest research
- [2026-06] Mouse study: acarbose redirected gut microbial carbohydrate use, raising P. distasonis and succinate, and suppressed anaphylaxis. A human cohort analysis linked AGI use to lower anaphylaxis incidence [PMID:42120908].
- [2026-05] Preprint (ANIMAL): acarbose at 1,000 ppm preserved spatial memory in mid-aged and aged UM-HET3 mice, but not in the 5xFAD Alzheimer model [PMID:42094534].
- [2026-05] HypoBar I crossover RCT (n=11): acarbose 50 mg TID did not reduce time below range in post-bariatric hypoglycemia. Secondary outcomes were exploratory [PMID:41810556].
- [2026-01] Preprint (ANIMAL, house crickets): intermittent acarbose reduced post-treatment lifespan in females, while rapamycin extended it [PMID:41542057].
- [2025-10] EMP16 phase 2 RCT (n=240): adding low-dose MR acarbose to orlistat increased 6-month weight loss (-7.73% vs -5.78%) [PMID:40769876].
- [2025-09] Older-adult RCT (n=43, mean age 77): acarbose attenuated the mean postprandial SBP fall, but PPH events were more frequent [PMID:40719030].
- [2025-09] Case report of acarbose-induced pneumatosis cystoides intestinalis [PMID:41000298].
- [2025-06] MARCH sub-analysis: higher baseline glucagon predicted larger HbA1c reductions with acarbose (about -1.3% vs -0.8%) but not with metformin [PMID:40319921].
- [2025-05] RCT, n=701: metformin+acarbose vs metformin+sitagliptin gave similar time in range, with lower time below range and lower glycemic variability on acarbose [PMID:40178116].
- [2025-05] Six-month RCT (n=100): acarbose lowered plasma TMAO more than vildagliptin [PMID:40395816].
- [2025-03] SI 43G>T variant linked to a blunted glucose response to acarbose [PMID:39805337].
- [2024-09] Taiwan cohort: acarbose use associated with lower gastric cancer risk (adjusted HR 0.43, 0.25-0.74). Observational [PMID:39290170].
- [2024-09] 3xTg Alzheimer mice on a Western diet (ANIMAL): acarbose improved metabolic health and cognition [PMID:39271570].
- [2024-06] SR of animal lifespan studies: acarbose is among the most-studied and most promising repurposed cardiometabolic drugs, and is in human aging trial registries [PMID:38966557].
- [2024-01] SR/MA of 19 RCTs: TNF-alpha reduced and adiponectin increased [PMID:38170330].

** Open questions
- Whether acarbose's ITP mouse lifespan extension (male-biased) translates to humans. No adequately powered human aging-outcome RCT has reported. The pilot NCT02865499 (n=8) has no located publication, and NCT02953093 was terminated for lack of funding [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02865499] [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02953093].
- STOP-NIDDM's CV signal (few events) versus the neutral ACE trial in CHD patients. It is unclear whether there is any macrovascular benefit [PMID:12876091] [PMID:28917545].
- How generalizable the findings are: MARCH and ACE were conducted in Chinese populations with high-carbohydrate diets [PMID:24622668] [PMID:28917545].
- Role of the microbiome/SCFA and of SI genotype in variable response [PMID:31195972] [PMID:39805337].
