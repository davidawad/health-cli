** Identity & forms
- Apigenin (4',5,7-trihydroxyflavone) is a dietary flavone found in parsley, celery and chamomile. In plants it occurs mostly as glycosides [PMID:27766890].
  - Chamomile tea mainly provides apigenin-7-O-glucoside. Parsley mainly provides apiin, i.e. apigenin-7-O-(2''-O-apiosyl)glucoside [PMID:35452808].
- Supplement forms: isolated apigenin (often 50 mg in sleep stacks) [PMID:42829807], and German chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla/recutita) extracts. Clinical chamomile extracts are often standardized to 1.2% apigenin [PMID:39836709] [PMID:41761999].
- IMPORTANT: nearly all human efficacy data come from *chamomile* products (extract capsules or tea), not from isolated apigenin. Chamomile also contains other actives, e.g. essential-oil terpenoids such as chamazulene and alpha-bisabolol [PMID:16137701]. Chamomile results should not be read as apigenin results.
- Human pharmacokinetics (PK):
  - Aglycone apigenin: poorly absorbed. Urinary metabolites account for about 0.5% of intake [PMID:35452808].
  - Glycosides: urinary recovery was 11.2% from a parsley drink and 34% from chamomile tea. Tmax of apigenin-4'-glucuronide was 2 h with chamomile (upper-gut absorption) and 4-6 h with parsley (lower gut). Circulating forms are glucuronides and sulfates [PMID:35452808].
  - Parsley bolus (n=11): plasma Cmax 127 +/- 81 nmol/L at about 7 h, with wide between-person variation. Plasma levels fell below detection by 28 h, and urinary recovery was 0.22% [PMID:16407641]. A crossover diet study found 0.58% urinary recovery [PMID:10615220].
  - Poor solubility and extensive metabolism limit oral bioavailability, so novel carriers are being explored [PMID:27766890] [PMID:42832459].

** Mechanisms
- GABA-A / benzodiazepine site (preclinical):
  - Isolated from chamomile as a central benzodiazepine-receptor ligand (Ki 4 uM for flunitrazepam binding). In mice it was anxiolytic without sedation, and mildly sedative at 10-fold higher doses [PMID:7617761].
  - In oocyte studies apigenin itself antagonized GABA-A currents. Those effects were flumazenil-insensitive [PMID:12586201] [PMID:15451406].
  - At 1 uM it enhanced diazepam's positive modulation of GABA-A receptors by up to 22% [PMID:15451406].
  - Chamomile is among 10 phytomedicines with both preclinical GABA-modulating data and human anxiety trials [PMID:29168225].
- CD38 inhibition / NAD+ (preclinical only):
  - Apigenin and quercetin inhibit the NADase CD38. In cells this raised NAD+ and lowered protein acetylation. In obese mice apigenin raised NAD+ and improved glucose and lipid homeostasis [PMID:23172919].
  - CD38 drives age-related NAD+ decline and is the main enzyme degrading NMN in vivo [PMID:27304511]. This is the rationale for pairing CD38 inhibitors with NAD+ precursors.
  - In diabetic rats apigenin restored the renal NAD+/NADH ratio and Sirt3 activity [PMID:32507768].
  - Apigenin and nicotinamide riboside each raised brain NAD+ and suppressed LPS neuroinflammation in mice [PMID:33871064].
  - No human study has shown that apigenin raises NAD+ or inhibits CD38 at achievable plasma concentrations [PMID:38476603].
- Platelets: apigenin antagonizes the thromboxane A2 receptor (Ki about 155 uM) and potentiated aspirin ex vivo [PMID:18410117]. Platelet inhibition in vitro needed very high concentrations, and dietary apigenin (84 mg/d) had no effect in humans [PMID:9459373].
- CYP inhibition (in vitro/animal):
  - CYP2C9 (losartan, diclofenac): IC50 4-10 uM, competitive [PMID:27287328]. Mixed-type inhibition of CYP3A4/CYP2C9 [PMID:19883715].
  - CYP3A4: reversible inhibition [PMID:30301254] [PMID:34069400].
  - CYP1A2: potent inhibition [PMID:20806393].
  - Chamomile itself: crude extract inhibited CYP3A4 in vitro [PMID:10969720]. Essential-oil constituents inhibited CYP1A2 (and to a lesser extent CYP3A4, 2C9 and 2D6) [PMID:16137701]. In mice a chamomile extract *induced* hepatic Cyp3a11/Cyp1a2 [PMID:41091411].
- Sex-steroid pathways (in vitro/animal):
  - Aromatase inhibition: in human placental/ovarian microsomes [PMID:6474163]; IC50 20 uM in H295R cells [PMID:15319488]. In rats at 50 mg/kg there was no uterine estrogenic or anti-aromatase effect, attributed to poor bioavailability [PMID:11595503].
  - Partial estrogen-receptor agonist in ovariectomized mice [PMID:34048736].
  - Mixed progesterone-receptor agonist that blunted genistein-driven uterine proliferation [PMID:29736565].
- Other preclinical: antidepressant/anxiolytic-like effects in 21 rodent studies [PMID:42488186]. Vasodilatory/antihypertensive effects in animals [PMID:42832459].

** Dosing in trials
- Chamomile extract for GAD: 1500 mg/d (500 mg three times daily) open-label for 8-12 weeks, then 26 weeks randomized continuation [PMID:27912875] [PMID:27912871]. The 2009 RCT used chamomile extract over 8 weeks [PMID:19593179].
- Chamomile for sleep: 270 mg twice daily for 28 days in insomnia [PMID:21939549]; 200 mg twice daily for 28 days in older adults [PMID:29154054]; oral solution 5 mL three times daily during chemotherapy [PMID:41538720].
- Menopause: 100 mg capsules (1.2% apigenin) four times daily (400 mg/d) for 12 weeks [PMID:39836709] [PMID:41761999].
- Chamomile tea: 3 g/150 mL three times daily after meals for 8 weeks in T2DM [PMID:26437613]. A coagulation study used 9 tea bags/day or extract capsules three times daily for 7 days [PMID:37730613].
- Isolated apigenin: 50 mg nightly (with magnesium L-threonate 2 g and L-theanine 200 mg) for 2 weeks [PMID:42829807]. No dose-finding trial of isolated apigenin exists.
- Dietary apigenin: 84 mg/d from 5 g dried parsley for 7 days [PMID:9459373].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Chamomile, SR of adverse events (72 trials, 2896 exposed; 11 case reports) [PMID:40374153]:
  - 65 AEs across 10 trials. Most common were GI complaints and drowsiness, both self-limiting.
  - AE reporting was absent in 28 trials.
  - 6 of the 11 case reports were allergic, including 3 anaphylaxis cases.
- GAD trials: similar low AE rates to placebo over 26 weeks [PMID:27912875]. AEs in 11.7% of the open-label phase, with no serious events [PMID:27912871].
- Menopause RCT: 2 withdrawals for mouth sores, skin spots or itching [PMID:39836709].
- Allergy: type I allergy and anaphylaxis to chamomile occur, with cross-sensitization to mugwort and birch pollen (Bet v 1 homologue) and cross-reactivity with celery [PMID:10998021]. NCCIH notes higher risk in people allergic to ragweed, chrysanthemums, marigolds or daisies [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/chamomile].
- Coagulation: a case of multiple internal hemorrhages occurred with chamomile tea plus lotion during warfarin therapy [PMID:16636327]. In healthy volunteers not on warfarin, 7 days of chamomile tea or extract did not change PT, INR, aPTT or fibrinogen [PMID:37730613].
- Isolated apigenin: rodent studies report a favorable safety profile even at high doses [PMID:42488186], and toxicity is reported only above 5000 mg/kg in animals [PMID:42832459]. Human safety data for concentrated apigenin supplements are limited to short trials [PMID:42829807].
- NCCIH: chamomile is likely safe in food amounts and possibly safe short-term at medicinal doses. Side effects may include nausea, dizziness and hypersensitivity [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/chamomile].

** Contraindications
- Known allergy to chamomile or other Asteraceae (ragweed, mugwort, chrysanthemum), or birch-pollen/celery cross-sensitization [PMID:10998021] [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/chamomile].
- Pregnancy and lactation: there are insufficient data for recommendations [PMID:40314057] [PMID:40374153].
- Estrogen-sensitive conditions (e.g. breast or uterine cancer): NCCIH cautions about possible estrogen-like effects [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/chamomile]. Supporting data are from animals and in vitro work [PMID:34048736] [PMID:6474163].
- Warfarin therapy: avoid unsupervised starting or stopping of chamomile (case report; warfarin SRs) [PMID:16636327] [PMID:32478963].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin: one case report of serious bleeding [PMID:16636327]. Reviews list chamomile among herbs with a major-severity interaction [PMID:24790635] and among foods/herbs that raise bleeding risk [PMID:32478963]. No PT effect was seen in people not on warfarin [PMID:37730613]. Monitor INR.
- CYP2C9 substrates (losartan, warfarin, some NSAIDs): apigenin competitively inhibits CYP2C9 in vitro (IC50 about 4-10 uM) [PMID:27287328] [PMID:19883715]. Human plasma levels after food are sub-micromolar [PMID:16407641], so clinical relevance is uncertain.
- CYP3A4 / P-gp / BCRP substrates:
  - In vitro, apigenin inhibits CYP3A4 [PMID:30301254] [PMID:34069400].
  - In rats it raised exposure to dasatinib (via CYP3A2, P-gp and BCRP) [PMID:36838589], paclitaxel (via P-gp) [PMID:24871039] and venlafaxine (AUC +40.9% at 250 mg/kg) [PMID:26202346].
  - No human PK interaction study was found.
- Benzodiazepines and other sedatives: apigenin enhanced diazepam's GABA-A modulation in vitro [PMID:15451406]. Drowsiness is among the commonest chamomile AEs [PMID:40374153], and NCCIH lists sedatives as a theoretical interaction [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/chamomile].
- Oral contraceptives: NCCIH cites preliminary data that chamomile may reduce their effect [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/chamomile].
- Antidiabetic drugs: chamomile tea lowered HbA1c and insulin resistance in T2DM [PMID:26437613], so additive glucose lowering is possible (minor).
- Aspirin/antiplatelets: apigenin potentiated aspirin ex vivo [PMID:18410117]. No in vivo antiplatelet effect was seen at dietary doses [PMID:9459373].
*** Other supplements
- NMN and nicotinamide riboside: there is a mechanistic rationale only. CD38 degrades NMN and drives NAD+ decline [PMID:27304511]. Apigenin inhibits CD38 [PMID:23172919]. Apigenin and NR each raised brain NAD+ in mice [PMID:33871064]. No human combination data exist.
- Quercetin: also a CD38 inhibitor [PMID:23172919]. Combination effects in humans are unknown.
- Magnesium (+/- L-theanine): synergistic sleep-promoting effect in mice [PMID:41701207]. However, a 2-week human RCT of apigenin + magnesium L-threonate + L-theanine showed no benefit over placebo [PMID:42829807].
- Melatonin: melatonin is mainly cleared by CYP1A2 [PMID:11317475], and apigenin and chamomile oil constituents inhibit CYP1A2 in vitro [PMID:20806393] [PMID:16137701]. Higher melatonin levels and additive sedation are therefore possible. This is mechanistic only.
- Genistein: apigenin blocked genistein-stimulated uterine proliferation in mice [PMID:29736565]. Opposing hormonal effects are possible but clinical relevance is unknown.
*** Conditions
- Asteraceae/ragweed allergy, pregnancy/lactation, estrogen-sensitive cancers: see Contraindications.
- Iron deficiency: chamomile tea taken with a meal cut non-heme iron absorption by 47% [PMID:10999016]. Separate it from iron-rich meals and non-heme iron supplements.
- Surgery: chamomile alone did not prolong PT in volunteers [PMID:37730613]. The perioperative bleeding concern applies mainly to patients on warfarin.
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. INR may change through the warfarin interaction [PMID:16636327], whereas chamomile alone did not alter PT/INR/aPTT [PMID:37730613]. In T2DM, true decreases in HbA1c and insulin were reported with chamomile tea [PMID:26437613].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Multi-compound RCT (n=103): a nightly stack with 50 mg apigenin, magnesium L-threonate and L-theanine for 2 weeks did not beat placebo on stress or sleep. Benefits were seen with the morning ashwagandha/Rhodiola arm [PMID:42829807].
- [2026-10] Review of apigenin and hypertension: poor oral bioavailability, modulation of drug-metabolizing enzymes and transporters, and antihypertensive effects in animals only [PMID:42832459].
- [2026-05] SR of 21 rodent studies: consistent antidepressant/anxiolytic-like effects. No human trials of isolated apigenin [PMID:42488186].
- [2026-03] Mouse study: apigenin + magnesium prolonged sleep more than either alone (+32% to +44% sleep duration) [PMID:41701207].
- [2026-02] Chamomile 400 mg/d (1.2% apigenin) RCT: vasomotor QOL MD -0.82 (CI -1.25 to -0.39); no change in other QOL domains [PMID:41761999].
- [2026-02] PCOS SR/MA: chamomile had no significant effect on testosterone or lipids in humans [PMID:41767834].
- [2026-01] Triple-blind RCT (n=80) during paclitaxel: chamomile oral solution did not prevent neuropathy. Sleep quality improved, with no AEs [PMID:41538720].
- [2025-10] Mouse study: chamomile extract induced hepatic Cyp3a11/Cyp1a2 via BMAL1, a possible induction pathway with chronic use [PMID:41091411].
- [2025-10] Musculoskeletal pain SR/MA (8 studies): possible analgesic effect, high heterogeneity [PMID:41394268].
- [2025-07] Anti-inflammatory SR/MA (11 RCTs): reduced mucositis severity and pain [PMID:40665590].
- [2025-05] SR of chamomile AEs (72 trials, n=2896): mostly GI upset and drowsiness. Case reports include anaphylaxis. Pregnancy data are insufficient [PMID:40374153].
- [2025-04] Menopause RCT (n=80): total symptom score -10.36 (CI -13.84 to -6.92) [PMID:39836709].
- [2025-03] SR of chamomile in pregnancy/postpartum (23 studies, n=2065): evidence too weak for recommendations [PMID:40314057].
- [2024-09] Sleep SR/MA (10 studies, n=772): PSQI WMD -1.88. No improvement in sleep duration or efficiency [PMID:39106912].

** Open questions
- Does isolated apigenin (rather than chamomile) improve anxiety or sleep in humans? There are no adequately powered RCTs.
- Does oral apigenin inhibit CD38 or raise NAD+ in humans at achievable plasma levels? In vitro IC50s are micromolar, while dietary plasma levels are sub-micromolar [PMID:16407641] [PMID:23172919].
- What is the clinical magnitude of CYP2C9/CYP3A4/CYP1A2 inhibition versus possible chamomile-induced CYP induction [PMID:41091411]? No human probe-drug studies were found.
- Dose, PK and safety of high-dose (50 mg+) apigenin supplements over months.
- The mechanism of the warfarin interaction. Only one case and indirect evidence exist [PMID:16636327] [PMID:37730613].
- Whether estrogenic or aromatase effects are clinically relevant in hormone-sensitive conditions.
