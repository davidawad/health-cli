** Identity & forms
- Apigenin is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: 4',5,7-trihydroxyflavone, parsley/chamomile flavone. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:38629096, PMID:38629096].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:38629096, PMID:38629096].

| No reliable clinical efficacy estimate can be made for isolated apigenin | Systematic review/meta-analysis or human trial synthesis | Mixed/possible benefit; no established clinical disease prevention | See evidence summary below; pooled estimates vary by outcome and population | very-low | PMID:38629096, PMID:38629096 |

- A systematic review of metabolic syndrome literature primarily summarizes preclinical and mechanistic evidence; high-quality isolated-apigenin human supplementation trials are essentially absent [PMID:38629096].
- No reliable clinical efficacy estimate can be made for isolated apigenin; animal and cell findings cannot establish human benefit [PMID:38629096].

** Dosing in trials
- No validated therapeutic dose for isolated apigenin established in human RCTs [PMID:38629096].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Supplement-level safety data in humans are inadequate; dietary exposure in foods does not establish safety of concentrated extracts [PMID:38629096].

** Contraindications
- Avoid extrapolating from food exposure; insufficient human safety data in pregnancy/lactation [PMID:38629096].

** Interactions
- CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein interaction signals are preclinical; clinical magnitude unknown [PMID:38629096].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2024 systematic review remains largely mechanistic/preclinical and highlights need for translational human trials [PMID:38629096].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
