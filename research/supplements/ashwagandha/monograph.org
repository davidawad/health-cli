** Identity & forms
- Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal (Solanaceae), an Ayurvedic "Rasayana" herb sold mainly as root or root-and-leaf extracts [PMID:33338583] [PMID:34254920].
- Main bioactives are withanolides (steroidal lactones), e.g. withaferin A, withanolide A, withanoside IV and 12-deoxywithastramonolide. Human plasma Cmax after oral products is low (0.1-49.5 ng/mL) and varies widely by product and dose [PMID:39599622].
- Branded extracts used in trials:
  - KSM-66: root extract, used e.g. at 300 mg twice daily in a pregnancy trial [PMID:41767760].
  - Sensoril: aqueous root-and-leaf extract, 500 mg/d in the STAR strength trial [PMID:30463324]. Its OECD-guideline toxicology package found no genotoxicity and a rat NOAEL of 4000 mg/kg/day [PMID:41987003].
  - Shoden: high-withanolide-glycoside extract (120 mg/d for sleep; 21 mg withanolide glycosides/day in aging men) [PMID:32540634] [PMID:30854916].
- Withanolide content differs between preparations, and hormonal effects in a meta-analysis varied with withanolide dose [PMID:41740946]. Extract variability prevents a consensus on the best preparation or dose [PMID:34254920].

** Mechanisms
- HPA-axis modulation: lowers serum cortisol in RCT meta-analyses [PMID:40746175] [PMID:41740946]. Reviews propose central regulation of the HPA, thyroid and gonadal axes rather than direct receptor binding [PMID:41454558].
- GABAergic and serotonergic activity in preclinical models [PMID:29168225] [PMID:34254920]. A meta-analysis reported higher serotonin (MD +31.75 ng/mL) [PMID:41740946].
- Thyroid axis: raises T3/T4 and lowers TSH in subclinical hypothyroidism (RCT) [PMID:28829155]. Pooled RCT data show a modest T4 rise without TSH/T3 change [PMID:41740946]. Mechanistic reviews: [PMID:38003702] [PMID:41454558].
- Gonadal axis: raises testosterone in men (meta-analysis) [PMID:41740946]. A review reports higher LH/FSH in men [PMID:38003702].
- Immune: classed among the 15 herbs with the strongest multi-level evidence of immunostimulatory activity (TLR, NF-kB, pro-inflammatory cytokines). This is mostly in vitro and animal data [PMID:41475897].
- Hepatotoxicity is proposed to involve CYP450-mediated reactive intermediates and oxidative stress. This mechanism is hypothetical, from a critical review [PMID:41502355].

** Dosing in trials
- Stress/anxiety: root or root-and-leaf extracts at 125-600 mg/d for 8 weeks are typical. An aqueous root-and-leaf extract reduced stress at 125, 250 and 500 mg/d [PMID:38732539]. 300 mg twice daily was used in a hypertension-comorbid stress RCT [PMID:42105298].
- Dose-response: stress effects showed linear and non-linear associations with dose [PMID:41644067].
- Sleep: >=600 mg/d and >=8 weeks gave larger effects in subgroup analysis [PMID:34559859]. 120 mg/d Shoden for 6 weeks improved actigraphic sleep [PMID:32540634].
- Exercise: 500-600 mg/d is the dose range with the most supporting evidence [PMID:42356302]. 500 mg/d Sensoril was used for 12 weeks [PMID:30463324].
- Subclinical hypothyroidism: 600 mg/d root extract for 8 weeks [PMID:28829155].
- Male infertility: 5 g/d root powder for 90 days [PMID:29770466].
- Longest prospective safety data: a standardized root extract for 12 months (observational, n=191) [PMID:41063394].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short-term trials report mostly mild, transient adverse events [PMID:39348746] [PMID:34559859].
  - An 8-week RCT in 80 healthy adults (300 mg twice daily) found no changes in hematology, biochemistry, liver tests or thyroid hormones [PMID:33338583].
  - A 12-month observational study (n=191) reported 18 mild AEs and no clinically significant ALT/AST change. Cortisol fell and testosterone rose [PMID:41063394].
- Hepatotoxicity (idiosyncratic herb-induced liver injury, HILI):
  - Iceland/US DILIN series: 5 cases. Jaundice after 2-12 weeks latency, cholestatic or mixed injury (R 1.4-3.3), prolonged pruritus. Liver tests normalized in 1-5 months; no liver failure [PMID:31991029].
  - India series: 8 single-ingredient cases. In patients with pre-existing chronic liver disease it caused acute-on-chronic liver failure, and all 3 such patients died. Chemical analysis found no adulterants [PMID:37756041].
  - 2026 scoping review: 13 publications, 25 patients. Mostly cholestatic/mixed injury, usually recovering after stopping. One transplant and 3 deaths, all in patients with cirrhosis [PMID:42367407].
  - RUCAM-assessed case (2026): probable HILI after only 3 days of use [PMID:42529606].
  - Ashwagandha is one of the 6 botanicals most often reported as hepatotoxic in the US [PMID:39102266].
- Thyroid: case report of painless thyroiditis/thyrotoxicosis that resolved after stopping [PMID:38559552]. A critical review also cites thyrotoxicosis and adrenal suppression [PMID:41502355].
- Regulatory actions (summarized from a 2025/2026 regulatory review [PMID:41420287] and an editorial [PMID:38969606]):
  - Denmark: banned ashwagandha in food supplements in 2023, based on a 2020 DTU risk assessment that cited effects on sex hormones/reproduction, thyroid, immune system, CNS and possible abortifacient effects. Sweden said the Danish assessment can also apply there.
  - EU: in June 2024 the EU Heads of Food Safety Agencies recommended a prioritized Article 8 procedure, which could lead to an EU ban.
  - Germany: BfR (Sept 2024) advised that children, pregnant and breastfeeding women, and people with liver disease avoid ashwagandha supplements.
  - Australia: TGA issued a Feb 2024 liver-injury advisory after 12 reports. It also requires a pregnancy label warning on some products.
  - Critics argue the DTU report relied on leaf/berry and solvent-extract data [PMID:38969606].
- Reproductive/developmental: a 2025 systematic and ethnobotanical review judged teratogenic/abortifacient claims inconclusive and traced them to secondary citations [PMID:40887707]. An open-label 12-week trial in 70 second-trimester pregnant women (300 mg twice daily) reported no adverse events [PMID:41767760]. Both are small or industry-adjacent, so human pregnancy safety data remain limited.

** Contraindications
- Pre-existing chronic liver disease or cirrhosis: case series show decompensation and deaths [PMID:37756041] [PMID:42367407]. BfR and TGA advise against use [PMID:41420287].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding: advised against by German BfR and restricted by label warnings in Australia. Denmark's ban cited abortifacient concerns. Human evidence of harm is weak, though [PMID:41420287] [PMID:40887707].
- Hyperthyroidism or thyroiditis: the herb can raise thyroid hormones, and thyrotoxicosis has been reported [PMID:28829155] [PMID:38559552].
- Autoimmune disease: immunostimulatory properties may flare autoimmune skin disease. This is mainly preclinical data plus a clinical expert review [PMID:41475897].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Thyroid hormone (levothyroxine): additive raises in T3/T4 and lowering of TSH are possible [PMID:28829155] [PMID:41740946]. Check thyroid tests. No direct interaction study exists.
- Immunosuppressants: the herb's immunostimulatory activity [PMID:41475897] could theoretically oppose immunosuppression. This is inferred from mechanism; no clinical interaction study was found.
- Sedatives/CNS depressants (benzodiazepines, hypnotics): GABA-modulating activity [PMID:29168225] and sleep-promoting effects [PMID:39083548] could be additive. This is mechanistic only.
- Antidepressants (SSRIs/SNRIs): a pharmacovigilance chart review found adverse events with Withania plus sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine and reboxetine. Proposed mechanisms are CYP/P-gp inhibition and pharmacodynamic overlap [PMID:37829299].
- Antidiabetics: small FBG reduction (very low certainty) [PMID:42641800]. A possible additive glucose-lowering effect is minor.
- Antihypertensives: one RCT in hypertensive patients reported favorable BP trends [PMID:42105298]. A possible additive effect is minor and poorly quantified.
- Hepatotoxic drugs: combining risk factors for liver injury is not studied. A clinical review notes ashwagandha among the commonest botanical hepatotoxins [PMID:39102266] [PMID:42367407].
- Testosterone/androgen therapy and hormone-sensitive conditions: ashwagandha raises testosterone in men [PMID:41740946]. Clinical significance with exogenous hormones is unknown.
*** Other supplements
- Rhodiola: co-implicated in one DILIN liver-injury case [PMID:31991029].
- Other potentially hepatotoxic botanicals (turmeric/curcumin, green tea extract, Garcinia cambogia, black cohosh, red yeast rice): stacking adds liver-injury exposure [PMID:39102266].
- GABAergic/sedating herbs (kava, valerian, passionflower): additive sedation is plausible (mechanistic) [PMID:29168225].
- No cited interaction with other supplements in this batch.
*** Conditions
- Chronic liver disease, pregnancy, breastfeeding, hyperthyroidism, autoimmune disease: see Contraindications.
- Subclinical hypothyroidism: may normalize indices [PMID:28829155]. This means thyroid tests may shift during use.
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. Ashwagandha causes true physiological changes in thyroid tests (T4 up, TSH down) [PMID:28829155] [PMID:41740946], cortisol (down) [PMID:40746175] and testosterone/DHEA-S (up) [PMID:30854916]. Liver enzymes and bilirubin should be checked if symptoms of liver injury appear [PMID:42367407].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Resistance-training MA (3 RCTs, n=161): strength gains (bench SMD 0.60) are fragile under HKSJ adjustment; GRADE low. No effect on body fat [PMID:42738987].
- [2026-08] Glycemia dose-response MA (11 RCTs, n=667): FBG -3.09 mg/dL; HbA1c inconclusive; very low certainty [PMID:42641800].
- [2026-07] RUCAM-assessed case of probable cholestatic HILI after 3 days of use [PMID:42529606].
- [2026-06] Three-level exercise MA (13 RCTs): g 0.47 overall, most consistent for aerobic endurance; prediction interval crosses zero [PMID:42356302].
- [2026-05] Scoping review of ashwagandha liver injury (25 patients): cholestatic/mixed pattern, deaths only in patients with cirrhosis [PMID:42367407].
- [2026-05] Cognition/physical MA (20 RCTs, n=1249): memory SMD 0.52; testosterone SMD 0.33; strength benefit only in active people [PMID:42199854].
- [2026-05] Phase III RCT in stressed hypertensive patients (n=60): lower anxiety and cortisol; favorable BP trends [PMID:42105298].
- [2026-04] Sensoril toxicology package: not genotoxic; rat NOAEL 4000 mg/kg/day [PMID:41987003].
- [2026-02] Hormone MA (23 RCTs, n=1706): cortisol SMD -1.18; T4 +0.61 ug/dL; no TSH/T3 change; testosterone +57 ng/dL in men [PMID:41740946].
- [2026-02] Mental-health dose-response MA (22 RCTs): reduces stress, anxiety and depression, with a dose-response for stress [PMID:41644067].
- [2026-02] Open-label RCT in pregnancy (n=70): no AEs; Hb improved [PMID:41767760].
- [2025-12] Regulatory review: Danish 2023 ban, EU HoA Article 8 recommendation (2024), BfR and TGA warnings [PMID:41420287].
- [2025-12] Scoping review lists ashwagandha among the most strongly supported immunostimulatory herbs that may flare autoimmune skin disease [PMID:41475897].
- [2025-10] 12-month observational safety study (n=191): no clinically significant liver-enzyme change [PMID:41063394].
- [2025-08] Cortisol vs PSS MA: cortisol -1.16 ug/dL, no significant PSS effect [PMID:40746175].
- [2025-08] Systematic/ethnobotanical review: evidence for teratogenic or abortifacient effects is inconclusive [PMID:40887707].
- [2024-09] Stress/anxiety MA (9 RCTs, n=558): PSS MD -4.72; HAM-A MD -2.19 [PMID:39348746].
- [2024-07] Anxiety/insomnia MA (5 RCTs): HAM-A MD -5.96, high heterogeneity [PMID:39083548].

** Open questions
- Incidence and risk factors for hepatotoxicity. It is unknown whether risk differs by extract (root vs leaf, solvent) or by withanolide/withaferin A content.
- Long-term (>12 months) safety from randomized data. Most trials are small, short, Indian-sited and often industry-funded.
- Whether thyroid hormone changes are clinically meaningful in euthyroid users, and how they interact with levothyroxine.
- Pregnancy and lactation safety. Human data are limited to one small open-label trial.
- Pharmacokinetic herb-drug interactions (CYP/P-gp) in humans. No controlled human interaction studies were found.
- The very large SMDs in some mental-health MAs suggest pooling or reporting problems. Effect sizes need confirmation in independent, preregistered trials.
- Outcome of the pending EU Article 8 procedure.
