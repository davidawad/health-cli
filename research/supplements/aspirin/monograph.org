** Identity & forms
- Acetylsalicylic acid is a non-selective COX inhibitor (NSAID). It is sold over the counter as immediate-release (chewable, plain) and enteric-coated/delayed-release tablets (usually 81 mg in the US, 75-100 mg elsewhere, 325 mg analgesic strength). An extended-release capsule (Durlaza) is prescription-only [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72b16a5c-825f-4f3f-a8b3-b723e4242da7] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].
- Aspirin is rapidly deacetylated to salicylic acid (plasma half-life of aspirin 20-60 min). It is excreted in urine mainly as salicylic acid metabolites, including phenolic and acyl glucuronides [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].
- Enteric coating delays and reduces absorption. After a single 325 mg dose, up to 49% of healthy volunteers looked "aspirin-resistant" on enteric-coated aspirin vs 0% on immediate-release. No true pharmacological resistance was found among 400 people [PMID:23212718].

** Mechanisms
- Antiplatelet: aspirin acetylates a serine in platelet COX-1 and inactivates it irreversibly. This blocks thromboxane A2 for the life of the platelet (7-10 days) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37]. Once-daily 75-100 mg fully suppresses platelet COX-1 [PMID:41592353].
- Anticancer hypotheses: platelet TXA2 may drive COX-2/PGE2 induction at gastrointestinal mucosal lesions and suppress T-cell anticancer immunity. This is supported by mechanistic and biomarker studies, not proven in humans [PMID:41592353]. In Add-Aspirin, 100 mg/d lowered urinary thromboxane metabolite by about 77-82%; 300 mg added nothing [PMID:37420000].
- GI bleeding risk is raised across the 75-325 mg/d range. Neither dose nor duration consistently changed the relative risk of upper GI bleeding [PMID:27490468].
- Urate: aspirin handles uric acid in two directions. Low doses cause urate retention and high doses (>3 g/d) are uricosuric. Even 75 mg/d reduced urate excretion by 15% in elderly inpatients [PMID:10643705].
- Respiratory: NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease (N-ERD/AERD) is a chronic eosinophilic airway disorder in which aspirin and other NSAIDs worsen asthma and rhinosinusitis symptoms [PMID:30216468].

** Dosing in trials
- Cardiovascular primary prevention: 100 mg/d (enteric-coated in ASPREE and ARRIVE) [PMID:30221597] [PMID:30158069] [PMID:30146931].
- Secondary prevention: 81 mg was as effective as 325 mg. Patients assigned 325 mg often switched to 81 mg (41.6% vs 7.1%) [PMID:33999548].
- Bodyweight modified the effect in an IPD analysis of 10 trials (n=117,279). 75-100 mg reduced events only below 70 kg (HR 0.75); doses >=325 mg helped only at higher weight [PMID:30017552].
- Lynch syndrome: 600 mg/d (CAPP2). CaPP3 suggests 100 mg is similar to 600 mg with fewer bleeds (serious bleeding 0% vs 1.5%) [PMID:32534647] [PMID:42425127].
- Adjuvant cancer trials: 100 or 300 mg/d (Add-Aspirin); 160 mg/d (ASAC) [PMID:31477558] [PMID:42777749].
- Preeclampsia: 150 mg/d from 11-14 to 36 weeks [PMID:28657417].
- AERD desensitization: a mean of about 800 mg/d [PMID:31518069].
- OTC analgesic label (81 mg delayed-release): 4-8 tablets (324-648 mg) every 4 h, maximum 48 tablets (about 3.9 g) per 24 h, for adults and children 12 years and older [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72b16a5c-825f-4f3f-a8b3-b723e4242da7].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Major bleeding is the key harm.
  - ASPREE: major hemorrhage 8.6 vs 6.2 per 1000 person-years [PMID:30221597].
  - Upper GI bleeding HR 1.87. Absolute 5-year risk ranged from 0.25% (age 70, no aspirin) to 5.03% (age 80 on aspirin with risk factors) [PMID:32747412].
  - Intracranial bleeding HR 1.38 (1.03-1.84), with no significant reduction in ischemic stroke [PMID:37494038].
  - Observational meta-analysis: GI bleeding RR 1.4 and ICH RR 1.4 with 75-325 mg/d [PMID:27490468].
- Anemia and iron loss: aspirin raised incident anemia (HR 1.20) and lowered ferritin by 11.5% in older adults, even without major bleeding [PMID:37335992].
- Older adults: excess all-cause mortality (HR 1.14), mainly cancer deaths [PMID:30221595]. Higher rate of advanced-stage cancer [PMID:32778876]. MACE rose after aspirin stopped in post-trial follow-up (HR 1.17) [PMID:40796244].
- Perioperative and adjuvant cancer settings: in ASAC, aspirin caused more serious AEs (8% vs 2%), including duodenal ulcer and cerebral haemorrhage [PMID:42777749].
- Reye syndrome: rare encephalopathy with liver failure, historically linked to salicylates during viral illness (influenza B, varicella) in children. The label warns children and teenagers [PMID:41362566] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72b16a5c-825f-4f3f-a8b3-b723e4242da7]. A dissenting review argues the causal evidence is weaker than assumed [PMID:17523700].
- Hypersensitivity: AERD affects about 7% of adult asthmatics and about 15% of those with severe asthma [PMID:25282015]. The label warns of hives, facial swelling, shock and asthma [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72b16a5c-825f-4f3f-a8b3-b723e4242da7].
- Urate and gout: low-dose aspirin on 2 consecutive days was associated with recurrent gout attacks (OR 1.81; OR 1.91 at <=100 mg). Allopurinol removed the effect [PMID:23345599]. A 2-week RCT subanalysis found no rise in serum urate at 50-100 mg [PMID:32196623].
- G6PD deficiency: occasional hemolysis, including a late-onset case on 100 mg/d followed by fatal subdural hemorrhage. Safety evidence is contradictory [PMID:33313989] [PMID:38292218].
- Overdose/salicylism: respiratory alkalosis plus anion-gap metabolic acidosis, tinnitus, seizures and pulmonary edema. Hemodialysis is used for severe cases [PMID:41049912].
- Pregnancy: avoid in the third trimester (premature ductus closure; maternal and neonatal hemostasis effects) unless directed. Low-dose use for preeclampsia is the exception [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37] [PMID:28657417].

** Contraindications
- NSAID hypersensitivity, or the syndrome of asthma, rhinitis and nasal polyps (labeled contraindication) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37] [PMID:30216468].
- Children and teenagers with or recovering from chickenpox or flu-like illness (Reye syndrome) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72b16a5c-825f-4f3f-a8b3-b723e4242da7].
- Active peptic ulcer or bleeding, or high bleeding risk. The USPSTF population excluded people at increased bleeding risk [PMID:35471505] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].
- Primary prevention starting at age 60 or older: USPSTF grade D (no net benefit) [PMID:35471505].
- Third trimester of pregnancy and the week before delivery. Severe hepatic impairment. GFR <10 mL/min [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Oral anticoagulants: any bleeding with OAC plus aspirin vs OAC alone, OR 1.36 (RCTs) and 1.42 (observational studies) [PMID:38263263].
- Ibuprofen: taken before aspirin, it blocks aspirin's irreversible platelet inhibition [PMID:11752357].
  - FDA advice for immediate-release 81 mg aspirin: take ibuprofen 400 mg at least 30 min after aspirin or at least 8 h before it. No timing advice is possible for enteric-coated aspirin [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/76636/download] [PMID:17844731].
  - Durlaza label: take ibuprofen 2-4 h or more after a dose [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].
- Naproxen also interferes with platelet COX-1 inhibition by aspirin [PMID:15837265]. Diclofenac, acetaminophen and rofecoxib did not [PMID:11752357].
- Other NSAIDs: additive GI bleeding (pooled observational data) [PMID:27490468].
- SSRIs: in UK Biobank aspirin users, GI bleeding aHR 1.27 (1.01-1.58); paroxetine aHR 1.73 [PMID:41435617]. Also increased in an observational meta-analysis [PMID:27490468].
- PPIs: reduce upper GI bleeding on aspirin.
  - Meta-analysis of RCTs vs placebo: RR 0.30 (0.19-0.46). Non-randomized data suggest more lower GI bleeding (RR 2.85) [PMID:42479073].
  - COMPASS: pantoprazole did not reduce overall upper GI events (HR 0.88) but reduced bleeding gastroduodenal lesions (HR 0.52) [PMID:31054846].
- Methotrexate:
  - The label says salicylate can inhibit renal methotrexate clearance and cause marrow toxicity [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].
  - PK study with high-dose ASA (about 3.4 g/d): clearance fell only after excluding one outlier; exposure to 7-OH-MTX rose [PMID:2273459].
  - Low-dose aspirin did not delay elimination of high-dose MTX in one cohort [PMID:28705331]. A Cochrane review found low-to-moderate-quality data without clear harm in RA [PMID:22071858].
- Other labeled interactions: RAS inhibitors and diuretics (renal impairment); phenytoin and valproate displacement [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4c2a1403-3862-1efd-0a91-444989222b37].
- Urate-lowering therapy: low-dose aspirin causes urate retention [PMID:10643705]. Allopurinol cancelled the aspirin-linked increase in gout attacks [PMID:23345599].
*** Other supplements
- EPA/DHA/DPA (omega-3): an MA of 11 RCTs (n=120,643) found no overall bleeding increase (RR 1.09), and background antiplatelet therapy did not modify risk. Bleeding was higher with high-dose purified EPA [PMID:38742535]. In 182 patients on fish oil plus aspirin and clopidogrel, there was no excess bleeding [PMID:19801023].
- Vitamin E: raised hemorrhagic stroke risk (RR 1.22) in an MA of RCTs [PMID:21051774]. In the PPP factorial trial, vitamin E 300 mg added no benefit to aspirin [PMID:11197445].
- Garlic: no measurable effect on platelet aggregation at up to 3 tablets/d, but 30% of the high-dose group reported bleeding [PMID:27761425]. In vitro and human antiplatelet signals are reviewed in [PMID:25839871].
- Garlic (Kyolic): aged garlic extract with warfarin showed no excess bleeding in a small RCT [PMID:16484565].
- Turmeric and Curcumin: the Meriva curcumin formulation did not change bleeding time on aspirin over 10 days [PMID:30070343]. Turmeric shows in-vitro antiplatelet activity [PMID:25839871].
- Ginger Root: reduced platelet aggregation in human studies [PMID:25839871], and was additive with nifedipine on aggregation [PMID:16883626].
- Green Tea Extract: catechins inhibit platelet aggregation in vitro [PMID:11707691]. Green tea has a reported warfarin interaction [PMID:25839871].
- Ginseng (Panax): reduces platelet aggregation [PMID:25839871].
*** Conditions
- Asthma with nasal polyps (AERD/N-ERD), peptic ulcer or prior GI bleeding, CKD, age 70 and older, G6PD deficiency, gout, pregnancy, and children with viral illness: see Safety and Contraindications.
- Lynch syndrome: a population with an RCT-supported cancer-prevention benefit [PMID:32534647].
*** Lab-test interference
- Platelet function tests: aspirin is designed to change them (serum TXB2, AA-induced aggregation, VerifyNow Aspirin, PFA). "Aspirin resistance" results agree poorly across assays and partly reflect platelet reactivity before treatment [PMID:18983514]. Enteric coating causes pseudoresistance [PMID:23212718].
- Serum uric acid: true physiological rise at low doses [PMID:10643705].
- Chloride and anion gap: high salicylate levels falsely raise chloride on direct ion-selective electrodes. This can produce a normal or negative anion gap that masks salicylate poisoning [PMID:33864761] [PMID:35971785].
- Hemoglobin and ferritin: true declines [PMID:37335992].

** Genotype notes
- LPA rs3798220-C carriers in ASPREE (n=12,815): MACE was higher on placebo (HR 1.90) but not on aspirin, with an interaction P of 0.049 [PMID:36175048]. Pooled carrier data gave HR 0.37-0.39, but results were inconsistent and certainty very low [PMID:42138694] [PMID:42018111].
- An integrative ischemic-stroke polygenic score in ASPREE: in the top quintile, aspirin was linked to ischemic stroke HR 0.49 without excess bleeding (post hoc) [PMID:42100829].
- G6PD deficiency (X-linked): risk of hemolysis [PMID:33313989] [PMID:38292218].
- Lynch syndrome (germline mismatch-repair variants): CRC prevention benefit [PMID:32534647] [PMID:42425127].
- Tumour somatic PIK3CA mutation: possible adjuvant benefit [PMID:41621633] [PMID:42475879].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] ASAC RCT: adjuvant 160 mg after CRC liver metastases did not improve DFS (HR 1.08). OS was worse (death HR 1.64) [PMID:42777749].
- [2026-07] ASCOLT translational study: no significant DFS benefit in PIK3CA-mutant CRC. Meta-analysis of exon 9/20 mutations: HR 0.61 [PMID:42475879].
- [2026-07] PPI meta-analysis (16 RCTs + 14 non-RCTs): upper GI bleeding RR 0.30 vs placebo; possible increase in lower GI bleeding [PMID:42479073].
- [2026-07] CaPP3 (n=1866, Lynch syndrome): 100 mg looked similar to 600 mg for cancer and caused fewer serious bleeds [PMID:42425127].
- [2026-05] Lp(a)/LPA meta-analysis: no significant MACE benefit (HR 0.60, 0.31-1.17) [PMID:42138694].
- [2026-05] ASPREE polygenic score: possible ischemic stroke benefit in the top genetic-risk quintile [PMID:42100829].
- [2026-04] Lp(a) SR/MA (GRADE very low): MACE HR 0.99 [PMID:42018111].
- [2026-03] ASPREE 10-year cancer follow-up: no effect on incidence; cancer mortality HR 1.15 during the trial with no legacy effect [PMID:41609798].
- [2026-01] Adjuvant NSAID meta-analysis in PIK3CA-mutated CRC: DFS HR 0.65 [PMID:41621633].
- [2025-12] UK Biobank: SSRI use with aspirin, GI bleeding aHR 1.27 [PMID:41435617].
- [2025-12] Pharmacol Rev synthesis: proposed platelet-TXA2 mechanisms in cancer [PMID:41592353].
- [2025-11] ASPREE extended follow-up: no long-term MACE benefit (HR 1.04). Major hemorrhage HR 1.24 [PMID:40796244].
- [2025-11] ASPREE individualized-effect model: CHIP >=10% VAF predicted cancer benefit (non-prespecified analysis) [PMID:40996732].
- [2025-11] Review of adult Reye syndrome and low-dose aspirin rechallenge: the evidence is sparse [PMID:41362566].

** Open questions
- Who benefits in primary prevention: Lp(a), polygenic scores, coronary calcium and CKD subgroups rest on post hoc or very-low-certainty data.
- Why aspirin raised cancer mortality in people 70 and older (ASPREE) but lowered delayed CRC risk in younger cohorts. Final Add-Aspirin results are pending.
- The best dose by bodyweight and formulation, especially enteric-coated vs immediate-release.
- Whether chronic low-dose aspirin is safe in G6PD deficiency. Prospective data are lacking.
- Supplement-aspirin bleeding interactions: most data are in vitro, short-term or surrogate-based, and no outcome trials exist (except omega-3).
- Whether PPI co-therapy increases lower GI bleeding. Current evidence is non-randomized only.
