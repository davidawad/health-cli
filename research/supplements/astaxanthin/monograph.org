** Identity & forms
- Xanthophyll carotenoid (3,3'-dihydroxy-beta,beta'-carotene-4,4'-dione) found in the microalgae /Haematococcus pluvialis/ and /Chlorella zofingiensis/, in /Chlorococcum/, and in the yeast /Phaffia rhodozyma/. /H. pluvialis/ accumulates up to 3.8% of its dry weight as astaxanthin [PMID:24402174].
- /H. pluvialis/ is the non-genetically-modified organism with the greatest capacity to accumulate astaxanthin and is the main source of "natural" astaxanthin. It accumulates the compound when it moves from its green motile stage to its red haematocyst stage [PMID:34424829].
- Astaxanthin also occurs in marine seafood [PMID:12885395]. Fish and crustaceans are the background dietary source considered by EFSA [PMID:32874213].
- Synthetic astaxanthin is chemically different from natural (algal) astaxanthin. Safety data for one should not be assumed for the other [PMID:31788888].
- Commercial dosage forms include tablets, capsules, syrups, oils, softgels, creams, biomass and granulated powders [PMID:24402174].
- Astaxanthin is highly lipophilic, and its oral bioavailability is low. In healthy men, lipid-based formulations raised bioavailability 1.7-3.7-fold over a reference supplement, and the elimination half-life was about 16 h (15.9 +/- 5.3 h) [PMID:12885395]. In a crossover study of an 8 mg dose, a micellar formulation reached a higher Cmax faster than native astaxanthin (7.21 vs 3.86 ug/mL; Tmax 3.67 vs 8.5 h) [PMID:38748358].

** Mechanisms
- Antioxidant: a review of in vitro data reports oxygen radical absorbance capacity (ORAC) 100-500 times higher than alpha-tocopherol. These are mechanistic laboratory measurements and do not show clinical benefit [PMID:33549728].
- Nrf2 activator. The NIA Interventions Testing Program (ITP) chose to test it for this reason [PMID:38041783].
- Anti-inflammatory: human meta-analyses show modest reductions in CRP and IL-6 in some subgroups (see the evidence table) [PMID:35091276] [PMID:33998846].
- Adiponectin: in RCTs, astaxanthin raised serum adiponectin, and the rise correlated with HDL-C changes [PMID:19892350] [PMID:29384321].
- Antithrombotic (animal and in vitro): in rats and mice, and in human platelets and liver cells in vitro, astaxanthin reduced platelet activation through ROS-PI3K/Akt/mTOR and MAPK/ERK pathways. It also reduced hepatic transcription of coagulation factors through ROS-responsive HNF4-alpha [PMID:41702374]. In rats given 10 mg/kg/day for 10 days, it lowered vitamin K levels and vitamin K-dependent factors (II, VII, IX, X) and prolonged PT and aPTT [PMID:32725659].

** Dosing in trials
- Typical trial doses are 4-20 mg/day for 4 weeks to 12 months.
  - Lipid trial: 6, 12 or 18 mg/day for 12 weeks [PMID:19892350].
  - Type 2 diabetes: 8 mg/day for 8 weeks [PMID:29384321].
  - PCOS: 12 mg/day (2 x 6 mg) [PMID:37874168].
  - Renal transplant recipients: 12 mg/day for 1 year [PMID:26675778].
  - Heart failure and rheumatoid arthritis: 20 mg/day [PMID:42303733] [PMID:40569081].
  - Children aged 10-14: 4 mg/day for 84 days [PMID:40014233].
- In the liver-enzyme meta-analysis, trials used 6-12 mg/day for 4-48 weeks [PMID:38407143].
- Subgroup analyses suggested larger aerobic-performance effects at >=20 mg/day and with >12 weeks of use [PMID:38243785]. CRP reduction was seen only at >12 mg/day or with >=12 weeks of use [PMID:32755613].
- A single 40 mg dose was used in a pharmacokinetic study [PMID:12885395].
- Absorption improves when astaxanthin is taken with fat or lipid formulations [PMID:12885395].
- Regulatory limits:
  - EFSA assessed 8 mg/day from food supplements [PMID:32874213].
  - Approved or recommended doses in different countries range from 2 to 24 mg/day [PMID:31788888].

** Safety & adverse effects
- A safety review covered 87 human studies of natural astaxanthin, 35 of them at >=12 mg/day. None found safety concerns [PMID:31788888].
- In an 8-week RCT of 6 mg/day from /H. pluvialis/ extract, there were no clinically relevant differences in a comprehensive metabolic panel, blood count or blood pressure [PMID:12804020].
- EFSA:
  - The current ADI is 0.2 mg/kg bw/day, set by the FEEDAP Panel in 2019. It replaced the 2014 ADI of 0.034 mg/kg bw.
  - With background diet plus 8 mg/day from supplements, intake is safe for adults.
  - Adolescents aged 14 to <18 reach the ADI.
  - The ADI is exceeded by 28% in children aged 10 to <14 and by up to 524% in infants aged 4-6 months [PMID:32874213].
- Brendler and Williamson argue that an EFSA ADI based on a rat study of synthetic astaxanthin should not be applied to natural astaxanthin [PMID:31788888].
- Liver enzymes: one meta-analysis found a small ALT rise (+1.92 U/L), of uncertain clinical significance [PMID:38407143]. A 2025 GRADE-assessed meta-analysis of carotenoids found no significant overall ALT change [PMID:41437050].
- Bleeding:
  - A case report described INR rising from 1.4 to 10.38, with ecchymosis, one day after astaxanthin was added to warfarin. Causality was rated probable [PMID:31073352].
  - In animals, astaxanthin lowered vitamin K-dependent clotting factors and prolonged PT and aPTT [PMID:32725659].
  - In animal thrombosis models, it inhibited platelets and coagulation but did not cause excessive bleeding [PMID:41702374].
- A 2025 meta-analysis found that all oral supplements studied for photoaging (typically for <=24 weeks) were safe [PMID:40761858]. In the pediatric eye-strain RCT, safety variables did not differ from placebo [PMID:40014233].

** Contraindications
- There are no established absolute contraindications in the cited sources.
- Caution applies with bleeding disorders and with anticoagulant therapy, based on a single case report and animal coagulation data [PMID:31073352] [PMID:32725659].
- Children and infants: EFSA found that 8 mg/day plus background diet exceeds the ADI in children under 14 [PMID:32874213].
- No pregnancy- or lactation-specific safety source was identified in this review.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin and other anticoagulants (caution): one probable case of a large INR rise with bleeding [PMID:31073352]. Animal data show inhibition of vitamin K-dependent factors [PMID:32725659] and of coagulation and platelets [PMID:41702374]. The interaction is supported mainly by mechanism and animal data, plus one human case.
- Antiplatelet agents (theoretical): in animal models astaxanthin inhibited platelet aggregation about as much as aspirin, so additive effects are possible [PMID:41702374].
- Antihypertensives (theoretical, minor): some RCTs found lower SBP [PMID:29384321] [PMID:36727984], but a meta-analysis found no significant effect on blood pressure [PMID:32755613].
- Antidiabetic agents (theoretical, minor): small FBG reductions in people with T2D (very low certainty) [PMID:39327454]. Fructosamine fell in one RCT [PMID:29384321].
- Orlistat (fat-absorption inhibitor): orlistat reduces absorption of beta-carotene and fat-soluble vitamins (FDA label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6240792b-9224-2d10-e053-2a91aa0a2c3e]. Astaxanthin absorption depends on fat [PMID:12885395]. That orlistat lowers astaxanthin absorption is an extrapolation and has not been measured.
*** Other supplements
- Fish Oil (Omega-3): in a meta-analysis of antioxidant lipid supplements, both n-3 fatty acids and astaxanthin lowered TG and raised HDL-C [PMID:39064656]. Lipid effects may add up. This is indirect evidence; the combination has not been tested.
- No cited data were found on other antioxidant stacks (e.g. Vitamin C, CoQ10).
*** Conditions
- Bleeding disorders and anticoagulated states: caution [PMID:31073352] [PMID:32725659].
- Children under 14: intake from supplements plus diet may exceed the EFSA ADI [PMID:32874213].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference was found. ALT may rise slightly as a true biological effect rather than an assay artifact (+1.92 U/L in one meta-analysis) [PMID:38407143]. INR should be monitored when astaxanthin is combined with warfarin [PMID:31073352].

** Latest research
- [2026-06] RCT in heart failure (20 mg/day for 8 wk, n=80): TNF-alpha and MCP-1 fell vs placebo; VLDL fell; no change in hs-CRP, other lipids or anthropometrics [PMID:42303733].
- [2026-05] SR/MA of 24 RCTs: astaxanthin lowered creatine kinase (SMD -0.45) and LDH (SMD -0.93, high heterogeneity). It had no effect on VO2max, time-trial performance, power, MDA or IL-6 [PMID:42197030].
- [2026-03] ANIMAL (ITP): a second test of astaxanthin at a different dose and later start age did not extend lifespan in UM-HET3 mice. A signal of reduced female lifespan disappeared after excluding an outlier control site [PMID:41843349].
- [2026-02] ANIMAL and in vitro: astaxanthin inhibited both platelet activation and coagulation-factor transcription and matched aspirin in thrombosis models, without excess bleeding [PMID:41702374].
- [2026-01] SR of 15 human studies (2020-2025): consistent reductions in inflammatory cytokines and oxidative-stress indices (qualitative synthesis, no pooled estimates) [PMID:41596351].
- [2025-12] GRADE-assessed dose-response MA of carotenoid RCTs (15 studies, n=757): no significant overall effect on liver enzymes [PMID:41437050].
- [2025-10] RCT in heart failure (20 mg/day for 8 wk): TAC and SOD rose; MDA and uric acid fell vs placebo [PMID:41162864].
- [2025-07] MA of 40 photoaging RCTs: insufficient evidence for astaxanthin or carotenoids; oral supplements were safe for <=24 weeks [PMID:40761858].
- [2025-07] RCT in rheumatoid arthritis (20 mg/day for 8 wk, n=60): DAS-28, HAQ and ESR improved [PMID:40569081].
- [2025-02] Industry-funded RCT in children (4 mg/day for 84 days, n=64): digital eye strain (CVS-Q) and visual fatigue improved [PMID:40014233].
- [2025-01] SR of 47 studies on oral photoprotection: carotenoid-based supplements are among those with the strongest evidence, but studies are small and short [PMID:39804624].
- [2024-09] MA of carotenoid RCTs on glycemia: astaxanthin and fucoxanthin lowered FBG by 4.36 mg/dL in T2D; certainty very low [PMID:39327454].
- [2024-07] MA of antioxidant lipid supplements: astaxanthin improved LDL-C, HDL-C, TC, TG and fasting insulin [PMID:39064656].
- [2024-05] PK crossover: a micellar formulation nearly doubled Cmax and shortened Tmax [PMID:38748358].
- [2024-02] MA of 5 RCTs: small ALT increase (+1.92 U/L); no effect on AST, GGT or ALP [PMID:38407143].
- [2024-01] MA of 11 RCTs: astaxanthin with training improved physical performance (SMD 0.62); effect on cognition was marginal [PMID:38243785].

** Open questions
- Is there any effect on hard outcomes (cardiovascular events, diabetes incidence)? The evidence base is surrogate biomarkers from small, short trials, many from a few centers or industry-funded [PMID:40014233] [PMID:36777084].
- How significant is the warfarin interaction clinically? It rests on one human case report plus animal data, and no human pharmacodynamic study exists [PMID:31073352] [PMID:32725659].
- Does the ITP male-lifespan signal replicate? It did not at other doses or start ages [PMID:38041783] [PMID:41843349].
- Should natural and synthetic astaxanthin have separate ADIs? [PMID:31788888] [PMID:32874213]
- What is the meaning of the small ALT rise? [PMID:38407143]
