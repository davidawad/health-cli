** Identity & forms
- "B complex" is a combination of the eight water-soluble B vitamins. They act as interrelated coenzymes in energy metabolism, DNA/RNA synthesis and repair, methylation, and neurotransmitter synthesis [PMID:26828517]:
  - B1 (thiamine)
  - B2 (riboflavin)
  - B3 (niacin as nicotinic acid, or niacinamide/nicotinamide)
  - B5 (pantothenic acid)
  - B6 (pyridoxine, pyridoxal, pyridoxamine; active form pyridoxal-5'-phosphate)
  - B7 (biotin)
  - B9 (folate)
  - B12 (cobalamin)
- Folate forms are not interchangeable [PMID:24494987]:
  - Folic acid is the synthetic, oxidized form used in fortification and most trials.
  - Folinic acid is another supplemental form.
  - 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF, "methylfolate") is the natural circulating form. It is claimed to be less likely to mask B12-deficiency anemia and not to depend on MTHFR activity. These claims come from a narrative review, not from outcome RCTs.
- Products differ widely. Many "B-50"/"B-100" or "stress" formulas supply B6 at 50-100 mg/day. That is far above the EFSA adult UL of 12 mg/day [PMID:37207271]. Biotin content varies, and high-dose biotin is the main source of immunoassay interference [PMID:35953126].
- Most of the hard-outcome evidence comes from trials of folic acid + B6 + B12 ("homocysteine-lowering B vitamins"), not from full eight-vitamin complexes. Trials of full B complexes are mostly small mood/stress studies [PMID:31527485].

** Mechanisms
- One-carbon metabolism: folate (as 5-MTHF), B12 (methionine synthase cofactor), B6 (cystathionine beta-synthase) and B2 (FAD, the cofactor of MTHFR) together remethylate or transsulfurate homocysteine [PMID:26828517]. Supplementation lowers plasma total homocysteine:
  - HOPE-2: about 2.4 umol/L vs placebo rise of 0.8 [PMID:16531613]
  - CSPPT: 11% relative reduction with 0.8 mg folic acid [PMID:29371246]
- Brain (human trial data plus mechanistic interpretation):
  - In the VITACOG MCI trial, B vitamins slowed whole-brain atrophy, and the effect depended on baseline homocysteine [PMID:20838622].
  - The cognitive benefit appeared only when omega-3 status was good (post hoc) [PMID:26757190].
  - A 2025 metabolomics re-analysis found lower quinolinic acid, glutamate, alpha-ketoglutarate and glucose with treatment [PMID:40684250].
- Neurotransmitters: B6 (as PLP) is the cofactor for synthesis of GABA, serotonin and dopamine. The authors of a high-dose B6 RCT proposed enhanced GABAergic inhibition as the mechanism [PMID:35851507] [PMID:42569537]. This is mechanistic interpretation, not proven.
- Biotin assay artifact: biotin in serum competes for streptavidin in biotin-streptavidin immunoassays [PMID:35953126]:
  - sandwich formats (e.g. TSH, troponin) read falsely low
  - competitive formats (e.g. free T4/T3) read falsely high
- Niacin (nicotinic acid) at pharmacologic doses lowers LDL and raises HDL, causes flushing, and (especially sustained-release forms) can cause hepatotoxicity [PMID:31643504] [PMID:12240702].

** Dosing in trials
- VITACOG (MCI, 24 months): folic acid 0.8 mg + B12 0.5 mg + B6 20 mg daily [PMID:20838622].
- HOPE-2 (vascular disease or diabetes, about 5 years): folic acid 2.5 mg + B6 50 mg + B12 1 mg daily [PMID:16531613] [PMID:19228852].
- VITATOPS (recent stroke/TIA, median 3.4 years): folic acid 2 mg + B6 25 mg + B12 0.5 mg daily [PMID:20688574] [PMID:20976769].
- CSPPT (hypertension, median 4.5 years): folic acid 0.8 mg + enalapril 10 mg as a single pill [PMID:25771069].
- Stroke dose-response MA: folic acid <=0.8 mg/d and B12 <=0.4 mg/d appeared most effective in non- or partially fortified regions (RR 0.65, 0.48-0.86) [PMID:38432716].
- Mood/stress: high-dose B-complex for 90 days in a work-stress RCT (n=60) [PMID:21905094].
- High-dose single-B mood RCTs:
  - B6 100 mg/day (pyridoxine) or B12 1,000 ug for 1 month [PMID:42569537] [PMID:35851507].
  - These B6 doses are about 8x the EFSA UL and are for short durations only.
- Methotrexate co-therapy: low-dose folic or folinic acid (starting dose <=7 mg/week) [PMID:24737913].
- Hemodialysis cognition RCT: thiamine 90 mg + folic acid 30 mg daily for 96 weeks [PMID:42059045].

** Safety & adverse effects
- In large trials, serious adverse events were not increased:
  - Cochrane: SAE RR 1.07 (1.00-1.14) [PMID:28816346]
  - VITATOPS: no unexpected serious adverse reactions [PMID:20688574]
  - HOPE-2: more hospitalisations for unstable angina (RR 1.24, 1.04-1.49) [PMID:16531613]
- *Vitamin B6 peripheral neuropathy* (main safety concern for high-B6 complexes):
  - Excess B6 causes a predominantly sensory, axonal neuropathy that tends to improve after the supplement is stopped [PMID:37447150].
  - EFSA (2023) set the adult UL at 12 mg/day, including pregnancy and lactation. This was based on a reference point of 50 mg/day from a case-control study and vigilance data, with an uncertainty factor of 4 for the inverse dose/time-to-onset relationship [PMID:37207271].
  - Netherlands: in 2018 the maximum daily dose in supplements was capped at 21 mg/day. Neuropathy reports then fell, but cases at lower doses were still reported. Dose explained only 15% of the variability in plasma B6 [PMID:39888171].
  - Australia (TGA): products containing >=50 mg pyridoxine are being rescheduled to Schedule 3 (pharmacist only), and products above 100 mg were withdrawn in 2023 [PMID:42503003].
  - Australian sales data for 2022-2025 show large volumes of >=50 mg products and frequent stacking of several products [PMID:42503003].
  - B6 neuropathy is among the prominent supplement adverse events in older-adult case reports [PMID:39747809].
- *Niacin*:
  - Immediate-release nicotinic acid commonly causes flushing. Sustained-release formulations are associated with hepatotoxicity [PMID:12240702].
  - LiverTox: high doses and certain formulations cause aminotransferase elevations and clinically apparent acute liver injury, which can be severe or fatal [PMID:31643504].
  - Observational/mechanistic (2024): the terminal niacin metabolites 2PY and 4PY were associated with 3-year MACE (4PY adjusted HR about 1.9-2.0), and 4PY induced VCAM-1 in mice [PMID:38374343]. Causality in humans is unproven.
- *Folic acid and B12 deficiency*:
  - High-dose folic acid can correct the anemia of B12 deficiency while neurological damage progresses ("masking"). It may also worsen B12 status. This evidence is largely correlative [PMID:38987872].
  - Folic acid product labeling states that doses above 0.1-0.4 mg/day may obscure pernicious anemia [URL:https://api.fda.gov/drug/label.json?search=set_id:%22025fee85-53a8-4161-a806-44fd95d0ca74%22].
- *Riboflavin*: harmless bright yellow urine. Oral riboflavin 400 mg reliably intensified urine yellow colour in an RCT [PMID:30633139].
- *Mendelian randomization (genetic-observational, 2025)*: mixed associations of genetically predicted B6, B12 and folate with neuropsychiatric disorders, some protective and some adverse [PMID:39952338]. This is not evidence about supplementation.

** Contraindications
- Pernicious anemia or untreated B12 deficiency: folic-acid-containing products are labelled as contraindicated or improper as sole therapy because they may obscure the diagnosis [URL:https://api.fda.gov/drug/label.json?search=set_id:%22025fee85-53a8-4161-a806-44fd95d0ca74%22] [PMID:38987872].
- Existing peripheral neuropathy or prior B6-associated neuropathy: avoid high-B6 products [PMID:37447150] [PMID:37207271].
- Active liver disease: avoid pharmacologic-dose nicotinic acid, especially sustained-release forms [PMID:31643504] [PMID:12240702].
- Upcoming immunoassay testing (thyroid, troponin, hormones): disclose high-dose biotin to the laboratory [PMID:31473202].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Levodopa: per the carbidopa/levodopa label, pyridoxine 10-25 mg orally may reverse levodopa's effects by speeding peripheral decarboxylation, and carbidopa inhibits this action [URL:https://api.fda.gov/drug/label.json?search=set_id:%22015bc056-ee86-4006-ae7c-689b95e566f4%22]. The interaction mainly matters for levodopa without a decarboxylase inhibitor.
- Methotrexate (low-dose, RA): folic or folinic acid is used intentionally to reduce GI and hepatic toxicity and MTX withdrawal [PMID:24737913].
- Phenytoin:
  - Phenytoin lowers serum folate.
  - Folic acid supplementation in folate-deficient patients can lower phenytoin levels, with possible seizure breakthrough [PMID:8520091].
  - The phenytoin label lists folic acid among drugs that may decrease phenytoin levels [URL:https://api.fda.gov/drug/label.json?search=set_id:%22035a8d4e-2063-4240-83cb-d7eebcabe301%22].
- Metformin: higher risk of B12 deficiency (RR 2.09, 1.49-2.93) and lower serum B12 (MD -63.7 pmol/L), depending on dose and duration [PMID:30615306].
- Proton pump inhibitors: modestly higher odds of B12 deficiency (OR 1.42, 1.16-1.73), with heterogeneous evidence [PMID:37060552].
- Antiplatelet therapy: in HOPE-2, the stroke benefit was larger in participants not taking antiplatelet or lipid-lowering drugs at enrolment (subgroup analysis) [PMID:19228852].

*** Other supplements
- Fish oil (omega-3): in VITACOG, the B-vitamin cognitive benefit was seen only with good baseline omega-3 (DHA) status (post hoc) [PMID:26757190].
- Stacking: taking several B6-containing products is common and can push total intake above ULs [PMID:42503003].
- Biotin: stand-alone biotin added to a B complex raises the risk of assay interference [PMID:35953126].

*** Conditions
- Pernicious anemia / B12 deficiency (see Contraindications) [PMID:38987872].
- Peripheral neuropathy [PMID:37447150].
- Food folic acid fortification: benefits on stroke and cognition were seen mainly in non-fortified populations [PMID:38432716] [PMID:39064642] [PMID:39155933].
- Chronic kidney disease on dialysis: an RCT of thiamine + folic acid showed improved cognition [PMID:42059045].

*** Lab-test interference
- Biotin and biotin-streptavidin immunoassays:
  - Falsely low TSH and troponin (sandwich formats); falsely high free T4/T3 (competitive formats). This can mimic Graves' disease [PMID:35953126] [PMID:31473202].
  - Roche hs-cTnT showed a negative bias of 24% at serum biotin 50 ug/L and 97% at 500 ug/L [PMID:30429682].
  - The US FDA received 92 reports of suspected biotin interference from 2016 [PMID:31473202].
  - The 2026 ETA thyroid guideline lists biotin as a pharmaceutical interferent [PMID:42466561].
  - Reviews flag thyroid and troponin tests specifically [PMID:41278416].
- Riboflavin causes bright yellow urine, a visual change only [PMID:30633139].

** Genotype notes
- MTHFR C677T in CSPPT [PMID:29371246]:
  - Homocysteine lowering with 0.8 mg folic acid varied by genotype.
  - TT carriers had a steeper L-shaped homocysteine-folate curve and needed serum folate of at least about 15 ng/mL to remove the genotype difference in homocysteine.
  - The trial randomized participants stratified by MTHFR genotype [PMID:25771069].
- MTHFR and riboflavin: the 2025 Cochrane review planned an MTHFR C677T subgroup analysis, but the overall evidence on riboflavin and blood pressure is very uncertain [PMID:41123035].
- Claims that 5-MTHF "overcomes" MTHFR polymorphisms come from a narrative review, not outcome trials [PMID:24494987].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Depress Anxiety RCT (n=267, 1 month): pyridoxine 100 mg/day reduced DASS depression scores vs placebo or B12 only in participants with at least mild baseline depression (n=58; partial eta-squared 0.20). There was no effect on anxiety or stress. The dose is about 8x the EFSA UL [PMID:42569537].
- [2026-08] ETA guideline on immunoassay interference in thyroid function testing names biotin among pharmaceutical interferents [PMID:42466561].
- [2026-07] Australian sales analysis (2022-2025): 16.75% of B6 product units were >=50 mg, and multiple/duplicate purchases were frequent. TGA rescheduling of >=50 mg products to pharmacist-only is pending [PMID:42503003].
- [2026-04] Hemodialysis RCT (n=215, 96 weeks): thiamine 90 mg + folic acid 30 mg improved ADAS-Cog vs placebo and lowered homocysteine [PMID:42059045].
- [2026-01] MA in coronary heart disease (13 studies, n=14,539): combined B vitamins lowered homocysteine (MD -2.36 umol/L) and restenosis (RR 0.65), with no effect on MACE or CV mortality [PMID:41615824].
- [2025-12] Nutr Rev MA (17 RCTs, n=5,275, age >=60): B6/B9/B12 gave a very small benefit on global cognition (g 0.11, high certainty after removing outliers and single-blind trials) [PMID:40966571].
- [2025-10] Cochrane: riboflavin alone for BP, very low-certainty evidence [PMID:41123035].
- [2025-09] RCT in cerebral small-vessel-disease cognitive impairment (n=220, 6 months): folic acid 0.8 mg/day improved MoCA and MMSE vs placebo and lowered homocysteine [PMID:40945966].
- [2025-07] VITACOG metabolomics: B vitamins reprogrammed TCA-cycle and glutamate metabolism [PMID:40684250].
- [2025-05] Network MA of nutraceuticals for depression: folate as an adjunct to antidepressants SMD 0.64 [PMID:40314175].
- [2025-02] Dutch pharmacovigilance: after the 21 mg/day cap on B6, reports involving high doses largely disappeared, but low-dose cases persisted [PMID:39888171].
- [2025-02] Mendelian randomization meta-analysis: heterogeneous B-vitamin associations with neuropsychiatric disorders [PMID:39952338].
- [2024-08] MA of folic acid for primary stroke prevention: overall RR 0.92 (0.80-1.05); RR 0.78 (0.68-0.89) in non-fortified areas [PMID:39155933].
- [2024-07] MA: folate-based B-vitamin benefit on cognition only in countries without mandatory fortification [PMID:39064642].
- [2024-06] Review: excess folic acid may mask or exacerbate B12 deficiency; vigilance advised in at-risk groups [PMID:38987872].
- [2024-03] MA: combined B vitamins reduced stroke 34% in non-fortified areas, with no benefit in fortified areas [PMID:38432716].
- [2024-02] Nat Med: niacin terminal metabolites 2PY/4PY were associated with MACE (observational plus mouse mechanistic work) [PMID:38374343].

** Open questions
- Whether full eight-vitamin B complexes add anything beyond B6/B9/B12 for cognition or vascular outcomes. Most outcome RCTs did not test full complexes.
- Whether B vitamins delay conversion from MCI to dementia, and whether omega-3 status modifies the effect. VITACOG findings were post hoc and await confirmation [PMID:26757190].
- The safe chronic B6 dose. Neuropathy is reported below 50 mg/day, and plasma levels correlate poorly with dose [PMID:39888171] [PMID:37207271].
- Clinical significance of niacin metabolites (4PY) for routine niacin/niacinamide intake [PMID:38374343].
- Whether genotype-guided (MTHFR) folate dosing improves hard outcomes [PMID:29371246].
- Not verified: official TGA, FDA and NIH ODS web pages could not be fetched (403/404/timeouts). The TGA scheduling details and the FDA biotin communication are cited via peer-reviewed secondary sources only.
