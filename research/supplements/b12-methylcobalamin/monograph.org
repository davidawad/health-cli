** Identity & forms
- Methylcobalamin (mecobalamin, MeCbl) is one of the two intracellular coenzyme forms of vitamin B12; the other is 5'-deoxyadenosylcobalamin (AdCbl). Supplements also use cyanocobalamin (CNCbl, synthetic, used in fortification) and hydroxocobalamin (OHCbl) [PMID:28223907] [PMID:36615431].
- Sold OTC as oral or sublingual tablets and lozenges. It is also widely used in Asia as a drug for peripheral neuropathy and hyperhomocysteinemia [PMID:18491996] [PMID:32716261]. Japan approved ultra-high-dose IM mecobalamin (Rozebalamin, 25 mg ampoules) for ALS [PMID:41765421] [PMID:41565302].
- This monograph covers methylcobalamin specifically. A separate "Vitamin B12" monograph covers cyanocobalamin and generic B12; general B12 evidence is summarized here because most outcome trials did not use MeCbl.
- Forms comparison:
  - All forms are reduced to a common cob(II)alamin core inside cells and then rebuilt into MeCbl and AdCbl in a ratio that does not depend on the form ingested. The methyl group of supplemental MeCbl is cleaved and not reused [PMID:28223907].
  - A form review favors "bioidentical" forms over CNCbl on safety and bioavailability grounds, but concludes that for most people all forms have similar bioavailability and effects [PMID:28223907].
  - In cell kinetics, OHCbl is accumulated and processed about 2-fold faster than CNCbl [PMID:38337663]. In a meta-regression, OHCbl lowered homocysteine more than other forms [PMID:37495210].
  - MeCbl is light-sensitive and less stable than CNCbl in products; label-content quality of B12 supplements varies [PMID:36615431].
  - No head-to-head human RCT was found showing that oral MeCbl corrects deficiency better than oral CNCbl. In a randomized 14-day study (n=20), 3 mg CNCbl raised plasma B12 faster at first, while MeCbl rose linearly [PMID:8914118].
- Absorption: B12 is absorbed through intrinsic factor (IF). High-dose oral therapy is an accepted alternative to injections for treating deficiency [PMID:28660890] [PMID:29543316].

** Mechanisms
- Cofactor for methionine synthase (homocysteine to methionine, linked to the folate cycle via 5-methyltetrahydrofolate). AdCbl is the cofactor for methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Deficiency raises homocysteine (Hcy) and methylmalonic acid (MMA) [PMID:28660890].
- Neural effects claimed for pharmacological MeCbl doses (mostly preclinical): myelination support, axonal regeneration, reduced ectopic firing, anti-glutamate neurotoxicity [PMID:32722436].
- ALS rationale: Hcy lowering and SAM-dependent methylation, mitochondrial protection, dose-dependent CNS effects at ultra-high doses [PMID:42657487] [PMID:41765421].
- Circadian: oral MeCbl 3 mg/d phase-advanced the melatonin rhythm by 1.1 h and lowered plasma melatonin in a crossover study [PMID:1516676]. It also lowered urinary aMT6s in healthy volunteers [PMID:8914118].
- Acne link: B12 represses B12 biosynthesis genes in Cutibacterium (Propionibacterium) acnes and increases inflammatory porphyrin production [PMID:26109103].

** Dosing in trials
- Deficiency repletion: 1000-2000 ug/d oral matched IM in RCTs [PMID:29543316]. In mild deficiency in older adults, 647-1032 ug/d oral CNCbl was needed for 80-90% of the maximal MMA reduction [PMID:15911731]. Sublingual 500 ug/d equaled oral 500 ug/d [PMID:14616423].
- Neuropathy: Chinese combination trials gave MeCbl IV or IM for 2-4 weeks [PMID:23664235]. Regimens in the mecobalamin MA were heterogeneous [PMID:32716261]. 1000 ug/d oral was given for 12 months in metformin-treated DPN [PMID:33513879].
- ALS: 25 or 50 mg IM in the phase 2/3 trial (n=373, up to 182 weeks); 50 mg IM twice weekly in JETALS [PMID:30636701] [PMID:35532908].
- Other: 3 mg/d oral for circadian studies [PMID:9413873]; 1.5 mg/d oral for hand-foot syndrome [PMID:40935571]; 250 ug/d in pregnancy [PMID:41850742]; 1000 ug/d with L-methylfolate 800 ug in hemodialysis [PMID:41580678].

** Safety & adverse effects
- No tolerable upper intake level has been set. Trials report few adverse events: none serious in the neuropathy MAs [PMID:32716261] [PMID:23664235], and AE rates similar to placebo in ALS trials [PMID:35532908] [PMID:30636701].
- ALS open-label extension (50 mg IM twice weekly, up to 52 wk, n=144): adverse drug reactions in 3.5%, including proteinuria (2.1%); none led to discontinuation [PMID:41475066].
- Acneiform eruption: a monomorphic papulopustular eruption after high-dose B12, including injections and IM hydroxocobalamin. It resolves after stopping [PMID:39712431] [PMID:37305488]. A mechanistic study found 1 of 10 supplemented volunteers developed acne within a week [PMID:26109103].
- Rosacea: rosacea fulminans has been reported with high-dose B6+B12 [PMID:11763399]. Dermatology reviews list acne, rosacea, injection-site reactions and anaphylaxis (injections, cobalt sensitivity) [PMID:25559140].
- Observational signals at high intake (not causal):
  - Hip fracture with combined high B6 (>=35 mg/d) and B12 (>=20 ug/d) intake: RR 1.47 (Nurses' Health Study) [PMID:31074816].
  - Lung cancer in men using >55 ug/d single-supplement B12: HR 1.98 (VITAL cohort) [PMID:28829668].
  - In B-PROOF, folic acid + B12 raised colorectal cancer risk at long-term follow-up (HR 1.77) [PMID:30341095].
- A scoping review found no consistent temporality or dose-response for B12 intake and cancer, and judged causality unproven [PMID:36364737].

** Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cobalt or cobalamin [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190] [PMID:25559140].
- Early Leber hereditary optic neuropathy: label warning for cyanocobalamin (severe, swift optic atrophy) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190]. No equivalent signal was found for MeCbl. B12 deficiency itself is more common in LHON carriers (21% vs 5-7%) and may precipitate optic neuropathy, so repletion with a non-cyano form is reasonable [PMID:35945620].
- Do not treat suspected B12 deficiency with folate alone, because it can correct the anemia while neurological damage progresses [PMID:38987872] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Metformin: B12 fell 19% vs placebo over 4.3 y in the HOME RCT (deficiency NNH 13.8) [PMID:20488910]. In DPP/DPPOS, deficiency risk rose with years of use (OR 1.13 per year) [PMID:26900641]. Meta-analyses give deficiency OR 2.45 and RR 2.09 [PMID:25502588]. Screening is recommended but uncommon in practice [PMID:42009383]. MeCbl 1 mg/d improved neuropathy in metformin users [PMID:33513879].
- PPIs/H2RAs: >=2 y use was associated with deficiency (PPI OR 1.65; H2RA OR 1.25; dose-dependent) [PMID:24327038]. A 2025 MA of 6 studies found no difference in serum B12 or Hcy [PMID:40823471].
- Nitrous oxide: inactivates B12 and causes a functional deficiency. Serum B12 can be normal while Hcy/MMA are high [PMID:34427020]. In an IPD-MA, 28% of cases had subacute combined degeneration [PMID:31018613]. Self-supplemented oral B12 did not prevent myelopathy in recreational users [PMID:35253567].
- Colchicine: reversibly reduces ileal IF-B12 receptors and causes B12 malabsorption [PMID:759260] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
- Capecitabine: a beneficial interaction. MeCbl reduced hand-foot syndrome and numerically reduced capecitabine dose reductions (7.7% vs 13.7%) in a phase 3 RCT [PMID:40935571].
- Antibiotics, methotrexate, pyrimethamine: invalidate microbiological folate/B12 assays (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
*** Other supplements
- Quatrefolic (5-MTHF) and folic acid: 5-MTHF is the methyl donor that methionine synthase passes to B12, so the two are co-dependent for Hcy remethylation. Folic acid + B12 lowered Hcy markedly in T2DM RCTs (SMD -2.77) [PMID:41393929]. MeCbl + L-methylfolate lowered Hcy in hemodialysis patients [PMID:41580678]. Folate alone can mask B12 deficiency and may worsen its neurology; this is disputed but widely cited [PMID:38987872] [PMID:32228273].
- Vitamin B12 / B Complex: duplicate cobalamin sources. Combined serum B12 can become very high without added benefit. Combined high B6+B12 intake was linked to hip fracture [PMID:31074816]. A B complex corrected deficiency as well as B12 alone [PMID:14616423].
- Vitamin B6: high-dose B6 arms raised hip fracture risk (HR 1.42) in extended follow-up of two large RCTs; folic acid + B12 had no effect [PMID:28574605]. B6+B12 single supplements were linked to lung cancer in male smokers [PMID:28829668]. Rosacea fulminans has been reported with B6+B12 [PMID:11763399].
- Trimethylglycine (TMG): an alternative betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase route. Betaine supplementation lowers Hcy (WMD -1.30 umol/L) but at >=4 g/d raises total and LDL cholesterol [PMID:33764214]. Additive Hcy lowering is plausible but not tested with MeCbl.
- Vitamin B2 (riboflavin): MTHFR cofactor. It lowers Hcy and raises SAM in MTHFR 677TT carriers, which complements B12 [PMID:12560354] [PMID:32330571].
- Vitamin C: the 1974 claim that ascorbate destroys food B12 [PMID:4479087] was not replicated by two laboratories using standard methods [PMID:1274888]. Stability reviews list reductants and other water-soluble vitamins among compounds that can affect B12 in products [PMID:36615431].
- Melatonin: MeCbl lowers melatonin levels and phase-advances the melatonin rhythm [PMID:1516676] [PMID:8914118]. The clinical relevance for melatonin users is unknown.
*** Conditions
- CKD:
  - High-dose B vitamins (folic acid 2.5 mg, B6 25 mg, CNCbl 1 mg) sped GFR loss (-5.8 mL/min) and doubled vascular events in diabetic nephropathy (DIVINe) [PMID:20424250].
  - Folic-acid-based Hcy lowering does not prevent CV events in CKD [PMID:22695899].
  - Spence argues that cyanide from CNCbl accumulates in renal failure and that MeCbl should be used instead. This is a hypothesis from secondary analyses [PMID:23449527] [PMID:33214054].
  - MMA is confounded by low eGFR [PMID:40164116].
- Leber hereditary optic neuropathy: see Contraindications.
- Pernicious anemia, gastrectomy, malabsorption: high-dose oral and sublingual routes raised B12 comparably to IM, including in gastrectomy subgroups [PMID:41487531] [PMID:29543316].
- Acne/rosacea-prone skin: see Safety.
*** Lab-test interference
- Serum B12 rises with supplementation, and a high value often reflects intake. Unexplained high B12 (not from supplements) is a marker of cancer, especially in the first year (SIR 6.27 at >800 pmol/L; IRR 4.72 at >1000 pmol/L). It is also associated with higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.04 per 100 pmol/L) [PMID:24249744] [PMID:30642843] [PMID:38252787].
- Macro-B12 (immunoglobulin-B12 complexes) falsely raises B12 on Cobas and Architect assays. It was found in 16-30% of hypercobalaminemic samples and is detected by PEG precipitation [PMID:31665115] [PMID:42142694].
- Anti-intrinsic-factor antibodies cause falsely normal or high B12 in pernicious anemia on competitive-binding assays, masking deficiency. MMA/Hcy should be checked when suspicion is high [PMID:26277634] [PMID:40964663].
- MMA rises with age and low eGFR; eGFR-adjusted MMA has been proposed [PMID:31127807] [PMID:40164116]. HoloTC has the best AUC in older adults (0.90) [PMID:21482749]. In N2O myelopathy, serum and active B12 can be normal [PMID:34427020].

** Genotype notes
- FUT2 (rs601338/rs602662, secretor status): a GWAS top hit for serum B12. It acts via haptocorrin-bound B12 (holoHC), not holoTC, so it shifts total serum B12 without changing the active fraction [PMID:19303062] [PMID:29040465].
- TCN2 776C>G (rs1801198, P259R): GG genotype has lower holoTC and higher Hcy when B12 is low [PMID:15941899]. In elders with high folate intake, GG had 6.9-fold odds of neuropathy [PMID:27733392].
- MTHFR C677T and MTRR A66G: in a B6/B9/B12 RCT for migraine, Hcy and disability responses were larger in C-allele and A-allele carriers [PMID:22926161]. MTHFR 677TT responds to riboflavin [PMID:32330571] [PMID:12560354].
- No genotype has been shown in trials to require MeCbl over other forms. The form review states that the relevant SNPs are not reported in commercial tests and the advantage is theoretical [PMID:28223907].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] SR of routes (13 studies): oral, sublingual and IM all raise serum B12; oral is often comparable or better; sublingual evidence is limited [PMID:42831074].
- [2026-08] HoloTC-based algorithm detects macro-B12, which was present in 16% of hypercobalaminemic samples [PMID:42142694].
- [2026-08] Review of high-dose MeCbl in ALS: benefit is dose-dependent and limited to early treatment [PMID:42657487].
- [2026-06] Cochrane (16 RCTs, children): B12 lowers deficiency (RR 0.27) with no effect on growth or cognition [PMID:42227307].
- [2026-04] Scoping review: B12 screening in long-term metformin use is uncommon [PMID:42009383].
- [2026-03] MATCOBIND RCT: 250 vs 50 ug/d MeCbl in vegetarian pregnancy improved infant mental DQ [PMID:41850742].
- [2026-03] B-vitamin CVD MA (13 RCTs, n=68,363): no overall MACE benefit; stroke RR 0.79 in primary prevention [PMID:41830012].
- [2026-02] Japanese ALS guideline addendum includes high-dose IM mecobalamin for early disease [PMID:41565302] [PMID:41765421].
- [2026-01] JETALS-era open-label extension (n=144, advanced ALS): no new safety issues [PMID:41475066].
- [2026-01] Hemodialysis RCT (n=51): L-methylfolate + MeCbl lowered Hcy [PMID:41580678].
- [2025-12] Routes MA (n=6098): no difference between oral, sublingual and IM; no dose-response [PMID:41487531].
- [2025-12] Cognition MA: very small B-vitamin benefit (g=0.11) in older adults [PMID:40966571].
- [2025-11] Folic acid + B12 in T2DM: large Hcy reduction (SMD -2.77); complication RR 0.30, with high heterogeneity [PMID:41393929].
- [2025-09] BMJ phase 3 RCT: MeCbl halved capecitabine hand-foot syndrome [PMID:40935571].
- [2025-09] Anti-IF antibody interference masked pernicious anemia with B12 >2000 pmol/L [PMID:40964663].
- [2025-08] PPI MA: no difference in serum B12 or Hcy [PMID:40823471].

** Open questions
- Whether oral MeCbl offers any clinical advantage over CNCbl or OHCbl. No adequately powered head-to-head RCT on functional markers or outcomes was found.
- Neuropathy efficacy of MeCbl monotherapy in B12-replete patients. Most trials are small, open, Chinese or Japanese, and use "clinical efficacy" composites.
- Whether JETALS results generalize beyond early, moderately progressing Japanese ALS patients. Approval outside Japan is unresolved.
- Causality of the high-B12 cancer and mortality associations (reverse causation vs genetic MR signals for lung and colorectal cancer) [PMID:30499135].
- Whether CNCbl-derived cyanide is clinically meaningful in CKD. This needs a randomized form comparison.
