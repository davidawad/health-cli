** Identity & forms
- Methylcobalamin is one supplemental form of vitamin B12; other forms include cyanocobalamin, hydroxocobalamin and adenosylcobalamin. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:28223907]
** Mechanisms
- B12 cofactor for methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase; deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia and neurologic injury. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:28223907]

** Dosing in trials
- Oral treatment trials often use 1000-2000 mcg/day; route/dose depend on cause, severity and absorption. No evidence methylcobalamin is generally superior. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:28223907]
** Safety & adverse effects
- Usually well tolerated; injection reactions uncommon. High-dose chronic use is not known to cause toxicity, but unnecessary supplementation has no proven benefit. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:28223907]
** Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cobalt/cobalamin; evaluate optic neuropathy/leber hereditary optic neuropathy risk with high-dose B12. Do not treat folate alone when B12 deficiency possible. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:28223907]
** Interactions
- Metformin and proton-pump inhibitors can contribute to low B12 status; chloramphenicol may blunt hematologic response. Nitrous oxide inactivates B12. MMA interpretation affected by renal impairment. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:28223907]
** Genotype notes
- A review of B12 forms/genetic variants found no evidence that common SNPs establish a need for methylcobalamin over other forms [PMID:28223907].
