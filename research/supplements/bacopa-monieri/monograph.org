** Identity & forms
- Bacopa monnieri (L.) Wettst. (traditionally placed in Scrophulariaceae) is a creeping wetland herb [PMID:18478974]. Ayurveda classes it as a "Medhya Rasayana" (intellect promoter) and uses it for memory, anxiety, insomnia and epilepsy [PMID:36943953] [PMID:28588366] [PMID:40507208].
- Main bioactives are dammarane-type triterpenoid saponins (bacosides). "Bacoside A" is a mixture of bacoside A3, bacopaside II, the jujubogenin isomer of bacopasaponin C (bacopaside X) and bacopasaponin C [PMID:15688952] [PMID:29676230]. Wild Indian samples contained 0.05-0.85% (w/w) of each, so bacoside content varies widely [PMID:15688952].
- Branded extracts used in trials: CDRI-08 (marketed as KeenMind; Synapsa is a product name with no trial found by that name): >=55% bacosides [PMID:27401287]. Used at 300 mg/d (2 x 150 mg) for 90 days [PMID:18683852] [PMID:11498727], 320-640 mg acutely [PMID:23281132] [PMID:23788517], and in children with ADHD-type symptoms [PMID:35041248]. BacoMind: an enriched, multi-marker extract; 300-450 mg/d in adults [PMID:17442556] [PMID:20590480] and 225 mg/d in children [PMID:24682000]. Bacognize: 150 mg twice daily [PMID:27803728]. Bacumen: 300 mg/d [PMID:41091332].
- Traditional forms include Brahmi vati tablets [PMID:39631219], ghee-based Brahmi Ghrita [PMID:42102930], freeze-dried decoction [PMID:41836921] and juice-based "Brahmi essence" [PMID:34063409].
- Authenticity: a qPCR barcode assay found that 60% of Bacopa/Brahmi-labelled products were adulterated (Bacopa absent or reduced) [PMID:41008247].
- Pharmacokinetics: human data are minimal. After 12 weeks of Brahmi essence, jujubogenin and pseudojujubogenin aglycones appeared in feces [PMID:34063409]. Poor solubility and permeability have prompted phospholipid complexes and nano-formulations [PMID:27590125] [PMID:42353204].

** Mechanisms
- Cholinergic: a 12-week RCT in elderly adults reduced plasma AChE activity [PMID:23320031]. In animal models the herb reverses scopolamine (anticholinergic) amnesia [PMID:19757037] and is reported to have anticholinesterase activity comparable to donepezil, rivastigmine and galantamine [PMID:28588366].
- Amyloid and oxidative stress: lowered cortical Abeta 1-40/1-42 by up to 60% in PSAPP mice, without changing fibrillar plaque area [PMID:16914834]. Metal-reducing, ROS-scavenging and lipoxygenase-inhibiting activity [PMID:17604373]. Bacosides act via antioxidant enzymes and AMPA/NMDA/GABA receptor regulation [PMID:29676230].
- GABAergic and serotonergic: restores GABA-A/B receptor expression in epileptic rats [PMID:22364254] and modulates GABA-A subunit genes in alcohol-withdrawal anxiety models [PMID:30841457]. Serotonergic actions are reviewed in [PMID:26413131]. BacoMind inhibited COMT, prolyl endopeptidase and PARP and antagonized 5-HT6/5-HT2A receptors in vitro [PMID:27761079].
- Stress axis: a single 320-640 mg dose lowered salivary cortisol during a multitasking stressor [PMID:23788517]. A systematic review of human HPA-axis trials found the plant data inconsistent overall [PMID:33650944].
- Vascular and smooth muscle: endothelial NO-dependent vasodilation and hypotension in rats (bacoside A3, bacopaside II) [PMID:21762768]. Calcium-channel antagonism in gut and vascular smooth muscle [PMID:10433473]. In a 3-month open-label study, serum BDNF did not change, but pCREB rose and NF-kB p65 phosphorylation fell [PMID:37032999].

** Dosing in trials
- Adults, chronic: 300-450 mg/d of standardized extract (often 50-55% bacosides) for at least 12 weeks [PMID:22747190] [PMID:24252493]. Effects emerge by 5-12 weeks, with maximal effects at 12 weeks [PMID:11498727].
- Higher dose: >=600 mg/d ranked best for working memory in an NMA [PMID:41678913]. 300 vs 600 mg/d was tested in elderly adults [PMID:23320031].
- Children: 225 mg/d BacoMind for 6 months [PMID:24682000]. CDRI-08 was dosed for 14-16 weeks in boys aged 6-14 [PMID:26633481] [PMID:35041248].
- Brahmi vati: 500 mg three times daily for 60 days, combined with other Ayurvedic preparations [PMID:39626587].
- Longest planned trial: 12 months of 300 mg/d CDRI-08 (ARCLI) [PMID:22390677].
- GI effects are the main reason people stop [PMID:20590480] [PMID:37032999].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects are the most consistent adverse events: increased stool frequency, abdominal cramps and nausea vs placebo [PMID:20590480]. More digestive complaints and headaches with Bacumen (p=0.024) [PMID:41091332]. 11% stopped early for nausea or diarrhea in an open-label study [PMID:37032999]. Mild GI events in a phase I dose-escalation study [PMID:17442556]; stomach upset in elderly adults [PMID:18611150].
- Children: mild side effects in 2.3% of participants across trials [PMID:27912958].
- Toxicology: BacoMind rat LD50 was 2400 mg/kg and the 90-day NOAEL 500 mg/kg [PMID:17560704]. It was not clastogenic in human lymphocytes [PMID:18478974].
- Liver: LiverTox reports that Bacopa has not been linked to liver-enzyme elevations or clinically apparent liver injury [PMID:38718157]. The only case found was severe hepatitis after a multi-product Ayurvedic regimen. "Brahmi tablets" (Bacopa or Eclipta) scored as a possible secondary cause, but psoralen-containing Bakuchi was the probable cause [PMID:19841509]. In Brahmi vati trials, liver tests stayed normal [PMID:39631219] [PMID:39626587].
- Thyroid: Bacopa raised serum T4 by 41% in male mice [PMID:12065164] and reversed pesticide-induced hypothyroidism in female mice [PMID:41169277]. No human thyroid data were found. Reviews advise caution in hyperthyroidism [PMID:40507208].
- Male fertility (animal data conflict): Brahmi 250 mg/kg/d for 28-56 days reversibly suppressed spermatogenesis and fertility in mice, without changing testosterone [PMID:19041444]. CDRI-08 at 40-80 mg/kg improved sperm quality in mice [PMID:27401287] [PMID:29054405]. No human data were found.
- Heavy metals and contamination: Bacopa accumulates metals. Wild Indian samples held 1.3-6.7 mg/kg Cd and 1.5-22 mg/kg Pb, and most exceeded limits for medicinal raw material [PMID:23824547]. 20% of South-Asian Ayurvedic products in Boston stores contained Pb, Hg or As [PMID:15598918]. 20.7% of Ayurvedic medicines sold online did too, including US-made products [PMID:18728265]. Case reports describe severe pediatric lead poisoning [PMID:41244251], fatal adult lead toxicity [PMID:40978857] and arsenic poisoning [PMID:37492071] from Ayurvedic products. Another analysis found metal levels in Bacopa extracts within permissible limits [PMID:27030208]. Product source and testing matter.
- Calcium: serum calcium rose but stayed within the normal range in a 6-week RCT [PMID:27803728].

** Contraindications
- Pregnancy and lactation: safety data are lacking [PMID:40507208]. No teratogenicity signal was found in experimental studies [PMID:28588366].
- Hyperthyroidism: animal T4-raising effect [PMID:12065164] [PMID:40507208].
- Active GI disease (e.g., peptic ulcer, IBS-D, obstruction): dose-related GI effects [PMID:20590480] [PMID:41091332]. Calcium-antagonist spasmolytic and cholinergic actions [PMID:10433473] [PMID:23320031].
- Bradycardia, asthma/COPD and urinary obstruction: these are theoretical cholinergic concerns based on AChE suppression [PMID:23320031]. No clinical reports were found.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine): additive cholinergic effect is possible because Bacopa lowers AChE [PMID:23320031] [PMID:28588366]. Head-to-head trials vs donepezil reported no major safety issues [PMID:35612544].
- Muscarinic agonists (cevimeline, pilocarpine, bethanechol): one case of suspected cholinergic toxicity (sweating, nausea, tachycardia) with cevimeline plus a Bacopa/phosphatidylserine supplement [PMID:35765109].
- Anticholinergic drugs (scopolamine, oxybutynin, tricyclics): pharmacodynamic opposition. Bacopa reverses scopolamine amnesia in rodents [PMID:19757037] [PMID:26677075]. Clinical relevance is unknown.
- Sedatives/CNS depressants (benzodiazepines, alcohol, hypnotics): GABAergic and anxiolytic actions [PMID:22364254] [PMID:30841457]. Traditional use is as a calming agent [PMID:36943953]. Additive effects are theoretical. Bacopa also eased diazepam-induced amnesia in rodents [PMID:19757037].
- CYP3A4 substrates: In vitro, the extract (not isolated bacosides) inhibited CYP2C19 (Ki 9.5 ug/mL), CYP2C9, CYP1A2 and CYP3A4. Estimated gut concentrations would cut CYP3A4/2C9/2C19 activity to <10% [PMID:24566323]. In rats, 31 mg/kg/d for 7 days raised carbamazepine AUC 4-fold [PMID:24015255]. Another in vitro study found only weak inhibition (CYP2C9 IC50 185 ug/mL) [PMID:27030208]. No human probe-drug study was found. CYP2C19/2C9/1A2 substrates (e.g., clopidogrel, PPIs, warfarin, clozapine) carry the same unproven in vitro signal [PMID:24566323].
- P-gp substrates (digoxin): rat digoxin AUC rose 1.3-fold [PMID:24015255]. Bacopaside II inhibited P-gp in vitro [PMID:25869246].
- Antidepressants/serotonergic drugs: a pharmacovigilance series reported one serotonin syndrome after Bacopa was added to SSRI/SNRI therapy [PMID:30659561]. Chart review cases included agomelatine (hyperhidrosis) and moclobemide (MI) [PMID:37829299]. Bacopa potentiated citalopram in one study [PMID:33436817].
- Thyroid hormone (levothyroxine): T4 rose in mice [PMID:12065164]. Check TFTs. No human data.
- Antihypertensives/calcium-channel blockers: vasodilator and calcium-antagonist actions [PMID:21762768] [PMID:10433473]. A 320-640 mg dose did not change cardiovascular measures in humans [PMID:23281132]. Phenytoin: in mice, Bacopa reversed phenytoin cognitive impairment without reducing anticonvulsant effect [PMID:10940574].
*** Other supplements
- Citicholine and Alpha-GPC (choline donors): cholinergic overlap. Choline/phosphatidylserine-plus-Bacopa combinations were neuroprotective in preclinical models only [PMID:37762339] [PMID:41751239]. Watch for additive cholinergic GI effects [PMID:20590480].
- Ashwagandha: often co-formulated [PMID:39683490] [PMID:39631219]. Both may raise T4 [PMID:12065164] [PMID:28829155], and both are adaptogens in antidepressant AE reports [PMID:37829299].
- Caffeine: CYP1A2 metabolizes ~95% of caffeine [PMID:29514871]. Bacopa inhibits CYP1A2 in vitro [PMID:24566323], so caffeine exposure could rise (theoretical). A Bacopa-ginseng-coffee-fruit formula was tolerated [PMID:38512715].
- L-Theanine: both are studied for ADHD and attention [PMID:40362791]. No interaction study was found; additive calming is theoretical.
- Rhodiola Rosea: both appear among cognition supplements with low-certainty evidence [PMID:34370563]. Rhodiola was reported in herb-psychotropic adverse interactions [PMID:30659561]. No direct Bacopa-Rhodiola data were found. Ginkgo: a 4-week Ginkgo-Bacopa combination showed no cognitive effect [PMID:14994318].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy/lactation, hyperthyroidism, GI disease, male fertility planning: see Contraindications and Safety [PMID:19041444] [PMID:40507208].
- Children: products may contain lead [PMID:41244251] [PMID:15598918]. Choose third-party-tested products.
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact found. Physiological changes are possible in thyroid tests (animal T4 rise) [PMID:12065164], plasma AChE (lower) [PMID:23320031] and serum calcium (small rise) [PMID:27803728]. Measure blood lead if a contaminated Ayurvedic product is suspected [PMID:40978857].

** Genotype notes
No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source found for Bacopa itself. A case report notes that CYP polymorphisms may modify herb-drug interactions [PMID:35765109]. CYP1A2 variants change caffeine clearance [PMID:29514871].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] PRISMA-based AD review: bacosides act on cholinergic, amyloid, tau/GSK-3beta, BDNF and NF-kB pathways; clinical gains are modest [PMID:42616216]. A pharmacology review of the same month [PMID:42852756].
- [2026-08] Ayurveda insomnia review (2016-2026 literature): standardized ashwagandha has the most rigorous sleep data; Bacopa sleep claims rest on weaker evidence [PMID:42739755] [PMID:41830548].
- [2026-07] SR of plant therapies in Parkinson disease: Bacopa improved emotional symptoms (Grade B) [PMID:42489905].
- [2026-06] Node-resolved Ayurvedic SR/MA (49 reports): Bacopa dominates the cognition evidence, but heterogeneity and small-study effects limit confidence [PMID:42359794]. Neuroprotection appraisal flags bioavailability and formulation problems [PMID:42353204].
- [2026-05] 1H-MRS pilot: Brahmi Ghrita partly reversed age-related cingulate metabolite changes (n=9/arm) [PMID:42102930].
- [2026-04] NMA of 29 RCTs (n=2107): >=600 mg/d Bacopa ranked first for working memory vs Ginkgo [PMID:41678913].
- [2026-02] SR of 7 RCTs: no Bacopa benefit on processing speed, attention or working memory [PMID:41575193]. Protocols published for an aMCI RCT [PMID:41740144] and an elderly decoction RCT [PMID:41836921]. Preclinical Bacopa-phosphatidylserine-choline study [PMID:41751239].
- [2026-01] NMA in healthy older adults: Bacopa compound ranked high for executive and language function [PMID:41640686].
- [2025-12] Bacumen RCT (n=101, 12 wk): cognition null; less stress reactivity and fatigue; more GI AEs [PMID:41091332].
- [2025-10] Pediatric lead-poisoning case from an Ayurvedic product (BLL 123 ug/dL) [PMID:41244251]. ASD review flags heavy-metal accumulation as a drawback [PMID:40769392].
- [2025-09] qPCR authentication: 60% of Bacopa products were adulterated [PMID:41008247].
- [2025-08] Fatal lead toxicity from Ayurvedic supplements [PMID:40978857].
- [2025-07] RCT in 180 boys: Brahmi raised the total memory score by 9.3%, equivalent to Centella [PMID:40609195].
- [2025-06] Review: generally non-toxic; caution in hyperthyroidism and pregnancy [PMID:40507208].
- [2025-04] ADHD supplement SR: Bacopa consistently improved inattention and hyperactivity, but cannot replace stimulants [PMID:40362791].
- [2024-11] Brahmi vati RCTs vs escitalopram in MDD and GAD; normal LFTs [PMID:39631219] [PMID:39626587]. Bacopa-ginseng-coffee-fruit RCT improved delayed recall [PMID:38512715]. Schizophrenia SR (preclinical) [PMID:39498770].

** Open questions
- Whether effects persist with independently funded, preregistered, adequately powered trials. Recent RCTs are null on primary cognitive endpoints [PMID:41091332] [PMID:41575193].
- Human pharmacokinetics of bacosides and clinically relevant CYP/P-gp interactions. No human probe-drug study was found [PMID:24566323] [PMID:24015255].
- Thyroid and male reproductive effects in humans [PMID:12065164] [PMID:19041444].
- Extract equivalence, adulteration and heavy-metal testing standards [PMID:41008247] [PMID:23824547].
- Efficacy in MCI/AD with clinically meaningful outcomes [PMID:35612544] [PMID:41740144].
- Long-term (12-month) data from ARCLI [PMID:22390677]. Pediatric ADHD efficacy beyond secondary outcomes [PMID:35041248] [PMID:29165401].
