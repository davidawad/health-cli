** Identity & forms
- Berberine is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: berberine hydrochloride, Berberis alkaloid. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:40269802, PMID:39640489].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:40269802, PMID:39640489].

- Overview of 54 systematic reviews assessed berberine across multiple conditions; methodological quality and certainty varied, with many reviews low/critically low quality [PMID:40269802]. Diabetes meta-analysis found improvements in glycemic markers, but substantial heterogeneity and variable co-interventions limit inference [PMID:39640489].
- Glycemic and lipid surrogate markers may improve, particularly in type 2 diabetes/metabolic syndrome; evidence does not establish prevention of complications or equivalence to standard medicines [PMID:40269802, PMID:39640489].

** Dosing in trials
- Trials frequently use 0.9–1.5 g/day divided into 2–3 doses for 8–24 weeks, often berberine hydrochloride; formulations vary [PMID:39640489].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects (constipation, diarrhea, nausea, abdominal discomfort) are common; short trials provide limited long-term safety evidence [PMID:40269802].

** Contraindications
- Avoid in pregnancy, breastfeeding and neonates because of bilirubin displacement/kernicterus concern; use particular caution in hepatic disease [PMID:40269802].

** Interactions
- May add to glucose-lowering therapy; human PK evidence indicates effects on CYP enzymes and P-glycoprotein, so interactions with narrow-therapeutic-index drugs are plausible [PMID:40269802].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2025 overview of 54 systematic reviews found substantial methodological limitations and uneven evidence quality [PMID:40269802].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
