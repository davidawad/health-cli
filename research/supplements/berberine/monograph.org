** Identity & forms
- Berberine is an isoquinoline alkaloid found in Berberis spp. (barberry), Coptis chinensis (huanglian) and Hydrastis canadensis (goldenseal) [PMID:42653808] [PMID:8513024]. It is sold as a metabolic-health supplement, sometimes marketed as "nature's Ozempic" [PMID:42653808].
- Most trials use purified berberine hydrochloride or chloride at 0.9-1.5 g/d [PMID:39640489] [PMID:36941490].
- Oral bioavailability is very low: under 1% [PMID:36221815], and 0.68% in rats, with low ng/mL plasma levels in humans [PMID:42653808]. The main circulating metabolite in humans is berberrubine, which is more lipophilic and better absorbed than berberine itself [PMID:24593257].
- Elimination: fecal excretion (11-23%) exceeds urinary and biliary excretion of the parent compound [PMID:36221815]. CYP2D6 is the main human CYP forming its unconjugated metabolites, followed by CYP1A2, 3A4, 2E1 and 2C19 [PMID:21787170].
- Enhanced-absorption forms:
  - Dihydroberberine: 100 mg gave a higher plasma berberine Cmax than 500 mg berberine (3.76 vs 0.4 ng/mL) in a 5-person crossover pilot [PMID:35010998].
  - Micellar berberine (LipoMicel): 1000 mg/d for 30 days showed no safety-marker changes (n=19) [PMID:40278369].
  - Berberine ursodeoxycholate (HTD1801): an investigational salt studied as a drug in phase 2 trials [PMID:40029660].
- Goldenseal products contain berberine plus other alkaloids, so goldenseal interaction data are only partly transferable to purified berberine [PMID:33174626] [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/goldenseal].

** Mechanisms
- AMPK activation: in cells and rodents, berberine activated AMPK, increased GLUT4 translocation and lowered lipogenic gene expression [PMID:16873688].
- Glucose-dependent insulinotropic effect: berberine is proposed to inhibit beta-cell voltage-gated K+ channels and lower glucose mainly under hyperglycemic conditions [PMID:36467075].
- Gut microbiome: in the PREMOTE RCT, the glucose-lowering effect was linked to inhibited deoxycholic acid biotransformation by Ruminococcus bromii [PMID:33024120]. A 2026 systematic review of 7 RCTs found consistent shifts in microbiota composition, not all of them favorable (e.g. depletion of butyrate producers) [PMID:42356246].
- LDL receptor upregulation: berberine stabilizes LDLR mRNA through an ERK-dependent, SREBP-independent mechanism that differs from statins [PMID:15531889]. It is also described as a natural PCSK9 inhibitor [PMID:37183391].
- Alpha-glucosidase: computational docking showed weaker binding than acarbose (exploratory only) [PMID:42213651].
- Sex hormones: lowered total testosterone in women (-0.42 SD) and raised it in men (+0.43 SD) in paired RCTs [PMID:42470424]. A proteomic and sex-hormone "berberine signature" was associated with lower ischemic heart disease and diabetes risk in Mendelian randomization [PMID:41882153].
- Drug-metabolism liability:
  - In humans, 300 mg three times daily for 2 weeks inhibited CYP3A4, CYP2D6 and CYP2C9 [PMID:21870106].
  - CYP2D6 inhibition is quasi-irreversible, through a metabolite-intermediate complex [PMID:32987920].
  - Berberine inhibits intestinal P-gp in rats [PMID:19370549].
  - Reviews add OCT1/2 and MATE1 effects, possible CYP3A4 induction, and pharmacodynamic axes: hypoglycemia, hypotension and QT prolongation [PMID:42653808] [PMID:39141022].

- Overall evidence quality: an overview of 54 SRs rated only 1 as high quality by AMSTAR-2 (45 critically low or very low). Of the GRADE-rated outcomes, 8 were high, 22 moderate and 110 low [PMID:40269802]. An earlier umbrella review called for better-quality RCTs [PMID:36999891]. Most trials were done in China, and many are short (1-3 months) [PMID:39640489] [PMID:36941490].

** Dosing in trials
- Glucose/lipids: 0.9-1.5 g/d in 2-3 divided doses for 1-3 months is typical [PMID:39640489] [PMID:36941490]. Examples:
  - 0.5 g three times daily vs metformin [PMID:18442638].
  - 1.0 g/d vs placebo in T2D with dyslipidemia [PMID:18397984].
  - 500 mg twice daily for 12 weeks in the sex-specific lipid RCTs [PMID:42470424].
- MASLD/obesity: 1 g/d for 6 months [PMID:41543854]; 1500 mg/d for 12 weeks in MAFLD [PMID:41305635].
- Colorectal adenoma prevention: 0.3 g twice daily for 2 years [PMID:31926918].
- Combinations used in trials: berberine 500 mg + red yeast rice 200 mg + policosanol 10 mg [PMID:19699071]; berberine 1200 mg + cinnamon 600 mg [PMID:39998703]; berberine 1000 mg + white mulberry + chromium [PMID:42738972].
- HTD1801 (berberine ursodeoxycholate): 500 or 1000 mg twice daily lowered HbA1c by 0.4% and 0.7% vs placebo [PMID:40029660].
- Short-term safety only: the longest randomized exposure is 2 years (adenoma trial) [PMID:31926918].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Gastrointestinal: the most common AEs are constipation, diarrhea, nausea and abdominal discomfort.
  - GI AEs occurred in 2-23% on berberine vs 2-15% on placebo across 12 RCTs; no serious AEs [PMID:36941490].
  - Transient GI AEs affected 34.5% in an open diabetes study [PMID:18442638].
  - In PREMOTE, berberine caused more GI side effects than placebo [PMID:33024120].
  - In the adenoma trial, constipation was most common (1% vs <0.5%), with no serious AEs [PMID:31926918]. A meta-analysis of 3 adenoma RCTs found more AEs with berberine (RR 2.91, CI 1.24-6.85), mainly constipation that resolved after stopping [PMID:34509605].
- Placebo-controlled metabolic MAs found no significant difference in overall AEs vs placebo [PMID:40740996]. Hypoglycemia was not significantly increased, alone or with oral hypoglycemics (RR 0.48, CI 0.21-1.08) [PMID:36467075].
- Liver/kidney: no hepatic or renal damage in early diabetes trials [PMID:18442638]. Liver enzymes fell in NAFLD MAs [PMID:38429794]. Safety markers did not change with a micellar form over 30 days [PMID:40278369]. The 6-month MASLD RCT showed similar AE rates to placebo [PMID:41543854].
- QT prolongation / torsades de pointes: a 2026 case report described a 36-year-old woman with prolonged QT and cardiac arrest from TdP after months of online-purchased berberine. The ECG normalized after stopping [PMID:42488242]. Reviews list QT prolongation as a pharmacodynamic interaction axis [PMID:42653808].
- Skin: a case of symmetrical drug-related intertriginous and flexural exanthema (SDRIFE) attributed to berberine [PMID:29808929].
- Neonatal kernicterus risk:
  - On a molar basis, berberine displaced bilirubin from albumin about 10-fold more potently than phenylbutazone in vitro.
  - In rats it raised unbound and total bilirubin.
  - The author advised avoiding it in jaundiced neonates and pregnant women [PMID:8513024].
- Product quality: unstandardized products and national limits that differ more than 10-fold across Europe add exposure uncertainty [PMID:42653808].

** Contraindications
- Pregnancy: berberine displaces bilirubin [PMID:8513024]. NCCIH advises that pregnant or breastfeeding women should not use goldenseal and that its berberine constituent can harm newborns [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/goldenseal].
- Breastfeeding: there are no milk-transfer data. LactMed notes bilirubin-displacement concern and that most sources recommend avoiding neonatal exposure [PMID:30000926].
- Neonates and infants, especially if jaundiced: kernicterus risk [PMID:8513024] [PMID:30000926].
- Transplant recipients on cyclosporine, tacrolimus or sirolimus without level monitoring: berberine raised cyclosporine levels [PMID:16133554]. See Interactions.
- Long QT or QT-prolonging therapy: case of TdP [PMID:42488242] (single report; caution rather than absolute).

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- CYP3A4 substrates: berberine 300 mg three times daily for 2 weeks raised midazolam AUC 40% and Cmax 38%, and cut oral clearance 27% [PMID:21870106].
  - Goldenseal (berberine-standardized) raised midazolam AUC GMR 1.43 (90% CI 1.35-1.53) [PMID:33174626].
  - Relevant to narrow-therapeutic-index CYP3A4 substrates (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, sirolimus) [PMID:42653808].
- Cyclosporine: RCT in 104 renal-transplant recipients given 0.2 g three times daily for 3 months. Trough was 29.3% higher than in controls [PMID:16133554].
  - PK substudy (n=6): AUC +34.5%, Cmin +88.3%, CL/F -40.4% [PMID:16133554].
  - In rats, berberine raised oral cyclosporine AUC by 62-96% through intestinal P-gp inhibition [PMID:19370549].
- Sirolimus (rapamycin) and tacrolimus: no direct human study was found. Berberine inhibits CYP3A4 and P-gp [PMID:21870106] [PMID:19370549], and reviews flag immunosuppressants as high-risk victim drugs [PMID:42653808].
- CYP2D6 substrates (e.g. dextromethorphan, nebivolol): the urinary dextromethorphan/dextrorphan ratio rose 9-fold [PMID:21870106]. Inhibition is quasi-irreversible, and berberine reduced nebivolol clearance in microsomes [PMID:32987920].
- CYP2C9 substrates (e.g. losartan): the losartan/E-3174 ratio doubled [PMID:21870106]. No changes were seen for CYP2C19 (omeprazole) or CYP1A2 (caffeine) [PMID:21870106].
- P-gp substrates (e.g. digoxin): oral digoxin AUC rose 23-70% in rats [PMID:19370549]. There are no human berberine-digoxin data.
- Metformin:
  - Goldenseal lowered metformin AUC (GMR 0.77, CI 0.71-0.83) in healthy volunteers [PMID:33174626].
  - In T2D patients on therapeutic doses there was no clinically meaningful interaction (GMR 0.93, 90% CI 0.86-1.01); HbA1c fell slightly [PMID:39943692].
  - Pharmacodynamically, adding berberine to hypoglycemic drugs lowered HbA1c further (MD -0.69%) [PMID:39640489].
- Glucose-lowering drugs (sulfonylureas, insulin, acarbose):
  - Additive glucose lowering is expected [PMID:39640489].
  - Hypoglycemia was not significantly increased in MAs (RR 0.48, CI 0.21-1.08) [PMID:36467075], but reviews list hypoglycemia as a pharmacodynamic axis [PMID:42653808].
  - For acarbose, the shared alpha-glucosidase target is only computational [PMID:42213651].
- Statins:
  - No clinically relevant PK interaction with simvastatin or fenofibrate in 60 healthy subjects [PMID:30587933].
  - Berberine plus simvastatin lowered TG (MD -0.33 mmol/L) and TC (MD -0.36 mmol/L) more than simvastatin alone, with fewer transaminase rises and muscle aches but more constipation [PMID:31094214].
  - The mechanism (LDLR mRNA stabilization) is complementary to statins [PMID:15531889].
- QT-prolonging drugs: possible additive risk [PMID:42488242] [PMID:42653808].
- Antihypertensives: hypotension is listed as a pharmacodynamic axis [PMID:42653808], but MAs showed no significant BP effect [PMID:40740996]. Minor.
*** Other supplements
- Red yeast rice (incl. corpus product Yeast Rice + Garlic):
  - Berberine-RYR combinations lowered TC by 19.6 mg/dL and LDL by 18.8 mg/dL [PMID:37183391].
  - Armolipid Plus (berberine + RYR + policosanol) MA, 12 RCTs: LDL -26.67 mg/dL, well tolerated [PMID:33669333]. An RCT gave LDL -1.06 mmol/L with no AEs [PMID:19699071].
  - Monacolin K (lovastatin) exposure could theoretically rise through berberine's CYP3A4 inhibition [PMID:21870106]. This is not studied.
- Garlic: both garlic and berberine lowered LDL-C vs placebo in a network MA of 131 trials [PMID:35988871]. No combination trial exists, so additive effects are inferred.
- Goldenseal: contains berberine, so stacking adds berberine exposure and CYP3A/2D6 inhibition [PMID:33174626].
- Probiotics: PREMOTE found no added HbA1c benefit from probiotics plus berberine over berberine alone (-1.04% vs -0.99%) [PMID:33024120].
- Cinnamon; white mulberry + chromium: combination RCTs improved glycemia or body composition [PMID:39998703] [PMID:42738972]. Additive glucose lowering is possible.
- Other glucose-lowering supplements (curcumin, resveratrol, silymarin): a network MA ranked these alongside berberine for T2D cardiometabolic factors [PMID:40439602]. Combined effects are untested.
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy, breastfeeding and neonatal jaundice: see Contraindications [PMID:8513024] [PMID:30000926]. T2D on medication: monitor glucose [PMID:39640489]. Organ transplant: monitor immunosuppressant levels [PMID:16133554]. Long QT syndrome [PMID:42488242].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. Expect physiological changes: higher unbound/total bilirubin (rats) [PMID:8513024]; lower glucose, HbA1c, lipids, apoB and Lp(a) [PMID:36467075] [PMID:42470424]; testosterone down in women and up in men [PMID:42470424]; lower ALT/AST in NAFLD [PMID:38429794]; higher troughs of cyclosporine and other CYP3A4/P-gp drugs [PMID:16133554].

** Genotype notes
- CYP2D6 is the main human CYP metabolizing berberine, and the authors advise considering CYP2D6 pharmacogenetics [PMID:21787170]. Berberine itself inhibits CYP2D6 [PMID:21870106]. No clinical genotype-stratified outcome data were found.

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Paired RCTs (100 women, 84 men; 500 mg twice daily): apoB -1.18 SD, Lp(a) -0.71 SD; larger effects in women; testosterone fell in women and rose in men [PMID:42470424].
- [2026-08] Narrative DDI review: four mechanistic axes, with cyclosporine as the human clinical anchor. Proposes a 3-tier risk stratification [PMID:42653808].
- [2026-08] Berberine + white mulberry + chromium RCT (n=93): BMI -1.24 kg/m2; Matsuda index ratio 1.28. No single-agent arms [PMID:42738972].
- [2026-06] Case report: torsades de pointes and cardiac arrest attributed to berberine; resolved after stopping [PMID:42488242].
- [2026-06] SR/MA of adjunctive berberine in schizophrenia with metabolic syndrome (3 RCTs): weight SMD -0.56 [PMID:42433212].
- [2026-06] SR of 7 RCTs on gut microbiota: consistent compositional shifts, not uniformly favorable [PMID:42356246].
- [2026-05] T2D MA of probiotics, synbiotics and berberine: FPG -0.71 mmol/L, HbA1c -0.19%; modest [PMID:42213651].
- [2026-03] RCT-derived proteomic "berberine signature" associated with lower IHD (OR 0.85) and diabetes (OR 0.88) in MR [PMID:41882153].
- [2026-01] JAMA Netw Open RCT in MASLD/obesity (n=337, 1 g/d, 6 months): no reduction in VAT or liver fat; LDL-C and apoB fell [PMID:41543854].
- [2025-11] Obesity MA (23 studies): weight -0.88 kg, BMI -0.48 kg/m2 [PMID:41310257].
- [2025-11] MAFLD RCT (n=70, 1500 mg/d): VEGF rose; no arterial-stiffness change; well tolerated [PMID:41305635].
- [2025-08] 6-year follow-up of the adenoma RCT: recurrence 34.7% vs 52.1% [PMID:40795846].
- [2025-07] Metabolic syndrome MA of placebo-controlled RCTs: TG, FPG and WC improved; BP and HDL unchanged; AEs not different from placebo [PMID:40740996].
- [2025-04] Overview of 54 SRs: mostly very low methodological quality [PMID:40269802].
- [2025-03] Phase 2 RCT of HTD1801 (berberine ursodeoxycholate): HbA1c -0.7% at 1000 mg twice daily [PMID:40029660].
- [2025-02] Goldenseal-metformin PK interaction in T2D is dose-dependent and not clinically meaningful overall [PMID:39943692].
- [2025-02] Berberine + cinnamon RCT in T2D: FBS, HbA1c and LDL-C lower vs placebo [PMID:39998703].
- [2024-11] T2D MA (50 RCTs, n=4150): berberine alone and with hypoglycemics improved FPG, HbA1c and lipids [PMID:39640489].

** Open questions
- No hard-outcome trials (cardiovascular events, diabetes complications, mortality). The overall evidence base is dominated by short, Chinese, low-quality trials [PMID:40269802].
- Discordance between surrogate and imaging outcomes: liver enzymes fall in MAs, but liver fat did not change in a large RCT [PMID:38429794] [PMID:41543854].
- Clinical significance of CYP3A4/2D6/2C9 inhibition for sirolimus, tacrolimus and other narrow-therapeutic-index substrates. Only cyclosporine has human clinical data [PMID:16133554] [PMID:42653808].
- QT risk: frequency and dose dependence beyond a single case [PMID:42488242].
- Whether enhanced-bioavailability forms change efficacy, interaction potential or safety [PMID:35010998] [PMID:40278369].
- Generalizability of the adenoma-prevention result outside China, and of sex-specific lipid responses [PMID:31926918] [PMID:42470424].
- Actual transfer into breast milk and fetal exposure [PMID:30000926].
