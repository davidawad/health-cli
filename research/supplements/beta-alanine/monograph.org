** Identity & forms
- Beta Alanine is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: β-alanine, 3-aminopropanoic acid. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:39724872, PMID:38952910].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:39724872, PMID:38952910].

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| May improve high-intensity exercise capacity through increased muscle carnosine | Systematic review/meta-analysis or human trial synthesis | Mixed/possible benefit; no established clinical disease prevention | See evidence summary below; pooled estimates vary by outcome and population | moderate | PMID:39724872, PMID:38952910 |

- Systematic review in older adults included five studies (163 participants); exercise capacity may improve, while strength/function did not [PMID:39724872]. Meta-analysis of beta-alanine plus bicarbonate found context-dependent exercise outcomes [PMID:38952910].
- May improve high-intensity exercise capacity through increased muscle carnosine; benefit is task-specific and not consistently seen for strength or functional outcomes [PMID:39724872, PMID:38952910].

** Dosing in trials
- Common loading protocols use 3.2–6.4 g/day in divided doses for at least 4 weeks; older-adult studies averaged 2.7 g/day for about 12 weeks [PMID:39724872].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Transient paresthesia (tingling) is dose-related; divided or sustained-release dosing reduces peak symptoms [PMID:39724872].

** Contraindications
- No established contraindication in healthy adults; long-term safety and use in pregnancy/lactation are insufficiently studied [PMID:39724872].

** Interactions
- No established clinically meaningful drug interaction; co-use with sodium bicarbonate has been studied, with mixed performance effects [PMID:38952910].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2024/2025 review of older adults found only five eligible studies, indicating evidence base remains small [PMID:39724872].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
