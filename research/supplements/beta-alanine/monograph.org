** Identity & forms
- Non-proteinogenic amino acid. It is the rate-limiting precursor of the dipeptide carnosine (beta-alanyl-L-histidine) in human skeletal muscle; L-histidine is not rate-limiting [PMID:28106620].
- Commercial forms:
  - Pure (rapid-release) powder or capsules, usually as 400-800 mg units [PMID:16554972].
  - Sustained/slow-release tablets (e.g. CarnoSyn 800 mg tablets), used in many trials [PMID:22143432] [PMID:30552505].
  - Sustained-release microgranule powder, studied at up to 15 g/d [PMID:37766731].
- Kinetics: plasma beta-alanine peaks within ~0.5-1 h and returns to baseline by ~2 h [PMID:16554972] [PMID:22139410]. Urinary loss is small (<5%) [PMID:16554972]. Whole-body retention of slow-release product is 97-98%. Only ~3% of an oral dose ends up in muscle carnosine; most is probably oxidized [PMID:23439427].
- Widely used in multi-ingredient pre-workout products, often alongside caffeine [PMID:39438314].

** Mechanisms
- Muscle carnosine loading. Supplementation raises muscle carnosine, an intracellular H+ buffer [PMID:26175657].
  - 3.2 and 6.4 g/d for 4 weeks raised vastus lateralis carnosine by ~42% and ~64% [PMID:16554972]. Increases were +58.8% at 4 weeks and +80.1% at 10 weeks, in both type I and IIa fibres [PMID:16868650].
  - The rise depends on total cumulative dose: about 2.01 mmol/kg wet weight per 100 g consumed, independent of baseline or daily dose (1.6-3.2 g/d). Washout is slow (~2%/week) [PMID:21847611].
  - Bayesian E-max meta-analysis: muscle has a large, non-linear capacity to accumulate carnosine. Common protocols probably do not saturate it. Nearly everyone responds (99.3%, 95% CrI 96.2-100) [PMID:32922303].
  - Loading is larger when taken with meals (+64% vs +41% between meals in soleus) [PMID:23439427]. Women have lower baseline carnosine and a larger relative rise [PMID:24389513].
- Histidine. L-histidine is not rate-limiting. Beta-alanine alone lowered plasma (-30.6%) and muscle (-31.6%) histidine; co-supplementing histidine prevented this [PMID:28106620].
- Performance pathway: better buffering delays acidosis-related fatigue. Benefit is therefore greatest in high-intensity efforts of ~1-4 min [PMID:26175657] [PMID:22270875]. It is small or absent where phosphocreatine depletion limits performance (repeated sprints) [PMID:41971372].
- Paresthesia mechanism: in mice, beta-alanine itch/tingling needs MrgprD, a receptor on a histamine-independent subset of skin sensory neurons. In humans, intradermal beta-alanine causes itch with no wheal or flare [PMID:23077038].
- Taurine transporter (TauT) competition. Beta-alanine binds TauT as a substrate analogue (cryo-EM) [PMID:40789850]. At high doses in animals this depletes tissue taurine; see Safety [PMID:8838582] [PMID:31473259].
- Brain carnosine: 6.4 g/d for 28 days did not change the brain homocarnosine/carnosine MRS signal [PMID:25875297].

** Dosing in trials
- ISSN position stand: 4-6 g/d for at least 2-4 weeks, in divided doses. Single doses of ~1.6 g or sustained-release forms reduce paresthesia [PMID:26175657].
- Divided 0.8 g doses up to 4-6.4 g/d over 5-8 weeks gave the most consistent strength/power effects. Single large or sustained-release doses did less well in that review [PMID:40995761].
- Higher-dose loading: 12 g/d sustained-release for 2 weeks raised muscle carnosine about as much as 6 g/d for 4 weeks, without more paresthesia [PMID:28910200]. However, 12 g/d for 2 weeks did not improve futsal performance [PMID:40981477].
- Maintenance: ~1.2 g/d kept carnosine 30-50% above baseline after loading [PMID:24389513].
- Timing: taking it with meals improves loading [PMID:23439427]. Acute single doses (10-20 g before a 10-min time trial) did not improve performance [PMID:42347476].
- Older adults: 2.4-3.2 g/d for ~10-12 weeks (mean 2.7 g/d) [PMID:39724872] [PMID:36839281].
- Longest controlled exposures: 6.4 g/d sustained-release for 24 weeks [PMID:30552505]; 4.8 g/d for 3 months in adults with overweight/obesity [PMID:39800667].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Systematic risk assessment and meta-analysis (Dolan 2019; 101 human and 50 animal studies, GRADE) [PMID:30980076]:
  - Paresthesia was the only reported side effect: OR 8.9 (95% CrI 2.2-32.6) vs placebo.
  - Dropout rates were similar to placebo.
  - Small ALT rise (ES 0.274, CrI 0.04-0.527), with means staying inside clinical reference ranges.
  - No effect on muscle taurine (ES 0.156, CrI -0.38 to 0.72) or muscle histidine (ES -0.15, CrI -0.64 to 0.33) in humans.
  - Conclusion: no adverse effects at the doses studied.
- Paresthesia (tingling, "pins and needles"):
  - Doses above ~800 mg often cause it. Symptoms on the arms and trunk peak ~15 min after a 1.6 g solution and track the plasma curve [PMID:22139410].
  - Slow-release tablets lowered peak plasma (82 vs 248 umol/L) and gave symptoms no different from placebo [PMID:22139410]. Sustained-release 12 g/d gave no more paresthesia than placebo [PMID:28910200].
  - Even 15 g/d sustained-release microgranules caused mostly mild tickling (VAS <3/10). Triglycerides, LDL and urea rose within the active group, but not vs control [PMID:37766731].
  - The tingle is not ergogenic: acute paresthesia did not improve 1-km cycling time-trial performance [PMID:25636080].
- Long-term labs: 24 weeks at 6.4 g/d did not change muscle taurine or renal, hepatic and muscle markers (AST, ALT, ALP, creatinine, eGFR, CK). No chronic sensory side effects were reported [PMID:30552505].
- Clinical populations: no supplement-related side effects in COPD [PMID:35977911]. Good tolerance and adherence in overweight/obesity; low probability of cardiometabolic, hepatic or renal changes [PMID:39800667].
- Ocular/haemodynamic: 3 g did not change intraocular pressure or mean arterial pressure; caffeine did [PMID:39732141].
- Taurine depletion (animal data):
  - Cats given 5% beta-alanine in drinking water for 20 weeks had greatly reduced brain taurine and cerebellar damage, worse when taurine-deprived [PMID:8838582].
  - Rats on 3% for 2 months had lower plasma taurine, thinner retinal nerve fiber layer and impaired retinal ganglion cell axonal transport [PMID:31473259].
  - Murine taurine fell only at >=3% in drinking water, far above human doses [PMID:30980076].
- Case report: exertional rhabdomyolysis (CK >120,000 U/L) in a young man using creatine, beta-alanine, citrulline malate and beta-ecdysterone after a >4 h resistance session. Causality cannot be assigned to any single product [PMID:41952948].
- A review of amino-acid supplements notes gaps in long-term high-dose data and in children, pregnancy, lactation, diabetes and liver disease [PMID:35043647].

** Contraindications
- None established in healthy adults at studied doses [PMID:30980076] [PMID:26175657].
- Pregnancy, lactation and children: no safety data. Avoid in these groups as a precaution [PMID:35043647].
- Known low taurine status or taurine-transport disorders: theoretical concern, based only on animal taurine-depletion data [PMID:8838582] [PMID:31473259] [PMID:40789850].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- No pharmacokinetic drug interaction studies were found.
- Glucose-lowering drugs: carnosine/beta-alanine meta-analyses suggest lower FBG and HbA1c in prediabetes/T2DM, mostly from carnosine trials [PMID:40999397] [PMID:34333586]. An additive effect is possible but unquantified (minor).
- Caffeine: often co-formulated. 200 mg caffeine plus 3 g beta-alanine raised resting intraocular pressure, an effect driven by caffeine [PMID:39732141]. The combination gave no acute performance benefit in superset resistance training [PMID:39438314].
*** Other supplements
- Sodium bicarbonate: MA of 10 studies (n=243). BA alone SMD 0.18 (CI -0.06 to 0.43), SB alone SMD 0.17 (CI -0.08 to 0.41), combination SMD 0.32 (CI 0.07-0.57) [PMID:38952910]. The 2017 MA found the largest effect for the combination vs placebo (ES 0.43, CI 0.22-0.64) [PMID:27797728]. A systematic review recommends co-supplementation for 30 s-10 min efforts [PMID:34882489]. In a 2026 RCT in female basketball players, gains were driven by bicarbonate [PMID:41838453].
- Creatine:
  - A 2025 SR of 7 RCTs (n=263) found greater high-intensity/anaerobic power and repeated-bout performance with the combination. Strength, body composition and aerobic capacity did not improve beyond either alone [PMID:40647180].
  - A 2026 network MA of 52 RCTs found no synergy; creatine alone was most consistent [PMID:42384726].
  - A 28-day RCT in women found no consistent additive benefit [PMID:25505854].
  - Multi-supplement use with extreme exercise appeared in a rhabdomyolysis case [PMID:41952948].
- Taurine: beta-alanine competes for the taurine transporter [PMID:40789850] and can raise plasma taurine acutely [PMID:16554972]. High-dose animal models show tissue taurine depletion [PMID:8838582] [PMID:31473259]. Human muscle taurine is unchanged after 10-24 weeks [PMID:16868650] [PMID:30552505] [PMID:30980076]. Whether co-supplementing taurine reduces any loading benefit is not studied.
- L-histidine: beta-alanine lowers plasma and muscle histidine; co-supplementing histidine prevents this but does not increase carnosine further [PMID:28106620]. Sustained-release 12 g/d lowered plasma histidine [PMID:28910200].
- Caffeine (corpus item): see Drugs.
*** Conditions
- Older adults: exercise capacity improves; strength and function do not [PMID:39724872] [PMID:29336621].
- COPD: carnosine rises without functional benefit [PMID:35977911].
- Prediabetes/T2DM: possible glycemic improvement (low certainty) [PMID:40999397].
- Aquagenic pruritus: an online survey (n=75, self-report, uncontrolled) reported relief at ~1.6 g/d; 39.6% had paresthesia [PMID:40657651].
- Pregnancy/lactation: no data [PMID:35043647].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. Real physiological changes:
  - ALT: small rise within reference range [PMID:30980076].
  - Plasma histidine: lower with high doses [PMID:28106620] [PMID:28910200].
  - Plasma taurine: acute rise [PMID:16554972].
- Standard renal, hepatic and haematological panels were unchanged at 6.4 g/d for 24 weeks [PMID:30552505].

** Genotype notes
No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source found. An IPD meta-analysis found no true between-person variation in the performance response; variation was common to both groups and unrelated to the supplement. Muscle carnosine response does vary between people [PMID:34098531].

** Latest research
- [2026-07] RCT (n=22, 6.4 g/d, 4 wk): +17-18% repetitions in drop-set and bi-set resistance protocols; no change in perceived exertion [PMID:42413109].
- [2026-07] Network MA (52 RCTs): creatine alone most consistent; creatine plus beta-alanine shows no synergy [PMID:42384726].
- [2026-06] MA in women (11 RCTs, n=312): TTE SMD 0.49; other outcomes imprecise; GRADE very low [PMID:42370349].
- [2026-06] Acute 10 g or 20 g before a 10-min cycling TT (n=85): no performance benefit; lower mechanical work linked to bicarbonate [PMID:42347476].
- [2026-05] RCT in swimmers (4.8 g/d plus plyometric training, 8 wk): greater gains in upper-body power and swim-ergometer output [PMID:42218755].
- [2026-05] Kickboxing RCT (n=28, 6.4 g/d, 4 wk): better total anaerobic score and push-/pull-up endurance; no jump effect [PMID:42127019].
- [2026-03] Multilevel MA (17 RCTs): no effect on repeated-sprint ability [PMID:41971372].
- [2026-03] Female basketball RCT (n=68): gains were with bicarbonate-containing arms; beta-alanine alone gave no benefit [PMID:41838453].
- [2026-03] Case report: rhabdomyolysis with a multi-supplement stack including beta-alanine [PMID:41952948].
- [2025-09] Dosing SR (9 studies): divided 4-6.4 g/d over 5-8 weeks best for strength/power [PMID:40995761].
- [2025-09] Two weeks at 12 g/d did not improve futsal performance [PMID:40981477].
- [2025-09] MA in prediabetes/T2DM (8 RCTs, carnosine or beta-alanine): FBG SMD -0.53, HbA1c SMD -0.36 [PMID:40999397].
- [2025-08] Cryo-EM structures show beta-alanine binding the human taurine transporter TauT [PMID:40789850].
- [2025-06] Creatine plus beta-alanine SR (7 RCTs): better anaerobic power, no added strength or body-composition effect [PMID:40647180].
- [2025-06] Adolescent runners RCT: TTE +6.5% vs +1.4%; no VO2peak change [PMID:40528157].
- [2025-01] Feasibility RCT in overweight/obesity (4.8 g/d SR, 3 mo): well tolerated; no cardiometabolic signal [PMID:39800667].
- [2025-01] 14 weeks of beta-alanine plus HIIT: no BDNF or Stroop improvement [PMID:39832765].
- [2024-12] Older-adult SR (5 studies, n=163): capacity may improve; strength/function not [PMID:39724872].
- [2024-12] Beta-alanine plus caffeine: caffeine, not beta-alanine, raised intraocular pressure [PMID:39732141].
- [2024-11] MA in trained young men (18 RCTs): ES 0.39; 4-10 min efforts ES 0.55 [PMID:39032921].

** Open questions
- Long-term (>6 months) safety at high doses (>=12 g/d) and of sustained-release microgranule products. Lipid and urea changes at 15 g/d need confirmation [PMID:37766731].
- Whether chronic use lowers taurine in human tissues other than muscle (retina, brain), given animal depletion data.
- Cognitive and mood effects in older adults with low baseline cognition. Current data are small subgroup analyses [PMID:36839281] [PMID:38971131].
- Whether carnosine loading gives clinical benefit in disease (COPD, diabetes, heart). The COPD RCT was null for function.
- True added value of combining with creatine. Systematic reviews disagree [PMID:40647180] [PMID:42384726].
- Effects in women and adolescents. Evidence is sparse and very low certainty [PMID:42370349].
