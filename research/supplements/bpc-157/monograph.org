** Identity & forms
- Synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide (GEPPPGKPADDAGLV, MW ~1419), described as a partial sequence of a "body protection compound" found in human gastric juice. It is reported to be stable in gastric juice [PMID:17186181] [PMID:28035768].
- Development codes include PL-10, PLD-116 and PL 14736 (Pliva, Croatia) and Bepecin [PMID:17713731] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. FDA treats the free base and the acetate salt as two different bulk drug substances. Nomenclature is inconsistent and does not follow USAN/INN/IUPAC conventions [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].
- It is not a component of any approved medicine in any country, and no USP, European, Japanese or International Pharmacopoeia monograph exists [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].
- Products on the US market include oral capsules (250-500 ug), oral and nasal sprays, and injectable vials (5-10 mg). Some are sold as "dietary supplements" and others as "research use only" products [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. Gray-market sale online is widespread [PMID:42437212] [PMID:42757290] [PMID:41966639].
- Pharmaceutical development is rudimentary. There is no validated formulation, no BCS or permeability data, and no completed phase II trial [PMID:42198317].
- Evidence base: of 235 PubMed records for BPC-157 retrieved on 2026-10-13, 175 (~74%) are co-authored by one Zagreb group (Sikiric P) [URL:https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=%28BPC-157+OR+%22BPC+157%22+OR+BPC157+OR+%22body+protection+compound%22+OR+%22pentadecapeptide+BPC%22%29+AND+Sikiric+P%5Bau%5D]. Independent replication is limited.

** Mechanisms
All mechanistic data are preclinical (cell culture, rodents, chick CAM), plus one human ex vivo vessel study.
- VEGFR2: BPC-157 raised VEGFR2 (but not VEGF-A) expression, promoted VEGFR2 internalization and activated VEGFR2-Akt-eNOS. It increased vessel density in CAM/tube assays and blood-flow recovery in rat hind-limb ischaemia [PMID:27847966]. Angiogenesis/VEGF modulation has also been seen in healing muscle and tendon, but there was no direct angiogenic effect in cell culture [PMID:20388964].
- Nitric oxide system: Endothelium-dependent, NO-mediated vasodilation of rat aorta through Src-Caveolin-1-eNOS [PMID:33051481]. Human internal mammary artery rings (n=12 patients): concentration-dependent relaxation, attenuated by L-NAME [PMID:42123221]. The Zagreb group frames BPC-157 as a modulator of the NO system that counteracts both L-NAME and L-arginine effects [PMID:23755725] [PMID:29358856].
- FAK-paxillin: in rat tendon fibroblasts it increased outgrowth, survival under H2O2 and migration, with dose-dependent FAK and paxillin phosphorylation [PMID:21030672].
- Growth hormone receptor: dose- and time-dependent upregulation of GH receptor in tendon fibroblasts. This potentiated GH-induced proliferation through JAK2 [PMID:25415472].
- Other proposed pathways: FBXO22-dependent stabilization of BACH1 in endothelial cells (2026) [PMID:41606641]. egr-1 induction and granulation tissue/collagen organization [PMID:17628536]. KLF4 upregulation in radiation liver injury [PMID:36228773]. VEGF-A/VEGFR1-AKT/p38 signalling in gastric mucosa [PMID:33376304]. Gene-expression shifts (Egr1, Akt1, Vegfr2, Nos3) in hippocampal ischaemia [PMID:32558293].
- Neurotransmitter systems (rodent): Altered regional brain serotonin synthesis [PMID:15531385]. Interactions with dopamine agonists and antagonists [PMID:9547930] [PMID:10499368]. A review argues for neurotransmitter-like actions [PMID:38675421]. In vitro, BPC-157 is a weak reversible competitive acetylcholinesterase inhibitor [PMID:42278509].
- FDA's 2026 evaluation states that no molecular target has been identified, dose-response relationships are not established, and the mechanism remains poorly understood [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].

** Dosing in trials
- No dose is established as safe or effective in humans [PMID:42198317] [PMID:41476424].
- Human exposures summarized by FDA [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]: Rectal enema up to 2 mg/kg daily x 8 days (n=24 healthy subjects). 80 mg enema daily x 2 weeks (UC). 2-4 mg intra-articular x 1-2 doses (knee pain). 10 mg intravesical (interstitial cystitis). 10 mg then 20 mg IV infusions (n=2).
- IV pilot: 10 mg in 250 mL saline over 1 h on day 1 and 20 mg on day 2 in 2 adults who had used IV BPC-157 before [PMID:40131143].
- Registered trials: Oral PCO-02 (Bepecin) 1-6 mg single dose or 3 mg every 8 h x 2 weeks. Phase 1, Mexico, status unknown, no results [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02637284]. SC daily x 14 days for grade II hamstring strain. Phase 2 RCT, n=120, recruiting 2026 [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07437547]. Rotator cuff repair RCT, not yet recruiting [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07803250]. Single-arm 500 ug "peptide gummy" study [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07752381].
- Animal regimens typically use 10 ug/kg or 10 ng/kg i.p., per-orally in drinking water, or topically [PMID:20225319] [PMID:18668315]. Human-equivalent doses have not been validated.
- Pharmacokinetics (animals) [PMID:36588717]: t1/2 <30 min (5.3 min in dogs, 15.2 min in rats IV) [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. IM bioavailability 14-19% (rat) and 45-51% (dog). Rapid breakdown to small peptides and amino acids; excreted in urine and bile.
- Human PK: BPC-157 was mostly undetectable in plasma after rectal enema. No human oral, SC or nasal PK data exist [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Human data are minimal: under 30 subjects across 3 uncontrolled pilots, plus abstract-only enema studies [PMID:42198317] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. The IV pilot (n=2) reported no changes in cardiac, hepatic, renal, thyroid or glucose markers [PMID:40131143]. The enema studies most often reported headache and flatulence [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].
- FDA FAERS search through Dec 2025 found 3 reports, all with injectables [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]: Injection-site redness and swelling (with co-injected thymosin). Dyspnoea leading to an ER visit. Diffuse hyperpigmentation and gingival darkening that recurred on rechallenge with a "research purposes only" BPC-157/TB-500 blend. The human foods complaint system (HFCS) held 3 further confounded reports.
- Nonclinical toxicology (Xu 2020, IM dosing) [PMID:32334036] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]: No acute toxicity at 20 mg/kg in rats or 10 mg/kg in dogs. Negative in Ames, chromosome aberration and micronucleus tests. No embryo-fetal toxicity in rats at up to 4 mg/kg. FDA's re-analysis of the 28-day studies flagged aPTT shortening (rats) and prolongation (dogs), and liver-associated signals (raised ALT, glucose, triglycerides). No carcinogenicity, full reproductive-cycle, or longer-term studies exist, and none use the marketed routes.
- Immunogenicity and quality: FDA considers BPC-157 not well characterized. Peptide-related impurities and aggregates could raise immunogenicity risk, especially for SC and nasal routes [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. Before the nominations were withdrawn, FDA had listed BPC-157 among bulk substances that "may present significant safety risks" (immunogenicity, impurities, limited safety data) [URL:https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks].
- Gray-market purity: No peer-reviewed purity survey of BPC-157 products was found. Falsified internet peptide drugs in Belgium had 5-75% purity, variable content, and arsenic/lead above parenteral limits [PMID:30029448]. BPC-157 has been identified in confiscated vials [PMID:28035768]. Reviews flag contamination, sterility, mislabelling and dosing errors [PMID:42437212] [PMID:42757290] [PMID:40756949] [PMID:42160466].
- Angiogenesis and theoretical cancer concern: The drug's pro-angiogenic actions [PMID:27847966] [PMID:41606641] raise a theoretical concern about tumour growth. Critics list pathological angiogenesis and carcinogenesis as potential risks [PMID:42757290] [PMID:40005999] [PMID:40789979]. The Zagreb group disputes this and argues for "controlled" angiogenesis [PMID:40573323] [PMID:41155565]. Another group proposes BPC-157 for cancer cachexia [PMID:29898649]. No human or carcinogenicity data resolve the question [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].

** Contraindications
None are formally established, because there is no approved label. The following are precautionary:
- Active or prior malignancy: pro-angiogenic mechanism and no carcinogenicity studies [PMID:27847966] [PMID:42757290] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].
- Pregnancy and lactation: only one IM rat embryo-fetal study; no human data [PMID:32334036] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].
- Bleeding or clotting disorders, and perioperative use: aPTT changes in 28-day animal studies [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. Rodent data also show effects on bleeding and platelet function [PMID:21840572] [PMID:31976029].
- Competitive athletes: prohibited at all times under WADA S0 [URL:https://www.wada-ama.org/en/prohibited-list?item-id=5027] [URL:https://nsa.gov.cz/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/3_Priloha-I_en.pdf].
- Injection of unregulated "research use only" products: sterility, impurity and immunogenicity risk [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download] [PMID:42437212].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
No human interaction studies exist. All entries below are theoretical or extrapolated from rodent studies.
- Anticoagulants (heparin, warfarin): In rats, BPC-157 shortened bleeding time and reduced thrombocytopenia under heparin or warfarin [PMID:21840572] [PMID:25897838]. It is proposed to counteract both haemorrhage and thrombosis [PMID:41901308]. FDA flagged aPTT changes in rats and dogs [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. Possible pharmacodynamic opposition or INR/aPTT perturbation is unquantified.
- Antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel, cilostazol): in rats, BPC-157 largely restored platelet aggregation inhibited by these drugs [PMID:31976029] [PMID:21840572]. This could theoretically blunt antiplatelet efficacy.
- Gastric injury from antiplatelets and NSAIDs: BPC-157 reduced clopidogrel- and NSAID-induced gastric injury in rodents [PMID:33376304] [PMID:22950504] [PMID:28839430].
- NO-related vasodilators (nitrates, PDE5 inhibitors): NO-mediated vasorelaxation in rat and human arteries [PMID:33051481] [PMID:42123221] suggests a possible additive hypotensive effect. This is mechanistic only.
- Dopaminergic drugs (rodent): Counteracted amphetamine stereotypy, haloperidol catalepsy and haloperidol-induced supersensitivity [PMID:9547930] [PMID:10499368]. Reversed vascular "occlusion-like" syndromes induced by neuroleptics and amphetamine [PMID:37375736]. Opposed antipsychotic- and metoclopramide-induced QTc prolongation [PMID:28797793].
- Serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, MAOIs): attenuated pargyline + L-tryptophan serotonin syndrome in rats [PMID:15840402] and altered brain 5-HT synthesis [PMID:15531385]. Clinical relevance is unknown.
- Opioids: counteracted haloperidol-enhanced morphine analgesia in mice [PMID:20388962].
- Antidepressants and anxiolytics: antidepressant- and anxiolytic-like effects in rodents [PMID:10791689] [PMID:11742568].
- Cholinesterase inhibitors: weak in vitro AChE inhibition [PMID:42278509]. A theoretical additive effect has not been studied in vivo.
- Corticosteroids: opposed corticosteroid-impaired tendon and muscle healing in rats [PMID:16583442] [PMID:20190676].
- Lithium, magnesium and insulin overdose: protective in rodent toxicity models [PMID:34829735] [PMID:28210905] [PMID:20388953]. There is no evidence of benefit in human poisoning.
*** Other supplements
- TB-500 (thymosin beta-4 fragment), often co-injected: In a 2026 rat study the combination gave no additive tendon benefit [PMID:42542926]. The only rechallenge-positive FAERS case involved a BPC-157/TB-500 blend [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. TB-500 is also prohibited in sport [PMID:41476424].
- Aspirin, Lithium and Magnesium: rodent data only (see Drugs) [PMID:31976029] [PMID:34829735] [PMID:28210905].
- No cited interaction found with other corpus supplements, including Cerebrolysin.
*** Conditions
- Malignancy, pregnancy and lactation, coagulation disorders, competitive sport: see Contraindications.
- Ulcerative colitis: approved therapies exist. FDA judged the evidence inadequate for BPC-157 in UC, a serious disease [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download].
*** Lab-test interference
- Anti-doping tests: BPC-157 and its metabolites are detectable in urine (LOD 0.1 ng/mL) [PMID:28035768] [PMID:37959764] and in dried blood spots [PMID:42328738]. A positive test is a true finding, not an artefact.
- Coagulation and liver tests: aPTT and ALT changes in animal studies [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download]. A creatinine decrease was seen in dogs [PMID:32334036]. No human assay interference has been documented.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Critical review of rodent ischaemia-reperfusion studies: consistent antioxidant, NO and VEGFR2-related protection, but short follow-up, single doses and unclear risk of bias. BPC-157 is "hypothesis-generating", not an established therapy [PMID:42794771].
- [2026-08] Scoping review of 6 sports peptides: 67% of publications were preclinical. Human benefits are "unsubstantiated"; not recommended as an adjunct to orthopaedic care [PMID:42578445].
- [2026-08] Sports Med review of approved vs gray-market peptides: scarce human safety data; placebo effects amplified by social media [PMID:41966639]. Similar reviews on pain and ageing [PMID:42635865] [PMID:42021992].
- [2026-07] FDA PCAC briefing: FDA staff recommended against adding BPC-157 to the 503A bulks list. Reasons: poor characterization, immunogenicity, aPTT and liver signals in animals, no UC efficacy [URL:https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/advisory-committees/advisory-committee-calendar/july-23-24-2026-meeting-pharmacy-compounding-advisory-committee-07232026].
- [2026-07] The committee nonetheless voted 8-6 (1 abstention) to recommend listing for UC. The vote is non-binding, and FDA rulemaking is pending [URL:https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-advisory-committee-backs-two-controversial-peptides.html].
- [2026-07] Randomized rat Achilles study (n=32): BPC-157 improved histology; no additive benefit with TB-500 [PMID:42542926].
- [2026-07] Review of "biohacking" peptides flags contamination, impurities and carcinogenesis [PMID:42757290]. A compounding-pharmacy analysis reviews the PCAC recommendations on synthetic peptides [PMID:42752426].
- [2026-06] Gray-market peptide review: products of uncertain identity, purity, potency and sterility [PMID:42437212].
- [2026-05] Human internal mammary artery rings: endothelium/NO-dependent vasorelaxation [PMID:42123221].
- [2026-05] Biopharmaceutical review: no validated formulation; PK-PD disconnect (t1/2 <30 min vs prolonged effects) [PMID:42198317].
- [2026-05] JBJS Rev structured review: BPC-157 remains investigational, with product-quality and antidoping concerns [PMID:42160466].
- [2026-05] In vitro: BPC-157 is a reversible competitive AChE inhibitor [PMID:42278509].
- [2026-03] Review of BPC-157 in tissue repair and pain: human data limited to small pilots [PMID:41898733].
- [2026-02] Phase 2 hamstring-strain RCT registered (n=120, SC x 14 d) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07437547].
- [2026-01] Proposed mechanism: BPC-157 binds FBXO22 and stabilizes BACH1, promoting angiogenesis [PMID:41606641].
- [2026-01] AJSM primer: the knee case series has major methodological flaws; dosing is unknown [PMID:41476424].
- [2025-11] HSS J systematic review (36 studies, 35 preclinical): promising animal data, no clinical safety data [PMID:40756949].
- [2025-09] IV safety pilot (n=2, 10-20 mg): no adverse effects [PMID:40131143].
- [2025-08] Narrative review "Regeneration or Risk?": only 3 human pilots; treat as investigational [PMID:40789979].
- [2025-06] Zagreb group rebuttal of tumorigenesis and NO-toxicity speculation [PMID:40573323].
- [2025-01] Literature and patent review raises possible toxicity concerns [PMID:40005999]. It states WADA no longer lists BPC-157, which conflicts with the 2026 List naming it under S0 [URL:https://nsa.gov.cz/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/3_Priloha-I_en.pdf].
- [2024-10] Interstitial cystitis pilot (n=12, compounded 503A product) [PMID:39325560].

** Open questions
- Does BPC-157 work in humans? Adequately powered, blinded RCTs are needed. The first (hamstring, rotator cuff) were only registered in 2026.
- Human PK by realistic routes (SC, oral, nasal) and the PK-PD disconnect.
- Long-term toxicity, carcinogenicity and tumour-promotion risk, given the pro-angiogenic mechanism.
- Coagulation effects (aPTT signals) and interactions with anticoagulants and antiplatelets in humans.
- Immunogenicity and the impurity/aggregate profile of compounded and gray-market products. No independent peer-reviewed product-quality survey was found.
- Independent replication outside one research group.
- Final FDA decision on the 503A bulks list after the July 2026 PCAC vote.
