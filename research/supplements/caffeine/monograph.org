** Identity & forms
- Caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine) is a methylxanthine. Dietary sources include coffee, tea, cocoa, energy drinks and energy shots. Supplement forms include anhydrous caffeine capsules/tablets, chewing gum, mouth rinses, gels and pre-workout products [PMID:33388079] [PMID:36862943].
- Pure or highly concentrated powdered caffeine: in an April 2018 guidance, FDA said some such dietary supplements are adulterated because they present a significant or unreasonable risk of illness or injury. It linked these products to at least two US deaths [URL:https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-highly-concentrated-caffeine-dietary-supplements]. Per FDA, toxic effects such as seizures can occur after rapid intake of about 1,200 mg, which is less than 1/2 teaspoon of pure caffeine [URL:https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much].
- Pharmacokinetics: CYP1A2 is the main enzyme metabolizing caffeine. Its primary metabolites are paraxanthine, theobromine and theophylline [PMID:8807660]. Elimination half-life is about 5 h in healthy volunteers (4.9-5.2 h) [PMID:16236038] [PMID:2729942] [PMID:8807660]. Salivary half-life was 7.3 h in one metabolic-ward study [PMID:7836625].
- Rx context: caffeine is among the commonly used medications in preterm infants [PMID:41945073]. Theophylline is partly N-methylated to caffeine (about 6% of a dose) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b0d5d1cd-c79b-4229-929c-2eb3e4f43298].
- Much of the long-term health evidence concerns coffee, not isolated caffeine. Coffee also contains polyphenols such as chlorogenic acid [PMID:10999016], and decaffeinated coffee often shows similar associations [PMID:24459154] [PMID:41845455].

** Mechanisms
- Adenosine receptor antagonism: at concentrations reached with normal consumption, the only established biochemical action of caffeine is blockade of adenosine A1 and A2A receptors. Blocking A2A receptors influences dopaminergic transmission [PMID:7746802]. A2A antagonism is also the proposed neuroprotective mechanism in Parkinson's disease [PMID:32580456].
- Hemodynamics: acute pressor effect. 200-300 mg raised SBP by 8.1 mm Hg and DBP by 5.7 mm Hg for at least 3 h in hypertensive people [PMID:21880846].
- Renal: acute increases in urinary calcium, magnesium, sodium and chloride. The hypercalciuric effect is blocked by adenosine agonists [PMID:8360789] [PMID:7836625].
- Pharmacologic stress testing: caffeine competitively antagonizes the adenosine receptors that adenosine, dipyridamole and regadenoson act through, which blunts hyperemia [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=057289f4-2e18-4c1e-bed4-6c1858e2ef16] [PMID:19496111].
- Shared CYP1A2 pathway: caffeine competes with other CYP1A2 substrates (clozapine, melatonin, theophylline). Smoking (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons) induces CYP1A2 [PMID:17823102] [PMID:9337943] [PMID:12906366].

** Dosing in trials
- Exercise: Consistently ergogenic at 3-6 mg/kg about 60 min pre-exercise. The minimal effective dose may be as low as 2 mg/kg. 9 mg/kg causes frequent side effects without extra benefit [PMID:33388079]. Low doses (about 3 mg/kg) in capsules were the top-ranked strategy for time trials [PMID:41374083]. Moderate doses (4-6 mg/kg) gave more consistent effects [PMID:42356375]. Gum may need a shorter lead time than capsules [PMID:33388079]. Mouth rinsing gives only trivial-to-small benefits (g 0.12) [PMID:41774525].
- Cognition with L-theanine: 40 mg caffeine plus 97 mg L-theanine improved task-switching accuracy and alertness [PMID:21040626].
- Regulatory safety levels (not efficacy doses), from EFSA 2015 [URL:https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine]: Healthy adults: single doses up to 200 mg (about 3 mg/kg) and up to 400 mg/day are not a safety concern. Pregnancy and lactation: up to 200 mg/day. Children and adolescents: 3 mg/kg/day. FDA likewise cites 400 mg/day for most healthy adults [URL:https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much].
- Sleep timing: 100 mg had no significant sleep effect even 4 h before bed, while 400 mg disrupted sleep when taken within 12 h of bedtime [PMID:39377163]. A meta-analysis suggests coffee (107 mg) at least 8.8 h before bed and a pre-workout dose (217.5 mg) at least 13.2 h before bed [PMID:36870101].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Common effects: insomnia, anxiety, tremor, palpitations and GI upset. EFSA notes that single doses of 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults [URL:https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine]. Caffeine raises heart rate in female athletes (g -0.30) [PMID:42588052].
- Withdrawal (reviewed by Juliano and Griffiths [PMID:15448977]): 10 validated symptoms, including headache (50% incidence in experimental studies), fatigue and difficulty concentrating. 13% had clinically significant distress or functional impairment. Onset 12-24 h after stopping, lasting 2-9 days. Stopping even 100 mg/day can produce symptoms.
- Arrhythmia: in the CRAVE trial, coffee did not increase premature atrial contractions but raised PVCs (154 vs 102/day) and cut nightly sleep by 36 min [PMID:36947466].
- Toxicity and deaths: A systematic review of 92 deaths from caffeine alone found infants, psychiatric patients and athletes over-represented. It gives serious toxicity (seizures, arrhythmia) at plasma levels of 15 mg/L or more and lethal levels of 80-100 mg/L [PMID:29757951]. In 51 poisoning deaths, median postmortem blood caffeine was 180 mg/L. All involved tablets or powder, and beverages alone were judged unlikely to reach toxic levels [PMID:28334840].
- Energy drinks: ISSN advises against them for children, during pregnancy/breastfeeding and in caffeine-sensitive people. It advises caution in cardiovascular, metabolic, hepatorenal or neurologic disease [PMID:36862943].
- Bone and minerals: net 24-h urinary losses of 0.32 mmol Ca and 0.16 mmol Mg after about 6 mg/kg lean mass [PMID:7836625]. Older women with low calcium intake may not compensate [PMID:8360789].

** Contraindications
- Pure or highly concentrated powdered caffeine products: FDA considers some to be adulterated [URL:https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-highly-concentrated-caffeine-dietary-supplements].
- Within 12 h of regadenoson (or adenosine/dipyridamole) pharmacologic stress MPI [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=057289f4-2e18-4c1e-bed4-6c1858e2ef16].
- Pregnancy above 200 mg/day: EFSA sets 200 mg/day as the safety level [URL:https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine]. ACOG: under 200 mg/day does not appear to be a major factor in miscarriage or preterm birth, and the effect on growth restriction is undetermined [PMID:20664420].
- Panic disorder: high-dose challenges trigger panic attacks in about half of patients [PMID:34871964].
- Use caution in insomnia [PMID:36870101], uncontrolled hypertension (acute BP rise) [PMID:21880846] and with fluvoxamine therapy (caffeine half-life rises to 31-56 h) [PMID:8807660] [PMID:16236038].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Fluvoxamine (strong CYP1A2 inhibitor): Caffeine clearance fell from 105 to 9.1 mL/min and half-life rose from 4.9 to 56 h [PMID:16236038]. In another study, clearance fell from 107 to 21 mL/min and half-life rose from 5 to 31 h, and the authors warned of possible caffeine intoxication [PMID:8807660]. A review of antidepressants notes that tricyclics also reduce caffeine metabolism [PMID:39870954].
- Ciprofloxacin: caffeine half-life rose from 5.2 to 8.2 h and clearance fell about 45% [PMID:2729942].
- Combined oral contraceptives: caffeine clearance fell about 54-55% after one cycle and recovered within a month of stopping [PMID:7589032].
- Smoking (CYP1A2 inducer): smokers clear caffeine, clozapine and theophylline faster. After quitting, the induction wears off and doses of interacting drugs may need reduction [PMID:17823102].
- Clozapine: CYP1A2 inhibitors, caffeine included, can raise plasma clozapine levels [PMID:9337943].
- Lithium: in two cases, lithium tremor worsened after coffee was stopped, attributed to lower renal lithium clearance and higher serum lithium [PMID:3338980]. Changes in caffeine intake may therefore shift lithium levels.
- Theophylline/aminophylline: an overlapping methylxanthine with the same caffeine-like adverse effects (nausea, headache, insomnia). Caffeine is a theophylline metabolite [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b0d5d1cd-c79b-4229-929c-2eb3e4f43298]. Theophylline is also a caffeine metabolite [PMID:8807660].
- MAO inhibitors: High-dose caffeine with tranylcypromine or phenelzine has been linked to hypertension [PMID:39870954]. A 2026 review judges caffeine use during MAOI therapy manageable with monitoring [PMID:41546846].
- Stimulants (dextroamphetamine/Dexedrine): the label lists rises in BP (2-4 mm Hg) and heart rate (3-6 bpm) and a sudden-death risk in serious cardiac disease. Adding caffeine's acute pressor effect [PMID:21880846] is plausible, but no combination trial was found [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Ephedrine: a Danish registry of 257,364 users of prescribed ephedrine/caffeine found no substantially increased risk of death, MI or stroke (adjusted OR 0.95). Ephedra was banned in the US in 2004 [PMID:18756018].
- Adenosine, dipyridamole and regadenoson: see Lab-test interference.
*** Other supplements
- L-Theanine (synergy, cognition): MA: the combination beat placebo on attention-switching accuracy (SMD 0.33) and digit vigilance accuracy (SMD 0.20) at 2 h [PMID:40314930]. RCT: 40 mg caffeine plus 97 mg L-theanine improved task switching and alertness [PMID:21040626].
- Creatine: SR of 10 studies. Creatine loading did not blunt acute caffeine benefits, but chronic caffeine during creatine loading may interfere with creatine's effect, possibly through opposite effects on muscle relaxation time and GI distress [PMID:35016154].
- Taurine: Bayesian NMA (12 studies). Caffeine plus taurine had credible advantages over either alone for anaerobic capacity (g 0.46) and reaction time (g 0.75) [PMID:41032459].
- Rhodiola Rosea: a 4-week RCT in 48 volleyball players found the largest jump-height gains and lowest RPE with the combination [PMID:40005009].
- Green Tea Extract: green tea preparations contain both catechins and caffeine [PMID:23235664]. Stacking adds to total caffeine intake (inference).
- Ginseng (Panax): a common energy-drink co-ingredient. Any benefit beyond caffeine is unproven [PMID:36862943].
- Alpha-GPC: only a head-to-head RCT exists, with no group difference vs 200 mg caffeine or placebo on isometric strength or psychomotor vigilance. No combination data [PMID:29042830].
- Melatonin: 200 mg evening caffeine increased nocturnal melatonin AUC by 32%, consistent with shared CYP1A2 metabolism [PMID:12906366]. Caffeine also opposes sleep [PMID:36870101].
- Magnesium and calcium: caffeine increases urinary excretion of both [PMID:7836625] [PMID:8360789].
- Iron (non-heme): A cup of coffee cut iron absorption from a meal by 39%, and drinking coffee 1 h before the meal did not inhibit absorption [PMID:6402915]. Polyphenol-containing beverages reduce non-haem iron absorption by 50-90% [PMID:10999016]. The effect comes from coffee/tea polyphenols, not isolated caffeine. Heme Iron (Proferrin): no cited study of heme iron with coffee was found.
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy: observational dose-response harm (pregnancy loss, LBW) [PMID:26329421] [PMID:34224282], but MR is null [PMID:35679582]. Keep intake at or below 200 mg/day (EFSA, ACOG) [URL:https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine] [PMID:20664420].
- Panic and anxiety disorders [PMID:34871964]. Insomnia [PMID:36870101] [PMID:41124973].
- Hypertension: acute BP rise of at least 3 h, without long-term BP increase or higher CVD risk in hypertensive people [PMID:21880846].
- Atrial fibrillation: abstinence is not supported. In DECAF, coffee lowered AF recurrence after cardioversion [PMID:41206802].
- Low calcium intake or older women at risk of osteoporosis [PMID:8360789] [PMID:29167102].
*** Lab-test interference
- Pharmacologic stress MPI: The regadenoson label requires avoiding methylxanthines (coffee, tea, caffeine-containing drugs, theophylline) for at least 12 h before radionuclide MPI. In the label's caffeine study, 200 or 400 mg reduced detected reversible defects by about 60% [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=057289f4-2e18-4c1e-bed4-6c1858e2ef16] [PMID:24737255]. Dipyridamole: caffeine caused false-negative dipyridamole-thallium scans in 6 of 8 patients [PMID:1869975]. A review recommends a 24-h caffeine hold for dipyridamole [PMID:19496111]. Adenosine CMR: coffee less than 4 h before the scan blunted adenosine but not regadenoson hyperemia [PMID:28547666].
- Theophylline assays: caffeine and xanthine metabolites can falsely raise some dry-reagent office theophylline readings in neonates or renal dysfunction [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b0d5d1cd-c79b-4229-929c-2eb3e4f43298].
- Urinary calcium and magnesium are transiently raised for about 6 h after intake [PMID:7836625].

** Genotype notes
- CYP1A2 rs762551 (-163A>C; *1F): Cornelis et al., Costa Rica case-control study (2,014 cases): coffee raised the risk of nonfatal MI only in slow-allele (*1F) carriers. OR 1.64 at 4 or more cups/day, and 2.33 in those under 59 years. No increase was seen with *1A/*1A [PMID:16522833]. Guest et al. RCT (n=101): 4 mg/kg improved 10-km cycling time by 6.8% in AA but worsened it by 13.7% in CC; no effect in AC [PMID:29509641]. ISSN attributes inter-individual variation partly to genetics [PMID:33388079].
- ADORA2A: A systematic review links ADORA2A variants to caffeine-related anxiety and sleep disturbance, and CYP1A2 variants to cognitive effects [PMID:37029915]. 1976TT (rs5751876) carriers showed the largest caffeine-potentiated startle to unpleasant pictures [PMID:22012471].
- AHR and CYP1A1-CYP1A2: rs6968865 near AHR and rs2472297 between CYP1A1 and CYP1A2 associate with about 0.2 more cups/day per allele [PMID:21357676]. A GWAS meta-analysis added further loci, including ABCG2, POR, BDNF and SLC6A4 [PMID:25288136].
- Genotype-guided intake is not validated by prospective outcome trials.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Three-level MA of post-exercise recovery: small functional benefit (g -0.208) but poorer sleep recovery (g 0.751); prediction intervals cross null [PMID:42823978].
- [2026-07] Preterm-infant MA: better motor and cognitive outcomes, cerebral palsy OR 0.65 [PMID:41945073].
- [2026-07] Female team-sport MA (26 RCTs): physical performance g 0.32; no reliable cognitive benefit [PMID:42588052].
- [2026-06] Aerobic dose-response MA: low dose SMD -0.27, moderate SMD -0.52 for time-trial completion [PMID:42356375].
- [2026-05] Updated CVD MA (38 studies): stroke HR 0.89, arrhythmia HR 0.94, but MI OR 1.48 at high intake [PMID:42098622].
- [2026-05] Migraine SR: genetically higher caffeine intake is linked to lower migraine risk, while acute excess or withdrawal triggers attacks [PMID:42151857].
- [2026-03] Heart-failure dose-response MA: HR 0.925 at 2-4 cups/day; GRADE low [PMID:41845455].
- [2026-03] MAOI and psychoactive-substance review: caffeine is manageable with monitoring [PMID:41546846]. A 2025 review covers caffeine-antidepressant interactions [PMID:39870954].
- [2026-01] DECAF RCT: caffeinated coffee vs abstinence after cardioversion, AF/flutter recurrence 47% vs 64% (HR 0.61) [PMID:41206802].
- [2026-01] MR evidence: no causal effect of coffee on HCC (OR 0.92) [PMID:40852740]; genetically predicted coffee intake lowers SBP and DBP [PMID:41087205].
- [2025-12] Caffeine mouth-rinse MA (31 studies): exercise g 0.12, GRADE moderate [PMID:41774525]. Caffeine plus taurine NMA: combination best for anaerobic capacity and reaction time [PMID:41032459].
- [2025-12] Time-trial NMA: low-dose capsules ranked best (SMD -0.34) [PMID:41374083].
- [2025-12] Sleep MA (22 trials): TST -34.67 min; SWS reduced at high dose [PMID:41124973].
- [2025-11] Repeated-sprint MA: peak power WMD 5.28; stronger at >=6 mg/kg [PMID:41228548].
- [2025-10] Tea/L-theanine MA: theanine plus caffeine gave small cognitive and mood benefits [PMID:40314930].
- [2025-04] Dose and timing RCT: 100 mg is fine up to 4 h before bed; 400 mg disrupts sleep within 12 h [PMID:39377163].
- [2025-02] Caffeine plus Rhodiola RCT in volleyball players [PMID:40005009]. SR of 9 RCTs on sleep in athletes [PMID:39551351].

** Open questions
- Whether coffee's inverse associations (mortality, T2D, liver, Parkinson's) are causal and attributable to caffeine. MR results for HCC are null [PMID:40852740].
- The MI signal at high intake in the 2026 MA [PMID:42098622] and genotype-specific MI risk [PMID:16522833] need confirmation.
- Pregnancy: the gap between observational harm and null MR results [PMID:26329421] [PMID:35679582].
- Prospective validation of CYP1A2- or ADORA2A-guided dosing for performance, sleep and anxiety [PMID:37029915].
- Chronic caffeine-creatine interference [PMID:35016154]. Long-term safety of multi-ingredient energy products [PMID:36862943].
- Few performance studies in women and older adults [PMID:30926628].
