** Identity & forms
- Caffeine is a methylxanthine naturally present in coffee, tea, cocoa and guarana; supplement forms include anhydrous powder/tablets and beverages [https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine].

** Mechanisms
- At ordinary dietary doses caffeine primarily antagonizes adenosine receptors; higher exposure increases CNS stimulation and catecholaminergic effects [https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine].

** Dosing in trials
- EFSA concludes single doses up to 200 mg and total intake up to 400 mg/day do not raise safety concerns for healthy adults; pregnancy limit assessed at 200 mg/day. These are safety thresholds, not efficacy prescriptions [https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Excess exposure may cause insomnia, anxiety, tremor, palpitations, gastrointestinal symptoms, and dependence/withdrawal; FDA notes rapid consumption of highly concentrated caffeine can be dangerous [https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much].

** Contraindications
- Avoid concentrated/uncertain-dose products. Individual susceptibility, pregnancy, arrhythmia, anxiety and sleep disorders alter risk; EFSA thresholds apply to healthy populations [https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine].

** Interactions
- Drugs, supplements, conditions, and relevant laboratory interference are summarized in the structured interaction object. Caffeine can add to other stimulants. CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g. fluvoxamine) may slow caffeine clearance; smoking induces metabolism (review-level pharmacology). Alcohol co-use does not reverse impairment; EFSA cautions caffeine does not reliably mask intoxication [https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine]. No genotype-specific claim is made because no directly relevant pharmacogenomic source was identified.

** Genotype notes
- No directly relevant, clinically validated pharmacogenomic/nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed.

** Latest research
- 2024: systematic review assessed caffeine/placebo effects in sports performance, reflecting active research on expectancy and ergogenic outcomes [PMID:39339818].

** Open questions
- Dose-response by genotype, sleep timing, habitual exposure, pregnancy, and combinations with energy-drink ingredients require better trials [https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine].
