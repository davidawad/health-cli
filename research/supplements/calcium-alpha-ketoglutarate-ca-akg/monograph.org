** Identity & forms
- Alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG; 2-oxoglutarate) is an endogenous intermediate of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA, Krebs) cycle and the carbon skeleton of glutamate [PMID:34952764] [PMID:9527195].
- Ca-AKG is its calcium salt, calcium 2-oxopentanedioate, C5H4CaO5, MW 184.16 [URL:https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/3018235]. From the formula, calcium is about 21.8% of anhydrous Ca-AKG by mass (40.08/184.16). So 1 g supplies about 0.22 g elemental calcium and 2 g about 0.44 g. Hydrated forms contain somewhat less. This is a calculation from the formula, not a figure measured in a trial.
- The 2020 mouse lifespan study used pure calcium 2-oxoglutarate at 2% (w/w) of the diet [PMID:32877690].
- Other AKG salts that have been studied in humans: ornithine alpha-ketoglutarate (OKG) for burns and pressure ulcers [PMID:10890617] [PMID:19621198], L-arginine alpha-ketoglutarate (AAKG) in sports supplements [PMID:16928472], and intravenous or parenteral AKG in surgical settings [PMID:1969067] [PMID:9527195]. Results from these salts cannot be assumed to apply to oral Ca-AKG.
- Commercial products include sustained- or delayed-release Ca-AKG, which is the form used in the ABLE RCT [PMID:37217632], and Rejuvant, an AKG-plus-vitamins formulation [PMID:34847066] [PMID:42166733].
- Ketoanalogue products for CKD, which are calcium salts of essential amino-acid keto-acids, are a different product. They are mentioned here only as evidence for a calcium-salt effect [PMID:39340710].

** Mechanisms
- *TCA cycle and nitrogen metabolism:* AKG is a Krebs-cycle intermediate. It takes part in amino-acid synthesis and nitrogen and ammonia balance [PMID:34952764] [PMID:36934991].
- *Cofactor for 2-OG-dependent dioxygenases:* About 60 Fe(II)/2-oxoglutarate-dependent oxygenases use AKG as a co-substrate. In animals they catalyse hydroxylations and N-demethylations, including collagen prolyl hydroxylation, transcriptional regulation and nucleic-acid modification and repair. Some are drug targets for anemia (HIF prolyl hydroxylases) [PMID:29494239].
  - TET DNA demethylases use AKG as a cofactor. In middle-aged mice, AKG increased DNA demethylation at the Prdm16 promoter [PMID:31691468]. In rodent CKD models, AKG's anti-calcification effect depended partly on TET2 [PMID:40383231].
  - Jumonji-C (JmjC) histone demethylases also depend on 2-OG [PMID:29494239]. In aged mice, AKG reduced H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 in bone-marrow MSCs and improved bone mass [PMID:33154378]. These are animal and mechanistic data.
- *Energy sensing and TOR (C. elegans):* AKG binds and inhibits ATP synthase subunit beta, lowers ATP and oxygen use, raises autophagy, and extends worm lifespan through a TOR-dependent route that mimics dietary restriction [PMID:24828042]. In mice given CaAKG, mTORC1 signalling did not fall in the tissues tested [PMID:32877690].
- *Inflammation (mice):* CaAKG lowered systemic inflammatory cytokines and induced IL-10 in T cells of female mice [PMID:32877690]. After myocardial infarction in mice, AKG reduced infarct size through FTO-dependent anti-inflammatory macrophage polarisation [PMID:40817969].
- *Possible harm signal (mice):* In high-fat-fed Ldlr-/- mice, AKG acting through its receptor OXGR1 expanded granulocyte-monocyte progenitors and worsened atherosclerotic plaque inflammation [PMID:41931905]. AKG biology depends on context.
- *Age-related decline:* AKG levels change with fasting, exercise and aging [PMID:32877690]. Circulating AKG was lower in 10-month-old than in 2-month-old mice [PMID:31691468]. The 2020 Cell Metabolism paper repeats a report that human plasma AKG falls about 10-fold between ages 40 and 80, citing a 2008 review [PMID:32877690] [PMID:18802218]. I could not open the primary human data behind that figure, so treat it as unverified. Human follicular-fluid AKG tended to be lower with age [PMID:33450127]. A 2025-26 multi-organ metabolic aging atlas in mice identified AKG among metabolic drivers in its organ clocks [PMID:41297544].

Overall: AKG has a credible mechanistic and animal basis for an anti-aging effect. The largest mammalian lifespan signal comes from one mouse study, whose authors held a related patent and equity in a company selling AKG [PMID:32877690]. The human evidence for biological aging is a single uncontrolled retrospective series and one cross-sectional association. The case series had disclosed manufacturer ties [PMID:34847066] [PMID:42166733]. No placebo-controlled human efficacy result for Ca-AKG on aging outcomes had been published as of 2026-10. Overall certainty for the primary use (healthy aging or biological-age reduction) is *very low*. A 2022 review by the group running ABLE also says no recent human studies show AKG treats aging or age-related disease [PMID:34952764].

** Dosing in trials
- ABLE RCT: 1 g/day sustained-release Ca-AKG for 6 months [PMID:37217632]. Supplement adherence was above 90% [PMID:42789532].
- NCT07114536 (China, completed 2025-11, n=34): 2 g/day Ca-AKG (0.5 g tablets, 2 twice daily) for 12 weeks; primary outcome PhenoAge. No results posted [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07114536].
- NCT07269704 (US, open-label, n=17): 2 g/day Ca-AKG for 7 days before eye surgery; outcome was aqueous-humor AKG [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07269704].
- Planned trial NCT07861867: 1 g/day Ca-AKG for 24 weeks on neurovascular coupling in older adults [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07861867].
- Bone RCT: 6 g AKG with 1.68 g calcium daily for 6 months [PMID:17896582].
- Mice: 2% (w/w) of diet from 18 months of age [PMID:32877690].
- OKG: 10 g/day for pressure ulcers [PMID:19621198]; 20 g/day enterally for burns [PMID:10890617]. AAKG: 12 g/day [PMID:16928472].
- Calcium load: at the ABLE dose, Ca-AKG adds roughly 0.2 g/day of elemental calcium (calculated from the formula [URL:https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/3018235]). Multi-gram doses add 0.4-1.7 g/day. That amount is comparable to the 1,000 mg/day calcium dose that raised kidney-stone risk in WHI [PMID:16481635].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Human safety data for oral Ca-AKG are limited to small, short trials. The 2020 mouse paper notes AKG has been used in human clinical studies without associated adverse effects [PMID:32877690]. In the 6-month bone RCT, 6 g AKG plus calcium caused no reported safety problems, but that abstract gives no adverse-event data [PMID:17896582].
- OKG at 20 g/day enterally was safe and well tolerated in burn patients [PMID:10890617]. In the OKG pressure-ulcer RCT, serious adverse events were similar between arms (15 vs 13 patients) and none were judged treatment-related [PMID:19621198]. AAKG at 12 g/day for 8 weeks appeared safe and well tolerated [PMID:16928472].
- In mice, lifelong 2% CaAKG produced no significant adverse changes on the measures reported [PMID:32877690].
- Calcium-related risks apply to the calcium part of the salt:
  - In WHI, 1,000 mg/day calcium carbonate plus 400 IU vitamin D3 increased kidney stones [PMID:16481635].
  - A patient-level meta-analysis found calcium supplements without vitamin D associated with more myocardial infarction (HR 1.31, 95% CI 1.02-1.67) [PMID:20671013]. This is contested, and the IOM found the extraskeletal evidence inconclusive [PMID:21118827].
  - High calcium intake, especially with alkali, can cause calcium-alkali (milk-alkali) syndrome: hypercalcemia, metabolic alkalosis and renal impairment [PMID:33732556].
- Theoretical or animal concerns:
  - AKG worsened atherosclerotic plaque inflammation in hyperlipidemic mice [PMID:41931905].
  - AKG and 2-OG enzymes are involved in cancer-cell behaviour and epigenetics [PMID:36934991] [PMID:29494239]. Long-term human oncologic safety has not been studied.

** Contraindications
- No formal contraindications have been established. None of these has been tested in Ca-AKG trials. Based on the calcium content, caution is reasonable in:
  - existing hypercalcemia or hyperparathyroidism [PMID:33732556] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=00f67e1a-6999-4295-9c9f-fcb1ce9711b8]
  - history of calcium-containing kidney stones [PMID:16481635]
  - advanced CKD or dialysis, where any calcium-salt load changes serum calcium and phosphate and should be managed within renal care [PMID:39340710] [PMID:8893139]
- Pregnancy, lactation and children: no human safety data were found. The ABLE trial enrolled only adults aged 40-60 [PMID:37217632].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- *Levothyroxine / thyroid hormone:* The FDA label lists calcium carbonate among phosphate binders that may bind levothyroxine. It advises taking levothyroxine at least 4 hours apart, because absorption may fall and cause hypothyroidism [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=008de8fd-150f-4022-8ccb-c8bfa94875c7]. Ca-AKG-specific data are absent; the risk is inferred from its calcium.
- *Fluoroquinolones (e.g., ciprofloxacin):* Take ciprofloxacin at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after products containing calcium, iron or zinc [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0c355077-7361-41d0-9e82-eb31beaf5daa].
- *Tetracyclines (e.g., doxycycline):* Antacids containing calcium impair tetracycline absorption [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=00b2aa98-dea2-90c3-e063-6294a90a35d6].
- *Bisphosphonates (e.g., alendronate):* Calcium supplements interfere with alendronate absorption [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=09765234-01bc-40f7-b9bb-c167056f3909].
- *Thiazide diuretics:* Thiazides reduce urinary calcium excretion and may raise serum calcium. Added calcium from Ca-AKG increases hypercalcemia risk [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=00f67e1a-6999-4295-9c9f-fcb1ce9711b8] [PMID:33732556].
- No AKG-specific pharmacokinetic drug-interaction studies were found.

*** Other supplements
- *Vitamin D-3:* In long-term RCTs, vitamin D raised the risk of hypercalcemia (RR 1.54, 95% CI 1.09-2.18) and hypercalciuria (RR 1.64, 95% CI 1.06-2.53) [PMID:27604776]. Combined calcium and vitamin D increased kidney stones in WHI [PMID:16481635]. Stacking Ca-AKG adds to the calcium load.
- *Calcium (other supplements, calcium antacids):* Doses add up. For stones and cardiovascular signals, see Safety [PMID:16481635] [PMID:20671013].
- *Other AKG salts (OKG, AAKG, AKG in pre-workout products):* They overlap in AKG content [PMID:16928472] [PMID:10890617].
- *Rejuvant-type formulations:* These combine AKG with vitamins [PMID:34847066], so check for duplicate vitamin intake.

*** Conditions
- Hypercalcemia or hyperparathyroidism; history of kidney stones; CKD or dialysis. See Contraindications.
- Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease: there is a mouse harm signal for AKG [PMID:41931905] and a contested human signal for calcium supplements and MI [PMID:20671013]. A mouse study also reported AKG protecting against myocardial infarction [PMID:40817969], so the direction is uncertain.

*** Lab-test interference
- No direct assay interference was found for AKG. Calcium salts can raise measured serum calcium; ketoanalogue calcium salts raised serum calcium in a CKD meta-analysis [PMID:39340710]. Serum calcium should be interpreted with supplement use in mind.
- Epigenetic-clock tests are the main outcome in AKG marketing and trials. Results come from proprietary clocks with sizeable error; for example, the 9-CpG clock had a mean absolute error of 5.4 years [PMID:42166733].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] ABLE trial operations paper: 120 adults aged 40-60 (59 AKG, 61 placebo) on 1 g/day for 6 months. Supplement adherence was above 90% and dropout 17%. Efficacy results are still unpublished [PMID:42789532].
- [2026-06] Cross-sectional study of 4,260 saliva epigenetic-test users: a delayed-release Ca-AKG + vitamin product was associated with lower epigenetic age residual; plain AKG was not significant. Healthy-user bias acknowledged [PMID:42166733].
- [2026-06] In Ldlr-/- mice on a high-fat diet, AKG worsened atherosclerotic plaque inflammation through OXGR1 [PMID:41931905].
- [2026-02] Multi-organ metabolic atlas of mouse aging identified AKG among metabolic drivers of aging in organ clocks (online 2025-11) [PMID:41297544].
- [2025-11] NCT07114536 (2 g/day Ca-AKG vs placebo for 12 weeks; PhenoAge) listed as completed with n=34. No results posted [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07114536].
- [2025-10] AKG improved muscle mass, endurance and grip in D-galactose-aged mice [PMID:41228408].
- [2025-08] ABLE recruitment: 467 people expressed interest and 120 enrolled; biological age exceeded chronological age in 80.2% [PMID:40819772].
- [2025-08] AKG reduced infarct size after MI in mice through FTO-mediated macrophage polarisation [PMID:40817969].
- [2025-08] AKG reduced vascular calcification in CKD rodents, partly through TET2/NLRP3 [PMID:40383231].
- [2024-11] Narrative review of AKG and skeletal muscle: promising preclinical findings, but large clinical trials are lacking [PMID:39599754].
- [2024-11] Meta-analysis of ketoanalogues (calcium keto-acid salts, not Ca-AKG) in CKD: GFR preserved, serum calcium higher [PMID:39340710].
- [2024-02] Cochrane review of nutrition for pressure ulcers: evidence for OKG on healing is very uncertain [PMID:38345088].
- Ongoing or registered trials: AEGIS (NCT07031128, AKG around CABG, n=250, recruiting) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07031128]; AKG for abdominal aortic aneurysm (NCT04723888, n=300, recruiting) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04723888]; ABLE (NCT05706389, active, not recruiting) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05706389].

** Open questions
- Does Ca-AKG change any validated clinical or functional aging outcome in humans? ABLE and NCT07114536 results are needed.
- Do the mouse lifespan gains (female-predominant) replicate in independent labs such as the NIA Interventions Testing Program, given the original authors' financial interests and the calcium-free control diet [PMID:32877690]?
- Oral bioavailability of AKG and dose-response in humans; whether sustained-release forms matter [PMID:42166733].
- How large and how real is the human age-related plasma AKG decline? The 10-fold figure is cited second-hand [PMID:32877690].
- Long-term cardiovascular and oncologic safety, given the opposing mouse atherosclerosis findings [PMID:41931905] [PMID:40817969] and AKG's role in cancer epigenetics [PMID:36934991].
- How much of any effect comes from calcium versus AKG?
