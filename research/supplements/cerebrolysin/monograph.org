** Identity & forms
- Cerebrolysin is a mixture of low-molecular-weight peptides and amino acids derived from porcine (pig) brain proteins, given parenterally [PMID:37818733] [PMID:28430363] [PMID:31710397]. Older literature also calls it FPF-1070 or "Cere" [PMID:23440834] [PMID:12111446].
- Product (Austrian prescribing information): solution for injection, 215.2 mg Cerebrolysin concentrate per mL; excipients sodium hydroxide and water for injection. Marketing authorisation holder is EVER Neuro Pharma GmbH (Unterach, Austria), and it is "only available on prescription and in pharmacies" [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/about-cerebrolysin].
- Labelled indications (Austrian SmPC summary): cerebrovascular disorders, especially senile dementia of Alzheimer's type, vascular dementia, stroke and craniocerebral trauma (concussion and contusion) [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/about-cerebrolysin].
- Regulatory status:
  - Widely used for acute ischaemic stroke in Russia, Eastern Europe, China and other Asian and post-Soviet countries [PMID:37818733].
  - A 2021 review states it "is not approved for use in the USA" but is used clinically in over 50 countries [PMID:33515100]. The manufacturer states that Cerebrolysin "is not registered with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and is not approved for sale or distribution in the United States" [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/].
  - It is registered in all CIS countries reviewed and is on Russia's essential medicines list. It is not on the WHO Model EML, and US, EU and UK dementia guidelines do not mention it. Of the national guidelines reviewed, only the Russian Alzheimer's guideline recommends it [PMID:41467376].
  - A 2026 survey of 21 Asian countries or regions found it available in about a third to half of them, with low use where it is not in national guidelines [PMID:42525569].
- RX_STATUS choice: coded "rx" because it is a licensed prescription medicine in its home markets [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/about-cerebrolysin] [PMID:33515100]. Since it is not approved for sale or distribution in the US [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/], any US supply falls outside approved channels. Commercial websites describe US "research-use" or grey-market sale, but no peer-reviewed or regulatory source documenting this was found, so it is not treated as established here.
- Cerebrolysin-like products: the 2023 Cochrane review pooled Cerebrolysin with Cortexin, a peptide mixture derived from cattle brain [PMID:37818733], so results may not transfer between products.

** Mechanisms
- The peptides are proposed to mimic endogenous neurotrophic factors (NGF, IGF-1, BDNF, VEGF) and to modulate their expression. Proposed effects include neuroplasticity, neurogenesis, angiogenesis and anti-inflammatory actions (review) [PMID:37052231]. Broader reviews describe multiple regeneration-related signalling pathways [PMID:33515100] [PMID:35268364].
- Sonic hedgehog (Shh): in rats with embolic stroke, treatment from 24 h raised Shh, patched and smoothened. It increased subventricular-zone progenitor proliferation, neurogenesis, oligodendrogenesis and axonal remodelling and improved function. The Shh inhibitor cyclopamine abolished all of these effects [PMID:23696546].
- PI3K/Akt: in rat MCAo, 2.5-5 mL/kg started 24-48 h after stroke increased neurogenesis and cut TUNEL+ cells by about 50% without reducing infarct volume. Blocking PI3K/Akt abolished the progenitor proliferation [PMID:20857512].
- Neurovascular unit and blood-brain barrier: reviews and imaging data from CEREHETIS suggest blood-brain barrier stabilisation, used as the rationale for combining it with reperfusion therapy [PMID:36973684] [PMID:41349847].
- Evidence quality caveat: several preclinical Cerebrolysin papers have been retracted, including tauopathy (2025) and Rett-model (2026) studies [PMID:41382024] [PMID:41874695].

** Dosing in trials
- Route: IV infusion or injection, with IM injection used in some paediatric trials [PMID:28074392]. The manufacturer's guidance allows undiluted IV injection up to 10 mL or IM up to 5 mL, each given slowly over 3 minutes. Doses of 10-50 mL are diluted to at least 100 mL with saline, Ringer's or 5% glucose and infused within 15 minutes [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/how-to-use-cerebrolysin]. The treatment handbook reports best experience with 100 mL infused over 30-45 minutes, no longer than one hour [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
- Manufacturer-recommended daily doses: stroke 20-50 mL/day for 10-21 days; TBI 20-50 mL/day for 7-30 days; Alzheimer's disease or cognitive problems 10-30 mL/day, 5 days a week for 4 weeks, 2-4 cycles a year [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/how-to-use-cerebrolysin].
- Acute ischaemic stroke trials:
  - CASTA: 30 mL/day IV for 10 days, started within 12 h, with aspirin 100 mg, in more than 50 Asian centres [PMID:19765131] [PMID:22282884]. CARS: 30 mL/day for 21 days from 24-72 h, plus rehabilitation [PMID:26564102].
  - Pooled RCTs used 30-50 mL/day for 10-21 days [PMID:29248999]. The 2021 EAN/EFNR GRADE guideline recommends 30 mL/day IV for at least 10 days for early motor neurorehabilitation [PMID:34152062].
- Thrombolysis/thrombectomy add-on:
  - With rt-PA: 30 mL/day for 10 days, started 1 h after alteplase [PMID:23009193].
  - CEREHETIS: 30 mL/day for 14 days, started at the same time as alteplase via a separate line [PMID:36973684].
  - After thrombectomy: 30 mL/day for 21 days, with a second course at days 69-90 [PMID:40325343].
- TBI (CAPTAIN): 50 mL/day for 10 days, then two cycles of 10 mL/day for 10 days [PMID:33620612]. Mild TBI RCT (n=32): 30 mL/day for 5 days improved CASI at week 12 [PMID:23656173]. CAPTAIN-rTMS (n=93): no significant primary-outcome difference [PMID:37360156].
- Vascular dementia: 20 mL/day IV in 2 cycles [PMID:20656516]. Alzheimer's: 30 mL IV 5 days a week for 4 weeks [PMID:12111446] [PMID:11129744]. Combination with donepezil used 10 mL [PMID:21679156]. Amnestic MCI (open, 3 years): yearly 20 x 20 mL infusions; lower dementia conversion reported [PMID:39435777].
- Paediatric: 0.1 mL/kg IM daily for 10 days, then weekly for 4 months (cerebral palsy) [PMID:28074392]. 0.1 mL/kg weekly for 3 months (preterm infants) [PMID:33935111].
- Other: 10-day postoperative course in degenerative cervical myelopathy (RCT, n=90; mJOA better at 1 year) [PMID:36730671]. 50 mL/day for 5 days around CABG [PMID:40350671].
- Timing: the handbook advises giving later doses in the morning because the infusion "is stimulating and may cause excitability" [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Cochrane stroke review (2023):
  - Total adverse events: RR 1.03 (0.92-1.14), low certainty. Non-fatal SAEs increased: RR 2.39 (1.10-5.23; 3 trials, n=1335), moderate certainty. The increase was larger with 30 mL for 10 days (RR 2.87). Fatal SAEs: RR 0.90 (0.59-1.38) [PMID:37818733].
  - The 2017 and 2020 versions found the same signal: non-fatal SAEs 20/667 vs 8/668, RR 2.47 [PMID:28430363], and RR 2.15 (1.01-4.55) [PMID:32662068].
- A separate safety meta-analysis: 12 RCTs (n=2202) found no significant differences. SAEs were lowest at 50 mL (RR 0.6) [PMID:34959697]. A 2025 MA of 14 RCTs reported SAE RR 1.08 (0.84-1.40) [PMID:41018475]. A network MA ranked cerebrolysin among agents with a higher adverse-effect rate than conventional treatment [PMID:39575393].
- Common adverse reactions: vertigo, agitation and feeling hot, with incidence similar to placebo in pooled trials [PMID:22514795].
  - The manufacturer FAQ lists dizziness, headache, sweating and nausea [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/faqs].
  - In one Alzheimer's RCT, AEs occurred in 64% on Cerebrolysin vs 73% on placebo, mostly headache, dizziness, weight loss and anxiety [PMID:12111446].
- Infusion-rate effects: increased heart rate, blood pressure and arrhythmia are attributed to fast IV bolus. Fever can occur rarely [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
- Seizures: the handbook says post-stroke/TBI seizures in non-epileptic patients are not a contraindication, and that "there is no evidence that Cerebrolysin causes seizures". Yet the SmPC summary lists epilepsy as a contraindication [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf] [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/about-cerebrolysin].
- Sterility: microbial contaminants may grow once the ampoule is opened, so single-use sets and immediate administration are required [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/how-to-use-cerebrolysin] [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
- Liver: a translational study raised the theoretical concern that Shh amplification could speed NAFLD progression. In stroke patients with raised transaminases, however, AST improved in most [PMID:36362945].
- Bias: the manufacturer supported three of the Cochrane-included stroke studies. Risk of bias was unclear or high in most domains [PMID:37818733].
- No cited source on prion or other transmissible-agent risk from porcine sourcing was found.

** Contraindications
- Per the Austrian SmPC summary: hypersensitivity to any component, epilepsy, and severe renal impairment [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/about-cerebrolysin].
- The treatment handbook phrases these as hypersensitivity (including to the excipients), status epilepticus, and severe renal failure. It specifies no use at GFR categories 4 and 5 [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
- Porcine origin [PMID:37818733] means known hypersensitivity to porcine proteins falls under the component-hypersensitivity contraindication. This is an inference; no case series of porcine-allergy reactions was found.
- Pregnancy and lactation: the SmPC has a section on these, but its content was not accessible [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/about-cerebrolysin]. No cited human safety data.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- MAO inhibitors: high-dose MAOIs combined with higher Cerebrolysin doses (30 mL or more) "have been reported to increase blood pressure" [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf]. The FAQ notes possible additive effects with antidepressants or MAOIs [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/faqs].
- Antidepressants: the handbook advises attention to additive effects and suggests lowering the antidepressant dose if this is psychiatrically safe [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
- Alteplase (rt-PA): co-administration was safe in a placebo-controlled RCT (no difference in AEs) [PMID:23009193]. In CEREHETIS, symptomatic haemorrhagic transformation was lower (OR 0.248) [PMID:36973684]. A post hoc analysis suggested earlier stabilisation of HT hazard, which is hypothesis-generating for anticoagulation timing [PMID:41640685].
- Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine): the combination was safe in an RCT, with the best cognitive scores in the combination arm [PMID:21679156]. A safety review reached the same conclusion [PMID:22514795].
- IV admixtures: do not mix in the same infusion with balanced amino-acid solutions, vitamins or cardiovascular drugs. These may be given concomitantly but separately [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/how-to-use-cerebrolysin].
- Antiplatelets and statins: CASTA and the vascular dementia RCT co-administered aspirin [PMID:22282884] [PMID:20656516]. The manufacturer reports no incompatibilities with statins, aspirin or NOACs [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/cerebrolysin/faqs].
*** Other supplements
- Citicoline: a retrospective severe-TBI cohort (n=80) found a non-significant trend to better outcome with citicoline plus Cerebrolysin vs citicoline alone [PMID:41409218].
- Aspirin: co-administered in large RCTs without a specific interaction signal [PMID:22282884]. No other cited supplement interaction was found.
*** Conditions
- Epilepsy, status epilepticus, and severe renal impairment (GFR categories 4-5): contraindicated (see Contraindications).
- Acute ischaemic stroke: watch for non-fatal SAEs, especially with 30 mL for 10 days [PMID:37818733].
- Hypertension and arrhythmia: blood pressure and heart-rate rises are linked to fast infusion and to MAOI co-use [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
- Chronic liver disease or NAFLD: theoretical Shh-related concern; limited clinical data [PMID:36362945].
- Children and infants: only small open trials [PMID:33935111] [PMID:28074392]; the handbook mentions infant use only as "experience reports" [URL:https://www.cerebrolysin.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cerebrolysin_TreatmentHandbook_2023_SCREEN_nocomments.pdf].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference was found. Cerebrolysin was not associated with major changes in vital signs or laboratory parameters in trial safety data [PMID:22514795].

** Latest research
- [2026-07] Asian survey (21 countries/regions): Cerebrolysin is available in about a third to half; use is low where it is not in guidelines [PMID:42525569].
- [2026-06] Thai prospective non-randomised cohort in moderate TBI with ICH (n=340): better CRS-R. The reported 6-month survival (59.4% vs 27.8%) is implausibly large for moderate TBI and comes from a single-blind, non-randomised design [PMID:42110924].
- [2026-05] CIS regulatory review: registered across the CIS and recommended only in Russian Alzheimer's guidelines; not on the WHO EML [PMID:41467376].
- [2026-03] MA of 3 observational thrombectomy studies (n=294): mRS 0-3 RR 1.56; sICH RR 0.12; authors caution evidence is limited [PMID:41880098]. A Rett-syndrome mouse study was retracted [PMID:41874695].
- [2026-02] 12-month thrombectomy cohort (propensity-matched): functional independence aOR 6.10 (1.64-22.66). Mortality similar (18% in both groups) [PMID:41739286].
- [2026-01] CEREHETIS post hoc: in high HT-risk patients, symptomatic HT HR 0.245; earlier hazard stabilisation [PMID:41640685].
- [2025-12] CERECAP programme review of Cerebrolysin with recanalisation therapy [PMID:41349847]. Single-centre thrombectomy add-on study (n=50 vs 50 historical controls): mRS 0-2 at 90 days 68% vs 44%; secondary ICH 14% vs 40% [PMID:40325343].
- [2025-12] Retraction of a Cerebrolysin tauopathy mouse study [PMID:41382024]. Retrospective severe-TBI cohort of citicoline plus Cerebrolysin: no significant difference [PMID:41409218].
- [2025-11] VCI systematic review and MA (173 trials): small-to-moderate cognitive improvement for cerebrolysin, with substantial heterogeneity [PMID:41198594].
- [2025-10] C-REGS2 comparative-effectiveness registry (n=1769, 16 countries): ordinal mRS day 90 MW 0.6157. It is non-randomised [PMID:40851188]. CLINCH ICH pilot RCT protocol (50 mL/day x 14 d) published [PMID:41180520].
- [2025-08] MA of 14 RCTs (n=2884): NIHSS change MD +1.39; no significant effect on mRS 0-2, SAEs or mortality [PMID:41018475].
- [2025-05] CABG pilot (n=55): delirium 0 vs 3 cases (not significant) [PMID:40350671]. A CNS Drugs review lists cerebrolysin among TBI neuroprotectants still without established benefit [PMID:40087248].
- [2025-04] ESCAS pilot RCT (aphasia): WAB difference 14.8 points; no safety concerns [PMID:39957612].
- [2025-02] Cardioembolic thrombectomy cohort: mRS 0-2 64% vs 34.7% [PMID:40027163].
- [2025-01] Network MA of neuroprotectants (42 RCTs): butylphthalide and edaravone ranked highest; cerebrolysin did not rank first on any outcome [PMID:39834702].
- [2024-12] Narrative review of neuroprotective drugs in severe TBI [PMID:38748062].
- [2024-11] Network MA (35 studies): cerebrolysin was associated with lower mortality but a higher adverse-effect rate than conventional treatment [PMID:39575393].
- [2024-10] SAH observational cohort (n=47): mortality benefit only combined with neuromonitoring in severe SAH [PMID:39595058].

** Open questions
- Does Cerebrolysin improve patient-important long-term outcomes (death or dependency, quality of life)? The Cochrane review notes that no included trial reported these [PMID:37818733].
- What explains the non-fatal SAE signal (RR 2.39), and why does it conflict with other meta-analyses that found no excess [PMID:37818733] [PMID:34959697]?
- Positive results come mainly from trials using multivariate Wei-Lachin/Mann-Whitney endpoints, from subgroups (severe stroke; a review argues effect size rises with severity [PMID:30004268]) or from observational designs [PMID:22282884] [PMID:29248999] [PMID:40851188]. These need independent confirmatory RCTs.
- Thrombectomy add-on benefit rests on non-randomised cohorts with historical controls [PMID:41880098].
- Vascular dementia and Alzheimer's evidence is small, at high risk of bias, and short-term [PMID:31710397] [PMID:25832905].
- Retractions of preclinical papers undermine parts of the mechanistic evidence base [PMID:41382024] [PMID:41874695].
- Full SmPC text on pregnancy, lactation, renal dosing and the evidence behind the epilepsy contraindication was not accessible.
- No data exist on the safety, sterility or identity of product sold outside regulated channels.
