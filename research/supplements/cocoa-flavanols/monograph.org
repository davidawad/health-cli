** Identity & forms
- Cocoa flavanols are flavan-3-ols from the cacao bean (/Theobroma cacao/): the monomers (-)-epicatechin and (+)-catechin, plus oligomeric and polymeric procyanidins. Trials attribute much of the acute vascular effect to (-)-epicatechin [PMID:16418281].
- Standardized extract used in COSMOS (Mars Edge, sold commercially as CocoaVia): 500 mg/d cocoa flavanols including 80 mg/d (-)-epicatechin [PMID:35294962] [PMID:35288332]. It also delivered about 50 mg/d theobromine and about 15 mg/d caffeine [PMID:35294962].
- Food forms (cocoa powder, dark chocolate) vary widely in flavanol content because of processing, and they bring sugar, fat, methylxanthines and heavy metals with them. Short-term trials used 30 to 1218 mg/d flavanols delivered in 1.4 to 105 g/d of cocoa product [PMID:28439881]. Alkalized (Dutch-processed) cocoa has been used as a low-flavanol placebo [PMID:40614481].
- Regulatory: EFSA (2012) accepted the claim "Cocoa flavanols help maintain endothelium-dependent vasodilation, which contributes to normal blood flow" at 200 mg/d cocoa flavanols, an amount it says 2.5 g of high-flavanol cocoa powder or 10 g of high-flavanol dark chocolate can provide [DOI:10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2809]. In the US these products are sold as dietary supplements (OTC).
** Mechanisms
- Nitric oxide and endothelial function (human acute study plus ex vivo work): flavanol-rich cocoa raised circulating NO species and FMD. Plasma (-)-epicatechin and epicatechin-7-O-glucuronide independently predicted the response, NOS inhibition abolished the effect, and pure (-)-epicatechin reproduced it [PMID:16418281].
- Absorption and metabolism (human crossover): monomers are absorbed and appear as glucuronidated, sulfated and methylated epicatechin. Procyanidins mostly reach the colon, where gut microbiota convert them to 5-(3',4'-dihydroxyphenyl)-gamma-valerolactone. This conversion varies a lot between individuals [PMID:25546356].
- Inflammation: in a 2-year COSMOS ancillary study, cocoa extract lowered hsCRP and raised IFN-gamma [PMID:40966617]. A meta-analysis of food-based cocoa trials found lower MDA and higher NO, with no consistent effect on CRP, IL-6 or TNF-alpha [PMID:38925412].
- Platelets: a meta-analysis of RCTs reports beneficial effects on platelet function and arterial stiffness (PWV, AIx) [PMID:32126803]. In a small RCT, 100 g of dark chocolate (but not milk or white chocolate) inhibited collagen-induced platelet aggregation [PMID:12944249]. In contrast, a 12-week RCT of up to 2000 mg/d flavanols found no change in platelet function in healthy adults [PMID:26537937].
- Methylxanthines: cocoa contains small amounts of caffeine and larger amounts of theobromine [PMID:30372004]. The COSMOS authors suggest these may add to the vascular and CNS effects [PMID:35294962].

** Dosing in trials
- COSMOS and its ancillary studies: 500 mg/d cocoa flavanols including 80 mg (-)-epicatechin, given as capsules (with about 50 mg theobromine and 15 mg caffeine/d), median 3.6 y [PMID:35294962] [PMID:35288332].
- BP trials: 30 to 1218 mg/d flavanols (mean 670 mg) for 2 to 18 weeks [PMID:28439881]. Flavan-3-ol MA mean intake 586 mg/d; BP lowering was dose-related to epicatechin only in cocoa studies [PMID:40126033].
- FMD: dose-response MA suggests an inverted U, with the optimum around 710 mg total flavanols / 95 mg (-)-epicatechin [PMID:31524216]. EFSA claim dose: 200 mg/d [DOI:10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2809].
- Higher-dose safety: up to 2000 mg/d for 12 weeks was well tolerated in healthy adults [PMID:26537937]. An ongoing pilot is testing 500 vs 1000 mg cocoa extract for migraine [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06808230].
- PAD: 15 g cocoa with 75 mg epicatechin/d as a beverage for 6 months [PMID:32078436]. COCOA-PAD II (ongoing) uses about 500 mg flavanols/80 mg epicatechin capsules [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04794530].
** Safety & adverse effects
- Generally well tolerated. In the Cochrane review, GI complaints or nausea occurred in 1% of active vs 0.4% of control participants [PMID:28439881]. In the 2025 flavan-3-ol MA, adverse events were minor and infrequent (0.4%) [PMID:40126033].
- COSMOS: "no safety concerns." Self-reported nausea was slightly more common with cocoa extract (HR 1.06; 1.02-1.11), while self-reported migraine was lower (HR 0.85; 0.78-0.93), as were other headaches and flu-like symptoms (HR 0.95 each) [PMID:35294962].
- Up to 2000 mg/d for 12 weeks: adverse events were mild and did not differ from control, with no clinically relevant metabolic changes [PMID:26537937].
- Caffeine/theobromine: the COSMOS extract had modest methylxanthine content (about 50 mg theobromine and 15 mg caffeine/d), and the trial excluded people reporting extreme caffeine sensitivity [PMID:35294962]. Chocolate foods contain small amounts of caffeine and larger amounts of theobromine [PMID:30372004]. The total depends on product and dose; check the label.
- Heavy metals (cocoa foods): an FDA survey of US retail products found Cd 0.004-3.15 mg/kg and Pb up to 0.38 mg/kg. Both correlated with % cocoa solids, and Cd was higher in products of Latin American origin [PMID:29310543]. In 2023, Consumer Reports found detectable lead and cadmium in all 48 cocoa/chocolate products tested, with 16 of 48 above California MADL-based thresholds (Pb 0.5 ug/d, Cd 4.1 ug/d). Some cocoa powders were high in lead, while no milk chocolate bar exceeded either threshold [URL:https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2023/10/consumer-reports-urges-hersheys-to-get-heavy-metals-out-of-chocolate-products]. No published abstract reports heavy-metal levels for standardized cocoa-extract capsules. The 2024 SR found that none of 33 cocoa trials mentioned heavy-metal contamination risk [PMID:38925412].
- Migraine: chocolate is a patient-reported trigger for about 13.9% (7.0-21.0%) of people with migraine. This comes from observational data with very high heterogeneity, and the authors advise individualized diaries rather than universal avoidance [PMID:42662243]. In contrast, the RCT data from COSMOS show fewer self-reported migraines with cocoa extract [PMID:35294962].
- Lactation: low maternal chocolate intake is not considered problematic, but extreme amounts can affect the infant because the methylxanthines pass into milk [PMID:30372004].
** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindications are established in the cited trials. Cautions:
  - Extreme caffeine sensitivity (the COSMOS exclusion criterion) [PMID:35294962].
  - MAOI therapy, where large amounts of chocolate or caffeine may cause hypertensive reactions [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=40483372-448f-4284-976c-8462ef256661].
  - Pregnancy and young children: avoid high-cadmium or high-lead cocoa food products [URL:https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2023/10/consumer-reports-urges-hersheys-to-get-heavy-metals-out-of-chocolate-products].
  - COSMOS safety exclusions included renal failure/dialysis and cirrhosis, so there are no long-term trial data in those groups [PMID:35294962].
** Interactions
*** Drugs
- MAOIs (phenelzine and others): the phenelzine label warns "Excessive amounts of caffeine and chocolate may also cause hypertensive reactions" [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=40483372-448f-4284-976c-8462ef256661]. The risk with standardized extracts (low caffeine) is probably smaller but has not been studied.
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets (theoretical): cocoa products show antiplatelet effects in some RCTs [PMID:32126803] [PMID:12944249], but up to 2000 mg/d flavanols for 12 weeks did not change platelet function in healthy adults [PMID:26537937]. COSMOS found no increase in VTE (HR 0.88) [PMID:41651011]. No clinical bleeding interaction data were found.
- Antihypertensives: the BP-lowering effect is additive in principle. It is largest in hypertensive participants (about -4 to -5.9 mmHg SBP) [PMID:28439881] [PMID:40126033]. A clinically significant interaction has not been reported.
- Antidiabetic drugs: flavanols improve insulin-sensitivity biomarkers [PMID:27683874], but they did not change T2D incidence in COSMOS [PMID:37816167]. Risk of additive hypoglycemia appears low.
- Stimulants and caffeine-containing products: these add to the theobromine and caffeine in cocoa products [PMID:35294962] [PMID:30372004]. Co-ingesting 415 mg flavanols with 215 mg caffeine had no acute cognitive interaction, positive or negative [PMID:39607519].
*** Other supplements
- Multivitamin: in the COSMOS 2x2 factorial design there was no cocoa x MVM interaction on cognition [PMID:36102337].
- Fish Oil (Omega-3): in the 12-month CANN RCT (n=259, people with SCI/MCI), DHA-rich fish oil plus 500 mg/d flavan-3-ols from dark chocolate did not improve cognition. Executive function and alertness declined relative to control, and glucose rose slightly; this shows no evidence of a beneficial synergy [PMID:37315924].
- Ashwagandha, Vitamin C, CoQ10, Vitamin D-3, other batch supplements: no cited interaction source found.
*** Conditions
- Hypertension: greater BP reduction in hypertensive than normotensive participants [PMID:28439881] [PMID:40126033].
- Migraine: possible trigger for some people with chocolate foods [PMID:42662243], although RCT data with the extract show fewer self-reported migraines [PMID:35294962].
- Pregnancy and childhood: heavy-metal exposure from cocoa foods [PMID:29310543] [URL:https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2023/10/consumer-reports-urges-hersheys-to-get-heavy-metals-out-of-chocolate-products].
- Caffeine sensitivity [PMID:35294962].
- Lactation: methylxanthines pass into breast milk at high intakes [PMID:30372004].
*** Lab-test interference
- No cited assay interference. Flavanol intake can be tracked with urinary biomarkers (e.g., gamma-valerolactone metabolites) [PMID:37252983] [PMID:40568979].
** Genotype notes
No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source found. Note that gut-microbial production of valerolactone metabolites varies widely between individuals [PMID:25546356].
** Latest research
- [2026-09] COSMOS-Clinic: cocoa extract did not change 2-year NfL, NT1-tau or oligomeric Abeta [PMID:42775737].
- [2026-08] SR/MA: chocolate is a patient-reported migraine trigger in 13.9% of people with migraine, with very high heterogeneity [PMID:42662243].
- [2026-03] COSMOS win-ratio reanalysis: hierarchical CVD composite WR 0.84 (0.72-0.99), net treatment benefit -0.54% [PMID:41849087].
- [2026-03] COSMOS (Nat Med): cocoa extract had no effect on five epigenetic clocks over 2 y [PMID:41803341].
- [2026-02] COSMOS: VTE HR 0.88 (0.72-1.08), not significant [PMID:41651011].
- [2025-11] Whole-food FMD meta-analysis: cocoa +1.68% FMD [PMID:40972869].
- [2025-10] Flavan-3-ol MA (145 RCTs): office BP -2.8/-2.0 mmHg, FMD +1.7% chronic; dose-related to epicatechin in cocoa studies [PMID:40126033].
- [2025-08] COSMOS: no overall effect on incident hypertension (HR 0.96); HR 0.76 in people with SBP <120 [PMID:40832703].
- [2025-08] COSMOS: hsCRP -8.4%/y vs placebo [PMID:40966617].
- [2025-07] Biomarker-based COSMOS subcohort reanalysis (Mars-affiliated authors): CVD mortality HR 0.44 (0.20-0.97) and major CVD HR 0.48 when adherence and background diet were accounted for. About 33% of the active arm did not reach expected biomarker levels [PMID:40568979].
- [2025-06] Acute RCTs: no effect on cognitive control at 415-623 mg [PMID:40614481]; better Stroop performance under exercise fatigue at 500 mg (n=18) [PMID:40493074].
- [2025-05] COSMOS: no effect on AMD overall (HR 0.87) [PMID:40146119] or fractures (aHR 1.03) [PMID:39964350].
- [2025-04] SR: microvascular benefits are less consistent than conduit-artery benefits [PMID:40217225].
- [2024-11] RCT: no acute cognitive effect of cocoa flavanols plus caffeine [PMID:39607519].
- [2024-06] MAs: cocoa lowered SBP -2.52 and LDL -9.47 mg/dL [PMID:38931273]; lowered MDA and raised NO, with no consistent CRP effect [PMID:38925412].
- [2024-01] COSMOS-Clinic: no effect on 2-year cognition [PMID:38070683].
** Open questions
- Does cocoa extract reduce hard CVD events? The COSMOS primary endpoint was null and the CVD-death benefit is a secondary finding. Supportive reanalyses (per-protocol, win ratio, biomarker-based) partly involve industry-affiliated authors, and all trials were Mars-supported [PMID:35294962] [PMID:41849087] [PMID:40568979].
- Who benefits cognitively? Signals are limited to people with low habitual flavanol intake or poor diet quality [PMID:37252983] [PMID:38070683].
- Optimal dose and the relative roles of epicatechin, procyanidins and theobromine [PMID:31524216] [PMID:35294962].
- Heavy-metal content of standardized extract capsules (no published data found).
- Migraine: ongoing pilot RCT of 500 and 1000 mg [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06808230]. PAD: COCOA-PAD II [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04794530].
