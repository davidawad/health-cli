** Identity & forms
- Hydrolyzed collagen (collagen peptides) is enzymatically hydrolyzed gelatin from bovine, porcine, poultry or fish (marine) skin, bone or cartilage. Source animal matters for allergy and product quality [PMID:41804623] [PMID:42451143].
- Gelatin is the partially hydrolyzed, larger-molecule form. Low-molecular-weight or tripeptide-enriched hydrolysates are marketed for higher peptide absorption [PMID:26934933] [PMID:41788055].
- Branded "specific collagen peptides" (e.g. VERISOL for skin/nails, TENDOFORTE for tendon, and the peptides used in the König bone trial) are claimed to have distinct peptide profiles [PMID:28786550] [PMID:30609761] [PMID:29337906].
- Undenatured (native) type II collagen (UC-II, 40 mg/d) is a different product. It is chicken-sternum cartilage that is not hydrolyzed and is thought to act by oral tolerance rather than as a protein source. Its trial results should not be pooled with peptide trials [PMID:40253594] [PMID:26822714].
- Protein quality: collagen has no tryptophan, so by PDCAAS it is an incomplete protein. Modelling suggests up to 36% of dietary protein could be collagen peptides without breaking indispensable-amino-acid requirements in a Western diet [PMID:31096622].
- Collagen is rich in glycine, proline and hydroxyproline. Gelatin ingestion raises plasma glycine, proline, hydroxyproline and hydroxylysine dose-dependently, peaking about 1 h after intake [PMID:27852613] [PMID:42687500].

** Mechanisms
- Absorption: Gly-Pro-Hyp and Pro-Hyp appear intact in plasma and urine after ingestion, more so with tripeptide-enriched products [PMID:26934933]. Dissolving in coffee changed free Hyp/Pro uptake but not bioactive-peptide AUC or Cmax [PMID:40356137].
- Substrate supply plus exercise: 15 g vitamin C-enriched gelatin before exercise doubled serum PINP (amino-terminal propeptide of type I collagen), a marker of collagen synthesis [PMID:27852613]. 30 g hydrolysate raised PINP AUC more than 15 g or 0 g after resistance exercise [PMID:38007183].
- Vitamin C is a cofactor for collagen hydroxylation. Supporting human data come mainly from trials of combined products [PMID:30386805] [PMID:41900537].
- Muscle protein synthesis: collagen is a poor stimulus compared with whey or pea protein, consistent with its low leucine and indispensable-amino-acid content [PMID:31919527] [PMID:38762187]. Gains in fat-free mass may partly reflect connective tissue rather than contractile protein [PMID:31126103] [PMID:42376607].
- Hydroxyproline is metabolized to glycolate and oxalate. 30 g/d gelatin raised urinary oxalate by 43% versus whey [PMID:17021603].
- Glycine-mediated thermoregulatory/NMDA effects are proposed for sleep. Mediation has not been tested [PMID:42687500] [PMID:22293292].

** Dosing in trials
- Skin: 1-10 g/d (most often 2.5-5 g/d, median 3.5 g/d) for 8-24 weeks [PMID:40826844] [PMID:42342959]. 1 g/d low-molecular-weight peptides were used for 24 weeks in one trial [PMID:41788055].
- Osteoarthritis: hydrolysate 3 g/d for 180 days in one RCT [PMID:40977985]. UC-II is dosed at 40 mg/d [PMID:26822714] [PMID:41787523].
- Tendon: 15-30 g/d, with >=50 mg vitamin C, taken around high-load training [PMID:41900537] [PMID:40100255]. 10 g/d for 16 weeks without training increased Achilles stiffness [PMID:40623147].
- Bone: 5 g/d specific collagen peptides for 12 months [PMID:29337906]. 5 g/d plus calcium 500 mg and vitamin D3 400 IU [PMID:34980546].
- Muscle with resistance training: 15 g/d post-exercise for 12 weeks [PMID:26353786] [PMID:31010031] [PMID:31126103].
- Sleep: 15 g 1 h before bed [PMID:37874350].

** Safety & adverse effects
- RCT syntheses show no increase in adverse events or withdrawals versus control [PMID:38218227] [PMID:40806131]. Skin trials report a low adverse-event rate [PMID:42342959]. Supplements in photoaging trials of <=24 weeks were safe [PMID:40761858].
- 6 months of fish collagen 5 g/d (+/- calcium/vitamin D) did not change creatinine, ALT or AST [PMID:41002783].
- Allergy:
  - Fish collagen (type I) is a clinically relevant fish allergen. 21% of 101 fish-allergic patients were sensitized and collagen caused functional IgE cross-linking [PMID:32389794].
  - In Japan, 50% of fish-allergic patients had IgE to mackerel collagen, with high cross-reactivity across 22 species [PMID:26785247].
  - Bovine type I collagen is the main allergen in bovine-gelatin allergy. Some fish-allergic children have IgE to fish gelatin [PMID:10984381].
  - Mammalian gelatin carries alpha-gal, a trigger in alpha-gal syndrome [PMID:35879928] [PMID:40752174].
- Contaminants:
  - Among 30 sports protein products, mercury was mostly <0.01 mg/kg; the one exception was a collagen sample (0.02 mg/kg). Modelled hazard index rose to 0.90 at 90 g/d of protein products, and the 95th-percentile HI was 1.28 [PMID:41655677].
  - Bone broths contained several times the lead concentration of the water used to make them [PMID:23375414]. Source and contaminant quality vary by product [PMID:41804623].
- Oxalate (theoretical stone risk): 30 g gelatin raised urinary oxalate by 43% and glycolate 5.3-fold. Loads of 5-10 g changed urinary oxalate, but 1-2 g did not [PMID:17021603]. A 3-day 30 g/d study found no oxalate or CaOx supersaturation change [PMID:31161522].
- Protein displacement: collagen does not overcome anabolic resistance in older adults. Relying on it as the main protein source would lower protein quality [PMID:38762187] [PMID:31096622].

** Contraindications
- Fish allergy (marine collagen) and bovine/porcine gelatin allergy, including alpha-gal syndrome [PMID:32389794] [PMID:10984381] [PMID:35879928].
- Primary hyperoxaluria or recurrent calcium-oxalate stones: hydroxyproline is an oxalate precursor, and dietary gelatin/hydroxyproline restriction is suggested in primary hyperoxaluria [PMID:17021603] [PMID:30039216].
- No cited pregnancy, lactation or pediatric safety data.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- No pharmacokinetic drug interaction studies were found. Collagen is digested to amino acids and peptides [PMID:26934933].
- Antihypertensives: pooled SBP -5.04 mmHg, with extreme heterogeneity [PMID:35658958]. SBP also fell in an obesity RCT [PMID:39458544]. Any additive effect is minor.
- Antidiabetic drugs: one RCT in T2DM (13 g/d marine peptides) reported lower FBG and HbA1c [PMID:21164551]. A later MA found no glycemic effect [PMID:35658958]. Any additive effect is minor.
- Gelatin-containing vaccines and drugs: these are relevant to people sensitized to gelatin or alpha-gal [PMID:40752174] [PMID:35879928].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin C: claimed synergy, because ascorbate is a cofactor for collagen synthesis. Most tendon trials co-administer >=50 mg vitamin C, so no trial has isolated its added effect [PMID:27852613] [PMID:41900537] [PMID:34808597] [PMID:30386805].
- Hyaluronic Acid: adding 30 mg HA to 5 g collagen + vitamin C gave no extra skin benefit over collagen + vitamin C [PMID:38931263]. Oral HA alone showed no photoaging benefit in a MA [PMID:40761858].
- Glucosamine Sulphate 2KCL: UC-II beat glucosamine HCl + chondroitin on WOMAC [PMID:26822714]. Type II hydrolysate and glucosamine HCl gave similar WOMAC improvements [PMID:36918892]. No combination trial with glucosamine sulphate was found.
- Vitamin D-3 (with calcium): combined collagen + calcium/vitamin D gave larger BMD/marker changes in MA subgroups [PMID:41049371] and in RCTs [PMID:34980546] [PMID:41002783].
- Glycine: collagen is a glycine source, so intake adds to glycine supplements. Both are studied for sleep [PMID:42687500] [PMID:22293292].
- Whey protein: whey + collagen beat either alone for muscle and spine BMD (n=40) [PMID:42426859]. Whey is superior to collagen as a muscle-building protein [PMID:35042187].
*** Conditions
- Fish/gelatin allergy, alpha-gal syndrome, primary hyperoxaluria and calcium-oxalate stones: see Contraindications.
- Sarcopenia and older age: collagen should not replace high-quality protein. It did not raise integrated MPS above RDA intake [PMID:38762187] [PMID:31919527].
*** Lab-test interference
- Urinary hydroxyproline (an old bone-resorption marker), glycolate and oxalate rise after gelatin loads of 5 g or more [PMID:17021603]. Pro-Hyp and Gly-Pro-Hyp are excreted in urine [PMID:26934933].
- Bone and collagen turnover markers (PINP, CTX) shift physiologically with supplementation, so baselines should be interpreted with this in mind [PMID:29337906] [PMID:38007183] [PMID:42221768].

** Latest research
- [2026-11] Letter in Am J Med reporting inconsistencies in the 2025 funding-bias skin MA [PMID:42822893].
- [2026-09] Scoping review (22 trials): glycine from collagen is bioavailable, but its sleep mediation is untested [PMID:42687500].
- [2026-08] SR of collagen peptides in tendon/ligament tears (6 studies): cautiously positive, low certainty [PMID:42666580].
- [2026-08] RCT (3 g/d, 24 wk): better hair gloss, elasticity and diameter in damaged hair [PMID:42624827].
- [2026-07] RCT (n=40): whey + collagen gave the largest gains in muscle mass and lumbar BMD in trained men [PMID:42426859].
- [2026-06] SLR of 25 skin RCTs: benefits in most trials, but most were at high risk of bias [PMID:42342959].
- [2026-06] RCT: 15 g/d SCP + training raised intramuscular collagen type I by 29.8% vs 9.9% [PMID:42376607].
- [2026-05] Pilot RCT in female runners (20 g/d, 4 wk): PINP up, IL-6 down [PMID:42221768].
- [2026-03] Tendon SR (8 RCTs): 15-30 g/d raises tendon CSA/stiffness; no added strength [PMID:41900537].
- [2026-03] UC-II RCT in healthy adults with activity-related joint discomfort (n=74): earlier KOOS improvement [PMID:41787523].
- [2026-02] Heavy metals/microplastics in 30 protein products; the only mercury value above 0.01 mg/kg was in a collagen product [PMID:41655677].
- [2026-01] Umbrella review (113 RCTs, n=7983): favorable for skin, bone, muscle and OA; mixed for cardiometabolic outcomes [PMID:41809116].
- [2025-09] Bone/muscle MA: BMD up (I2=80%), turnover markers SMD 0.40-0.58, muscle performance SMD 0.60 [PMID:41049371].
- [2025-09] LMW peptide RCT in knee OA (n=80, 3 g/d, 180 d): WOMAC pain down; no joint-space change [PMID:40977985].
- [2025-08] Knee OA network MA (39 RCTs): collagen beneficial on some outcomes; Boswellia ranked best [PMID:40806131].
- [2025-07] RCT (n=50, 10 g/d, 16 wk): Achilles stiffness and RTD up; no change in CSA [PMID:40623147].
- [2025-05] Skin MA (23 RCTs): benefit only in industry-funded and low-quality trials [PMID:40324552].
- [2025-04] RCT in middle-aged men (30 g/d + training): greater patellar-tendon CSA and stiffness gains [PMID:40100255].
- [2024-10] Obesity RCT (20 g/d swelling collagen): weight and SBP down [PMID:39458544].

** Open questions
- Whether skin benefits survive in independent, preregistered, adequately powered trials. Industry-funded trials drive the pooled effect [PMID:40324552], and marketing claims exceed the evidence [PMID:34694676].
- OA meta-analyses pool hydrolysates with UC-II and have high heterogeneity, and critics dispute how strong the conclusions are [PMID:34636929].
- Fracture outcomes and BMD effects beyond one RCT and its open-label extension. A SR of 36 RCTs found bone studies too limited for definitive conclusions [PMID:39980497].
- Long-term kidney-stone risk at the 15-30 g/d doses used in tendon trials.
- Contaminant (heavy metal) testing standards for collagen products.
