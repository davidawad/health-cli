** Identity & forms
- Coenzyme Q10 (ubiquinone, ubidecarenone) is a fat-soluble molecule sold as a non-prescription nutritional supplement [PMID:35608922] [PMID:9621803].
- It exists in all human cell membranes in two interconvertible forms: oxidized ubiquinone and reduced ubiquinol [PMID:41789786].
- Bioavailability depends heavily on formulation. In a 14-person crossover of seven 100 mg products, the best-absorbed were soft-gel capsules of either ubiquinone or ubiquinol. Carrier matrix and additives such as vitamin C changed absorption, and individuals varied widely in response [PMID:30153575].
- Ubiquinol vs ubiquinone: a 12-subject crossover (2026) found a cocrystal ubiquinol soft gel had higher bioavailability than a ubiquinone reference. The baseline-corrected Cmax ratio was 2.20 (90% CI 1.59-3.04) and the AUC0-t ratio was 2.01 (1.51-2.70) [PMID:41789786]. This is a single small, formulation-specific comparison and does not show a general superiority of ubiquinol across products. The 2019 crossover found the top ubiquinone and ubiquinol soft gels comparable [PMID:30153575].
- Raising plasma levels does not necessarily raise tissue levels. In older adults, 400 mg/day for 12 weeks increased plasma CoQ10 but not muscle or mitochondrial CoQ10 [PMID:41604113].

** Mechanisms
- Electron carrier in the mitochondrial respiratory chain, coenzyme for mitochondrial enzymes, and antioxidant. It may stabilise calcium-dependent ion channels in heart muscle (background rationale in the Cochrane review) [PMID:35608922].
- Myocardial CoQ10 levels are low in heart failure and correlate with severity. This is the rationale for Q-SYMBIO [PMID:25282031].
- Statins lower circulating CoQ10. Across placebo-controlled RCTs the weighted mean difference (WMD) was -0.44 umol/L, seen with atorvastatin, simvastatin, rosuvastatin and pravastatin and with both lipophilic and hydrophilic statins [PMID:26192349]. A 2018 update (12 RCTs, n=1776) gave a standardized mean difference (SMD) of -2.12, unrelated to statin intensity or duration [PMID:30414615]. This is the proposed basis for statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS), but it is not proven to be causal.
- CoQ10 is chemically similar to vitamin K, which was proposed to explain a reduced warfarin response [PMID:9621803]. In vitro (rat and human liver microsomes), CoQ10 activated warfarin hydroxylation. The authors predicted about a 32% (S) and 17% (R) increase in warfarin clearance with 100 mg CoQ10 (mechanistic/in vitro extrapolation) [PMID:15853759].
- Ubiquinol supplementation (300 mg/day, 6 weeks) improved an index of oxidative phosphorylation coupling efficiency in muscle but did not change exercise tolerance [PMID:42249931].

** Dosing in trials
- Heart failure (Q-SYMBIO): 100 mg three times daily (300 mg/day) for 2 years on top of standard therapy [PMID:25282031].
- SAMS: 100-600 mg/day for 30-90 days [PMID:41158831].
- Blood pressure: greatest SBP reduction at about 100-200 mg/day (dose-response MA) [PMID:36130103].
- Parkinson's (QE3): 1200 or 2400 mg/day (with vitamin E 1200 IU/day) for up to 16 months. Well tolerated [PMID:24664227].
- Exercise/ageing: 400 mg/day for 12 weeks [PMID:41604113]. Ubiquinol 300 mg/day for 6 weeks [PMID:42249931]. Each additional 100 mg/day was associated with further lowering of CK and LDH [PMID:38479900].
- Warfarin interaction RCT: 100 mg/day for 4 weeks [PMID:12772396]. Doxorubicin co-administration trial: 300 mg/day [PMID:41261512].
- Formulation matters (soft gel, lipid carrier) [PMID:30153575].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Observed safe level (OSL) risk assessment: safety evidence is strong up to 1200 mg/day. Higher doses have been tested without adverse effects, but the data are insufficient for confident conclusions. GI effects such as nausea showed no dose-response between 60 and 1200 mg/day [PMID:16814438].
- No serious side effects reported. Mild insomnia or digestive upset may occur [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10]. NCI PDQ lists mild insomnia at 100 mg/day or more, plus nausea, dizziness, headache and fatigue [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/coenzyme-q10-pdq].
- 1200-2400 mg/day for 16 months was safe and well tolerated in early Parkinson's disease [PMID:24664227]. Q-SYMBIO reported long-term 300 mg/day as safe [PMID:25282031]. In the 2021 Cochrane review, adverse-event risk was inconclusive (RR 0.70, 0.45-1.10) [PMID:35608922].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindications identified in the cited sources.
- Caution during cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiotherapy. In an observational breast cancer cohort (SWOG S0221), any antioxidant use (including CoQ10) before and during chemotherapy was associated with a borderline higher recurrence hazard: adjHR 1.41 (0.98-2.04) [PMID:31855498]. NCCIH notes CoQ10 "may not be compatible with some types of cancer treatment" [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin: case reports of reduced warfarin response that resolved after CoQ10 was stopped [PMID:9621803]. In vitro, CoQ10 increased warfarin hydroxylation, with a predicted clearance increase [PMID:15853759]. However, a randomized double-blind crossover in 24 stable patients (100 mg/day, 4 weeks) found no change in INR or warfarin dose [PMID:12772396]. NCI PDQ and NCCIH both flag the interaction [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/coenzyme-q10-pdq] [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10]. Assessment: monitor INR when starting or stopping.
- Statins: statins lower plasma CoQ10 [PMID:26192349] [PMID:30414615]. Co-supplementation for SAMS has conflicting evidence [PMID:30371340] [PMID:32179207] [PMID:41158831], and NCCIH concludes the overall evidence does not support it [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10].
- Antihypertensives: possible additive modest SBP lowering in cardiometabolic patients [PMID:36130103]. The effect was not clinically significant in primary hypertension [PMID:26935713]. This interaction is theoretical; no interaction trials were found.
- Insulin / glucose-lowering drugs: NCCIH lists a possible interaction with insulin [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10]. MAs show a small fasting-glucose reduction [PMID:41630501].
- Chemotherapy (antineoplastics): antioxidant-efficacy concern (observational) [PMID:31855498] [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/coenzyme-q10-pdq]. Doxorubicin specifically: a phase I crossover found no clinically significant pharmacokinetic interaction at 300 mg/day, but it was very small (n=6) and closed early [PMID:41261512]. NCI PDQ describes small studies of reduced anthracycline cardiotoxicity [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/coenzyme-q10-pdq].
*** Other supplements
- Selenium: combined selenium + CoQ10 for 5 years in selenium-deficient Swedish elderly reduced CV mortality (5.9% vs 12.6%) and NT-proBNP [PMID:22626835]. A 10-year sex-stratified follow-up (2025) found greater benefit in women [PMID:40563319]. Evidence comes from the combination; neither component was tested alone.
- No cited interaction with Vitamin D-3 or other batch supplements was found.
*** Conditions
- Heart failure: adjunct with moderate-certainty mortality and hospitalisation benefit [PMID:35608922] [PMID:39462324]. Not a guideline-standard therapy; NCCIH calls the evidence inconclusive [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10].
- Primary CoQ10 deficiency: high-dose oral CoQ10 is the targeted therapy [PMID:28125198].
- Active cancer treatment: see Contraindications [PMID:31855498].
- Parkinson's disease: no disease-modifying benefit [PMID:24664227].
*** Lab-test interference
- No cited assay interference found. Statins and supplementation change plasma CoQ10 levels themselves [PMID:26192349] [PMID:36130103].

** Genotype notes
- Primary CoQ10 deficiency is a group of genetic disorders (defects in CoQ10 biosynthesis genes), managed with high-dose oral CoQ10 [PMID:28125198]. No cited pharmacogenomic source on response to supplementation in the general population was found.

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Network MA (14 RCTs, 791 participants): CoQ10 reduced migraine frequency by -1.91 attacks/month vs placebo. It was not superior to other supplements and did not reduce severity or duration [PMID:42707444].
- [2026-09] RCT, healthy males (n=54): ubiquinol 300 mg/day for 6 weeks raised plasma CoQ10 and improved a coupling-efficiency index, with no change in exercise tolerance or VO2 kinetics [PMID:42249931].
- [2026-03] RCT, poor ovarian responders (n=100): CoQ10 increased oocyte yield and peak E2, but chemical and clinical pregnancy rates did not differ [PMID:41800295].
- [2026-03] Crossover PK (n=12): cocrystal ubiquinol had about 2x the AUC of a ubiquinone reference [PMID:41789786].
- [2026-03] Umbrella review of 8 MAs: CoQ10 lowered fasting glucose. HbA1c, HOMA-IR and insulin results were inconsistent [PMID:41859772].
- [2026-02] MA of 39 RCTs: FBG -7.33 mg/dL; HbA1c effect non-significant; very low certainty [PMID:41630501].
- [2026-01] RCT, older adults (n=40, 400 mg/day for 12 weeks): plasma CoQ10 rose, but muscle and mitochondrial CoQ10, mitochondrial function, VO2max and glucose homeostasis were unchanged [PMID:41604113].
- [2026-01] MA of 5 RCTs: depressive symptoms SMD -0.68; no effect on fatigue [PMID:41294251].
- [2025-12] RCT, adults aged 65-75 doing HIIT (n=38): CoQ10 improved chair-stand outcomes only [PMID:41470903].
- [2025-11] Phase I crossover: 300 mg/day CoQ10 did not change doxorubicin pharmacokinetics (n=6; closed early) [PMID:41261512].
- [2025-10] SAMS MA (7 RCTs, n=389): pain WMD -0.96 (-1.88 to -0.03), a marginal benefit [PMID:41158831].
- [2025-09] Network MA, male infertility: CoQ10 gave the largest sperm-concentration gain [PMID:40813743].
- [2025-06] KiSel-10 10-year follow-up: selenium + CoQ10 CV survival benefit, larger in women [PMID:40563319].
- [2025-05] SR/MA of 50 RCTs, male infertility: CoQ10 improved motility, with no effect on pregnancy or live birth for any supplement [PMID:40431450].
- [2024-10] HF MA of 33 RCTs: mortality RR 0.64, HF hospitalisation RR 0.50 (GRADE moderate) [PMID:39462324].
- [2024-08] DOR IVF/ICSI MA of 6 RCTs: clinical pregnancy OR 1.84 [PMID:39129455].

** Open questions
- Heart-failure benefit rests mainly on Q-SYMBIO (n=420). No large, independent confirmatory outcome trial was found.
- SAMS: pooled results flip with trial selection. No trials of adequate size used objective statin-rechallenge designs.
- Clinical relevance of ubiquinol vs ubiquinone given poor plasma-to-tissue transfer [PMID:41604113].
- Whether CoQ10 compromises chemotherapy or radiotherapy efficacy in humans (observational signal only).
- Pregnancy and lactation safety: no cited source was found.
- The warfarin interaction is inconsistent: case reports conflict with a small null RCT.
