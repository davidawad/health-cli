** Identity & forms
- Creatine is synthesized from arginine, glycine and methionine and stored largely as phosphocreatine in muscle, where it buffers ATP during brief high-intensity work [PMID:39519498]. Monohydrate is the best-studied oral form [PMID:39519498].

** Mechanisms
- Creatine kinase reversibly transfers phosphate between phosphocreatine and ADP to buffer ATP during short intense contraction; supplementation increases intramuscular creatine stores. Resistance exercise remains an essential co-intervention in most positive strength trials [PMID:39519498; PMID:41388441].

** Dosing in trials
- Trials commonly use 3–5 g/day maintenance; loading protocols use ~20 g/day divided for 5–7 days then 3–5 g/day. Recent aging trials vary in duration and dose; the 2025 older-adult review included 8–104 week interventions [PMID:41388441]. Monohydrate dominates evidence; alternative salts/forms lack clear superiority [PMID:39519498].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Water-associated weight gain and GI symptoms can occur, particularly with large single doses. Meta-analysis in healthy people finds no meaningful reduction in GFR despite a small serum creatinine increase; creatinine can rise from conversion of supplemental creatine and confound creatinine-based eGFR, so cystatin C or measured GFR can clarify when clinically indicated [PMID:41199218].

** Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity is a contraindication. Evidence in pregnancy, breastfeeding, significant kidney disease and children outside clinical indications is limited; these are evidence gaps rather than established universal contraindications [PMID:38417175].

** Interactions
- High-dose caffeine may attenuate ergogenic effect in some studies and increase GI complaints; evidence is mixed. Nephrotoxic drugs plus dehydration raise theoretical renal concern, though controlled interaction evidence is sparse. Serum creatinine/eGFR by creatinine may be altered without kidney injury; disclose use before testing [PMID:41199218].

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source specific to Creatine was identified in this search; no genotype claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2025 meta-analysis in older adults found modest gains in lower-limb strength and lean tissue mass when creatine was combined with resistance exercise [PMID:41388441]. 2024 systematic review found cognitive effects equivocal despite some rise in brain creatine [PMID:38582412].

** Open questions
- Larger, better reported human studies with standardized products, clinically meaningful endpoints and longer follow-up are needed; current gaps are noted above.
