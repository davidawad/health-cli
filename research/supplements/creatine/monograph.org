** Identity & forms
- Creatine is a nitrogenous compound made endogenously and obtained from meat and dairy. About 1.6-1.7% of the total creatine pool is degraded non-enzymatically to creatinine each day and excreted in urine, so it must be continually replaced [PMID:34444869] [PMID:31083291].
- The first synthesis step (arginine:glycine amidinotransferase, AGAT) is a kidney function. Endogenous production appears to fall as CKD progresses, and dietary creatine may become conditionally essential [PMID:31083291].
- Creatine monohydrate (CrM): almost all trials use this form. Nearly 99% of an oral dose is taken up by muscle or excreted in urine. Supplementation raises muscle creatine and phosphocreatine by roughly 15-40% [PMID:21424716].
- "Novel" forms (HCl, ethyl ester, buffered, nitrate and others):
  - Two critical reviews found little or no evidence that any of them is more effective or safer than CrM. Their regulatory status is also less clear [PMID:21424716] [PMID:35268011].
  - Creatine HCl: no better than CrM alongside resistance training in an 8-week RCT (n=40) [PMID:39545789]. Creatine ethyl ester (CEE): raised muscle creatine less effectively than CrM and raised serum creatinine [PMID:19228401].
- All 16 RCTs in the main 2024 cognition MA used the monohydrate form [PMID:39070254].

** Mechanisms
- Phosphocreatine buffers ATP during brief high-intensity effort. More stored creatine allows more training volume and greater adaptation [PMID:21424716] [PMID:28615996].
- Brain creatine rises with supplementation, but probably needs higher or longer dosing than muscle. Effects show most when cognition is stressed, for example by sleep deprivation or hypoxia [PMID:30086660].
- A single 0.35 g/kg dose changed cerebral PCr/Pi and ATP during sleep deprivation (31P-MRS) [PMID:38418482].
- Supplementation down-regulates endogenous synthesis: plasma guanidinoacetate fell 10.6% on 3 g/d. Plasma homocysteine did not fall [PMID:26311810].
- Glucose: increased GLUT-4 translocation was seen in a type 2 diabetes RCT with exercise [PMID:20881878].
- Androgens: one small crossover study reported a rise in DHT:T ratio [PMID:19741313]. A 12-week RCT did not reproduce it [PMID:40265319].
- Inflammation: an RCT meta-analysis found no effect on CRP or IL-6 [PMID:41798953].

** Dosing in trials
- Typical regimens: 3-5 g/d or 0.1 g/kg/d [PMID:33557850]. An optional loading phase uses >=20 g/d for 5-7 days [PMID:34199420].
- Older adults: creatine on training days only still increased lean mass and strength [PMID:34199420]. Postmenopausal benefits appeared at >=5 g/d with RT; <=3 g/d without RT showed no effect [PMID:42141930]. The 2-year bone RCT used 0.14 g/kg/d [PMID:37144634].
- Brain targets: 5 g/d for 6 weeks gave small cognitive effects [PMID:37968687]. 20 g/d for 8 weeks was used in Alzheimer disease [PMID:40395689]. Single doses of 0.2-0.35 g/kg were tested during sleep deprivation [PMID:38418482] [PMID:42075005].
- Depression: 2-10 g/d for 4-8 weeks as an add-on [PMID:41558805]. 5 g/d was added to escitalopram [PMID:22864465].
- Neurological disease trials: 10 g/d for >=5 years (PD) [PMID:25668262]. Up to 40 g/d for up to 48 months (HD) [PMID:28701493].
- Pediatric TBI: 0.4 g/kg/d for 6 months [PMID:16917445].
- Low-dose HCl: 750-1500 mg/d in a menopause RCT [PMID:40854087].
- The ISSN position stand describes up to 30 g/d for 5 years as well tolerated [PMID:28615996].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Overall:
  - Reviews judge CrM safe in healthy people at recommended doses. Claims that it causes dehydration, cramping or cancer are not supported by controlled data [PMID:41404326] [PMID:33557850] [PMID:39720835].
  - Gastrointestinal upset is dose-dependent [PMID:41404326].
  - In a preregistered cognition RCT, side effects were reported more often on creatine (RR 4.25) [PMID:37968687]. GI adverse events were more common on creatine in the HD trial [PMID:28701493].
- Body weight: body mass rises modestly (g 0.12), mostly as lean mass [PMID:42765482].
- Kidney:
  - Healthy people: three recent MAs of RCTs show higher serum creatinine (MD +0.13-0.14 mg/dL). Urea is unchanged, and GFR measured by Cr-EDTA is unchanged [PMID:42035842] [PMID:42507286] [PMID:41199218]. A 3-month RCT at ~10 g/d found falling cystatin C [PMID:18188581]. A narrative review concludes that controlled trials do not support creatine-induced kidney failure [PMID:36986197].
  - Case report: acute interstitial nephritis in a weightlifter taking several supplements, resolved after stopping [PMID:17046619]. Evidence in pre-existing kidney disease is limited [PMID:36986197] [PMID:41404326].
- Psychiatric: hypomania/mania occurred in 2 of 17 creatine-treated participants in psychiatric RCTs [PMID:41558805].
- Muscle: one case of severe exertional rhabdomyolysis (CK >120,000 U/L) after a >4 h session while taking creatine plus beta-alanine, citrulline and ecdysterone. Causality was unclear [PMID:41952948].
- Androgens and hair: one 2009 crossover study (n=20 rugby players) found DHT +56% after loading [PMID:19741313]. The 2025 RCT found no effect on DHT or hair [PMID:40265319].
- Lipids and inflammation: no adverse change [PMID:42180567] [PMID:41798953].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindication is established in the cited literature.
- Pre-existing kidney disease: caution and nephrology input are advised because data are sparse [PMID:41404326] [PMID:36986197]. A 2026 MA did include CKD and hemodialysis patients and found no change in measured GFR [PMID:42507286].
- Pregnancy and lactation: evidence is insufficient [PMID:41404326] [PMID:39720835].
- Bipolar disorder: mania/hypomania was reported in adjunctive trials [PMID:41558805].
- McArdle disease: high-dose creatine worsened daily activities and muscle pain [PMID:23740606].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, calcineurin inhibitors, etc.): no controlled interaction studies were found. A case of interstitial nephritis with a multi-supplement stack [PMID:17046619] and gaps in data for impaired kidneys [PMID:41404326] support monitoring kidney function with a creatinine-independent marker [PMID:38835922].
- Drugs that block tubular creatinine secretion (trimethoprim, cimetidine, dolutegravir, cobicistat, some TKIs): these raise serum creatinine without true GFR loss [PMID:36859345]. Combined with creatine, the two effects could add and mimic AKI. This is inferred.
- Antidiabetic drugs: creatine with exercise lowered HbA1c by 1.1% in T2D [PMID:20881878]. Monitor glucose for additive lowering. Data do not cover insulin users [PMID:27306768].
- SSRIs (escitalopram): faster and larger HAM-D improvement as augmentation in an RCT (n=52) [PMID:22864465]. This is a potentially beneficial pharmacodynamic combination; watch for mood switching [PMID:41558805].
- Caffeine (as drug or supplement): see below.
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine:
  - A systematic review (10 studies) found that creatine loading does not blunt acute caffeine benefits. Chronic caffeine during creatine loading may blunt creatine's ergogenic effect in some studies [PMID:35016154].
  - Proposed mechanisms are opposing effects on muscle relaxation time and GI distress [PMID:26219105]. A 6-week RCT showed no group differences for the combination [PMID:33759701].
- Beta-alanine:
  - A 2026 NMA (52 RCTs) found no synergy beyond creatine alone [PMID:42384726]. A systematic review (7 RCTs) suggests a combined benefit for anaerobic and repeated-bout performance [PMID:40647180].
  - A multi-ingredient stack including both was present in one rhabdomyolysis case [PMID:41952948].
- Folate (5-MTHF/folic acid): creatine plus folic acid lowered homocysteine about as much as folic acid alone (-21% vs -23%). Creatine alone did not lower it [PMID:26311810].
- Methyl donors (TMG): creatine synthesis consumes ~50% of SAM-derived methyl groups, and supplementation suppresses guanidinoacetate. Any methyl-sparing effect in humans is unproven [PMID:26311810].
*** Conditions
- CKD/dialysis: dialysis patients may be creatine-depleted [PMID:34444869]. In a small RCT, creatine before hemodialysis cut cramps by 60% [PMID:12401856]. Creatinine-based eGFR becomes unreliable during use [PMID:42507286].
- Kidney transplant: CEE raised creatinine and prompted an allograft biopsy, which was normal [PMID:31384454].
- Type 2 diabetes, bipolar disorder, pregnancy, McArdle disease: see above.
*** Lab-test interference
- Serum creatinine / creatinine-based eGFR (major):
  - Creatine raises serum creatinine and lowers creatinine-based GFR (MD -10.75 mL/min). GFR measured with Cr-EDTA does not change [PMID:42507286]. Other MAs report a creatinine rise without significant eGFR change [PMID:42035842] [PMID:41199218].
  - CEE causes "pseudo renal failure" that reverses after stopping [PMID:25239988] [PMID:21411845] [PMID:31384454].
  - Cooked meat similarly raises creatinine and misclassified 6 of 16 CKD 3a patients; a 12 h fast removes the effect [PMID:24062331].
- Cystatin C as the alternative:
  - Creatine supplements are listed among scenarios that lower eGFRcr but not measured GFR or eGFRcys, and cystatin C confirmation can avoid misdiagnosis [PMID:38835922].
  - KDIGO 2024 puts greater emphasis on cystatin C [PMID:40063957]. European laboratory guidance favours eGFRcr-cys and measured GFR (iohexol) when eGFR is unreliable [PMID:39584585].
  - Cystatin C did not rise on creatine in an RCT [PMID:18188581] or with dietary creatine intake in NHANES [PMID:39778146].
- Albuminuria, proteinuria and urinary creatinine did not differ in pooled data [PMID:42507286].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Three-level MA (63 studies, n=1489): with RT, strength g 0.54-0.55, lean mass g 0.30, no fat-mass change; effects larger in younger people [PMID:42765482].
- [2026-08] Older-adult three-level MA (11 RCTs): strength g 0.31 (moderate certainty); mass and function uncertain [PMID:42712402].
- [2026-07] Kidney MA (26 studies, n=1036): creatinine +0.14 mg/dL; creatinine-based GFR -10.75 mL/min; no Cr-EDTA GFR change [PMID:42507286].
- [2026-07] Creatine/beta-alanine NMA (52 RCTs): creatine alone improves sprint and jump; no added benefit from the combination [PMID:42384726].
- [2026-05] Postmenopausal MA (7 RCTs): lean mass +0.37 kg, leg press +7.5 kg; BMD unchanged [PMID:42141930].
- [2026-04] Kidney MA of RCTs: creatinine +0.13 mg/dL, no eGFR or urea change [PMID:42035842].
- [2026-04] 0.2 g/kg single dose reduced sleep-deprivation cognitive decline by up to 12% [PMID:42075005].
- [2026-02] SR of creatine and cognition in older adults: 5 of 6 studies positive, mostly poor quality [PMID:40971619].
- [2026-01] SR of creatine in mental disorders (5 RCTs): promising as an SSRI or CBT adjunct; 2 mania cases [PMID:41558805].
- [2025-12] Older-adult MA (8 RCTs): lower-limb strength SMD 0.29, lean SMD 0.27 [PMID:41388441].
- [2025-12] Safety review: no support for cancer, dehydration or cramp claims; caution in kidney disease and pregnancy [PMID:41404326].
- [2025-11] Depression MA (11 RCTs): SMD -0.34, very low certainty, below MID [PMID:41189312].
- [2025-08] CONCRET-MENOPA RCT: 1.5 g/d creatine HCl raised frontal brain creatine and improved reaction time [PMID:40854087].
- [2025-05] Alzheimer pilot: 20 g/d for 8 weeks feasible; brain creatine +11% [PMID:40395689].
- [2025-04] Hair-loss RCT: no change in DHT, DHT:T or hair outcomes over 12 weeks [PMID:40265319].
- [2024-11] CBT add-on RCT (n=100): PHQ-9 MD -5.12 vs placebo [PMID:39488067].
- [2024-10] MA in adults <50: upper-body +4.43 kg, lower-body +11.35 kg; larger in males [PMID:39519498].

** Open questions
- Whether creatine benefits frail, sarcopenic or functionally limited older adults, as opposed to healthy ones [PMID:42712402].
- The true size of cognitive and antidepressant effects. Current estimates are small, heterogeneous and possibly biased [PMID:41189312] [PMID:39070254].
- Safety and efficacy in established CKD and after transplant. Long-term (>1 year) kidney RCTs are lacking [PMID:42035842].
- Pregnancy and lactation safety [PMID:41404326].
- Optimal brain-targeted dosing [PMID:30086660]. Whether TBI benefits seen in open-label pediatric pilots replicate in blinded trials [PMID:18053002].
- Whether low-dose high-solubility forms (HCl) match CrM over the long term [PMID:40854087] [PMID:35268011].
