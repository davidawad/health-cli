** Identity & forms
- Curcumin is the main yellow curcuminoid of the turmeric rhizome (Curcuma longa, ginger family). It makes up up to about 5% of turmeric [PMID:28074653] [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric]. Commercial "curcuminoids" are mixtures of curcumin, demethoxycurcumin and bisdemethoxycurcumin [PMID:22725836].
- This monograph covers concentrated curcumin/curcuminoid extracts. Whole turmeric spice/powder has a separate corpus entry ("Turmeric"), and so does the combination product "NAC, Ginger, Curcumin".
- Bioavailability of native curcumin is very low. Curcumin is chemically unstable, reactive and heavily conjugated on first pass. One medicinal-chemistry review classes it as a PAINS/IMPS compound and argues that much in vitro activity may be artefactual [PMID:28074653].
- Bioavailability-enhanced forms:
  - Piperine (black pepper alkaloid; inhibits glucuronidation): 20 mg piperine with 2 g curcumin raised human bioavailability by about 2000% in a 1998 study [PMID:9619120].
  - Formulation head-to-head (randomized crossover, n=12, 207 mg curcumin): only micellar curcumin (AUC 57-fold) and gamma-cyclodextrin complex (30-fold) beat native curcumin significantly. Piperine/adjuvant, liposome, phytosome, turmeric-oil and submicron forms did not. Only conjugated curcumin was detected in plasma [PMID:34665507].
  - Micellar curcumin 105 mg/d: Cmax about 39-fold and AUC about 14-fold higher than native curcumin (crossover RCT, n=15) [PMID:36101515].
- Only 3.5-6.4% of 171 systematic reviews accounted for formulation bioavailability in their analyses. Pooled "curcumin" effect sizes therefore mix products that are not exposure-equivalent [PMID:38399379].
- Contamination: hepatotoxic products in the DILIN series were chemically confirmed to contain turmeric, and 3 of 7 also contained piperine [PMID:36252717].

** Mechanisms
- Anti-inflammatory/antioxidant: RCT meta-analyses show lower CRP, TNF-alpha, IL-6 and MDA, and higher TAC/SOD [PMID:36804260] [PMID:39478418]. A crossover RCT of micellar curcumin found no ex vivo cytokine suppression despite 14-fold higher exposure [PMID:36101515].
- Hepcidin/iron: 6 g curcuma (2% curcumin) lowered serum hepcidin by 17-19% at 6-12 h in healthy men (STAT3-mediated mechanism proposed) [PMID:28370178]. In mice, 6 months of dietary curcumin depleted liver/spleen iron and suppressed hepcidin; zinc and copper were unaffected [PMID:24634837].
- Cholekinetic: 20-80 mg curcumin contracts the gallbladder dose-dependently on ultrasound. Volume fell 29% at 20 mg, 51% at 40 mg and 72% at 80 mg within 2 h [PMID:10102956] [PMID:12495265].
- Drug-handling proteins:
  - Curcumin inhibits recombinant CYP2C9 (IC50 4.3 uM), CYP3A4 (16.3 uM), CYP2B6, CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 in vitro. Low systemic exposure makes hepatic CYP inhibition uncertain [PMID:17433521].
  - Potent BCRP/ABCG2 inhibitor (Ki 0.70 uM). This is clinically shown with sulfasalazine [PMID:22300367].
  - P-gp effects are bidirectional in human talinolol studies [PMID:17468862] [PMID:22725663].
- Hemostasis: antiplatelet effects are proposed but human data are lacking. In rats, curcumin raised warfarin and clopidogrel exposure without changing PT or platelet aggregation [PMID:23807811].
- Hepatotoxicity is thought to be idiosyncratic and immune-mediated. Evidence includes strong HLA-B*35:01 linkage, eosinophils on biopsy, autoimmune-like phenotypes and more severe injury on rechallenge [PMID:36252717] [PMID:42160492] [PMID:42364655].

- Overall: 23 of 42 outcomes in a 103-RCT MA (n=7216) were significant. Credibility was high only for FBS, CRP, HDL and weight [PMID:39478418]. A 2025 umbrella review rated the methodological quality of 25 MAs as relatively poor [PMID:40538540].

** Dosing in trials
- Arthritis (29 RCTs, n=2396, incl. OA and RA): curcumin/Curcuma longa extract at 120-1500 mg/d for 4-36 weeks. Trial products and doses were heterogeneous [PMID:35935936].
- Glycemia/lipids: lipid benefits were larger with enhanced-bioavailability forms and >=8 weeks of use [PMID:40044446]. BP subgroup effects were seen with nano-curcumin, turmeric or curcumin+piperine above 1 g/d [PMID:41388744].
- Depression: in dose-response analysis, >1.5 g/d was associated with larger reductions in depressive symptoms [PMID:42859601].
- Cognition: optimal dose was about 0.8 g/d for >=24 weeks in one dose-response MA [PMID:40308636].
- NAFLD: in meta-regression, longer duration and higher relative dose (mg/kg/d) were linked to smaller aminotransferase reductions [PMID:38965866].
- Piperine is typically 5-20 mg per dose (5 mg/d with curcumin 500 mg/d in a 2026 OA trial; 24 mg with 4 g curcuminoids in a PK study) [PMID:42331134] [PMID:22725836] [PMID:9619120].
- NCCIH: conventionally formulated (non-enhanced) oral turmeric/curcumin is likely safe in recommended amounts for up to 2-3 months [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Common: GI effects (nausea, reflux, diarrhea, constipation). Topical products can cause urticaria/pruritus [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric]. In depression RCTs, dropouts and GI/neurological AEs were similar to placebo [PMID:33418373]. One cancer RCT reported more vomiting [PMID:39425780]. In cognition RCTs, AE incidence was higher with curcumin than control [PMID:40579315].
- Hepatotoxicity (idiosyncratic DILI):
  - LiverTox (updated June 2025): turmeric/curcumin cause a low rate of transient enzyme elevations. Several dozen clinically apparent acute liver injury cases have been reported [PMID:31643876].
  - US DILIN (2004-2022): 10 cases, all since 2011. 8 were women, median age 56. Injury was hepatocellular in 9, latency 1-4 months; 5 were hospitalized and 1 died of acute liver failure. 7/10 carried HLA-B*35:01 (allele frequency 0.45 vs 0.056-0.069 in controls) [PMID:36252717].
  - Italian phytovigilance (Tuscany): 7 cases, all with high-bioavailability, high-dose curcuminoid products. A systematic review found 23 more published cases, most with co-medications and 16 with positive dechallenge [PMID:32656820].
  - Pathology series (2025): 11 cases. Acute hepatitis (pan-lobular or zone 3) in 73%; RUCAM probable in 8 and possible in 3. All normalized after stopping, 4 after steroids [PMID:39381846].
  - USP expert review (2026): no organ toxicity in clinical trials. Case latency is 1-4 months, usually resolving on withdrawal, with rare acute liver failure. USP recommends a cautionary label: stop if abdominal pain, dark urine or jaundice; consult a clinician if there is a history of liver problems [PMID:42364655].
  - 2024-2026 case reports: HLA-B*35:01/DRB1*04 carrier with ALT 2230 U/L after curcumin 60 mg + black pepper twice daily [PMID:39297606]. Recurrent drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis on rechallenge with a piperine product, more severe the second time and steroid-responsive [PMID:42160492]. One-year turmeric+black-pepper use, initially mistaken for cholecystitis [PMID:41255460]. Hepatocellular DILI with ferritin >2000 in an HFE H63D heterozygote [PMID:40880678].
  - Turmeric/curcumin is the most commonly used of 6 potentially hepatotoxic botanicals among US adults (NHANES 2017-2020) [PMID:39102266]. DILI reviews now list turmeric with Garcinia, kratom and ashwagandha [PMID:42092331].
- Iron deficiency: case of iron-deficiency anemia on turmeric extract (6 x 538 mg/d) that resolved 2 weeks after stopping [PMID:30899609]. This is consistent with the hepcidin and murine iron data [PMID:28370178] [PMID:24634837].
- Oxalate: supplemental turmeric (4 weeks, 55 mg oxalate/d) raised urinary oxalate more than cinnamon. 91% of turmeric oxalate is water-soluble, a possible stone risk in susceptible people. This applies to turmeric powder, not purified curcumin [PMID:18469248].
- Pregnancy: NCCIH states turmeric supplements may be unsafe in pregnancy. Lactation data are lacking [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].

** Contraindications
- Pre-existing liver disease or prior turmeric/curcumin liver injury: rechallenge produced more severe recurrent injury [PMID:42160492]. USP advises consulting a clinician if there is a history of liver problems [PMID:42364655].
- Gallstones/biliary obstruction: curcumin causes dose-dependent gallbladder contraction (up to 72% at 80 mg) [PMID:12495265] [PMID:10102956]. This is a theoretical risk of biliary colic or obstruction; no outcome data exist.
- Pregnancy: avoid supplemental doses per NCCIH [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].
- Iron-deficiency anemia: relative caution [PMID:30899609] [PMID:28370178].
- Solid-organ transplant on tacrolimus: avoid unless levels are monitored [PMID:28104136] [PMID:37857794].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Tacrolimus (and by extension other calcineurin inhibitors): one case of tacrolimus level 29.9 ng/mL with acute nephrotoxicity after high-dose dietary turmeric in a liver-transplant recipient [PMID:28104136]. The Saudi FDA rated turmeric-tacrolimus a "probable" interaction for labeling [PMID:37857794]. In a structured 10 g/d turmeric challenge in a renal-transplant recipient, levels did not change [PMID:37586787]. Evidence is mixed; monitor trough levels.
- Sulfasalazine (BCRP substrate): curcumin 2 g raised sulfasalazine AUC 3.2-fold at therapeutic doses and 2.0-fold at microdoses (n=8) [PMID:22300367]. Other BCRP substrates could plausibly be affected by the same mechanism, but this is inferred.
- Talinolol (P-gp substrate): results conflict. 300 mg/d for 6 days lowered talinolol AUC by about 33% [PMID:17468862]. 1000 mg/d for 14 days raised AUC by 80.8%, with the largest effect in ABCB1 3435TT carriers [PMID:22725663].
- CYP3A/CYP2C9/UGT substrates: curcuminoids 4 g + piperine 24 mg over 2 days did not change midazolam, flurbiprofen or paracetamol PK (n=8) [PMID:22725836]. Strong in vitro CYP2C9/3A4 inhibition [PMID:17433521] has not translated into a short-term clinical effect.
- Piperine itself: 20 mg raised phenytoin AUC and Cmax at steady state in patients with epilepsy [PMID:16767797]. Curcumin-piperine products can therefore alter narrow-therapeutic-index drugs independently of curcumin.
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets (warfarin, clopidogrel, DOACs): a review links turmeric with bleeding risk in patients on anticoagulants [PMID:36304597]. In rats, curcumin increased warfarin/clopidogrel AUC 1.5-1.6x without PD change [PMID:23807811]. Monitor INR and bleeding.
- Antidiabetics (sulfonylureas, metformin, insulin): FBG falls about 4.6-5.9 mg/dL [PMID:37748368] [PMID:41393198]. With glyburide (n=8), glucose fell further without hypoglycemia [PMID:25044423]. Additive effect is minor.
- Antihypertensives: small SBP decrease (-2.7 mmHg) [PMID:41388744]. Minor.
- Chemotherapy: oncology RCTs are heterogeneous and at high risk of bias. Interaction data are incomplete [PMID:39425780] [PMID:28734960].
- General review: most curcumin-drug PK interactions come from animal/in vitro work. Few human trials exist [PMID:28734960].
*** Other supplements
- Iron (incl. heme iron): curcumin lowers hepcidin and is associated with iron deficiency (case report, mouse data) [PMID:28370178] [PMID:30899609] [PMID:24634837]. Separate dosing and check iron indices in long-term users.
- Other hepatotoxic botanicals (green tea extract, ashwagandha, Garcinia, red yeast rice, black cohosh): stacking adds DILI exposure [PMID:39102266] [PMID:42092331]. HLA-B*35:01 is a shared risk allele for green tea extract and turmeric injury [PMID:42092331] [PMID:36252717].
- Turmeric / multi-ingredient curcumin products: duplicate curcuminoid and piperine exposure [PMID:31643876] [PMID:36252717].
- Glucose-lowering supplements (e.g. berberine): possible additive FBG lowering. This is inferred from curcumin MA effects only; no combination study exists [PMID:37748368].
- Antiplatelet supplements (fish oil, garlic, vitamin E, ginkgo): the bleeding-risk review flags garlic for surgical bleeding and turmeric with anticoagulants. Combined risk is unquantified [PMID:36304597].
*** Conditions
- Liver disease, gallstones, pregnancy, iron deficiency, transplantation: see Contraindications.
- Kidney stones (calcium oxalate): turmeric-powder oxalate load [PMID:18469248].
- Upcoming surgery: stopping nonessential bleeding-associated supplements 2 weeks before surgery is advised [PMID:36304597].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. Real physiological effects include lower serum hepcidin and transiently higher ferritin [PMID:28370178]. With iron deficiency, iron/ferritin may be low [PMID:30899609]. In DILI, ferritin can be markedly high [PMID:40880678]. ALT/AST can be low in NAFLD trials [PMID:38213188] or high in DILI [PMID:36252717]. Lipids and FBG may shift modestly [PMID:37230418] [PMID:37748368].
- Drug-level monitoring: tacrolimus troughs may change [PMID:28104136].

** Genotype notes
- HLA-B*35:01: 7 of 10 DILIN turmeric-DILI cases were carriers (allele frequency 0.45 vs about 0.06 in population controls) [PMID:36252717]. A 2024 Spanish case was also a carrier [PMID:39297606]. USP notes emerging evidence of genetic susceptibility [PMID:42364655]. Pre-emptive testing is not validated.
- ABCB1 C3435T: curcumin's increase in talinolol exposure was most pronounced in TT homozygotes [PMID:22725663].
- HFE H63D heterozygosity: noted in one DILI case with hyperferritinemia. This is a single case, and causal relevance is unknown [PMID:40880678].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Dose-response MA (26 RCTs, n=1491): depression SMD -1.53, anxiety -1.67, stress -1.71; >1.5 g/d associated with larger effects; moderate-to-low certainty [PMID:42859601].
- [2026-09] T2DM inflammation MA (HKSJ): hs-CRP MD -1.06 mg/L; TNF-a g -1.20 [PMID:42797011].
- [2026-09] Nutraceutical NMA for MDD: in low-RoB analyses curcumin reduced anxiety (SMD -0.58; k=2) [PMID:42680855].
- [2026-09] Cardiovascular review: modest surrogate-marker changes; outcome evidence limited and GRADE low [PMID:42745984].
- [2026-07] Depression MA (19 RCTs): SMD -0.76, fragile on sensitivity analysis [PMID:42472817].
- [2026-06] USP DSAEL review recommends a hepatotoxicity cautionary statement for turmeric/curcuminoid monographs [PMID:42364655].
- [2026-06] ATLAS RCT (n=84): curcumin 500 mg + piperine + Boswellia/pine bark/MSM was no better than placebo for knee OA pain at 12 weeks [PMID:42331134].
- [2026-05] Recurrent drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis on rechallenge with a turmeric + black pepper supplement [PMID:42160492].
- [2026-05] DILI update: turmeric among newly recognized hepatotoxic supplements; HLA-B*35:01 also a risk allele for green tea extract injury [PMID:42092331].
- [2026-01] BP MA in prediabetes/T2DM (15 RCTs): SBP -2.69 mmHg [PMID:41388744].
- [2025-12] Curcumin + piperine glycemia MA (15 RCTs): FBG -5.89 mg/dL; HbA1c, insulin and HOMA-IR NS; GRADE low [PMID:41393198].
- [2025-12] RA/SLE MA (7 RCTs): no significant effect on DAS-28, ESR or CRP [PMID:41372521].
- [2025-11] DILI after 1 year of turmeric + black pepper taken for depressive symptoms [PMID:41255460].
- [2025-08] Lipid umbrella + updated MA (72 RCTs): LDL -5.84, TG -13.15 mg/dL; enhanced-bioavailability forms performed better [PMID:40044446].
- [2025-07] Knee OA inflammation MA (21 RCTs): CRP and TNF-a lower; IL-6 and IL-1b unchanged [PMID:40615851].
- [2025-06] Umbrella review of 25 MAs: possible benefits across many outcomes, but generally poor methodological quality [PMID:40538540].
- [2025-03] Turmeric DILI with hyperferritinemia in an HFE H63D heterozygote [PMID:40880678].
- [2024-10] Pathology series of 11 turmeric-associated hepatitis cases (pan-lobular/zone 3 injury) [PMID:39381846]. 103-RCT umbrella MA published the same month [PMID:39478418].

** Open questions
- Absolute incidence of curcumin DILI, and whether risk rises with piperine or other enhanced-bioavailability forms. Case series suggest it does, but no comparative data exist [PMID:32656820] [PMID:42160492].
- Whether HLA-B*35:01 testing has useful predictive value.
- Whether surrogate improvements (CRP, lipids, FBG, ALT) translate into clinical outcomes. Trials are small, short, mostly single-country and heavily heterogeneous.
- Formulation-specific efficacy: most MAs ignore bioavailability [PMID:38399379].
- Clinically relevant PK interactions with BCRP/P-gp substrates and tacrolimus at enhanced-bioavailability doses. Human data are limited to small studies [PMID:22300367] [PMID:22725663] [PMID:28104136].
- Long-term effect on iron status in humans.
