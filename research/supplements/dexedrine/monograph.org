** Identity & forms
- Dextroamphetamine sulfate is the dextro isomer of racemic amphetamine, a sympathomimetic amine (d-alpha-methylphenethylamine). It is a US Schedule II controlled substance [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Dexedrine Spansule capsules (5, 10, 15 mg) release an initial dose promptly and the rest gradually. Bioavailability is similar to immediate-release tablets. Tmax is about 8 h for a 15 mg capsule (Cmax 23.5 ng/mL) versus about 3 h for three 5 mg tablets (Cmax 36.6 ng/mL). Plasma half-life is about 12 h for both, and fed and fasted absorption are similar [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Labeled indications are narcolepsy and ADHD in patients aged 6-16 years, as part of a total treatment program. The label does not recommend it under age 6, because exposure and adverse reactions (e.g. weight loss) were higher in that age group [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Related products: lisdexamfetamine is a d-amphetamine prodrug [PMID:23539642]. Mixed amphetamine salts contain both isomers, and d-amphetamine was 2 to 2.3-fold more potent than l-amphetamine for activation and euphoria [PMID:1824]. Much of the class evidence below pools "amphetamines" (dextroamphetamine, mixed salts, lisdexamfetamine) [PMID:30097390] [PMID:40152309].

** Mechanisms
- Amphetamines release catecholamines by a non-exocytic mechanism. They redistribute dopamine and norepinephrine from synaptic vesicles to the cytosol and reverse transport through plasma-membrane uptake carriers. They also inhibit uptake and affect synthesis and metabolism [PMID:15955613].
- Peripheral effects include raised systolic and diastolic blood pressure and weak bronchodilator and respiratory stimulant actions. The label states that the mechanism of the behavioral effects in children is not established [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Dopamine dependence of subjective effects: acute phenylalanine/tyrosine depletion reduced d-amphetamine-induced striatal dopamine release on PET [PMID:14583741]. It also attenuated subjective stimulant effects but not anorexia [PMID:10475719].
- Elimination is pH-dependent. Urinary acidification speeds and alkalinization slows elimination, and the half-life difference between isomers was largest at basic urinary pH [PMID:25157]. Amphetamines are metabolized partly by CYP2D6 and weakly inhibit it [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].

** Dosing in trials
- Label, narcolepsy: 5-60 mg/day in divided doses. Ages 6-12 start at 5 mg/day, rising by 5 mg weekly. Age 12 and over start at 10 mg/day, rising by 10 mg weekly [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Label, ADHD (age 6 and over): start 5 mg once or twice daily, rising by 5 mg weekly. Only rarely is more than 40 mg/day needed. Spansules can be used once daily. Avoid late-evening doses because of insomnia [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Dose-effect: in children, amphetamine efficacy plateaus near 25 mg/day, with the best net clinical benefit at about 12-26 mg/day (peak 18-20 mg/day) [PMID:42134365] [PMID:41961095]. In adults it plateaus above about 50 mg/day, and AE discontinuations rise above that dose [PMID:42134365]. Doses above the licensed range gave no added symptom benefit [PMID:37878348].
- Narcolepsy type 2 / idiopathic hypersomnia RCT: amphetamine-dextroamphetamine titrated to at most 20 mg twice daily for 12 weeks [PMID:39306601].
- Dependence trials: in the ATLAS4Dependence protocol, dextroamphetamine is given flexibly at 30-120 mg/day for 12 weeks [PMID:41120148].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Boxed warning (class update): the FDA Drug Safety Communication of 2023-05-11 required consistent boxed warnings on misuse, abuse, addiction and overdose for all prescription stimulants. It also requires counseling never to share the drug. FDA cited past-year nonmedical use of 4.1-7.5% in ages 18-25 and found the most severe harms with non-oral routes [URL:https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-updating-warnings-improve-safe-use-prescription-stimulants-used-treat-adhd-and-other-conditions]. The current Dexedrine label carries this boxed warning and says the overdose/death risk rises with higher doses or with snorting/injection [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Abuse, misuse and use disorder:
  - Among US adults using prescription stimulants, 25.3% reported misuse and 9.0% had prescription stimulant use disorder. Amphetamine users had more misuse (PR 3.1) and use disorder (PR 2.2) than methylphenidate users [PMID:40105821].
  - In adults, nonmedical dextroamphetamine-amphetamine use rose 67.1% and ED visits rose 155.9% over 2006-2011 [PMID:26890573].
  - Diversion is common: 16-29% of students with prescriptions were asked to give, sell or trade them [PMID:18174822]. Nonmedical use does not appear to improve academic performance in people without ADHD [PMID:31326580].
- Cardiovascular:
  - Mean BP and HR increases are given in the evidence table [PMID:40152309]. Amphetamines did not raise haemodynamic values more than atomoxetine or viloxazine [PMID:40203844].
  - The label warns of sudden death in patients with structural cardiac abnormalities. It advises avoiding the drug in serious cardiac disease and monitoring for hypertension and tachycardia [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
  - Long-term use was associated with CVD in a Swedish case-control study, mainly hypertension and arterial disease [PMID:37991787]. In people aged 5-30, ADHD medication was associated with cardiac events (aOR 1.63), driven by undefined arrhythmias [PMID:40629932].
- Psychiatric:
  - In pooled short-term trials, new psychotic or manic symptoms occurred in about 0.1% on stimulants versus 0% on placebo (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
  - Observational studies show higher psychosis risk with amphetamines than methylphenidate, rising with dose [PMID:39262211] [PMID:30893533] [PMID:40900605].
- Growth: weight and height suppression in children; monitor growth (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78] [PMID:15910204] [PMID:28295312]. Mixed amphetamine salts and methylphenidate did not differ on height, but the salts reduced weight more [PMID:16670648].
- Peripheral vasculopathy/Raynaud: a systematic review found 61 cases, including (dex)amfetamine cases, mostly mild and reversible. Rare cases led to ulceration or amputation [PMID:39875750] [PMID:34655948].
- Other label warnings: seizure threshold lowering, tics/Tourette's worsening and serotonin syndrome. Other listed reactions include rhabdomyolysis, intestinal ischemia, alopecia, impotence/libido changes and frequent or prolonged erections [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. High-dose dextroamphetamine appeared to worsen tics in one short trial [PMID:29944175].
- Overdose: tachyarrhythmias, vasospasm, MI, aortic dissection, Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, hyperthermia above 104 F, rhabdomyolysis, seizures and coma. D-amphetamine is not dialyzable [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. In amphetamine-related emergencies, stroke odds are raised (OR 4.95) [PMID:40991352].
- Pregnancy:
  - Amphetamine use in the first trimester was not associated with malformations (aRR 1.05, CI 0.93-1.19; cardiac 0.96) [PMID:29238795].
  - Stimulant use was associated with preeclampsia (aRR 1.29). Continuing after 20 weeks was associated with preterm birth (aRR 1.30) [PMID:29112657].
  - Continuing into the 2nd/3rd trimester raised pre-eclampsia (RR 1.42), placental abruption (RR 1.63) and preterm birth (RR 1.34) [PMID:41400021].
  - In utero amphetamine exposure was not associated with neurodevelopmental disorders after adjustment (ADHD HR 1.07) [PMID:38265792] [PMID:36759544]. Pooled data show more NICU admissions (RR 1.88) [PMID:30585374].
  - Label: embryotoxic in mice at about 41 times the human dose. Infants of amphetamine-dependent mothers risk prematurity, low birth weight and withdrawal. Amphetamines pass into milk, and nursing is not advised [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Overall AE burden: across 93 RCTs, stimulants raised overall AEs (RR 1.34) [PMID:40343695]. The label also lists tolerance, physical dependence and a withdrawal syndrome (dysphoria, fatigue, vivid dreams, sleep changes) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].

** Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amphetamine or product components. Angioedema and anaphylaxis have been reported with other amphetamines [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- MAOIs, during use or within 14 days of stopping (including linezolid and IV methylene blue): risk of hypertensive crisis [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Strong label warnings (avoid or use with caution) rather than formal contraindications in the 2025 label: structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmia, coronary artery disease; pre-existing psychosis; bipolar risk; seizures; tics/Tourette's; peripheral vasculopathy; history of substance misuse [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78] [URL:https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-updating-warnings-improve-safe-use-prescription-stimulants-used-treat-adhd-and-other-conditions].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- MAO inhibitors (selegiline, tranylcypromine, phenelzine, isocarboxazid, linezolid, methylene blue): contraindicated. Possible outcomes include death, stroke, MI, aortic dissection and renal failure [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, TCAs, fentanyl, tramadol, buspirone, tryptophan, St John's wort): serotonin syndrome risk. Start low and monitor (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. Case report: serotonin toxicity with therapeutic dexamphetamine plus venlafaxine [PMID:11999244].
- CYP2D6 inhibitors (label examples: paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine, ritonavir; bupropion is included by class inference, as it is not named in the label): may raise amphetamine exposure and serotonin-syndrome risk [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. A DDI review advises treating an added CYP2D6 inhibitor as equivalent to a dose increase [PMID:31776871].
- Alkalinizing agents (sodium bicarbonate, acetazolamide, some thiazides): raise amphetamine levels and potentiate its action. The label says to avoid GI alkalinizers [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78] [PMID:25157].
- Acidifying agents (ascorbic acid, glutamic acid HCl, ammonium chloride, methenamine): lower amphetamine levels and efficacy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Proton pump inhibitors: omeprazole did not change total exposure to mixed amphetamine salts XR, but shortened Tmax (5 h to 2.75 h) [PMID:19820270]. The label says to monitor clinical effect [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Antihypertensives and adrenergic blockers: amphetamines may antagonize their effects (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. The BP rise itself is shown in [PMID:40152309].
- Tricyclic antidepressants (desipramine, protriptyline): striking, sustained rises in brain d-amphetamine; cardiovascular effects potentiated; also serotonergic [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Lithium: the label says lithium may inhibit stimulant effects [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. In small human studies lithium attenuated d-amphetamine euphoria and activation by 60% [PMID:1824] and blunted the systolic BP rise [PMID:443465]. Lithium is also on the label's serotonergic list [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Alcohol: a DDI review reports alcohol may raise plasma dextroamphetamine [PMID:31776871]. In an RCT, dextroamphetamine reduced ethanol-induced psychomotor impairment but not perceived intoxication [PMID:1558306]. FDA advises patients not to combine stimulants with alcohol [URL:https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-updating-warnings-improve-safe-use-prescription-stimulants-used-treat-adhd-and-other-conditions].
- Antipsychotics (haloperidol, chlorpromazine) block central stimulant effects. Other label interactions: anticonvulsant absorption delays (ethosuximide, phenobarbital, phenytoin), potentiation of meperidine and norepinephrine, and propoxyphene overdose seizures [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin C: the label lists ascorbic acid as a GI acidifier that lowers amphetamine levels and efficacy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. Supplemental ascorbic acid 4 g/day lowered urine pH by only 0.58 (not significant) [PMID:7361797], so the size of the effect at typical doses is uncertain.
- Caffeine: additive sympathomimetic effects are plausible. Both 300 mg caffeine and 20 mg d-amphetamine raised DBP/HR in controls [PMID:25414797]. In rats, caffeine potentiated amphetamine seizures and lethality [PMID:1586430]. No controlled human combination safety study was found.
- L-Tyrosine: mechanistic only. Tyrosine/phenylalanine depletion reduced amphetamine-induced dopamine release and subjective effects [PMID:14583741] [PMID:10475719]. Whether supplementation potentiates the drug is untested.
- Lithium (corpus item): see Drugs. It attenuates euphoria and is on the serotonergic list [PMID:1824] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- LSD: no cited human interaction study with dextroamphetamine was found, so no claim is made.
*** Conditions
- Serious cardiac disease, hypertension: BP/HR increases and sudden-death warning [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78] [PMID:40152309] [PMID:37991787].
- Psychotic and bipolar disorders: exacerbation and new-onset risk, especially at high doses [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78] [PMID:39262211] [PMID:40900605]. In schizophrenia-spectrum cohorts, lisdexamfetamine was not associated with more psychosis hospitalization [PMID:40595332].
- Substance use disorder history: high abuse potential [URL:https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-updating-warnings-improve-safe-use-prescription-stimulants-used-treat-adhd-and-other-conditions] [PMID:40105821].
- Epilepsy: may lower seizure threshold (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. RCT data in ADHD plus epilepsy exist only for methylphenidate [PMID:35844168].
- Tics/Tourette's [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78] [PMID:29944175]; Raynaud's phenomenon [PMID:39875750]; pregnancy and lactation [PMID:41400021] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
*** Lab-test interference
- Amphetamines can cause a significant rise in plasma corticosteroids, greatest in the evening. They may interfere with urinary steroid determinations (label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78].
- Urine drug screens: dextroamphetamine gives true-positive amphetamine immunoassay results. Many other drugs cross-react, and 3.9-9.9% of positive amphetamine screens were false positives [PMID:36367744]. Immunoassay results are presumptive until confirmed by GC-MS or LC-MS/MS [PMID:24986836] [PMID:41639014].

** Genotype notes
- CYP2D6: amphetamines are partly metabolized by CYP2D6 [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc717b9b-22ea-4c60-a1d4-ee38a40bce78]. In 214 youth treated with amphetamines, genotype-predicted poor metabolizers (corrected for phenoconversion) had higher odds of reported symptom improvement than intermediate metabolizers (OR 3.67, CI 1.15-11.7). There was no association with side effects [PMID:38517706].
- A toxicology review concludes that either poor or extensive/ultrarapid CYP2D6 metabolizers may be exposed to amfetamine toxicity, with substance-dependent evidence [PMID:25998998]. No CPIC-style dosing guideline for dextroamphetamine was found.

- [2025-05] Cardiovascular NMA (102 RCTs): amphetamines raise SBP/DBP/pulse but no more than non-stimulants [PMID:40203844].
- [2025-05] MA of 93 RCTs: stimulants raise overall AEs (RR 1.34) [PMID:40343695].
- [2025-03] Cochrane: amphetamines raise SBP 1.93 and DBP 1.84 mmHg and HR 3.71 bpm (high certainty) [PMID:40152309].
- [2025-03] SR of Raynaud syndrome with ADHD drugs (61 cases) [PMID:39875750].
- [2025-03] QoL MA: amphetamines g 0.51 [PMID:38823477].
- [2025-01] Adult component NMA: stimulants are among the only interventions effective on both self- and clinician-rated symptoms [PMID:39701638].
- [2024-11] RCT: amphetamine-dextroamphetamine vs modafinil in NT2/IH; ESS non-inferiority not shown; fewer AE dropouts (9.1% vs 31.8%) [PMID:39306601].
- [2024-10] McLean case-control: incident psychosis/mania AOR 2.68; 5.28-fold at >30 mg/day [PMID:39262211].

** Open questions
- Long-term (>1 year) randomized efficacy and safety data. No high-certainty long-term evidence exists for any ADHD intervention [PMID:41297970] [PMID:40203844].
- Whether the observational CVD association with cumulative use is causal, and which groups are at highest risk [PMID:37991787] [PMID:36416824].
- Mechanism and dose thresholds for amphetamine-associated psychosis versus methylphenidate [PMID:39262211] [PMID:40900605].
- Pregnancy: whether risks of continuing late in pregnancy reflect drug effects or confounding [PMID:41400021] [PMID:29112657].
- Clinical utility of CYP2D6 genotyping for amphetamine dosing, which needs replication [PMID:38517706].
- Size of interaction effects from dietary or supplemental acidifiers (vitamin C) and from caffeine in humans [PMID:7361797] [PMID:1586430].
- Results of dextroamphetamine agonist trials for amphetamine dependence [PMID:41120148].
