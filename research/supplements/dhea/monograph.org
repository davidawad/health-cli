** Identity & forms
- DHEA is a steroid prohormone converted to androgens and estrogens in peripheral tissues. Oral and intravaginal prasterone are distinct exposures; prescription status varies by formulation and jurisdiction. [PMID:40616152]

** Mechanisms
- Proposed biological activity and the best-established physiological context are described above; clinical mechanisms remain uncertain where outcome evidence is limited. ["PMID:40616152"]

| Long-term clinical outcomes | Evidence insufficient or mixed | No established benefit | Not established | very low | PMID:40616152; PMID:37788418 |

** Dosing in trials
- 50 mg/day orally in cognitive RCTs; vaginal prasterone 6.5 mg/day is a prescription product. [PMID:37788418] [PMID:41589851]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Acne and androgenic effects reported; long-term hormone-sensitive cancer and cardiovascular safety remain uncertain. [PMID:25879093]

** Contraindications
- Avoid unsupervised use in hormone-sensitive malignancy; this is precautionary given conversion to sex steroids. [PMID:40616152]

** Interactions
- May alter sex-steroid assays/levels and interact pharmacodynamically with estrogenic or androgenic therapies; no established routine lab assay interference. [PMID:40616152]

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed; no genotype-specific claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2025–2026 literature: 2025 meta-analysis quantified increases in testosterone and estradiol after oral supplementation. [PMID:40616152]

** Open questions
- Whether findings translate into durable patient-important outcomes, optimal formulation/dose, and long-term safety remains uncertain. [PMID:40616152; PMID:37788418]
