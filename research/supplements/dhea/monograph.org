** Identity & forms
- Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) is made mainly by the zona reticularis of the adrenal cortex. With its sulfate ester (DHEA-S) it is the most abundant circulating steroid. It is a precursor that peripheral tissues convert into androgens and estrogens [PMID:40943486] [PMID:41485982]. Levels fall steeply with age ("adrenopause") [PMID:40943486].
- Oral DHEA (US): sold over the counter as a dietary supplement [PMID:11144700] [PMID:37788418]. It is on the World Anti-Doping Agency prohibited list, because oral dosing can produce dose-dependent and, in women, supraphysiological rises in androgens [PMID:20122454] [PMID:18417762]. Rules outside the US vary. No PubMed-indexed source on country-by-country prescription or controlled status was retrieved, so none is claimed here.
- Prasterone is the pharmaceutical name for synthetic DHEA:
  - Intrarosa: 6.5 mg vaginal insert, given once daily at bedtime. FDA-approved for moderate-to-severe dyspareunia due to menopausal vulvar and vaginal atrophy (VVA) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7]. In the EU it is approved for VVA [PMID:31290076].
  - Oral prasterone (100-200 mg/d) was studied in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). FDA did not approve the lupus labeling. Prasterone got orphan status for preventing glucocorticoid-related bone loss in SLE [PMID:17090740].
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - Oral: in women whose adrenals were suppressed with dexamethasone, 50 mg restored baseline DHEA-S, while 100 mg gave supraphysiological DHEA-S and raised androgens [PMID:9626121].
  - Vaginal: 6.5 mg/d prasterone raised serum DHEA, testosterone and estradiol slightly. LC-MS/MS levels stayed within the normal postmenopausal range [PMID:26972555] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7].

** Mechanisms
- Intracrine conversion: tissue enzymes (3beta-HSD, 17beta-HSD, 5alpha-reductase, aromatase) turn DHEA into testosterone, DHT, estrone and estradiol inside target cells. The active steroids then leave as glucuronide or sulfate conjugates. This is why serum testosterone and estradiol can understate tissue exposure [PMID:26972555] [PMID:41485982].
- Systemic sex-steroid rise with oral dosing:
  - Postmenopausal women (MA of 21 studies): estradiol +7.86 pg/mL and testosterone +24.31 ng/dL [PMID:40616152].
  - All populations (MA of 42 publications): testosterone +28.02 ng/dL, larger in women and at doses >50 mg/d [PMID:33045358].
- Bone: in older adults, the hip-BMD gains from DHEA were explained by the rise in serum estradiol, not by DHEA-S itself [PMID:18812486]. Preclinical work shows effects on osteoblasts and on the RANKL/OPG balance [PMID:39767687].
- Neurosteroid actions: DHEA and DHEA-S modulate GABA-A, NMDA and sigma-1 receptors (preclinical/mechanistic) [PMID:40943486]. In Addison's disease, mood benefit in men without any change in testosterone suggests a direct central effect [PMID:11134123].
- Glucocorticoid axis: a meta-analysis found lower serum cortisol (WMD -53.6 nmol/L across 10 arms) [PMID:34342920].
- HSD3B1 genotype: the HSD3B1(1245C) "adrenal-permissive" allele makes a 3beta-HSD1 protein that resists breakdown. This increases peripheral conversion of DHEA to potent androgens [PMID:32565196].

** Dosing in trials
- Adrenal insufficiency: 50 mg/d oral micronized DHEA for 12 weeks to 12 months [PMID:11134123] [PMID:18000094]. 50 mg restored physiological DHEA-S in women whose adrenals were suppressed; 100 mg was supraphysiological [PMID:9626121]. Hypopituitary women: 25 mg twice daily [PMID:35481572].
- Older adults: 50 mg/d for 6 months to 2 years, usually with calcium and vitamin D in both arms [PMID:17050889] [PMID:19321570] [PMID:20863330].
- Doses >=50 mg/d gave larger rises in testosterone and, in women aged >=60, in estradiol [PMID:40616152] [PMID:33045358].
- IVF/DOR: pretreatment in trials typically ran for weeks to months before stimulation. The optimal regimen is not established [PMID:38837771] [PMID:39980032].
- Menopause (systemic): 60 mg/d for 3 months in a 2026 comparative trial [PMID:42328990].
- Depression and schizophrenia trials: up to 100 mg/d in divided doses; schizophrenia trials added DHEA to antipsychotics [PMID:12578430] [PMID:32930419].
- Lupus (oral prasterone): 100-200 mg/d, which produced supraphysiological hormone levels [PMID:17090740].
- Vaginal prasterone (Intrarosa): 6.5 mg insert nightly [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7]. Alternative regimens studied: 3.25 mg [PMID:29164377]; daily for 4 weeks then twice weekly [PMID:41903372].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Androgenic effects are the main dose-related adverse events:
  - Cochrane menopause review: androgenic side effects (mainly acne) OR 3.77 (1.36-10.4) [PMID:25879093].
  - Cochrane lupus review: acne RR 2.2 (1.65-2.83) [PMID:17943841].
  - Prasterone in SLE also caused hirsutism. Hematuria, hypertension and creatinine rises were reported [PMID:17090740].
  - Older women in the Addison trial reached supraphysiological DHEA-S and had mild androgenic effects [PMID:18000094].
  - High-dose use by female athletes is expected to cause hirsutism, acne and alopecia [PMID:20122454].
- Serious events: 2-year trials in older adults found no major adverse effects [PMID:17050889]. A meta-analysis of postmenopausal women found no effect on serious adverse events [PMID:25279571]. Trials were short and too small to detect rare harms.
- Lipids: HDL fell in women (-5.1 mg/dL) [PMID:32675010]. No liver-enzyme change was found [PMID:34342920].
- Psychiatric: case reports describe mania starting during DHEA use, including a severe case with homicidal threats [PMID:11144700].
- Hormone-sensitive cancers:
  - Pooled prospective data link higher endogenous DHEA, DHEA-S, testosterone and estradiol to higher postmenopausal breast cancer risk [PMID:11959894]. This is observational evidence about endogenous levels, not supplement trials.
  - A 2025 review argues that animal data show DHEA suppresses mammary tumors and that the association in humans reflects adrenal stress activation [PMID:40812951].
  - The adrenal-permissive HSD3B1 allele, which increases androgen production from DHEA, is linked to worse outcomes in advanced prostate cancer on androgen deprivation [PMID:32565196].
  - No trial has been powered for cancer outcomes.
- Vaginal prasterone:
  - The most common adverse reaction was vaginal discharge (>=2%), plus abnormal Pap smear in the 52-week trial [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7].
  - Discharge was about 6% in phase III [PMID:26731686].
  - Label warning: estrogen is a metabolite, and the product has not been studied in women with breast cancer [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7].
- Anorexia nervosa: DHEA monotherapy did not improve BMD. One study suggested harm to BMD in younger adolescents with open physes [PMID:35460091].
- Mendelian randomization: genetically higher DHEA-S was linked to shorter lifespan in men (-1.15 years per log unit) and showed no association in women. This argues against assuming DHEA is a longevity agent [PMID:40450423].

** Contraindications
- Current or past breast cancer or other estrogen-dependent cancer: estrogen is a metabolite of prasterone, and systemic DHEA raises estradiol [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7] [PMID:40616152]. Vaginal DHEA has been studied in survivors, but only in small or short trials [PMID:29164377] [PMID:35343852].
- Prostate cancer or other androgen-sensitive disease: DHEA is converted to testosterone and DHT [PMID:32565196] [PMID:33045358]. This is precautionary.
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding: a label contraindication for vaginal prasterone [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ada639d4-bac0-2ad0-e053-2a95a90afce7].
- Bipolar disorder or a history of mania: case reports of DHEA-associated mania [PMID:11144700].
- Hyperandrogenic states (for example PCOS, hirsutism, acne-prone women): androgenic adverse effects are dose-related [PMID:25879093].
- Adolescents with open growth plates (anorexia nervosa data) [PMID:35460091].
- Competitive athletes subject to anti-doping rules [PMID:20122454].
- Guidelines: the Endocrine Society recommends against routine DHEA use in women, including women with adrenal insufficiency or hypopituitarism [PMID:25279570]. The primary adrenal insufficiency guideline lists only glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement as standard therapy [PMID:26760044].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Estrogen therapy, oral contraceptives and progestins: additive or opposing hormonal effects are possible [PMID:17090740]. Systemic DHEA raises estradiol by about 8 pg/mL in postmenopausal women [PMID:40616152]. Combining DHEA with oral contraceptives changed bone results in anorexia nervosa [PMID:35460091].
- Testosterone and other androgens: additive androgen exposure [PMID:17090740] [PMID:33045358].
- Aromatase inhibitors: with vaginal DHEA, estradiol did not rise in women taking AIs, but DHEA-S and testosterone rose in a dose-dependent way [PMID:29164377]. In a 10-patient pilot study, serum estradiol stayed low on AIs [PMID:35343852]. Systemic oral DHEA with AIs has not been studied.
- 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride): potential interaction through the DHEA-to-DHT pathway [PMID:17090740]. This is theoretical.
- Glucocorticoids and hydrocortisone replacement: glucocorticoids suppress endogenous DHEA-S and reduce its value as a diagnostic marker [PMID:39657727]. Prasterone was studied for bone protection in lupus patients on chronic glucocorticoids [PMID:17090740].
- Antidiabetic drugs: small fall in fasting glucose (-2.2 mg/dL) [PMID:33220623]. Clinically minor.
- Antipsychotics and antidepressants: DHEA was added to antipsychotics in schizophrenia trials [PMID:12578430]. Mania case reports call for caution in bipolar disorder [PMID:11144700].
*** Other supplements
- Ashwagandha: an RCT found an 18% greater rise in DHEA-S and 14.7% greater rise in testosterone versus placebo [PMID:30854916]. Combined with DHEA this could add to androgen exposure and confuse DHEA-S monitoring. No combination study exists.
- CoQ10: both are used as IVF adjuvants for poor ovarian response. A network MA ranked CoQ10 best for live birth and DHEA strongly for implantation and oocyte number [PMID:37464357]. A DOR MA found combined supplement use correlated with better ovarian markers [PMID:41185971]. Evidence for any additive effect is low quality.
- Vitamin D-3 (with calcium): given as background in both arms of DHEA bone trials. Spine BMD gains in women occurred on top of it [PMID:19321570] [PMID:20863330]. No interaction was tested.
*** Conditions
- Breast, endometrial and prostate cancer, bipolar disorder, hyperandrogenism, adolescents with open physes, undiagnosed genital bleeding: see Contraindications.
- Adrenal insufficiency: DHEA is an optional add-on to glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement, not a substitute [PMID:26760044] [PMID:19773400].
- Dyslipidemia or cardiovascular risk in women: HDL falls [PMID:32675010].
*** Lab-test interference
- Testosterone immunoassays: DHEA-S cross-reacts in many direct testosterone immunoassays, including Abbott Architect, Roche Elecsys/E170, Beckman Access and Immulite. In the Architect assay, measured testosterone rose 0.12 nmol/L in serum for every 1 umol/L of DHEA-S [PMID:16704754] [PMID:17362583]. Supplemented women with high DHEA-S may show falsely high testosterone; LC-MS/MS avoids this. Package-insert cross-reactivity varies by platform [PMID:25071417].
- True rises in measured hormones: DHEA-S, testosterone, estradiol and estrone rise and SHBG falls [PMID:18812486] [PMID:40616152]. Interpret sex-hormone panels in light of supplement use.
- Adrenal insufficiency work-up: DHEA-S is used to screen for adrenal insufficiency. Low DHEA-S plus low cortisol predicted AI in 72% of patients [PMID:39657727], and a sex- and age-adjusted DHEA-S index had a negative predictive value of 98% [PMID:42591062]. Exogenous DHEA would raise DHEA-S and could mask a low result. This is inferred; no study tested it directly.
- Anti-doping urine tests: DHEA is detected by carbon isotope-ratio mass spectrometry when urinary DHEA exceeds 100 ng/mL [PMID:18417762].

** Genotype notes
- HSD3B1 rs1047303 (1245A>C): the adrenal-permissive 1245C allele makes a stable 3beta-HSD1 enzyme. This increases conversion of DHEA to potent androgens and is linked to worse outcomes in androgen-sensitive prostate cancer [PMID:32565196]. No study has tested whether genotype changes the response to supplemental DHEA.

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Sex- and age-adjusted DHEA-S index for suspected adrenal insufficiency: sensitivity 93%, NPV 98% in prospective validation [PMID:42591062].
- [2026-07] EDIPHY crossover RCT in PAH (n=26): no effect on RV longitudinal strain; DHEA raised DHEA-S and testosterone; well tolerated [PMID:42446134].
- [2026-07] Vaginal DHEA MA (5 RCTs, n=1611): dryness MD -0.23, dyspareunia MD -0.40 vs placebo [PMID:41589851].
- [2026-06] Oral DHEA 60 mg/d vs Femarelle (n=70): similar climacteric score reductions; within-group urinary and FSFI gains with DHEA [PMID:42328990].
- [2026-05] Open-label RCT of vaginal DHEA vs estradiol (n=172): dyspareunia improved 92% vs 82%; estradiol better on objective atrophy signs [PMID:41903372].
- [2026-04] Review of DHEA/DHEA-S anti-aging mechanisms (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, intracrine) in older people [PMID:41485982].
- [2025-12] DOR supplement MA (16 studies): supplements including DHEA lowered FSH and raised AMH, AFC and oocytes; CoQ10 outperformed DHEA [PMID:41185971].
- [2025-10] Mendelian randomization: genetically higher DHEA-S linked to shorter lifespan in men, null in women [PMID:40450423].
- [2025-09] Review of DHEA/DHEA-S neurosteroid and intracrine mechanisms and the evidence for vaginal and oral use [PMID:40943486].
- [2025-08] Single-center cohort (n=1135): DHEA-S AUROC 0.81 for adrenal insufficiency, lower after recent glucocorticoid use [PMID:39657727].
- [2025-07] MA (21 studies) in postmenopausal women: estradiol +7.86 pg/mL, testosterone +24.31 ng/dL, larger at >=50 mg/d [PMID:40616152].
- [2025-05] Pilot cohort (n=34): vaginal DHEA reduced stress urinary incontinence episodes in women with VVA (uncontrolled) [PMID:40068355].
- [2025-03] DOR MA (38 RCTs, POSEIDON criteria): DHEA increased oocytes (WMD 0.60); only testosterone improved live birth [PMID:39332623].
- [2025-02] IVF/ICSI MA (16 RCTs, n=1973): endometrial thickness +0.93 mm, CPR OR 1.34, low QoE; live birth inconclusive [PMID:39980032].
- [2025] Review: animal data show DHEA suppresses mammary tumors; it proposes cortisol rather than DHEA as the harmful factor behind the breast cancer association [PMID:40812951].
- [2024-10] Ann Intern Med SR of GSM therapies: vaginal DHEA may improve dryness and dyspareunia (low COE); systemic DHEA may give no benefit [PMID:39250810].

** Open questions
- Long-term (>2 years) safety of oral DHEA for breast, endometrial and prostate cancer and for cardiovascular events. No trial was powered for these.
- Whether the small HRQOL benefit in women with adrenal insufficiency is clinically meaningful, and who benefits most. There are few recent RCTs.
- The DHEA effect on live birth in poor responders. Cochrane finds little difference, while surrogate outcomes (oocytes, endometrium) improve.
- Variable content and purity of OTC products, and dose titration by DHEA-S target versus fixed dose.
- Whether the HSD3B1 genotype changes androgenic response or risk with supplemental DHEA.
- Safety of systemic DHEA with aromatase inhibitors, and long-term endometrial and breast safety of vaginal prasterone in cancer survivors.
- How much immunoassay testosterone overestimation inflates reported androgen rises in older trials, compared with LC-MS/MS.
