** Identity & forms
- Long-chain marine omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids: eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5n-3), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6n-3) and docosapentaenoic acid (DPA, 22:5n-3). Category "other" was chosen because these are essential fatty acids, not vitamins or minerals. Both OTC and prescription forms exist; RX_STATUS reflects the OTC fish/algal oils that most users take.
- DPA is a minor constituent of fish oil. Only two human studies have used pure n-3 DPA (1-2 g/day). Blood DPA rises within 3-4 days, and DPA behaves biologically more like DHA than EPA [PMID:33315722]. No outcome trial has dosed DPA separately.
- OTC forms: fish oil triglycerides or ethyl esters, concentrates, and algal oils, typically about 1 g capsules. Label accuracy and oxidation vary. In New Zealand, 69% of 32 products contained <67% of the labelled EPA+DHA and 83% exceeded peroxide limits [PMID:25604397]. A 2016 Australian/NZ survey using accredited labs found all 10 products met label claims [PMID:27827947].
- Prescription forms (FDA labels):
  - Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa): purified EPA ethyl ester, 4 g/day. Indicated with maximally tolerated statins to reduce MI, stroke, revascularization and unstable angina in adults with TG >=150 mg/dL plus established CVD, or diabetes with >=2 risk factors. Also indicated for TG >=500 mg/dL [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508].
  - Omega-3-acid ethyl esters (Lovaza/Omacor): each 1 g capsule has about 465 mg EPA and 375 mg DHA ethyl esters. Indicated only for TG >=500 mg/dL; the label states the effect on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality has not been determined [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
  - Omega-3 carboxylic acids (Epanova; EPA+DHA free fatty acids) were tested in EVOLVE II and STRENGTH [PMID:29289538] [PMID:33190147].
- Regulatory intake reference: EFSA found no basis for a UL. Combined EPA+DHA up to 5 g/day and EPA alone up to 1.8 g/day raise no safety concern for adults; 250-500 mg/day is the cardiovascular dietary recommendation [PMID:42016086]. In 2026 EFSA kept a safe level of 1 g/day for supplemental DHA alone, for all ages including pregnancy [PMID:41542352].

** Mechanisms
- Triglyceride lowering: EPA and DHA reduce hepatic VLDL-TG production and lower fasting and postprandial TG. DHA is retroconverted to EPA, but EPA is not converted to DHA in humans [PMID:28797250]. The Vascepa label lists increased beta-oxidation, DGAT inhibition, lower hepatic lipogenesis and higher lipoprotein lipase activity as possible mechanisms [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508].
- EPA vs DHA lipid effects differ. DHA raises LDL-C (+7.23 mg/dL vs placebo) and HDL-C, while EPA does not raise LDL-C [PMID:21975919].
- Inflammation: across 96 trials, EPA+DHA lowered arachidonic acid, CRP, TNF-alpha and IL-6. Doses of 1-3 g/day gave the most consistent cytokine reductions [PMID:41568426]. EPA, DHA and DPA give rise to specialized pro-resolving mediators (resolvins, protectins, maresins) [PMID:33315722].
- Hemostasis: omega-3s may reduce platelet aggregation and thromboxane A2. Bleeding-time prolongation in trials stayed within normal limits [PMID:22300597] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
- Atrial arrhythmia: a review proposes dose-dependent vagal-tone effects as the explanation for the biphasic link between omega-3 intake and AF [PMID:39617283]. In OMEMI, the rise in serum EPA (not DHA) mediated 65% of the excess AF [PMID:37515843].
- The REDUCE-IT benefit is not fully explained by TG lowering. The mineral-oil comparator raised IL-1beta, IL-6, hsCRP, oxidized LDL and LDL-C, which may have exaggerated the between-group difference [PMID:35762321].

** Dosing in trials
- Cardiovascular outcome trials: icosapent ethyl 4 g/day (2 g twice daily) in REDUCE-IT [PMID:30415628]; EPA 1.8 g/day in JELIS and RESPECT-EPA [PMID:17398308] [PMID:38873793]; omega-3 carboxylic acids 4 g/day in STRENGTH [PMID:33190147]; 1.8 g/day (930 mg EPA + 660 mg DHA) in OMEMI [PMID:33191772]; 1 g capsules (840 mg EPA+DHA) in VITAL and ASCEND [PMID:30415637] [PMID:30146932]; 4 g/day (1.6 g EPA + 0.8 g DHA) in PISCES [PMID:41201837].
- Severe hypertriglyceridemia: 4 g/day of Lovaza or Vascepa with meals, swallowed whole [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508]. Omega-3 carboxylic acids 2 g/day cut TG by 14.2% vs olive oil [PMID:29289538].
- TG and non-HDL-C fall almost linearly with dose, most clearly above 2 g/day [PMID:37264945]. Blood pressure effects plateau at 2-3 g/day [PMID:35647665].
- Depression: EPA-predominant (>=60% EPA) formulations at <=1 g/day EPA [PMID:31383846]. Late-life depression relapse prevention used 1.2 g EPA + 1 g DHA for 52 weeks [PMID:39306009].
- Pregnancy: 900 mg/day n-3 LCPUFA (ORIP) [PMID:31509674]; DHA 1000 vs 200 mg/day (ADORE) [PMID:34308309].
- Omega-3 index (RBC EPA+DHA as % of fatty acids): proposed targets are <4% (higher risk) and >8% (lower risk). Moving from 4% to 8% was estimated to lower fatal CHD risk by about 30% (cohort data) [PMID:28511049].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Atrial fibrillation is the main safety signal, and it depends on dose.
  - REDUCE-IT: AF/flutter hospitalization 3.1% vs 2.1% [PMID:30415628]. RESPECT-EPA: new-onset AF 3.1% vs 1.6% [PMID:38873793]. OMEMI: AF HR 1.84 (0.98-3.45), and AF or micro-AF HR 1.90 (1.16-3.11) [PMID:33191772] [PMID:37515843].
  - Meta-analyses: HR 1.25 overall, 1.49 at >1 g/day and 1.12 at <=1 g/day [PMID:34612056]. The 2026 MA of 35 RCTs confined the excess to high-risk patients taking >1.5 g/day (absolute risk +0.8%) [PMID:42517224]. Other MAs report RR 1.26 overall and 1.35 for EPA alone [PMID:34505026].
  - In the Lovaza trial in paroxysmal or persistent AF, symptomatic recurrence was more frequent (HR 1.25; 1.00-1.40) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
  - Observational data point the other way. In UK Biobank, regular fish oil use was linked to more incident AF in healthy people (HR 1.13) [PMID:38800667]. Higher blood DHA, DPA and EPA+DHA levels were linked to slightly lower AF risk (HR 0.89-0.93) across 17 cohorts [PMID:37468189].
- Bleeding:
  - A MA of 11 RCTs (n=120,643) found no overall excess (RR 1.09; 0.91-1.31). High-dose purified EPA raised relative bleeding risk by about 50% (absolute +0.6%) [PMID:38742535].
  - REDUCE-IT: any bleeding 12% vs 10%, serious bleeding 3% vs 2%. Rates were higher with concomitant aspirin, clopidogrel or warfarin [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508].
  - OMEMI major bleeding was 10.7% vs 11.0% [PMID:33191772]. VITAL-DEP GI bleeding was 2.6% vs 2.7% [PMID:34932079].
- LDL-C: DHA-containing products raise LDL-C. EFSA estimated about +3% at 2-6 g/day EPA+DHA, while EPA up to 4 g/day had no significant effect [PMID:42016086]. Lovaza raised LDL-C by 44.5% in severe HTG, and its label requires LDL monitoring [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
- GI and other effects:
  - Lovaza: eructation, dyspepsia and taste perversion [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75]. STRENGTH: more GI events than corn oil [PMID:33190147].
  - Vascepa: musculoskeletal pain, peripheral edema, constipation and gout [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508]. Real-world claims data show the same pattern at one-third to one-fifth of trial incidence [PMID:40148153].
- Depression: VITAL-DEP found a small excess of incident or recurrent depression with 1 g/day (HR 1.13) [PMID:34932079].
- Pregnancy: prolonged gestation >42 weeks rose (RR 1.61; 1.11-2.33) [PMID:30480773]. ORIP had more very-large-for-gestational-age infants (RR 1.30) [PMID:31509674].
- Fish allergy: both FDA labels warn of possible allergic reactions in people allergic to fish or shellfish [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508]. In a pilot study, 6 of 6 people allergic to finned fish had negative skin tests and oral challenges to two fish oil supplements [PMID:18926060].

** Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the product. This is the only labelled contraindication for Vascepa and Lovaza [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
- Relative (caution):
  - Paroxysmal or persistent AF, or high AF risk, with doses >1 g/day [PMID:42517224] [PMID:34612056].
  - Fish or shellfish allergy (label caution; algal oil avoids fish protein) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75] [PMID:18926060].
  - Active bleeding, or combined antithrombotic therapy, with high-dose EPA [PMID:38742535].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin: icosapent ethyl 4 g/day did not change R-/S-warfarin pharmacokinetics or INR in healthy volunteers [PMID:24760401]. A case report describes INR rising from 2.8 to 4.3 after fish oil was doubled to 2 g/day [PMID:14742793]. Labels advise periodic monitoring with anticoagulants [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
- Antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel) and other anticoagulants: bleeding in REDUCE-IT was higher with concomitant antithrombotics [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508]. In an RCT meta-analysis, bleeding tracked EPA dose rather than background antiplatelet use [PMID:38742535].
- Statins: the positive EPA trials were all run on a statin background [PMID:30415628] [PMID:17398308] [PMID:38873793]. The Vascepa CV indication requires maximally tolerated statin therapy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508].
- Antihypertensives: omega-3s give a small additive BP reduction (about -2.6 mmHg systolic at 2-3 g/day) [PMID:35647665].
- Antidiabetic drugs: no adverse glycemic effect at up to 5 g/day per EFSA [PMID:42016086]. ASCEND, in people with diabetes, showed no excess serious adverse events [PMID:30146932].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin E, Garlic, Ginger Root and Ginkgo biloba: a review lists these together with fish oil as supplements that may affect hemostasis. It found anecdotal bleeding cases but too few controlled studies to show a consistent additive effect [PMID:22300597].
- Vitamin D-3: no interaction on AF or depression in the VITAL factorial design [PMID:33724323] [PMID:34932079]. Additive slowing of the PhenoAge clock in DO-HEALTH [PMID:39900648].
*** Conditions
- Atrial fibrillation, fish allergy and bleeding risk: see Contraindications.
- Hepatic impairment: the Lovaza label advises periodic ALT/AST monitoring [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
- Pregnancy: lowers preterm birth risk but increases prolonged gestation [PMID:30480773]. The benefit is concentrated in women with low baseline omega-3 status [PMID:34308309] [PMID:31509674].
- Hemodialysis: the one large RCT found a cardiovascular benefit [PMID:41201837].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact identified. The changes below are real physiological effects:
  - TG falls and LDL-C rises with DHA-containing products [PMID:21975919] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=21cfa4ce-0b05-47ed-b268-339eb1b83b75].
  - The omega-3 index and EPA/AA ratio rise, which makes them adherence and exposure markers [PMID:28511049] [PMID:38873793] [PMID:41568426].
  - Bleeding time may lengthen but stays within normal limits [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9c1a2828-1583-4414-ab22-a60480e8e508].

** Genotype notes
- FADS Indel (rs66698963): in a post hoc ADORE analysis (n=761), I-allele carriers on 200 mg/day DHA had 4.3% early preterm birth vs 0.8% in D/D. High-dose DHA (1000 mg/day) lowered preterm birth in I-allele carriers but increased prolonged gestation in D/D women. This supports genotype-guided DHA dosing, but the finding needs prospective confirmation [PMID:42848825].
- APOE e4: 2 g/day DHA raised CSF DHA/AA independently of APOE e4 status. It had no cognitive or brain-volume effect at 24 months [PMID:42315445].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] FADS Indel genotype modified the effect of DHA dose on early preterm birth and prolonged gestation (ADORE secondary analysis) [PMID:42848825].
- [2026-08] MA of 35 RCTs (n=114,592): AF excess only at >1.5 g/day in high-CV-risk patients (OR 1.43). Low doses showed no excess [PMID:42517224].
- [2026-07] DHA 2 g/day for 24 months in older adults at risk of dementia: CNS target engagement, but no cognitive or MRI benefit [PMID:42315445].
- [2026-06] MA of 25 secondary-prevention RCTs: moderate-dose EPA+DHA did not reduce MACE or AF/POAF [PMID:42144851].
- [2026-04] Dialysis MA: fish oil HR 0.56 for CV events in hemodialysis [PMID:41851014]. Small RCT in psychological distress (750 mg/day): improvements in PSS, PHQ-9 and sleep [PMID:41461240].
- [2026-03] Pregnancy MA (23 studies): preterm birth OR 0.83 (0.75-0.93); slightly larger birth size [PMID:41773213].
- [2026-01] PISCES (NEJM): 4 g/day fish oil in hemodialysis, serious CV events HR 0.57 [PMID:41201837]. EFSA kept a 1 g/day safe level for DHA alone [PMID:41542352]. MA of 96 trials on EPA:DHA ratio and inflammation [PMID:41568426].
- [2025-10] MA of 16 RCTs: purified EPA lowered CV mortality (HR 0.79) more than EPA/DHA (HR 0.92) [PMID:40974959]. Metabolic syndrome MA: TG reductions of up to about 57 mg/dL at >2 g/day [PMID:41156531].
- [2025-09] Overview of SRs: MMSE g 0.16 in people without dementia or with MCI [PMID:41010527].
- [2025-07] Review: dose-dependent AF risk in RCTs (about +12% at ~1 g/day, about +50% at 1.8-4 g/day) vs lower risk with dietary intake [PMID:39617283].
- [2025-05] Real-world icosapent ethyl safety: AF, bleeding, edema and constipation at one-third to one-fifth of trial incidence [PMID:40148153].
- [2025-04] Older-adult depression MA: omega-3 SMD -0.14 [PMID:40169548].
- [2025-03] DO-HEALTH: omega-3 1 g/day slowed PhenoAge, GrimAge2 and DunedinPACE [PMID:39900648].
- [2025-01] 52-week RCT (n=39): fewer late-life depression recurrences (HR 0.30) [PMID:39306009].
- [2024-12] MA of prescription omega-3 (12 RCTs): CV events RR 0.88 [PMID:39639295].
- [2024-11] Cochrane: very uncertain benefit for depression in children and adolescents [PMID:39564892]. MA of 18 RCTs: EPA alone reduced revascularization more than EPA+DHA [PMID:38869144]. Umbrella review plus MR: benefit for RA and SLE [PMID:39357585].

** Open questions
- Is the REDUCE-IT benefit real or partly a mineral-oil artifact? STRENGTH (corn oil) was null, and RESPECT-EPA was open-label and borderline [PMID:35762321] [PMID:33190147] [PMID:38873793].
- Is EPA alone truly superior to EPA+DHA, or does this reflect dose, comparator and population? [PMID:40974959] [PMID:38869144]
- What dose threshold and patient profile define AF risk, and why do RCTs and biomarker cohorts disagree? [PMID:42517224] [PMID:37468189]
- Does the PISCES hemodialysis benefit replicate? [PMID:41201837]
- Should the omega-3 index be used to target supplementation? [PMID:28511049] [PMID:34308309]
- Does DPA have independent clinical effects? [PMID:33315722]
- Does OTC product quality (content, oxidation) matter clinically? [PMID:25604397]
