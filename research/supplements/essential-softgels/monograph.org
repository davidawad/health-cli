** Identity & forms
- Branded multi-ingredient dietary supplement from Blueprint (Bryan Johnson). It is now sold as "Advanced Antioxidants". The manufacturer FAQ says the name was changed and that "the only change is delivery": the softgel became a delayed-release liquid capsule [URL:https://blueprint.bryanjohnson.com/products/advanced-antioxidants].
- Current label, per 1 liquid capsule daily (30 per bottle) [URL:https://blueprint.bryanjohnson.com/products/advanced-antioxidants]:
  - Vitamin K1 (phytonadione) 1500 mcg (1250% DV)
  - Lycopene 15 mg
  - Astaxanthin (Haematococcus pluvialis microalgae) 12 mg
  - Lutein (marigold flower extract) 10 mg; zeaxanthin (marigold) 2 mg
  - Vitamin K2 MK-4 (menaquinone-4) 5 mg; vitamin K2 MK-7 (menaquinone-7) 600 mcg
  - Other ingredients: MCT oil (coconut), hypromellose capsule, candelilla wax.
- NIH DSLD label 328205 ("Essential Softgel", veggie softgel, entered 2025-04-24) lists the same seven actives. However, it gives lutein 15 mg and zeaxanthin 3 mg; the other amounts are identical [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/328205]. So older softgel lots may differ from the current capsule in lutein/zeaxanthin dose despite the "only delivery changed" statement. The bottle in hand governs.
- No beta-carotene, vitamin A, vitamin E or minerals are listed on either label [URL:https://blueprint.bryanjohnson.com/products/advanced-antioxidants] [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/328205].
- The lutein/zeaxanthin dose on the current label (10 mg/2 mg) is the AREDS2 dose [PMID:23644932]. The other doses do not match any single trial formula. No study of the whole product was found.

** Mechanisms
- Lutein/zeaxanthin: the only carotenoids (with meso-zeaxanthin) found in the macula. They raise macular pigment optical density (MPOD), act as antioxidants and filter blue light [PMID:34157098].
- Lycopene: hydrocarbon carotenoid antioxidant. Proposed vascular effects include better endothelial function (FMD) [PMID:42588128] [PMID:28129549].
- Astaxanthin: xanthophyll antioxidant. In RCT meta-analysis it lowers malondialdehyde [PMID:35091276].
  - Preclinical: inhibits platelet activation and hepatic transcription of coagulation factors in rodents and human cells (2026) [PMID:41702374]. In rats it lowered vitamin K and vitamin K-dependent factors II, VII, IX and X [PMID:32725659].
- Vitamin K1, MK-4 and MK-7 are cofactors for gamma-glutamyl carboxylation of coagulation factors, osteocalcin and matrix Gla protein (MGP) [PMID:17158229] [PMID:42851346].
  - MK-7 has a much longer half-life than K1. It accumulates 7-8-fold on repeated dosing and carboxylates osteocalcin more completely [PMID:17158229].
- In vitro, xanthophylls (lutein, zeaxanthin, astaxanthin) only weakly inhibit CYP enzymes, at concentrations well above human Cmax. CYP-mediated drug interactions are unlikely [PMID:23669408].

** Dosing in trials
- Lutein/zeaxanthin: 10 mg/2 mg daily for a median of 5 years in AREDS2 [PMID:23644932]. The same dose for 6 months in screen users and teenagers [PMID:39963662] [PMID:42413424]. MPOD response is larger at >=20 mg/d [PMID:34157098].
- Lycopene: trial doses ranged from 1.44 to 75 mg/d [PMID:32652029]. SBP lowering was more marked at >12 mg/d [PMID:24051501]. 15 mg for 8 weeks improved a reactive hyperemia index [PMID:42588128].
- Astaxanthin: 6-24 mg/d in lipid trials, with HDL gains at 12 mg/d [PMID:40872489]. 87 human studies were reviewed, 35 of them at >=12 mg/d [PMID:31788888].
- Vitamin K1: 500 mcg/d for 3 y (CAC trial) [PMID:19386744]; 5 mg/d for 2-4 y (ECKO) [PMID:18922041]. The product's 1500 mcg lies between these.
- MK-4: the Japanese osteoporosis dose is 45 mg/d [PMID:30734066] [PMID:16801507]. The product's 5 mg is about one-ninth of this.
- MK-7: 180 mcg/d for 1-3 y (bone, stiffness) [PMID:23525894] [PMID:25694037] [PMID:40077685]; 360 mcg/d for 2 y (VitaK-CAC) [PMID:42268593]; 720 mcg/d plus D3 25 mcg for 2 y (AVADEC, DANCODE) [PMID:35465686] [PMID:42661461]. The product's 600 mcg is near the upper end of trial doses.
- Carotenoids are lipophilic with low oral bioavailability. Lipid-based formulations raised astaxanthin bioavailability 1.7-3.7-fold [PMID:12885395]. This supports taking the product with food. The product is delivered in MCT oil [URL:https://blueprint.bryanjohnson.com/products/advanced-antioxidants].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Lutein/zeaxanthin: AREDS2 found no excess of adverse events or serious adverse events [PMID:24638908]. At 10 years, lutein/zeaxanthin showed no significant lung-cancer signal (OR 1.15, 0.79-1.66), whereas beta-carotene nearly doubled risk (OR 1.82) [PMID:35653117].
- Observed Safe Levels: 20 mg/d for lutein and 75 mg/d for lycopene [PMID:16814439]. The label doses fall below both.
- Astaxanthin:
  - EFSA judged 8 mg/day from supplements safe for adults, on top of dietary intake (ADI 0.2 mg/kg bw) [PMID:32874213]. The product's 12 mg exceeds the EFSA 8 mg supplement level, though for a 70 kg adult it stays under the ADI-derived 14 mg.
  - A safety review found no safety concerns across 87 human studies of natural astaxanthin [PMID:31788888].
  - Carotenoid supplementation did not change liver enzymes in a GRADE-assessed MA [PMID:41437050].
- Vitamin K:
  - 5 mg/d K1 for up to 4 years was well tolerated, with no excess adverse effects [PMID:18922041].
  - The MK-4 45 mg MA found more adverse events (RR 1.47) and adverse drug reactions (RR 1.29) than placebo, but no more serious AEs [PMID:30734066].
  - Vitamin K in dialysis patients was safe and did not change mortality [PMID:38046003]. VitaK-CAC reported no significant adverse effects at 360 mcg MK-7 [PMID:42268593].
- Main hazard: the combined K1 1500 mcg + MK-4 5 mg + MK-7 600 mcg is a large vitamin K load. MK-7 at 10-45 mcg/d already lowers INR during VKA therapy [PMID:23530987] (see Interactions).
- Carotenodermia (skin yellowing) is possible with high carotenoid intake. In one trial, 13 mg beta-carotene plus 2 mg lycopene caused detectable carotenodermia, but 3 mg plus 3 mg did not [PMID:9285071]. This product contains 39 mg of total carotenoids; no product-specific data exist.
- Bleeding: one case report describes INR rising from 1.4 to 10.38 with ecchymosis after astaxanthin was added to warfarin, judged a probable reaction [PMID:31073352]. Rodent data show anticoagulant effects of astaxanthin [PMID:32725659] [PMID:41702374]. In this product, the vitamin K content pushes in the opposite direction, so the net INR effect is unpredictable.

** Contraindications
- Vitamin K antagonist therapy (warfarin, acenocoumarol, phenprocoumon): the vitamin K content is many times the 10-45 mcg/d MK-7 doses that clinically lowered INR [PMID:23530987]. The authors concluded MK-7 supplements should be avoided with VKA therapy. The warfarin label lists increased vitamin K intake as a cause of decreased INR response [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=541c9a70-adaf-4ef3-94ba-ad4e70dfa057].
- Children and adolescents: the EFSA astaxanthin assessment found the ADI reached or exceeded in under-18 age groups at 8 mg/day [PMID:32874213]. Product dose is 12 mg.
- Pregnancy/lactation: no cited human safety data for this combination at these doses.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin and other VKAs (major): vitamin K competitively antagonizes VKAs [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=541c9a70-adaf-4ef3-94ba-ad4e70dfa057].
  - MK-7 at 45 mcg/d lowered INR by about 40% in acenocoumarol-treated volunteers. 10-20 mcg caused clinically relevant INR lowering in 40-60% [PMID:23530987]. MK-7 >=50 mcg/d is flagged as clinically relevant [PMID:17158229].
  - Low-dose K (150 mcg) is used deliberately in unstable warfarin patients, but only under supervision with dose titration [PMID:17110451] [PMID:24832594].
  - Starting or stopping the product therefore changes the INR. Astaxanthin may add an opposite, unpredictable effect [PMID:31073352].
- Orlistat (minor): reduces absorption of fat-soluble vitamins and beta-carotene. The label advises separating vitamins by >=2 h [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6240792b-9224-2d10-e053-2a91aa0a2c3e]. Plasma hydrocarbon carotenoids fell on orlistat [PMID:17707976], and vitamin K showed a nonsignificant decrease [PMID:12126214].
- Antiplatelet drugs/aspirin (minor, theoretical): astaxanthin inhibited platelet activation comparably to aspirin in rodent thrombosis models [PMID:41702374]. No human interaction data exist.
- Drugs metabolized by CYP450: interaction unlikely given weak in vitro inhibition [PMID:23669408].
- Direct oral anticoagulants: food-drug interaction evidence for DOACs is limited [PMID:39288907]. No study of vitamin K supplements with DOACs was found.
*** Other supplements
- K1, K2 MK-7, K2-MK4: duplicates the vitamin K content. This adds to the VKA hazard [PMID:23530987] [PMID:17158229]. Stacking with high-dose MK-4 (45 mg) adds to its adverse-event rate [PMID:30734066].
- Zeaxanthin + Lutein, Lycopene, Astaxanthin: duplicate carotenoid doses. Stacking can push lutein above its 20 mg/d Observed Safe Level [PMID:16814439] and astaxanthin above EFSA's 8 mg/day supplement level [PMID:32874213].
- Vitamin E: 1000 IU/d raised PIVKA-II (an under-carboxylated prothrombin marker), suggesting antagonism of vitamin K [PMID:15213041]. This may partly offset the product's vitamin K effect.
- Vitamin D-3: K2 plus D3 was the combination tested in AVADEC and DANCODE [PMID:35465686] [PMID:42661461]. The effect of K2 alone cannot be separated.
- Plant sterols/stanols (2.5 g/d): lower plasma lycopene (-12.3%), zeaxanthin (-12.9%) and lutein (-7.4%) [PMID:27591863].
*** Conditions
- Current or former smokers: the product contains no beta-carotene. Lutein/zeaxanthin did not significantly raise lung cancer risk, unlike beta-carotene [PMID:35653117] [PMID:23644932].
- Prostate cancer under PSA monitoring: lycopene does not change PSA overall [PMID:33355018] [PMID:35031434].
- CKD/dialysis: vitamin K improved status markers but did not reduce mortality or calcification scores [PMID:38046003] [PMID:42851346].
- VKA-treated conditions (AF, VTE, mechanical valves): see Contraindications.
*** Lab-test interference
- INR/PT: decreased in VKA-treated users [PMID:23530987]. No artifact in non-anticoagulated people.
- PIVKA-II/DCP (des-gamma-carboxy prothrombin), used as a hepatocellular carcinoma tumour marker: vitamin K (MK-4) lowers DCP [PMID:1309308]. Supplementation could mask an elevated DCP during HCC surveillance. Interpret DCP with knowledge of vitamin K intake [PMID:15213041].
- Vitamin K status markers: vitamin K lowers dp-ucMGP (-44.7%) and ucOC (-12%) [PMID:30514729] and the ucOC fraction [PMID:39657786]. These reflect intake, not disease change.

** Genotype notes
- AREDS supplement benefit did not vary by CFH/ARMS2 genotype in AREDS report 38 [PMID:24974817]. Note that AREDS tested beta-carotene formulas, not lutein.
- MPOD response to lutein/zeaxanthin was associated with SCARB1, RPE65, ABCA1 and FADS1 variants in a candidate-gene study (n=310), which is unreplicated [PMID:23891863].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] DANCODE RCT (n=398, CAC >=400): K2 720 mcg + D3 slowed CAC progression by 52 AU over 24 mo [PMID:42661461].
- [2026-08] VitaK-CAC RCT: MK-7 360 mcg for 2 y slowed CAC progression in symptomatic CAD (P=.02) [PMID:42268593].
- [2026-08] Lycopene MA (4 studies): FMD +1.36% [PMID:42588128].
- [2026-08] Review: vitamin K is pharmacodynamically active but has not reduced cardiovascular events or mortality [PMID:42851346].
- [2026-06] Teen RCT (n=82): lutein 10 mg/zeaxanthin 2 mg raised MPOD and some attention/processing-speed scores [PMID:42413424].
- [2026-05] Astaxanthin exercise MA (24 RCTs): CK SMD -0.45; no performance effect [PMID:42197030].
- [2026-03] Observational MA: high circulating lycopene associated with lower carotid IMT (OR 0.70) [PMID:41978094].
- [2026-02] Astaxanthin antithrombotic effects in rodents and human platelets [PMID:41702374].
- [2026-02] Myopia MA (10 RCTs): lutein/zeaxanthin reduced axial elongation (SMD -0.40) [PMID:41573009].
- [2025-12] Carotenoid MA: no overall change in liver enzymes [PMID:41437050].
- [2025-11] Vitamin K2 MA (9 RCTs): ucOC down, OC up in postmenopausal osteoporosis [PMID:41268154].
- [2025-10] AVADEC substudy: K2+D3 lowered dp-ucMGP but did not change epicardial fat or inflammation [PMID:41100911].
- [2025-07] Astaxanthin 6-20 mg/d lipid MA: HDL up, TG down [PMID:40872489].
- [2025-02] Lute-gen RCT (n=70): improved tear tests and photostress recovery [PMID:39963662].
- [2025-02] MK-7 180 mcg for 1 y attenuated stiffness in postmenopausal women [PMID:40077685].
- [2025-02] Cohort MA: high lycopene intake/levels associated with lower overall cancer risk (RR 0.95/0.89) [PMID:40013157].
- [2024-12] Vitamin K MA: lumbar spine BMD up [PMID:39657786].
- [2024-12] IV K1 RCT in hemodialysis: CAC progression 20.8% vs 44% [PMID:39680321].
- [2024-07] Post hoc AREDS/AREDS2: lutein/zeaxanthin slowed GA growth toward the fovea [PMID:39025435].

** Open questions
- No trial or pharmacokinetic study exists for this exact 7-ingredient formula. Ingredient interactions (e.g. carotenoid absorption competition) at these doses are unknown.
- Whether the lutein/zeaxanthin dose changed (15/3 mg softgel vs 10/2 mg capsule) despite the manufacturer saying only delivery changed.
- Long-term safety of 12 mg astaxanthin, above the EFSA supplement level, combined with about 7 mg of total vitamin K.
- Whether vitamin K slows vascular calcification enough to reduce clinical events. Trial results are mixed and no event benefit has been shown.
- Benefit in people without AMD risk. AREDS2 applies to intermediate AMD, not healthy adults.
