** Identity & forms
- Extra-virgin olive oil is a food fat from Olea europaea fruit. It is mostly oleic acid (MUFA), plus minor phenolics (hydroxytyrosol, tyrosol, oleuropein/ligstroside aglycones, oleocanthal, oleacein), tocopherols, sterols and triterpenes [PMID:35368142] [PMID:41429309].
- Phenolic content varies widely by cultivar and ripening stage. EFSA authorised a health claim for olive oil providing at least 250 mg/kg of hydroxytyrosol and derivatives. Several Spanish varieties reached 278-430 mg/kg, but not all did [PMID:33060000].
  - The claim requires 5 mg hydroxytyrosol and derivatives per 20 g of oil (LDL protection from oxidative damage) [PMID:40507112].
  - The claim applies only when hydroxytyrosol is consumed within EVOO, not as an isolated supplement [PMID:41010464].
- Common (refined/"pure") olive oil contains little phenolic material. In PREDIMED, common olive oil showed weaker CVD associations than EVOO [PMID:40907633].
- Forms tested in trials: EVOO as food (25-50 mL/d), high- vs low-phenolic EVOO [PMID:16954359], oleanolic-acid-enriched oil [PMID:42608317], and capsule extracts. Extracts include a 10 mg/d oleocanthal/oleacein extract [PMID:41429309] and 15 mg/d hydroxytyrosol [PMID:40690822].
- Authenticity: EVOO is a frequent fraud target (dilution with cheaper oils). Adulterated oil is nutritionally poorer and more readily oxidised. Stigmastadienes and the sterol profile are proposed markers [PMID:35368142].

** Mechanisms
- LDL oxidation: phenolic content dose-dependently lowered oxidised LDL and raised HDL in the EUROLIVE crossover (n=200, 25 mL/d) [PMID:16954359]. High-polyphenol oil lowered apoB-100 and small LDL particle number [PMID:26136585].
- Anti-inflammatory/antioxidant: high-phenolic EVOO lowered CRP and ox-LDL vs low-phenolic oils in a meta-analysis of 23 RCTs [PMID:40749711]. Antioxidant response depended on clinical status and baseline diet [PMID:42521082].
- Incretin/postprandial: EVOO with a meal lowered glucose and DPP-4 activity and raised GLP-1 and insulin in impaired fasting glucose [PMID:27289163]. Oleuropein 20 mg lowered postprandial glycaemia via reduced Nox2 oxidative stress [PMID:29577365]. In type 1 diabetes, EVOO slowed gastric emptying and raised GLP-1 [PMID:30567626].
- Platelets: olive oil phenol extracts inhibited human platelet aggregation in vitro via cAMP-phosphodiesterase inhibition [PMID:17927845]. Olive oil intake was independently associated with lower urinary 11-dehydro-TxB2 in anticoagulated AF patients [PMID:25288566].
- Endothelial function: 4 weeks of EVOO improved NIRS-measured endothelial function and lowered diastolic BP in hyperlipidaemia [PMID:41373940].
- Gut microbiota: virgin olive oil intake was associated with greater microbial diversity, and Adlercreutzia was a candidate mediator of cognitive associations (cohort) [PMID:41578342].
- Fat-soluble nutrient absorption: olive oil raises lycopene/carotenoid absorption [PMID:15927929] [PMID:36921903].

** Dosing in trials
- PREDIMED: about 1 L/week of EVOO per household plus MedDiet education, versus low-fat advice [PMID:29897866] [PMID:25961184]. The top tertile of cumulative intake averaged 49 g/d [PMID:40907633].
- PREDIMED-Plus tests an energy-reduced MedDiet plus physical activity. It cut weight by 2.5 kg vs control at 1 year (n=626) [PMID:30389673] and reduced total and visceral fat at 3 years [PMID:37851444]. It is not a test of EVOO alone.
- Biomarker RCTs: 25 mL/d for 3 weeks (EUROLIVE) [PMID:16954359]; 30 mL/d for 12 weeks (sarcopenia) [PMID:41787835]; 40 g/d for 8 weeks [PMID:41520410]; 3 tablespoons/d in gestational diabetes [PMID:41978170].
- Glycaemic dose-response: 25-50 g/d improved HOMA-IR [PMID:40971936]. For diabetes risk, a nonlinear benefit appeared at 10-20 g/d [PMID:40275388].
- Cohort intake: benefit plateaued around 18-22 g/d for CVD and all-cause mortality [PMID:38568797]. The top category in NHS/HPFS was >7 g/d [PMID:35027106].
- Isolated phenolics: hydroxytyrosol 15 mg/d for 16 weeks [PMID:40690822]; 45 mg/d for 8 weeks [PMID:28063380]; olive oil aldehydic phenols 10 mg/d for 12 weeks [PMID:41429309].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Food-level intake is well tolerated. In PREDIMED, an ad libitum high-fat MedDiet did not increase body weight over about 5 years [PMID:31003626]. Calories still count: at 9 kcal/g, 40-50 g/d is about 360-450 kcal, so added (not substituted) oil raises energy intake.
- One small RCT (n=27) found that 40 g/d raised LDL-C by 5.1 mg/dL vs sunflower oil [PMID:41520410]. Large MAs show no adverse lipid effect [PMID:37977313].
- Isolated hydroxytyrosol:
  - 45 mg/d for 8 weeks did not change liver, kidney or electrolyte markers, but lowered ferritin and folate [PMID:28063380].
  - A 20% HT olive extract was non-genotoxic and judged safe up to 2.5 mg/kg/day [PMID:36852231].
  - No adverse events with 15 mg/d for 16 weeks [PMID:40690822].
- Adulteration and quality: fraudulent dilution and oxidised/substandard oil can carry unhealthy processing by-products and lack phenolics [PMID:35368142].
- Aspiration: exogenous lipoid pneumonia after chronic olive oil aspiration (case report; resolved after stopping) [PMID:42309686].
- Allergy: olive fruit allergy is rare (pollen-food olive-olive syndrome case). Oral olive oil challenge did not provoke symptoms in that case [PMID:20128426].
- Gallbladder: 50 mL EVOO with a protein bar is used as a cholecystokinetic test meal and caused peak gallbladder contraction at 50-70 min. Only mild GI events occurred [PMID:42669445].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindication at food intakes was found in the cited literature.
- Known olive fruit allergy: avoid olive products. Oil itself may be tolerated [PMID:20128426].
- Aspiration risk or habitual vomiting: oral oil boluses can cause lipoid pneumonia [PMID:42309686].
- Symptomatic gallstones: oil is a potent gallbladder-contraction stimulus [PMID:42669445]. This is a mechanistic precaution; no cited outcome data on biliary colic.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Antihypertensives: an EVOO-rich MedDiet lowered DBP by 1.53 mmHg [PMID:24050803]. Phenolic olive oils lowered SBP by about 3 mmHg vs refined oil [PMID:31378629]. An olive phenol extract lowered SBP by 7.66 mmHg in metabolic syndrome [PMID:41429309]. Any additive effect is small.
- Antidiabetic agents/insulin: EVOO lowers postprandial glucose [PMID:27289163] [PMID:30567626]. In gestational diabetes it reduced the need for insulin (RR 0.595) [PMID:41978170]. An olive phenol extract lowered FBG by 7 mg/dL [PMID:41429309]. Glucose should be checked when intake changes; no hypoglycaemia reports were found.
- Metformin: no direct study. The same additive glucose-lowering considerations apply [PMID:41429309] [PMID:41978170].
- Vitamin K antagonists (warfarin/acenocoumarol):
  - Higher MedDiet adherence (90% olive oil users) did not change INR time in therapeutic range in AF patients [PMID:25995397].
  - Olive oil intake was associated with lower thromboxane production in VKA users [PMID:25288566].
  - In PREDIMED, MedDiet+EVOO lowered the rate of VKA initiation (HR 0.68) [PMID:33352771].
  - No cited case of warfarin potentiation.
- Aspirin and antiplatelet drugs: olive phenols inhibit platelet aggregation in vitro [PMID:17927845]. Olive oil and antiplatelet drugs were independent predictors of lower TxB2 [PMID:25288566]. Additive bleeding risk is theoretical; no bleeding signal appeared in the AF cohort.
*** Other supplements
- EPA/DHA/DPA: in rheumatoid arthritis, fish oil 3 g/d plus olive oil 9.6 mL gave earlier and greater improvement than fish oil alone [PMID:15723739].
- Lycopene: cooking tomatoes with 25 mL EVOO raised plasma trans-lycopene by 82% [PMID:15927929]. Olive oil raised lycopene isomers [PMID:25172701] and lycopene iAUC by 46% vs coconut oil [PMID:36921903].
- Zeaxanthin + Lutein: olive oil gave higher total carotenoid bioaccessibility than coconut oil [PMID:36921903]. In rat/in vitro models, butter gave better lutein/zeaxanthin bioavailability than olive oil [PMID:23228631].
- Curcumin: olive-oil-based excipient emulsions gave the greatest curcumin transport and protection from metabolism in a Caco-2 model (in vitro only) [PMID:34628238].
- Vitamin D-3: olive oil pinoresinol reduced vitamin D absorption by 25% in rats and in Caco-2 cells [PMID:27041321]. Human relevance is unknown.
- Inulin/prebiotics: EVOO plus FOS/inulin increased rectus femoris muscle size more than EVOO alone in probable sarcopenia (n=38) [PMID:41787835].
*** Conditions
- Gestational diabetes: EVOO lowered triglycerides by 43 mg/dL, insulin need, and neonatal complications [PMID:41978170].
- Chronic kidney disease: a MedDiet with high-phenolic EVOO lowered CRP and gave a modest eGFR benefit (MA, high heterogeneity) [PMID:41847236]. Oleanolic-acid-enriched oil slowed eGFR decline in T2D with mildly reduced eGFR [PMID:42608317].
- Weight-management plans: energy density is relevant, though PREDIMED showed no weight gain [PMID:31003626].
- Allergy, aspiration risk, gallstones: see Contraindications.
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. EVOO causes true physiological shifts: ox-LDL, CRP [PMID:40749711], postprandial glucose/insulin and triglycerides [PMID:27289163]. Olive oil is itself used as the test meal for ultrasound gallbladder-contraction testing [PMID:42669445].

** Genotype notes
- TCF7L2 rs7903146 (PREDIMED): TT carriers had higher stroke incidence on the control diet (HR 2.91). MedDiet (EVOO or nuts) cancelled this excess (HR 0.96) and the TT-linked rises in glucose and lipids [PMID:23942586].
- APOE e4: the olive oil-dementia death association was unchanged after adjusting for APOE e4 [PMID:38709531].

- [2026-03] OLIDIAG RCT (n=190, GDM): EVOO cut insulin need (RR 0.595), triglycerides and NICU admission [PMID:41978170].
- [2026-03] CKD MA (10 studies): high-phenolic EVOO MedDiet lowered CRP by 0.79 mg/L [PMID:41847236].

- [2026-02] Glycaemic MA (51 RCTs, n=4334): no overall effect on glucose/HbA1c; HOMA-IR benefit at 25-50 g/d [PMID:40971936].

- [2026-01] PREDIMED outcome-wide analysis: highest EVOO tertile (49 g/d) HR 0.75 for composite CVD; common olive oil not associated [PMID:40907633].
- [2026-01] Cohort (n=656): virgin olive oil linked to cognitive preservation, possibly via gut microbiota [PMID:41578342].
- [2025-11] Endothelial function improved with EVOO in hyperlipidaemia (post hoc RCT) [PMID:41373940].
- [2025-11] MA: MedDiet+olive oil vs MedDiet+nuts gave similar lipids/DBP; SBP slightly higher with olive oil [PMID:40651889].
- [2025-09] Hydroxytyrosol 15 mg/d RCT in prediabetes (n=49): lower ox-LDL and IL-6 [PMID:40690822].

- [2024-05] NHS/HPFS: >=7 g/d olive oil associated with 28% lower dementia-related death [PMID:38709531].
- [2024-05] Cohort dose-response MA (30 articles): all-cause mortality RR 0.85; benefit plateau at about 20 g/d [PMID:38568797].

** Open questions
- How much of the PREDIMED/CORDIOPREV benefit comes from EVOO itself vs the whole MedDiet? No trial isolates the oil against an isocaloric fat control on hard endpoints.
- Whether the PREDIMED-Plus hard-endpoint results (energy-reduced MedDiet plus exercise) will show added benefit beyond weight loss.
- Clinically meaningful phenolic dose: the EFSA threshold is set for LDL oxidation; dose-response data for events are lacking.
- Breast cancer: the RCT signal rests on 35 cases, and prospective cohorts are inconsistent.
- Whether isolated hydroxytyrosol or olive extracts replicate EVOO effects, since the authorised claim applies only to the oil matrix.
- Interactions with anticoagulants and antiplatelets are based on mechanism and observational data only.
- How common adulteration is in retail EVOO, and its health impact.
