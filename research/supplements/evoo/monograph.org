** Identity & forms
- Extra-virgin olive oil is mechanically extracted olive oil with low free acidity and sensory/chemical standards; it contains mostly oleic acid plus variable phenolic compounds. It is a food rather than a single-ingredient pharmacologic supplement [DOI:10.1016/j.tjnut.2023.10.028].

** Mechanisms
- Oleic acid is the predominant monounsaturated fatty acid; EVOO phenolics have antioxidant and signaling activity in experimental work. Cardiovascular outcome data chiefly test dietary patterns and fat substitution, not phenolics or isolated oil alone [PMID:29897866; PMID:37977313].

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---------+--------------------+-----------+-------------+-----------------------+-----------|
| Cardiovascular events in Mediterranean dietary pattern | Large RCT (PREDIMED) | Lower composite events in supplemented Mediterranean diet | EVOO arm ~1 L/week within whole dietary pattern; cannot isolate oil effect | moderate | [PMID:29897866] |
| Cardiometabolic markers | SR/MA of RCTs | Small/inconsistent | variable BP, lipids and inflammatory marker effects | low | [PMID:37977313] |
| Weight/adiposity | RCT meta-analysis | No reliable weight-loss effect when energy intake comparable | calorie substitution matters | low | [PMID:37977313] |

** Dosing in trials
- PREDIMED supplied 1 L/week per household for a Mediterranean dietary intervention; this was not an isolated EVOO pill trial [PMID:29897866]. Trials of EVOO as a food use roughly 25–50 mL/day, often substituted for other fats; caloric displacement is central [PMID:37977313].

** Safety & adverse effects
- EVOO is generally consumed as food; energy-dense intake can increase total calories if added rather than substituted. Allergy is uncommon but possible. Heat, light and oxygen degrade phenolics over time; clinical significance depends on storage and product quality [PMID:37977313].

** Contraindications
- Avoid in known olive allergy. No general contraindication at food amounts established; high intake may be unsuitable when energy restriction is required [PMID:37977313].

** Interactions
- No important drug interaction at culinary amounts established. A high-fat meal can affect absorption of some medicines, but this is not a unique EVOO interaction. EVOO is not known to interfere with routine laboratory assays [PMID:37977313].

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source specific to EVOO was identified in this search; no genotype claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized trials evaluated EVOO effects on anthropometric, inflammatory and cardiometabolic outcomes and found outcome-specific, mixed results rather than uniform benefit [PMID:37977313]. PREDIMED remains key event-level evidence for the whole Mediterranean dietary pattern with EVOO, not proof of isolated oil efficacy [PMID:29897866].

** Open questions
- Larger, better reported human studies with standardized products, clinically meaningful endpoints and longer follow-up are needed; current gaps are noted above.
