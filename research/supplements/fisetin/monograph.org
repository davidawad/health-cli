** Identity & forms
- Fisetin (3,3',4',7-tetrahydroxyflavone) is a dietary flavonol found in strawberry, apple, persimmon, grape, onion and cucumber [PMID:23121441] [PMID:38716552]. It is sold as an OTC supplement and is being tested as an investigational senolytic in academic trials [PMID:39384074] [PMID:39434114].
- Unformulated fisetin has poor water solubility, high lipophilicity and low oral bioavailability. Many nano- and macro-formulations have been made to overcome this [PMID:38716552] [PMID:37762460].
- Human pharmacokinetics (the only published human PK study; randomized crossover, n=15) [PMID:36304817]:
  - 1000 mg unformulated fisetin: Cmax 9.97 ng/mL, AUC0-12h 12.67.
  - 1000 mg FF-20, a fenugreek-galactomannan hydrogel (Hybrid-FENUMAT) delivering 192 mg fisetin: Cmax 238.2 ng/mL and 26.9-fold higher AUC0-12h (341.4). The formulation also reduced conversion to the methylated metabolite geraldol. No adverse events were reported.
- Metabolism (animal data):
  - Mice (223 mg/kg i.p.): Cmax 2.5 ug/mL at 15 min, terminal half-life 3.1 h. Metabolites were glucuronides and geraldol (3'-O-methyl fisetin), which was more cytotoxic than fisetin and reached higher levels than fisetin in tumors [PMID:21840301].
  - Rats: plasma AUC ratio of free fisetin to glucuronides to sulfates was 1:6:21. Sulfates dominate biliary excretion, and biliary excretion of fisetin is P-glycoprotein-mediated [PMID:29862816].
- One botanical source used in trials is Rhus succedanea extract [PMID:33802381]. A trial comparing fisetin kinetics in young versus older adults (fasted and fed; FISEKIN-1) is registered but has not yet reported [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06796374].

** Mechanisms
- Senolytic activity: fisetin selectively induced apoptosis in senescent human endothelial cells (HUVECs), but not in senescent IMR90 lung fibroblasts or preadipocytes, so its effect depends on cell type [PMID:28273655].
- Among 10 flavonoids screened, fisetin was the most potent senolytic [PMID:30279143]:
  - Acute or intermittent dosing reduced senescence markers in several tissues of progeroid and old mice.
  - Late-life dosing extended median and maximum lifespan in wild-type mice.
  - Fisetin reduced senescent cells in human adipose tissue explants.
- In the NIA Interventions Testing Program (genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice), dietary fisetin did not significantly extend lifespan in either sex at the dose and schedule used [PMID:38041783]. This is the most important replication failure for the lifespan claim.
- "Hit-and-run" rationale: senescent cells take weeks to reaccumulate, so senolytics can be given intermittently [PMID:32686219] [PMID:35953721].
- Vascular effects (mouse data):
  - Intermittent oral fisetin (1 week on, 2 off, 1 on) lowered vascular senescence and SASP and improved endothelial function and aortic stiffness in old mice. Genetic senolysis experiments showed the benefit worked through senescent-cell removal [PMID:38062873].
  - The same schedule (100 mg/kg/day) reversed doxorubicin-induced endothelial dysfunction and aortic stiffening in young mice [PMID:42144546].
- Other mechanisms:
  - Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant signalling through PI3K/Akt/mTOR, NF-kB and MAPK [PMID:40074915] [PMID:41538057].
  - Microtubule stabilization [PMID:27765854].
  - Neuroprotective effects across multiple dementia risk factors in animals [PMID:39004102].
  - Many responses are hormetic (biphasic dose-response), which complicates dose selection [PMID:40208387].
- Antiplatelet activity:
  - In rabbit platelets, fisetin inhibited arachidonic-acid-induced aggregation (IC50 22 uM) by blocking thromboxane formation and antagonizing the thromboxane receptor [PMID:1776142].
  - Oral fisetin (100 mg/kg/day for 7 days) protected rats against arterial thrombosis [PMID:25372471].

** Dosing in trials
- Intermittent "senolytic" dosing of about 20 mg/kg/day for 2 consecutive days (roughly 1.4 g/day for a 70 kg adult):
  - AFFIRM-LITE: once [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03675724].
  - AFFIRM (older women): repeated in 2 consecutive months [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03430037].
  - Knee OA: 2 days on, 28 days off, 2 days on [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04210986].
  - COVID-19: days 0-1 and 8-9 [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04771611].
- STOP-Sepsis (planned n=220, age >65): a single oral 20 mg/kg dose or two doses 1 day apart, versus placebo [PMID:39434114].
- TROFFi (breast cancer survivors after chemotherapy, n=88): 20 mg/kg/day on days 1-3 of a 14-day cycle for 4 cycles. Primary endpoint is 6-minute walk distance [PMID:41835341].
- Low daily dosing:
  - 100 mg/day for 7 weeks alongside chemotherapy [PMID:29541713].
  - 100 mg/day for 7 weeks (Danish suPAR trial protocol, age >=50) [PMID:42633989].
  - 100 mg/day for 3 weeks (REPROGRAM, age 70+, versus metformin or spermidine) [PMID:42308222].
  - 200 mg/day for 12 weeks in obese men [PMID:42218768] [PMID:41683255].
- Carpal tunnel syndrome: 100 mg on 2 consecutive days, repeated after 1 month (single-arm, n=40) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05416515].
- Single 1000 mg doses (unformulated or FF-20) in the PK study [PMID:36304817].
- No dose has been validated for any clinical outcome. Reviews call for dose-finding, PK and safety studies [PMID:39384074] [PMID:34699859].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Human safety data are limited to short exposures in small trials:
  - Single-dose PK study: no adverse events [PMID:36304817].
  - Knee-OA RCT (20 mg/kg intermittent): any treatment-emergent AE 28/34 on fisetin vs 33/40 on placebo (p>0.9); SAEs 2/34 vs 2/40 [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04210986].
  - COVFIS-HOME: SAEs 0/29 vs 2/26 [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04771611].
  - Carpal tunnel (single-arm): no SAEs; 15/40 had other AEs [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05416515].
- Long-term safety, pharmacokinetics and optimal dosing in humans are not established [PMID:39384074]. A 2026 review flags genotoxicity at relatively high concentrations (in vitro) [PMID:41538057].
- Senolytic class (indirect): in the D+Q IPF RCT, sleep disturbance and anxiety occurred more often with D+Q (4/6 vs 0/6). Most AEs were attributed to dasatinib or to IPF [PMID:36857968]. These data do not transfer directly to fisetin monotherapy.
- Related flavonol quercetin (indirect): long-term (>12 weeks) high-dose safety is not established. Animal data raise concerns about worsening a damaged kidney and promoting estrogen-dependent tumors [PMID:29127724].

** Contraindications
- No formal contraindications have been established. Fisetin is not an approved drug, and evidence is too thin to define risk groups [PMID:39384074].
- Cautions based on mechanism and preclinical data:
  - Active chemotherapy (CYP2C8 inhibition, chemosensitization) [PMID:29454704] [PMID:40074915].
  - Bleeding disorders or upcoming surgery (antiplatelet activity) [PMID:1776142] [PMID:25372471].
  - Pregnancy and lactation: no human data were found [PMID:39384074].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- CYP2C8 substrates (paclitaxel, among others): in human liver microsomes, fisetin selectively and reversibly inhibited CYP2C8 (noncompetitive; Ki 4.1 uM). Geraldol also inhibited it (Ki 11.5 uM). No human interaction study exists [PMID:29454704].
- CYP2C9 substrates (warfarin, NSAIDs): fisetin competitively inhibited CYP2C9 (diclofenac 4'-hydroxylation) with Ki <=2.2 uM in a recombinant system [PMID:19074529]. Together with antiplatelet activity [PMID:1776142], this means warfarin users should have their INR monitored. Clinical data are lacking.
- Antiplatelet agents and anticoagulants: fisetin has in vitro and rat antithrombotic effects [PMID:1776142] [PMID:25372471], so additive bleeding risk is plausible but not quantified. Perioperative guidance for antithrombotics also addresses supplements [PMID:38735483].
- Thrombolytics (rt-PA): one RCT gave fisetin with rt-PA and reported benefit. Bleeding outcomes are not reported in the abstract [PMID:31434498].
- Taxanes (paclitaxel, cabazitaxel): fisetin was synergistic with paclitaxel in A549 cells and enhanced cabazitaxel in prostate cancer cells and xenografts [PMID:26884726] [PMID:27765854]. CYP2C8 inhibition could raise paclitaxel exposure [PMID:29454704].
- Anthracyclines (doxorubicin): synergistic lymphoma killing in vitro and in mice [PMID:39929420]. Fisetin also protected against doxorubicin vascular toxicity in mice [PMID:42144546]. Human data are absent.
- Platinum agents (cisplatin): fisetin sensitized renal carcinoma cells to cisplatin [PMID:38426151] and reduced cisplatin neuropathy in rats [PMID:40167901].
- Capecitabine: 100 mg/day fisetin was given alongside chemotherapy in a CRC RCT [PMID:29541713]. In cells it had additive pro-apoptotic effects, including in capecitabine-resistant cells [PMID:38748229].
- OAT1 substrates: fisetin completely inhibited OAT1-mediated uptake at 50 uM and is itself an OAT1 substrate [PMID:25002746].
- P-glycoprotein substrates: fisetin is a weak P-gp inhibitor in KB-C2 cells [PMID:16327165]. It did not reverse daunomycin resistance in MCF-7/ADR cells [PMID:16114498]. Its own biliary excretion is P-gp-mediated [PMID:29862816].
- Antidiabetic drugs: FBS, insulin and HOMA-IR fell with 200 mg/day in obese men [PMID:42218768]. A minor additive effect is possible.
*** Other supplements
- Quercetin: a related flavonol with overlapping CYP2C9 inhibition [PMID:19074529] and antiplatelet activity [PMID:1776142]. It also has its own safety and drug-interaction concerns at high doses [PMID:29127724]. Stacking two senolytic flavonols has not been studied.
- EPA/DHA/DPA: n-3 fatty acids affect haemostasis but did not increase clinically significant bleeding [PMID:24472372]. Combining them with fisetin's antiplatelet effect [PMID:1776142] is untested and probably minor.
- Garlic, Ginger Root: perioperative antithrombotic reviews flag these supplements [PMID:38735483]. Stacking them with fisetin [PMID:25372471] adds theoretical antiplatelet load.
- Metformin ER, Spermidine: compared as alternative geroprotectors in REPROGRAM, not combined, so there are no interaction data [PMID:42308222].
- Dasatinib + quercetin is a separate senolytic regimen. Trials of it inform the class rationale but are indirect evidence for fisetin [PMID:31542391] [PMID:37679434].
*** Conditions
- Cancer under active treatment: preclinical chemosensitization and CYP2C8 inhibition cut both ways. Fisetin should not be added to chemotherapy outside trials [PMID:40074915] [PMID:29454704].
- Bleeding disorders and perioperative periods: see antiplatelet data [PMID:1776142] [PMID:25372471].
- Pregnancy and lactation: no human data [PMID:39384074].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference was found. Trials report real changes in inflammatory markers (IL-8, IL-6, TNF-alpha), FBS, insulin and HOMA-IR [PMID:29541713] [PMID:42218768]. Warfarin users should have their INR monitored, given CYP2C9 inhibition [PMID:19074529]. Senescence biomarkers such as T-cell p16 variants are still being developed and validated [PMID:39823170].

** Open questions
- Does fisetin clear senescent cells in humans at the doses used? No peer-reviewed human senescence-burden result exists for fisetin. Results from AFFIRM/AFFIRM-LITE (started 2018) remain unpublished. A 2024 systematic review of dietary senotherapeutics found only 5 human studies in total [PMID:38382678].
- The lifespan signal conflicts: extension in one lab versus a null ITP result. Dose, formulation and genetic background may explain the difference.
- What tissue exposures do intermittent ~20 mg/kg doses reach, given very low unformulated bioavailability? How do formulations, food and age change this (FISEKIN-1 pending)?
- Are the CYP2C8, CYP2C9 and OAT1 inhibition shown in vitro clinically relevant at supplement doses? This matters especially with paclitaxel and warfarin.
- Is it safe to add fisetin to chemotherapy, given preclinical sensitization of both tumor and normal tissue effects?
- The positive human RCTs are small, single-centre and from a few groups. They need independent replication with preregistered outcomes.
