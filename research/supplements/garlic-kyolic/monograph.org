** Identity & forms
- Aged garlic extract (AGE) is made by aging raw garlic (Allium sativum L.) in an alcoholic solution for more than 10 months [PMID:40171136] [PMID:39882336]. Kyolic is the branded AGE used in many of the trials [PMID:30619868] [PMID:11053504].
- AGE is rich in sulfur-containing amino acids, which have pharmacokinetic profiles different from allicin-type garlic compounds:
  - S-allylcysteine (SAC), the usual standardization and compliance marker.
  - S-1-propenylcysteine (S1PC), S-methylcysteine (SMC) and S-allylmercaptocysteine (SAMC) [PMID:40171136] [PMID:40171135].
- Pharmacokinetics (mostly rat data): SAC, S1PC and SMC are well absorbed (bioavailability 88.0-95.8%) and excreted in urine in N-acetylated form. SAMC is not detected in plasma [PMID:40171136]. Plasma SAC was used as a compliance marker in a coronary-calcium trial [PMID:15475033].
- Forms used in trials:
  - Capsules/tablets of AGE powder: 1.2 g/d (1.2 mg SAC) and 2.4 g/d (2.4 mg SAC) [PMID:26869811] [PMID:40084199].
  - Liquid Kyolic: 4-10 mL/d [PMID:15475033] [PMID:16484565] [PMID:11053504].
  - Fixed combinations: AGE plus B vitamins, folic acid and L-arginine [PMID:19573556], or AGE plus CoQ10 [PMID:22923934].
- "Aged black garlic" extracts are a different product class: a low-dose SAC (0.25 mg/d) black-garlic extract was tested in grade I hypertension [PMID:37686723]. Evidence for AGE should not be assumed to transfer to raw garlic, garlic powder or garlic oil, or the reverse [PMID:32143889].

** Mechanisms
- Blood pressure / vascular tone: mechanisms proposed in reviews include more nitric oxide and hydrogen sulfide signalling and less vascular inflammation [PMID:32010325] [PMID:40171135]. An optimized black-garlic SAC extract raised blood nitric oxide and lowered ACE activity [PMID:37686723]. A single dose of AGE improved microvascular reactivity without a rise in urinary thiosulfate (H2S marker), which argues against an H2S-only mechanism [PMID:35505122].
- Anti-atherosclerotic: in preclinical work, SAC/S1PC reduce oxidized-LDL accumulation, endothelial dysfunction and macrophage-driven inflammation [PMID:40171135]. AGE modulates macrophage phagocytosis, ROS production and polarization (experimental data) [PMID:39882336].
- Platelets: in humans, AGE inhibits ADP-induced aggregation, roughly doubling the ADP K(M), with no change in thromboxane B2 [PMID:11053504]. It also selectively inhibits platelet adhesion to fibrinogen and collagen in a dose-dependent way [PMID:11238801].
- Immune: AGE increases proliferation of gamma-delta T cells and NK cells [PMID:22280901] [PMID:26764332] and lowers IL-6 and TNF-alpha in obesity [PMID:29576354]. In a mouse lung-injury model, AGE inhibited NF-kB-dependent cytokines [PMID:42300751].
- Lipids: reviews describe less de novo cholesterol synthesis and absorption, more LDL uptake, better HDL biogenesis, and gut-microbiota/TMAO effects [PMID:41273868].
- Gut microbiota: prebiotic activity. Kyolic increased microbial richness and diversity, with more Lactobacillus and Clostridia [PMID:30619868].

** Dosing in trials
- Blood pressure: 1.2 g/d AGE powder (1.2 mg SAC) for 12 weeks was the best-supported dose in the Ried dose-response trial. 0.6 mg SAC was ineffective and 2.4 mg was borderline [PMID:23169470]. 1.2 g/d was used in the AGE at Heart and GarGIC trials [PMID:26869811] [PMID:30619868], and 960 mg (2.4 mg SAC) in the first trial [PMID:20594781].
- Meta-regression of 10 garlic RCTs found SAC dose negatively correlated with BP effect, with an optimal range of 0.5-1.5 mg SAC [PMID:41393949].
- Coronary plaque/calcium and microcirculation: 2.4 g/d for about 1 year [PMID:26764322] [PMID:32349742] [PMID:31518044]. The pilot used 4 mL/d liquid AGE alongside statins [PMID:15475033].
- Combination products: AGE 250 mg plus B12 100 ug, folic acid 300 ug, B6 12.5 mg and L-arginine 100 mg/d [PMID:19573556]. AGE 1200 mg plus CoQ10 120 mg/d [PMID:22923934].
- Immune: 2.56 g/d for 90 days over a cold/flu season [PMID:22280901].
- Athletes: 1.2 g/d vs 2.4 g/d for 12 weeks [PMID:40084199].
- Platelet studies: 5 mL/d liquid for 13 weeks [PMID:11053504]; dose-finding at 2.4-7.2 g/d [PMID:11238801].
- With warfarin: 5 mL twice daily for 12 weeks [PMID:16484565].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Tolerability in Kyolic trials is high, with 92-93% acceptability [PMID:20594781] [PMID:23169470]. The controlled aging process was developed partly because tolerance of raw garlic is low [PMID:37686723]. No side effects were reported in either arm of a 1-year 2.4 g/d trial [PMID:32349742].
- Garlic meta-analysis (10 RCTs): adverse reactions were slightly but not significantly more frequent with garlic. GI discomfort and bad breath were the most common [PMID:41393949].
- Garlic in general (case reports and reviews):
  - Breath and body odour.
  - Allergic reactions: contact dermatitis, urticaria, angioedema, pemphigus, anaphylaxis.
  - Burns from fresh garlic applied to skin.
  - Altered platelet function with possible bleeding [PMID:17918162].
- Bleeding:
  - AGE inhibits platelet aggregation and adhesion [PMID:11053504] [PMID:11238801]. Pooled RCT evidence on garlic and platelet aggregation is inconsistent (6 of 12 trials positive) [PMID:36222178].
  - Case report: unexplained heavy bleeding during spinal surgery after about 12 g/d of raw garlic, treated with DDAVP and cryoprecipitate [PMID:27782907].
  - AGE did not increase bleeding in patients on blood-thinning medication in AGE at Heart [PMID:26869811] or in the warfarin RCT [PMID:16484565].
- Pregnancy: a Cochrane review (one trial, 100 women) found insufficient evidence on garlic for preventing pre-eclampsia (RR 0.78, CI 0.31-1.93). Odour was more common (RR 8.50) and no serious harms were reported [PMID:16856110]. No AGE-specific pregnancy data were found.
- Evidence quality caveat: most AGE trials are small and several are manufacturer-supported. A 2024 AGE blood-pressure meta-analysis [PMID:39437887] was retracted in 2026 [PMID:42527229] and should not be relied on.

** Contraindications
- Known garlic/Allium allergy [PMID:17918162].
- Active bleeding or bleeding disorders: this is a caution inferred from antiplatelet activity [PMID:11053504] [PMID:11238801].
- Elective surgery: anesthesia societies (ASA/AANA) advise stopping herbal medicines 1-2 weeks before elective surgery. Garlic is among the herbs most often used before surgery [PMID:38613937] [PMID:27782907].
- No absolute contraindication specific to AGE was found in the retrieved literature.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin:
  - Pilot RCT (n=48, 5 mL AGE twice daily for 12 wk): no increase in bleeding vs placebo in closely monitored patients [PMID:16484565].
  - Crossover study in healthy men: garlic (not AGE) did not change warfarin PK or INR [PMID:18516070].
  - Case reports describe garlic enhancing warfarin/fluindione effects [PMID:17918162].
  - In warfarin-treated AF patients, frequent use of herbs in food (garlic was the most common) was associated with less time in the therapeutic INR range [PMID:21558883].
  - Net: monitor INR, especially when starting or stopping.
- Antiplatelet drugs:
  - In 10 patients with type 2 diabetes, AGE 600 mg plus cilostazol 100 mg did not enhance antiplatelet activity beyond either agent alone [PMID:21713089].
  - In men with CAD already on aspirin and a statin, AGE improved FMD with no reported safety signal [PMID:16041725].
  - AGE's own ADP-pathway inhibition [PMID:11053504] means additive effects with P2Y12 inhibitors are plausible but unstudied.
- Other anticoagulants (DOACs, heparins): no interaction studies. Caution is based on antiplatelet pharmacodynamics and perioperative guidance [PMID:38613937] [PMID:17918162].
- HIV protease inhibitors:
  - Garlic caplets (not AGE) cut saquinavir AUC by 51% and Cmax by 54%; levels were only 60-70% of baseline after a 10-day washout [PMID:11740713].
  - Garlic extract induced duodenal P-gp to 131% without changing CYP3A4 [PMID:20933082].
  - Short-term garlic did not significantly change single-dose ritonavir PK (AUC -17%) [PMID:12580992].
- CYP effects:
  - Garlic oil reduced CYP2E1 activity (chlorzoxazone) by 39% in young adults and about 22% in elderly adults [PMID:12235448] [PMID:15974642].
  - Garlic extract did not alter CYP2D6 (dextromethorphan) or CYP3A4 (alprazolam) [PMID:12891227], CYP3A4 by erythromycin breath test, or simvastatin/pravastatin AUC [PMID:20933082].
  - None of these studies used AGE. AGE-specific human CYP data were not found.
- Antihypertensives: AGE lowered SBP further when added to existing antihypertensive drugs [PMID:20594781] [PMID:23169470], as did a black-garlic SAC extract [PMID:37686723]. Additive effect; monitor BP.
- Antidiabetic drugs: modest FBG/HbA1c reductions with garlic [PMID:42398591]. AGE improved blood glucose in a 1-year trial [PMID:32349742]. Minor additive effect.
*** Other supplements
- Garlic (other garlic preparations): duplicate organosulfur exposure with additive antiplatelet effects and garlic adverse effects (odour, GI, allergy, bleeding) [PMID:11053504] [PMID:17918162] [PMID:36222178].
- CoQ10: AGE+CoQ10 combination trials found slower CAC progression, lower CRP and better PWV/endothelial function. Because the product was fixed, the individual contributions cannot be separated [PMID:22923934] [PMID:22858191].
- B Complex (B6, B12, folic acid) with L-arginine: the combination slowed CAC progression, lowered homocysteine and improved vascular function [PMID:19573556] [PMID:23453866] [PMID:29140808].
- B12 methylcobalamin: vitamin B12 status influenced BP responsiveness to Kyolic in about 17% of patients [PMID:30619868] [PMID:32010325].
- Other antiplatelet botanicals and foods (ginkgo, cocoa/cacao, grape, flaxseed, tomato): an SR of RCTs found inhibition of platelet aggregation for these plants as well as garlic, so stacking could add to bleeding risk [PMID:36380973] [PMID:38613937].
*** Conditions
- Uncontrolled hypertension: the best-studied population. Benefit was seen when SBP was >=140 mmHg, with no significant change below 140 [PMID:20594781].
- Perioperative period and bleeding disorders: see Contraindications [PMID:38613937] [PMID:27782907].
- Garlic allergy: avoid [PMID:17918162].
- Pregnancy: insufficient evidence [PMID:16856110].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. AGE causes true physiological changes in platelet aggregation tests (ADP, collagen, epinephrine thresholds) [PMID:11053504] [PMID:11238801].
- INR was not changed by garlic in healthy subjects [PMID:18516070], but INR should still be monitored on warfarin [PMID:21558883].
- CRP, IL-6, TNF-alpha, adiponectin and homocysteine may fall or rise as true effects [PMID:30949665] [PMID:23533302] [PMID:19573556].

** Genotype notes
- In a crossover study, garlic did not change warfarin PK/PD overall. The authors noted some evidence of a VKORC1 (not CYP2C9) genotype-dependent interaction that needs further study [PMID:18516070]. No AGE-specific pharmacogenomic data were found.

** Latest research
- [2026-07] Retraction of the 2024 AGE blood-pressure meta-analysis (Prostaglandins Other Lipid Mediat) [PMID:42527229]. Its results (SBP -4.03 mmHg in 9 RCTs) [PMID:39437887] should not be cited as evidence.
- [2026-07] Garlic glycemic dose-response MA (27 RCTs, n=1619): FBG -2.76 mg/dL and HbA1c -0.53%; insulin/HOMA-IR not significant [PMID:42398591].
- [2026-07] Mouse ARDS model: AGE plus low-dose dexamethasone suppressed NF-kB and lung injury more than either alone (preclinical) [PMID:42300751].
- [2026-01] Updated garlic CV-risk MA (108 RCTs, n=7137): SBP -3.71, TC -10.21 mg/dL, CRP -1.6 mg/L; no effect on apoB, IL-6 or weight [PMID:40580481].
- [2025-11] Garlic-for-hypertension MA (10 RCTs): SBP -4.21, DBP -3.13 mmHg. GI discomfort and breath odour were the most common AEs, and SAC dose was negatively correlated with effect [PMID:41393949].
- [2025-11] Review of garlic cholesterol mechanisms, including gut microbiota and TMAO [PMID:41273868].
- [2025-07] Aged-garlic GRADE dose-response MA (19 trials): SBP -2.49 mmHg and LDL -4.41 mg/dL; DBP/HDL not significant [PMID:40628369].
- [2025-03] Reviews of AGE sulfur-compound pharmacokinetics and anti-atherosclerotic mechanisms [PMID:40171136] [PMID:40171135].
- [2025-02] Kyolic RCT in middle-aged endurance athletes (n=75): VO2max, lactate threshold and PWV improved vs placebo [PMID:40084199].
- [2025-01] Review of experimental evidence that AGE modulates macrophage function [PMID:39882336].

** Open questions
- Whether AGE's effects on BP, CAC and plaque translate into fewer cardiovascular events. No outcome trials exist.
- Independent replication: most Kyolic trials are small, single-group or manufacturer-linked, and one AGE meta-analysis was retracted.
- Effects of AGE alone vs AGE combinations (CoQ10, B vitamins/arginine) on atherosclerosis.
- AGE-specific human CYP/P-gp interaction data. Existing studies used garlic caplets, garlic oil or other extracts.
- Bleeding risk with DOACs, P2Y12 inhibitors and dual antiplatelet therapy.
- Durability of the cold/flu severity benefit; only one AGE RCT exists.
- The non-linear dose-response for SAC in hypertension.
