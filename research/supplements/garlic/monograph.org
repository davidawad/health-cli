** Identity & forms
- Allium sativum L. bulb, used as food and as supplements. Its bioactivity is attributed to sulfur compounds: alliin, allicin, diallyl sulfide/disulfide/trisulfide, ajoene and S-allylcysteine (SAC) [PMID:41273868] [PMID:39275211].
- This monograph covers non-aged preparations. Aged garlic extract (AGE, e.g. Kyolic) has its own monograph ("Garlic (Kyolic)"). AGE is cited here only where meta-analyses pool it with other forms or where it is the only human data.
- Main supplement forms:
  - Dried garlic powder tablets/capsules, usually standardized on "allicin potential" (alliin plus alliinase). Kwai powder tablets gave about 80% allicin bioequivalence relative to crushed raw garlic, so they are a reasonable stand-in for raw garlic in older trials [PMID:29937536].
  - Enteric-coated tablets: allicin bioequivalence 36-104%, falling to 22-57% when taken with a high-protein meal. Non-enteric tablets gave 80-111% and powder capsules 26-109% [PMID:29937536].
  - Garlic oil (steam-distilled or solvent-extracted; mostly allyl polysulfides) and macerates. Garlic oil showed stronger TC/LDL lowering than powder in one subgroup analysis [PMID:39113105].
- Products are not interchangeable. In a 6-month RCT comparing raw garlic, powder and AGE (all chemically characterized and stable), none lowered LDL [PMID:17325296].

** Mechanisms
- Vasodilation: human red cells convert garlic-derived organic polysulfides to hydrogen sulfide (H2S). Aortic relaxation tracked H2S yield (ex vivo/in vitro) [PMID:17951430]. Reviews add raised nitric oxide, better endothelial function and antioxidant effects [PMID:39275211].
- Cholesterol metabolism (mostly preclinical): inhibits de novo cholesterol synthesis and dietary cholesterol absorption, increases LDL uptake and HDL biogenesis, and shifts gut microbiota (higher SCFA, lower TMAO) [PMID:41273868].
- Platelets: in vitro inhibition is consistent, but in vivo effects depend on dose and preparation.
  - Garlic extract inhibited collagen-induced aggregation in vitro, but only at concentrations above those reachable in vivo; oral dosing had no effect [PMID:8582091].
  - One large dose of garlic oil reduced adrenaline-induced aggregation by 12% only [PMID:17365851].
  - AGE for 13 weeks inhibited ADP-induced aggregation [PMID:11053504].
- Inflammation/oxidative stress: lowers CRP, IL-6 and TNF in RCT meta-analyses [PMID:30949665] [PMID:40580481].
- Drug metabolism:
  - Garlic oil inhibited CYP2E1 by 39% in young adults [PMID:12235448] and about 22% in older adults [PMID:15974642]. Diallyl sulfide reduced chlorzoxazone hydroxylation in volunteers [PMID:11530830].
  - No CYP3A4 or CYP2D6 change on probe-drug phenotyping [PMID:12235448] [PMID:15974642]. Yet saquinavir exposure fell about 50% [PMID:11740713]. Ex vivo work implicates higher intestinal P-gp/MRP2 efflux, which leaves CYP3A4 itself unchanged [PMID:21104925].
- Anti-Helicobacter: allicin is active against H. pylori, and RCTs have tested it as an add-on to eradication therapy [PMID:31660038].

** Dosing in trials
- Blood pressure: powder/extract doses in RCTs varied widely. A 2025 MA found SBP benefit with about 8 weeks of treatment and larger effects at higher baseline BP. SAC dose was inversely related to effect (optimal about 0.5-1.5 mg) [PMID:41393949]. Larger effects with higher baseline BP were also seen in an earlier meta-regression [PMID:18554422].
- Lipids:
  - Effects were seen when garlic was taken for >2 months by people with total cholesterol above about 200 mg/dL [PMID:26764326].
  - 900 mg/d powder for 12 weeks lowered LDL 14% in one RCT [PMID:9022529].
  - About one 4 g clove-equivalent per day (raw, powder or AGE), 6 d/wk for 6 months, had no effect [PMID:17325296].
- Glycemia: nonlinear dose-response with greater FBG reduction at <=0.5 g/day [PMID:42398591].
- Common cold: one capsule per day containing allicin (Cochrane: "180 mg of allicin content") for 12 weeks in winter [PMID:25386977] [PMID:11697022]. The AGE comparator trial used 2.56 g/d for 90 days [PMID:22280901].
- PCOS/metabolic syndrome: 500 mg tablets (2-3 mg allicin) twice daily for 8 weeks [PMID:40770379].
- Gastric cancer prevention: garlic extract plus oil for 7.3 years (Shandong trial) [PMID:31511230].
- Formulation matters. Allicin bioequivalence of 13 supplements ranged from about 22% to 111% depending on coating, capsule type and meal; enteric tablets taken with a high-protein meal performed worst [PMID:29937536].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Common effects:
  - Breath and body odour are the most frequent complaints [PMID:17918162]. In the Cochrane pre-eclampsia review, odour had RR 8.50 vs placebo [PMID:16856110].
  - GI discomfort (heartburn, flatulence, nausea): in a 2025 hypertension MA, adverse events were slightly but not significantly more frequent, mostly GI discomfort and bad breath [PMID:41393949]. GI side effects limited dose escalation in a platelet trial [PMID:8582091].
  - Rash and odour were the AEs in the common-cold RCT [PMID:25386977].
- Bleeding:
  - Case report: spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma with platelet dysfunction after heavy garlic intake [PMID:2352608].
  - Reviews link garlic to surgical bleeding independent of anticoagulants [PMID:36304597] and to altered platelet function/coagulation [PMID:17918162]. A review of supplement-associated bleeding (retrobulbar, subarachnoid, postoperative) found controlled studies too few to confirm a consistent effect [PMID:22300597].
  - Controlled data are weaker. 4.2 g raw garlic daily did not impair platelet function on PFA-100, Multiplate or TEG [PMID:17959943].
- Allergy and skin:
  - IgE-mediated garlic allergy is rare. Alliin lyase is the major, heat-labile allergen. Garlic powder resembles raw garlic on SDS-PAGE, so powder may be as allergenic as raw garlic [PMID:39022755].
  - Reported reactions include allergic contact dermatitis, urticaria, angioedema, pemphigus, anaphylaxis and photoallergy [PMID:17918162].
  - Raw garlic on skin or mucosa causes chemical burns, mostly second-degree (39 patients in a systematic review) [PMID:33571752].
- Lactation: garlic capsules flavour breast milk and change how long infants feed [PMID:8108198].
- Cancer signal: one MA of prospective studies associated garlic-supplement use with higher colorectal cancer risk (RR 1.18). This is observational and probably confounded [PMID:24681077].
- Long term: 7.3 years of garlic extract/oil in the Shandong trial showed no excess of other cancers or cardiovascular deaths [PMID:31511230].

** Contraindications
- Known garlic/Allium allergy. Powder may carry intact heat-labile allergens [PMID:39022755].
- Saquinavir-based (unboosted) HIV protease inhibitor regimens: exposure fell about 50% with lingering effect after washout [PMID:11740713].
- Scheduled surgery or neuraxial anaesthesia: ASA/AANA recommend stopping herbal products 1-2 weeks before elective surgery [PMID:38613937]. Earlier reviews and case reports flag garlic for perioperative bleeding [PMID:11448284] [PMID:2352608].
- Bleeding disorders or active bleeding: caution, based on case reports [PMID:2352608] [PMID:22300597].
- Do not apply raw garlic to skin or mucosa [PMID:33571752].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Saquinavir/HIV protease inhibitors:
  - Garlic caplets for 3 weeks cut saquinavir AUC 51%, trough 49% and Cmax 54%. Levels were still only 60-70% of baseline after a 10-day washout [PMID:11740713].
  - Short-term garlic did not significantly change single-dose ritonavir AUC (-17%, NS). Steady-state effects remain untested [PMID:12580992].
  - Mechanism: ex vivo, AGE increased intestinal efflux of saquinavir and darunavir while inhibiting their CYP3A4 metabolism [PMID:21104925]. Probe phenotyping found no CYP3A4 induction [PMID:12235448]. The popular "CYP3A4 induction" explanation is therefore not supported by human probe data; transporter effects are more likely.
- Warfarin and other vitamin K antagonists:
  - Case reports and reviews describe over-anticoagulation, including with fluindione [PMID:17918162] [PMID:19719333].
  - Controlled data are reassuring. In a crossover RCT (n=12), garlic did not change warfarin PK, INR or platelet aggregation [PMID:18516070]. AGE in 48 warfarin patients for 12 weeks caused no excess bleeding [PMID:16484565].
  - INR monitoring is reasonable when starting or stopping garlic [PMID:19719333].
- Antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors): additive effect is plausible. AGE inhibited ADP-induced aggregation [PMID:11053504], and garlic oil modestly reduced adrenaline-induced aggregation [PMID:17365851]. Reviews say controlled evidence of clinical bleeding with antiplatelets is lacking [PMID:22300597].
- Isoniazid: in rabbits, garlic extract for 14 days lowered isoniazid Cmax by about 65% and AUC by about 55%, with rifampicin unchanged [PMID:16699292]. No human data.
- CYP2E1 substrates (chlorzoxazone; theoretical relevance for paracetamol bioactivation, ethanol): garlic oil and diallyl sulfide inhibit CYP2E1 by 22-39% [PMID:12235448] [PMID:15974642] [PMID:11530830].
- Docetaxel (CYP3A substrate): garlic 600 mg twice daily did not significantly change docetaxel clearance in 10 patients [PMID:16899612].
- Antihypertensives: additive BP lowering is plausible (SBP -4 to -9 mmHg in hypertensive people) [PMID:26764326] [PMID:41393949].
- Antidiabetic drugs: small additive glucose lowering (HbA1c -0.53%) [PMID:42398591]. A chlorpropamide interaction is reported [PMID:19719333].
- Cardiovascular drugs generally: an SR found evidence insufficient for clinical harms. Low-strength evidence showed HDL rises with garlic plus nitrates or warfarin [PMID:22651380].
*** Other supplements
- Garlic (Kyolic): taking both duplicates the same bioactive class. Antiplatelet effects (AGE inhibits ADP-induced aggregation [PMID:11053504]) and BP/LDL lowering (AGE MA: SBP -2.49 mmHg, LDL -4.41 mg/dL [PMID:40628369]) are additive. Treat as one garlic exposure for bleeding-risk and perioperative purposes [PMID:38613937].
- EPA/DHA/DPA: complementary lipid effects. In a 12-week RCT, garlic lowered LDL 14% and fish oil lowered TG 37%, and the combination offset the LDL rise seen with fish oil alone [PMID:9022529]. Both appear in bleeding-risk reviews, although controlled bleeding data are limited [PMID:22300597].
- Vitamin E and Ginger Root: reviewed alongside garlic as supplements that may interfere with hemostasis. Stacking is a theoretical bleeding risk [PMID:22300597].
- Turmeric: associated with bleeding in people on anticoagulants [PMID:36304597]. Combining it with garlic adds antiplatelet exposure (inferred).
- Other antiplatelet herbs (ginkgo, ginseng): perioperative bleeding concern shared with garlic [PMID:11448284].
*** Conditions
- Surgery, bleeding disorders, garlic allergy: see Contraindications.
- Hypertension, dyslipidemia, type 2 diabetes: modest favourable changes [PMID:40580481]. These are additive to drug therapy, not a substitute for it [PMID:39275211].
- Pregnancy: one small RCT showed no clear effect on pre-eclampsia, and odour was more common [PMID:16856110]. No safety signal beyond food use was identified in the retrieved literature.
- Breastfeeding: garlic alters milk flavour and nursing behaviour [PMID:8108198].
- GI reflux/dyspepsia: GI discomfort is the commonest AE [PMID:41393949].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. Platelet aggregation tests may show true pharmacological inhibition after AGE (ADP Km roughly doubled) [PMID:11053504]. Dietary garlic did not alter PFA-100/Multiplate/TEG results [PMID:17959943].
- Physiological shifts in lipids, glucose/HbA1c, CRP and AST may appear during supplementation [PMID:40580481] [PMID:42398591].

** Genotype notes
- CYP3A5: in a docetaxel study, the day-15/day-1 docetaxel AUC ratio during garlic co-administration was 3.74 in three CYP3A5*1A/*1A patients vs 1.02 in six CYP3A5*3C/*3C patients. Very small numbers; hypothesis-generating [PMID:16899612].
- VKORC1: garlic showed some evidence of a VKORC1 (not CYP2C9) genotype-dependent pharmacodynamic interaction with warfarin in healthy men (n=12) [PMID:18516070].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Retraction notice for the 2024 AGE blood-pressure MA (9 RCTs; SBP -4.03 mmHg). Do not rely on its estimates [PMID:42527229] [PMID:39437887].
- [2026-07] GRADE dose-response MA (27 RCTs, n=1619): FBG -2.76 mg/dL, HbA1c -0.53%, no insulin/HOMA-IR effect; larger effects at <=0.5 g/day [PMID:42398591].
- [2025-12] Hypertension MA (10 RCTs): SBP -4.21, DBP -3.13 mmHg; GI discomfort and bad breath were the main AEs; SAC dose inversely associated with effect [PMID:41393949].
- [2025-11] Mechanistic review of garlic and cholesterol metabolism: synthesis/absorption inhibition, LDL uptake, HDL biogenesis, gut microbiota and TMAO [PMID:41273868].
- [2025-08] RCT in PCOS with metabolic syndrome (n=97, 1 g/d for 8 wk): FBS -10.5%, TG -17.8%, LDL -14.2%, CRP -24.7%, SBP -5.2% vs placebo [PMID:40770379].
- [2025-07] Comprehensive CV risk-factor MA (108 RCTs, n=7137): improved lipids, FBG, insulin, BP, CRP, TNF and MDA; no effect on apoB, IL-6 or weight [PMID:40580481].
- [2025-07] Aged-garlic dose-response GRADE MA (19 trials): SBP -2.49 mmHg, LDL -4.41 mg/dL; no significant DBP/HDL effect [PMID:40628369].

** Open questions
- Whether garlic's BP and lipid effects reduce cardiovascular events; no outcome trials exist.
- Why LDL-lowering in meta-analyses is not reproduced in rigorous, well-characterized RCTs (Gardner 2007, SPORT 2023), and how much publication bias and product variability (allicin release) explain this [PMID:17325296] [PMID:36351465] [PMID:29937536].
- Whether garlic interacts at steady state with boosted protease-inhibitor regimens, and the relative roles of transporters vs CYP3A4 [PMID:12580992] [PMID:21104925].
- Clinical bleeding risk with antiplatelets/anticoagulants at supplement doses; human controlled data are reassuring but small [PMID:18516070] [PMID:17959943].
- Whether the late gastric-cancer mortality benefit in Shandong generalizes beyond high-risk Chinese populations [PMID:31511230].
- Common cold: a single 2001 trial underpins any benefit; replication is needed [PMID:25386977].
- Possible Lp(a) increase with longer-term use [PMID:26522661].
