** Identity & forms
- Garlic (Allium sativum) preparations include fresh garlic, powder, oils and aged extracts; processing changes sulfur compounds and bioavailability. [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]

** Mechanisms
- Proposed biological activity and the best-established physiological context are described above; clinical mechanisms remain uncertain where outcome evidence is limited. ["PMID:39113105"]

| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | Certainty | Citations |

** Dosing in trials
- Trials commonly use garlic powder or aged extract for weeks to months; products are not interchangeable. [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Breath/body odor, heartburn, abdominal upset; supplements may increase bleeding. [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]

** Contraindications
- Caution before surgery and in bleeding disorders. [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]

** Interactions
- May increase bleeding with anticoagulants/antiplatelets; can reduce saquinavir exposure. [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed; no genotype-specific claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2025–2026 literature: 2024 meta-analysis of RCTs evaluated garlic and dyslipidemia; no newer robust outcome trial identified. [PMID:39113105]

** Open questions
- Whether findings translate into durable patient-important outcomes, optimal formulation/dose, and long-term safety remains uncertain. [PMID:39113105; URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]
