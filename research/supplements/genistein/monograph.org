** Identity & forms
- Genistein is the main isoflavone phytoestrogen of soybeans. It occurs in foods mostly as the glucoside genistin and is sold either as purified genistein aglycone or as part of mixed soy (or red clover) isoflavone extracts [PMID:40157603] [PMID:28168353].
- Category: classed as botanical because it is a plant-derived isoflavone and is mostly consumed in soy extracts. Note that some trial products are chemically synthesized: Bonistein is 99.4% synthetic genistein aglycone [PMID:15943224].
- Forms used in trials:
  - Pure genistein aglycone, 54 mg/d: used in the Italian (Marini/Squadrito group) trial program on bone, hot flushes, endothelium and metabolic syndrome [PMID:17577003] [PMID:12369794] [PMID:23824420].
  - Mixed soy isoflavone extracts: these contain genistein, daidzein and glycitein in varying ratios, so results do not transfer directly to pure genistein. Median dose in estrogenicity trials was 75 mg/d total isoflavones [PMID:39433088].
  - Products differ in total dose and in the proportions of individual isoflavones. This, plus large differences between people in isoflavone metabolism, is proposed to explain inconsistent trial results [PMID:40157603].
- Pharmacokinetics (single 30-300 mg doses of synthetic aglycone): Tmax 4-6 h, terminal half-life 7.5-10.2 h. AUC is dose-linear up to 300 mg, while absorption rate saturates above 150 mg [PMID:15943224].
- Equol is a gut-bacterial metabolite of daidzein, not of genistein. Only about one third to one half of people make it [PMID:31527435]. It matters for mixed soy extracts more than for pure genistein.

** Mechanisms
- Estrogen receptor-beta preference: genistein and daidzein act as ER-beta-selective agonists at physiological concentrations. They recruit coregulators to ER-beta more than to ER-alpha, which supports the description of isoflavones as natural SERMs [PMID:11279159] [PMID:40157603].
- Weak clinical estrogenicity: a 2025 meta-analysis of 40 RCTs in postmenopausal women found no effect on endometrial thickness, vaginal maturation index, FSH or estradiol (high-to-moderate certainty) [PMID:39433088].
- Bone: genistein lowered resorption markers (urinary pyridinoline/deoxypyridinoline) and raised formation markers (bone ALP, osteocalcin), whereas HRT lowered both. This suggests a different, partly anabolic profile [PMID:12369794]. At 3 years it also lowered RANKL and raised osteoprotegerin [PMID:18796517].
- Endothelium: raised nitrites/nitrates (+21 umol/L) and lowered endothelin-1 (-7 pg/mL) versus placebo over 1 year [PMID:12727580].
- Thyroid: genistein inactivates thyroid peroxidase in vitro and in rats, but serum thyroid hormones stayed normal. Iodine deficiency greatly increases soy's antithyroid effect, and iodine supplementation protects against it [PMID:12060828].
- Drug metabolism: in human liver microsomes genistein inhibits CYP3A4 (Ki 23 umol/L) and CYP2C9 (Ki 36 umol/L) noncompetitively. These concentrations are above usual plasma levels [PMID:27312150]. Soy components may also activate PXR and induce CYP3A in models [PMID:27440109].
- Also reported: a hypothesized inhibition of CYP24 that would raise vitamin D, which was not confirmed in an RCT [PMID:36919425].

** Dosing in trials
- Pure genistein aglycone 54 mg/d for 1-3 years, usually with calcium and vitamin D3, was used for bone, hot flushes, thyroid, endothelium and metabolic syndrome [PMID:17577003] [PMID:18796517] [PMID:23824420].
- Isoflavone extracts for hot flashes: median 54 mg/d (aglycone equivalents) for 6 weeks to 12 months. Products with >18.8 mg genistein were more than twice as effective on flash frequency [PMID:22433977]. The Cochrane review noted that extracts with >30 mg/d genistein consistently reduced flushes [PMID:24323914].
- Bone: benefit seen in subgroups with >=12 months' duration and >=50 mg/d genistein [PMID:37875614].
- Higher doses: 90 mg/d for 12 weeks [PMID:36919425]; 120 mg/d for 12 months in prodromal Alzheimer's disease [PMID:36329553]; 250 mg/d for 8 weeks in NAFLD [PMID:28647291].
- Very high dose: 160 mg/kg/day for 12 months in children with Sanfilippo syndrome showed no clinical efficacy [PMID:34047372].
- Single doses up to 300 mg of synthetic aglycone were well tolerated [PMID:15943224].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Gastrointestinal effects: 19% vs 8% with placebo (P=0.002) over 24 months at 54 mg/d, with more dropouts in the genistein arm [PMID:17577003]. Other trials at the same dose found no excess adverse events [PMID:23824420] [PMID:18796517].
- Endometrium:
  - Pure genistein 54 mg/d did not change endometrial thickness or vaginal maturation over 1-3 years [PMID:19034051] [PMID:17577003] [PMID:18796517].
  - A 5-year RCT of soy isoflavone tablets at 150 mg/d (n=376) found endometrial hyperplasia in 3.37% vs 0% with placebo, with no malignancy [PMID:15237003].
  - Pooled estrogenicity data showed no effect on endometrial thickness [PMID:39433088]. The Cochrane review found no endometrial or vaginal stimulation with up to 2 years of use [PMID:24323914].
- Breast: genistein did not raise mammographic density over 3 years, BRCA1/2 expression was preserved, and sister chromatid exchange fell [PMID:18796517]. A 2025 review argues that, based on in vitro, animal and clinical data, harmful proliferative effects of isoflavones on the breast at relevant doses cannot be excluded [PMID:40871649].
- Animal tumor data: a meta-analysis of 30 animal studies found that isoflavones reduced tumor volume and weight but increased tumor area. The authors advise caution with supplements in hormone-dependent cancers [PMID:39480044].
- EFSA (2015) risk assessment, as summarized by German BfR scientists:
  - In healthy postmenopausal women, human studies did not support adverse effects of isolated isoflavones on the breast, uterus or thyroid.
  - This does not amount to a general safety statement for supplements, especially in women with undetected precancerous breast lesions.
  - EFSA has rejected all submitted isoflavone health claims [PMID:28168353].
- Thyroid: see Contraindications and Interactions. In subclinical hypothyroidism, 16 mg phytoestrogens raised progression to overt hypothyroidism (rate ratio 3.6) [PMID:21325465]. A 66 mg dose did not [PMID:30254609].
- Haemostasis: 12 months at 54 mg/d lowered D-dimer and did not change PAI-1 or prothrombin fragment 1+2 [PMID:18410952].

** Contraindications
- Estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer, particularly during tamoxifen or aromatase-inhibitor therapy. Dietary genistein cancelled the effects of tamoxifen and letrozole in mouse xenografts [PMID:22266527] [PMID:18632754]. Reviews advise caution with soy during AI therapy [PMID:41514586]. Human cohort data on dietary soy do not show harm [PMID:38070485] [PMID:30382332]. Overall this is a precaution, applied mainly to supplements rather than food.
- Uterus intact with long-term high-dose isoflavone use: endometrial hyperplasia signal at 150 mg/d over 5 years [PMID:15237003].
- Subclinical hypothyroidism or iodine deficiency: caution, with thyroid testing [PMID:21325465] [PMID:12060828].
- Undiagnosed breast lesions: BfR notes that possible adverse effects cannot be excluded [PMID:28168353].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Levothyroxine: case reports describe soy protein supplements taken with levothyroxine reducing its absorption, which resolved when doses were separated [PMID:11421567]. A crossover PK study found 60 mg soy isoflavones given at the same time did not change levothyroxine Cmax or AUC (ratios 1.02/0.99) [PMID:26125285]. Reviews find limited evidence of reduced absorption and little clinical impact [PMID:33801406] [PMID:37633413]. Separating doses and checking TSH is a pragmatic approach.
- Tamoxifen: low-dose dietary genistein cancelled tamoxifen's inhibition of MCF-7 tumors in mice [PMID:22266527]. In a human PK study, S-equol supplement 10 mg/d did not change tamoxifen AUC [PMID:41359041]. This was equol, not genistein.
- Aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole, exemestane): genistein reversed letrozole's inhibition of tumor growth in mice in a dose-dependent way [PMID:18632754]. S-equol did not change AI exposure (PK only) [PMID:41359041]. A 2025 review lists soy among foods requiring caution [PMID:41514586].
- CYP3A4/CYP2C9 substrates: genistein inhibits these enzymes in vitro only at supraphysiological levels [PMID:27312150]. A red clover isoflavone supplement (120 mg/d) did not change caffeine, tolbutamide, dextromethorphan or alprazolam PK in women [PMID:33197178]. Soy may induce CYP3A via PXR in models [PMID:27440109]. Clinically relevant interactions appear unlikely.
- Warfarin: one case of subtherapeutic INR with soy milk, rated possible to probable [PMID:12452752]. This involved soy protein food, not purified genistein. Genistein did not activate haemostasis markers [PMID:18410952].
- Antidiabetic drugs: additive glucose lowering is possible (FBG -4 to -6 mg/dL) [PMID:28159061] [PMID:40134817].
- Antihypertensives: genistein lowered SBP by 8.32 mmHg in a meta-analysis and lowered BP in a metabolic syndrome RCT, so additive effects are possible [PMID:40134817] [PMID:23824420].
*** Other supplements
- Iodine (potassium iodide): iodine sufficiency protects against soy's antithyroid effect, while iodine deficiency increases it [PMID:12060828].
- Vitamin D-3: in an RCT, adding genistein 90 mg/d to calcium plus vitamin D3 gave no extra change in 25(OH)D, PTH or bone markers [PMID:36919425]. Calcium and vitamin D3 were background therapy in the genistein bone trials [PMID:17577003].
- Other isoflavone or phytoestrogen products (red clover, soy extracts, combinations with black cohosh): isoflavone exposure adds up across products. Combination products have been trialled [PMID:40131516] [PMID:33197178] [PMID:28168353].
*** Conditions
- ER+ breast cancer, subclinical hypothyroidism, iodine deficiency, long-term use with an intact uterus: see Contraindications.
- Metabolic syndrome: the largest lipid and BP effects were seen in this group [PMID:23824420] [PMID:40134817].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. Soy causes a small true TSH rise (+0.248 mIU/L) [PMID:30850697]. In an NHANES cross-sectional analysis, urinary phytoestrogens were associated with lower central thyroid hormone sensitivity [PMID:40604928].
- Genistein shifts bone turnover markers (CTX and pyridinolines down, bone ALP up) [PMID:18796517] [PMID:39708251]. It does not change FSH or estradiol in postmenopausal women [PMID:39433088].

** Genotype notes
- Equol-producer phenotype is a gut microbial metabotype that only one third to one half of people have [PMID:31527435]. In an RCT of about 100 mg/d isoflavones, equol producers and nonproducers showed opposite changes in adipose-tissue gene expression after a high-genistein supplement, and inflammation genes went up in producers and down in nonproducers [PMID:25332325].
- ESR2 genotype: in a crossover RCT (50 mg/d isoflavones, genistein:daidzein 2:1), the HDL-cholesterol response differed by ER-beta-cx Tsp509I genotype (effect only in AA). There was no overall lipid or glucose effect [PMID:16522905].
- These are exploratory nutrigenomic findings. No pharmacogenomic dosing guidance exists.

** Latest research
- [2026-01] Cognition MA (37 studies, 16 RCTs pooled): composite effect 0.25 and memory 0.33 with isoflavones [PMID:41483829].
- [2025-12] Narrative review of nutrition during aromatase-inhibitor therapy lists soy among foods needing caution. The evidence is preclinical [PMID:41514586].
- [2025-12] PK crossover study (n=24): S-equol 10 mg/d did not change anastrozole, letrozole, exemestane or tamoxifen exposure [PMID:41359041].
- [2025-08] Review argues that population studies show no convincing breast cancer link but harmful isoflavone effects cannot be excluded [PMID:40871649].
- [2025-07] Perimenopause MA (12 studies): overall symptom g -0.25; no effect on hot flushes or vasomotor symptoms [PMID:40718787].
- [2025-07] Secondary analysis of a vegan diet plus soybeans RCT: severe hot flashes fell 92%. The reduction was predicted by daidzein intake, not genistein [PMID:40663878].
- [2025-07] NHANES cross-sectional study: urinary phytoestrogens associated with reduced thyroid hormone sensitivity [PMID:40604928].
- [2025-03] Pure-genistein CV risk MA (21 RCTs): LDL-C -11.14 mg/dL, SBP -8.32 mmHg, FBG -3.98 mg/dL [PMID:40134817].
- [2025-03] Expert perspective: isoflavone safety concerns largely refuted, but benefits inconsistent because of product and metabolic variability [PMID:40157603].
- [2025-03] RCT of a black cohosh + soy isoflavone + lignan combination: MRS total -48%. Genistein's contribution cannot be isolated [PMID:40131516].
- [2024-12] Bone turnover MA (73 RCTs): isoflavones lowered CTX and pyridinoline [PMID:39708251].
- [2024-10] Estrogenicity MA (40 RCTs): no effect on endometrial thickness, VMI, FSH or estradiol [PMID:39433088].
- [2024-10] Animal MA of hormone-dependent cancers: isoflavones lowered tumor volume but raised tumor area. Authors advise caution with supplements [PMID:39480044].

** Open questions
- Whether the large BMD gains in the single-group Italian genistein aglycone program translate into fewer fractures. No fracture endpoint exists, and independent replication is lacking [PMID:17577003] [PMID:37875614].
- Why pure-genistein trials report larger vasomotor and metabolic effects than mixed-isoflavone meta-analyses and recent MAs [PMID:40718787] [PMID:40157603].
- Safety of supplements during tamoxifen or aromatase-inhibitor therapy in humans. Current evidence is mouse xenografts and observational dietary data [PMID:18632754] [PMID:38070485].
- Endometrial safety beyond 3 years for pure genistein, given the 5-year hyperplasia signal with high-dose soy tablets [PMID:15237003].
- Thyroid effects at supplement doses in people with low iodine intake or subclinical hypothyroidism [PMID:21325465] [PMID:12060828].
- Whether equol-producer status or ESR2 genotype should guide use [PMID:25332325] [PMID:16522905].
- Guideline position: NAMS 2023 does not recommend soy foods, soy extracts or equol for vasomotor symptoms (Level II) [PMID:37252752].
