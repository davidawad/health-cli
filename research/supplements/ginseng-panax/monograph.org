** Identity & forms
- Panax ginseng C.A.Mey. (Araliaceae) root. It is sold dried ("white ginseng") or steamed and then dried ("red ginseng"), as powder or tea, or as ethanolic/methanolic extracts [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ginseng-radix]. Asian ginseng is native to Korea, northeastern China and far-eastern Siberia, and the root is the part used [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng].
- Panax quinquefolius (American/North American ginseng) is the second main species [PMID:41017752]. Proprietary American-ginseng extracts include CVT-E002 (COLD-fX), which is rich in poly-furanosyl-pyranosyl-saccharides [PMID:16566675].
- Main bioactives are ginsenosides (panaxosides), grouped as protopanaxadiol (PPD) and protopanaxatriol (PPT) ginsenosides [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng] [PMID:12571655].
- Standardization matters. G115 (Ginsana) is a well-characterized standardized P. ginseng extract with decades of trials in glucose regulation, COPD, energy, cognition and immunity; marketed ginseng products vary widely in composition and quality [PMID:32148399].
  - One American ginseng batch with a "depressed" ginsenoside profile (1.66% total ginsenosides) did not reproduce the postprandial glucose-lowering seen with earlier batches. This suggests clinical effects depend on ginsenoside profile [PMID:12571655]. Digoxin-like immunoreactivity also varies greatly between commercial ginsengs [PMID:12580002].
- Bioavailability: natural PPD ginsenosides show low intestinal absorption, whereas compound K (one conjugated glucose) is better absorbed. In a human crossover study, a fermented compound-K product gave 118.3-fold higher Cmax and 135.1-fold higher AUC than red ginseng extract (median Tmax 3.0 h vs 10.0 h) [PMID:33884767].
- Not the same as "Siberian ginseng" (Eleutherococcus senticosus). It belongs to the same family but is a different genus, and its marker compounds are eleutherosides B (syringin) and E rather than ginsenosides [PMID:41235111]. "Indian ginseng" is ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) [PMID:16040294]. Results from these plants should not be pooled with Panax data.
- EU status: the HMPC monograph accepts only traditional use (symptomatic treatment of asthenia such as fatigue and weakness), "exclusively based upon long-standing medicinal use and not based on results from clinical studies" [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ginseng-radix].

** Mechanisms
- Classed as an adaptogen alongside Rhodiola rosea, Withania somnifera, Eleutherococcus and others. Proposed shared actions are HPA-axis regulation, Hsp70 induction and modulation of SAPK/JNK, FOXO and NF-kB. Ginsenosides are reported to suppress FKBP51. These data are mostly preclinical [PMID:41901106].
- Glucoregulation: acute reductions in postprandial glycemia with American ginseng [PMID:10761967] and in blood glucose with single doses of G115 [PMID:15982990]. The effect depends on ginsenoside profile [PMID:12571655].
- Vascular: pooled trials show higher flow-mediated dilation and nitric oxide and lower pulse-wave velocity [PMID:40646515]. American ginseng raised serum nitrates/nitrites in type 2 diabetes [PMID:29478187].
- Anti-inflammatory/antioxidant: pooled RCTs show lower hs-CRP, IL-6 and ROS and higher SOD [PMID:40923100] [PMID:39387709].
- Immune: ginseng is among the 15 herbs with the strongest multi-level evidence of immunostimulatory activity (TLR activation, NF-kB/MAPK, pro-inflammatory cytokines) [PMID:41475897]. G115 nearly doubled NK activity in a vaccine trial [PMID:8879982].
- Estrogenic activity: ginsenoside Rb1 activated estrogen-receptor-dependent transcription in MCF-7 cells at 50 uM. This was blocked by ICI 182,780 [PMID:12568360]. Human evidence for estrogenic effects is conflicting [PMID:18204104].
- Proposed American-ginseng cognitive mechanisms include frontoparietal activation, gut-microbiome changes and cholinergic modulation [PMID:41017752].

** Dosing in trials
- Standardized P. ginseng extract G115: 100 mg/d for 12 weeks (vaccine trial) [PMID:8879982]; 200-400 mg single doses (cognition) [PMID:15982990]; 200 mg twice daily for 24 weeks (COPD) [PMID:32273696].
- Korean red ginseng: 960 mg/d extract for 3 weeks (fatigue) [PMID:40453352]; 1 g/d for 6 weeks with warfarin [PMID:19913311]. A red ginseng root powder with 24 mg ginsenosides/day (200 mg/d) was used for 3 weeks for stress [PMID:40289951].
- Healthy-volunteer safety RCT: P. ginseng extract 1-2 g/d for 4 weeks [PMID:22909282]. Pharmacokinetic study: 500 mg twice daily for 28 days [PMID:21646440].
- Cognitive impairment: in subgroup analysis, 4.5 g/d was the dose that gave MMSE benefit [PMID:41863264].
- Cancer-related fatigue: American ginseng 2000 mg/d for 8 weeks [PMID:23853057]. Standardized P. ginseng 250 mg/d for 4 weeks [PMID:41838185].
- American ginseng and glycemia: 1-3 g taken 40 min before a glucose load gave equal effects across doses [PMID:10761967]. 1 g/meal (3 g/d) extract for 8 weeks was used in type 2 diabetes [PMID:29478187].
- American ginseng (CVT-E002) for ARI prevention: 2 x 200 mg/d for 4 months [PMID:16566675]. Preventive use for 8-16 weeks shortened colds in an SR [PMID:19592479].
- Higher doses of P. ginseng (>=3 g/d) were associated with small bilirubin increases [PMID:32379697].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Trials: adverse events with ginseng monopreparations occur at rates similar to placebo. The most common are headache, sleep disturbance and gastrointestinal upset [PMID:12020172]. In the Cochrane ED review, adverse events were RR 1.45 (0.69-3.03; low certainty) [PMID:33871063]. A fatigue MA found no increase in adverse events [PMID:36730693].
- Insomnia is the most common side effect [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng]. It was the main adverse event in the G115 vaccine trial [PMID:8879982]. Dyspepsia, hot flash, insomnia and constipation occurred in both arms of a 4-week healthy-volunteer RCT, with no laboratory changes [PMID:22909282].
- EU-listed effects: stomach discomfort, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, urticaria/itching and insomnia (frequency unknown) [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ginseng-radix].
- Blood pressure: an uncontrolled 1979 report coined "ginseng abuse syndrome" [PMID:430716]. An early observational study raised concern about BP elevation [PMID:15998708]. However, RCT meta-analyses show neutral or slightly lowering effects [PMID:27074879] [PMID:39387709]. In hypertensive patients, acute American ginseng 3 g had a neutral effect on 160-minute mean BP [PMID:15998708].
- Estrogen-like effects: reported cases include mastalgia (1978 letter) [PMID:656841] and postmenopausal vaginal bleeding attributed to topical ginseng face cream [PMID:3189445]. Ginsenoside Rb1 is a weak phytoestrogen in vitro [PMID:12568360].
  - RCTs of Korean red ginseng found no effect on endometrial thickness [PMID:35691259]. A 2026 menopause RCT found no change in hormone levels or endometrial thickness, and included breast ultrasound in its safety assessment [PMID:41788586].
- Psychiatric: ginseng was implicated in 5 of 35 published herbal mania case reports [PMID:30034348]. Two cases of manic psychosis with high-dose chronic use remitted within days of stopping [PMID:24689505].
- Liver: no hepatotoxic or hepatoprotective signal in pooled RCTs [PMID:32379697]. Liver damage is listed as an uncommon reported effect [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng].
- Bleeding: reviews list possible bleeding [PMID:37465522] [PMID:11448284]. However, an RCT found no clinically relevant effect on platelet function, PT/APTT or thrombin generation, alone or with aspirin [PMID:28732813].
- Combination products containing ginseng account for more serious events than monopreparations, but causal attribution is usually not possible [PMID:12020172].

** Contraindications
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding: not recommended in the EU monograph because of a lack of adequate data [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ginseng-radix]. A ginsenoside has caused birth defects in animals [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng]. A systematic review advises caution, especially in the first trimester and during lactation [PMID:18204104].
- Children and adolescents under 18: not recommended in the EU because of a lack of adequate data [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ginseng-radix].
- Known allergy to the plant [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ginseng-radix].
- Use with MAO inhibitors (phenelzine): case reports describe interactions [PMID:3597812] [PMID:11772128].
- Bipolar disorder or history of mania: case reports of ginseng-associated mania [PMID:24689505] [PMID:30034348].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin: in a double-blind RCT in 20 healthy volunteers, American ginseng lowered peak INR (difference -0.19, 95% CI -0.36 to -0.07). INR AUC, peak warfarin level and warfarin AUC also fell [PMID:15238367]. P. ginseng / Korean red ginseng showed no significant INR effect in ischemic stroke patients [PMID:18637764], in valve-replacement patients on warfarin (crossover RCT) [PMID:19913311] or in healthy subjects [PMID:15089812].
  - An SR of herb-warfarin RCTs found that only American ginseng altered warfarin pharmacodynamics [PMID:28797065]. Monitoring INR is still suggested [PMID:27864798].
- MAO inhibitors (phenelzine): case reports of interaction [PMID:3597812]. A systematic review reports that ginseng "induces mania if used concomitantly with phenelzine" [PMID:11772128] [PMID:19719333] [PMID:12020172].
- Antidiabetic agents: ginseng lowers fasting glucose [PMID:25265315] and postprandial glucose [PMID:10761967]. American ginseng added to conventional therapy lowered HbA1c by 0.29% [PMID:29478187]. Hypoglycemia is a listed perioperative concern [PMID:11448284], and diabetes is flagged for clinician consultation [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng].
- CYP3A4 substrates: 28 days of P. ginseng 500 mg twice daily lowered midazolam AUC (GMR 0.66, 0.55-0.78) and Cmax (0.74), consistent with weak CYP3A induction [PMID:21646440]. Red ginseng probe-cocktail studies found no clinically relevant CYP3A4 effect [PMID:30078466] [PMID:27495955].
  - Overall, interactions appear rare but warrant monitoring with narrow-therapeutic-index CYP3A substrates [PMID:27864798].
- Imatinib: case of acute lobular hepatitis (ALT 1069 U/L) in a man on imatinib for 7 years after 3 months of daily P. ginseng in energy drinks. Horn scale: probable interaction, proposed via CYP3A4. Re-challenge with imatinib alone caused no recurrence [PMID:20332334].
- P-gp substrates: fermented red ginseng raised fexofenadine AUC (GMR 1.322, 1.112-1.571) [PMID:27495955]. A P. ginseng study found no fexofenadine change [PMID:21646440].
- Digoxin: interference is with the assay rather than true pharmacokinetics. See Lab-test interference.
- Antiplatelet drugs / aspirin: no additive platelet inhibition with aspirin in an RCT [PMID:28732813]. Perioperative reviews still list bleeding risk [PMID:11448284].
- Immunosuppressants: ginseng's immunostimulatory profile [PMID:41475897] could in theory oppose immunosuppression. This is mechanistic only; no interaction study was found.
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine: ginseng is a common energy-drink ingredient alongside caffeine. Caffeine and carbohydrate appear to be the primary ergogenic components [PMID:36862943]. Additive stimulation and insomnia are a mechanistic inference from ginseng's insomnia signal [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng]. The imatinib-hepatotoxicity case involved ginseng taken via energy drinks [PMID:20332334].
- Ashwagandha ("Indian ginseng"): it also produces digoxin-like immunoreactivity on FPIA/MEIA assays [PMID:16040294]. It is also among the strongest-evidence immunostimulatory herbs [PMID:41475897]. Additive assay interference and immunostimulation are possible.
- Rhodiola Rosea, Eleutherococcus: overlapping adaptogen mechanisms (HPA axis, stress-response pathways) [PMID:41901106]. Exercise evidence for Panax and Rhodiola is limited and preliminary, while it is more consistent for ashwagandha [PMID:42588175]. No clinical interaction data exist.
- Siberian ginseng (Eleutherococcus) is a different plant. A case of raised measured digoxin levels involved Siberian, not Panax, ginseng [PMID:8705908].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy, lactation, children, mania/bipolar disorder: see Contraindications.
- Diabetes: glucose-lowering may add to medication effects [PMID:25265315] [PMID:29478187].
- Hypertension: RCT evidence is neutral or slightly lowering [PMID:27074879] [PMID:39387709]. Older reports of BP elevation [PMID:430716] justify monitoring rather than avoidance.
- Estrogen-sensitive conditions: in vitro estrogenic activity [PMID:12568360] and case reports of mastalgia and vaginal bleeding [PMID:656841] [PMID:3189445]. Human evidence on estrogenic effects is conflicting [PMID:18204104].
- Autoimmune disease: ginseng may worsen autoimmune disorders [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng] [PMID:41475897].
- Surgery: perioperative concerns are bleeding and hypoglycemia; a JAMA review advises eliciting herbal use preoperatively [PMID:11448284].
*** Lab-test interference
- Serum digoxin immunoassays: Asian, American and Indian ginsengs falsely raise digoxin on FPIA and falsely lower it on MEIA. Tina-quant was free of interference [PMID:16040294]. In digoxin-patient serum, Asian ginseng raised FPIA digoxin to 1.54 ng/mL vs 1.10 ng/mL control. Digoxin-like immunoreactivity varied greatly between commercial products [PMID:12580002].
  - Newer LOCI and ARCHITECT assays showed negligible or no interference [PMID:21860344] [PMID:24659366].
- Glucose: real (not artifactual) acute reductions in blood glucose [PMID:15982990] [PMID:10761967].
- Bilirubin: small real rise at >=3 g/d [PMID:32379697].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] AFU/SFMS secondary synthesis: ginseng shows only a "modest low-certainty" signal for ED; routine phytotherapy is not recommended [PMID:42229757].
- [2026-03] Cancer survivor RCT (n=65, P. ginseng 250 mg/d, 4 wk): BFI adjusted MD -1.38 (-1.90 to -0.86); no treatment-related AEs [PMID:41838185].
- [2026-03] MCI/AD/dementia MA (9 RCTs, n=617): MMSE MD 0.38; ADAS-Cog MD -2.21; below clinical significance [PMID:41863264].
- [2026-03] Korean red ginseng RCT in T2DM/impaired glucose regulation: within-group FBG 6.44 to 6.19 mmol/L, HbA1c 6.27% to 6.12% [PMID:41788588].
- [2026-03] Red ginseng beverage RCT in menopause (n=112, 90 d): improved Kupperman Index; no change in hormones or endometrial thickness [PMID:41788586].
- [2026-02] American ginseng neurocognition review: attention/working-memory benefits at 100-400 mg in healthy adults [PMID:41017752].
- [2026-01] Phytotherapy for cancer-related fatigue MA (14 RCTs): ginseng subgroup not significant; GRADE very low [PMID:41745077].
- [2026-01] Herbal ED MA (14 RCTs, n=1227): ginseng benefited erectile function, sexual desire and testosterone [PMID:41696741].
- [2026-01] Ginseng berry extract RCT in andropause (n=116, 700 mg/d): AMS and IIEF improved vs placebo [PMID:40583012].
- [2025-10] CVD risk-factor dose-response MA (70 studies, n=4506): hs-CRP SMD -0.23; no effect on BP, lipids or glycaemia [PMID:40923100].
- [2025-07] G1899 Korean red ginseng in long COVID (n=216 completers): fatigue relief in women at 12 weeks [PMID:40621076].
- [2025] Cognition MA (8 RCTs): MMSE MD 0.68; ADAS-Cog MD -1.10 [PMID:40774237].
- [2025-05] Korean red ginseng 960 mg/d in healthy Japanese adults (n=46): fatigue VAS improved; no safety signal [PMID:40453352].
- [2025-03] Red ginseng (24 mg ginsenosides/d) in stressed adults (n=149): larger PSS and negative-affect reductions vs placebo [PMID:40289951].
- [2024-12] Cognition MA (15 RCTs, n=671): memory SMD 0.19; no overall cognition effect [PMID:39474788].
- [2024-12] CV-biomarker MA (45 RCTs): SBP -2.57 mmHg, LDL-C -2.81 mg/dL; high heterogeneity [PMID:39387709].

** Open questions
- Which ginsenoside profile, preparation (white, red, fermented, berry, American) and dose gives reproducible effects? Product variability is well documented [PMID:12571655] [PMID:32148399].
- Whether the small effects on fatigue, memory and ED are clinically meaningful, given small, short, mostly Asian-sited trials of unclear risk of bias [PMID:37465522] [PMID:33871063].
- Long-term (>6 months) safety. Short-term use appears safe, but long-term safety has been questioned [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng].
- Pregnancy and lactation safety. Human data are limited [PMID:18204104].
- Whether the American-ginseng warfarin interaction applies to Panax ginseng products and to anticoagulated patients [PMID:15238367] [PMID:19913311].
- Clinical relevance of CYP3A induction and P-gp inhibition, which differ by product [PMID:21646440] [PMID:27495955] [PMID:30078466].
