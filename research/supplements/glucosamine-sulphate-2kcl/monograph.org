** Identity & forms
- Glucosamine is an amino-monosaccharide sold mainly for osteoarthritis (OA). Products come in three main forms that differ in molecular form, formulation and dosing [PMID:28332780]:
  - Glucosamine sulfate stabilized with potassium chloride (2KCl, "GS-K") or sodium chloride (GS-Na). This "regular" glucosamine sulfate (rGS) is the usual OTC supplement form [PMID:40806074] [PMID:20649060].
  - Patented crystalline glucosamine sulfate (pCGS; Rotta/Rottapharm-Meda, later Mylan/Viatris, "Dona"). It is sold as a prescription-grade drug in many countries and given as one 1500 mg daily dose [PMID:28332780] [PMID:16168682].
  - Glucosamine hydrochloride (GH), common in US supplements and in GAIT/MOVES-type chondroitin combinations [PMID:28332780] [PMID:25589511].
- Potassium content: sulfated imported products contained 197-280 mg potassium per daily dose, versus 0.165-3 mg in hydrochloride/N-acetylglucosamine products. The potassium comes from the KCl stabilizer and is rarely stated on labels [PMID:20118652]. The 2KCl form is sodium-free, unlike GS-NaCl [PMID:20649060].
- The two sulfate forms dissociate in gastric fluid. A 2025 pilot crossover PK study (n=10) found no significant AUC/Cmax advantage of crystalline over regular (KCl/NaCl-stabilized) sulfate [PMID:40806074].
- Most positive trials, and the Cochrane "Rotta preparation" subgroup, used pCGS. Pooled non-Rotta preparations did not beat placebo for pain [PMID:15846645]. Results from pCGS therefore cannot simply be applied to 2KCl supplements.

** Mechanisms
- Pharmacokinetics (pCGS): oral 1500 mg once daily raises plasma glucosamine about 30-fold, to a steady-state peak near 10 uM. PK is linear from 750 to 1500 mg but less than dose-proportional at 3000 mg. Tentative half-life is about 15 h [PMID:16168682].
- Joint exposure: after 14 days of 1500 mg pCGS in 12 OA patients, synovial glucosamine was about 4.3 uM (median), correlated with plasma (about 7.2 uM) [PMID:17353133]. In horses, synovial levels were higher after glucosamine sulfate than after hydrochloride at equal doses (oral bioavailability 9.4% vs 6.1%) [PMID:18295513].
- Anti-inflammatory signaling (in vitro): glucosamine sulfate inhibited IL-1beta-induced NF-kB activation, COX-2 expression and PGE2 release in human OA chondrocytes [PMID:12681956]. Prescription-grade GS plus celecoxib showed additive NF-kB-mediated anti-inflammatory effects in vitro [PMID:34445685].
- Hexosamine pathway / glucose metabolism: animal work raised concern that glucosamine alters insulin sensitivity through the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway. Human data are mixed (see Evidence) [PMID:21251987]. A 2026 mechanistic study identified endogenous glucosamine as a link between hyperglycemia and O-GlcNAc-dependent mTORC1 activation in islets and kidney [PMID:42171606].
- Longevity models: D-glucosamine extended lifespan in C. elegans and in ageing C57BL/6 mice through impaired glycolysis, AMPK activation and mitohormesis [PMID:24714520]. There are no human lifespan data.
- Glycosylation and neurodegeneration: in AD mouse models oral glucosamine worsened cognition, consistent with hyperglycosylation as a disease driver [PMID:42265388].

** Dosing in trials
- Standard: 1500 mg/day glucosamine sulfate, once daily (pCGS, LEGS, Rozendaal hip trial) or 500 mg three times daily (GAIT) [PMID:11214126] [PMID:24395557] [PMID:18283204] [PMID:16495392].
- Duration: symptom trials run 6 months. Structure trials run 2-3 years [PMID:12374520] [PMID:18821708].
- KCl form: 1500 mg/day GS-K was compared with GS-Na for 16 weeks [PMID:20649060]. A daily dose of KCl-stabilized product delivers about 200-280 mg potassium [PMID:20118652].
- Combinations: glucosamine 1500 mg with chondroitin 1200 mg is the most common combined dose across 146 studies [PMID:40647198]. MOVES used GH 500 mg plus CS 400 mg three times daily [PMID:25589511].
- Real-world: 1500 mg/day oral pCGS for 1 year gave functional gains similar to two PRP injections in a propensity-matched comparison [PMID:39126538].
- Doses above 1500 mg: plasma exposure was less than dose-proportional at 3000 mg [PMID:16168682].

** Safety & adverse effects
- In RCTs adverse events match placebo, mostly mild GI complaints [PMID:15846645] [PMID:16495392] [PMID:42643227].
- Post-marketing data: allergic reactions and GI disorders are the commonest AEs with non-pharmaceutical-grade glucosamine/chondroitin [PMID:40095377].
- Hypersensitivity: 263 of 366 Australian ADR reports (72%) for glucosamine/chondroitin were hypersensitivity reactions, 43 of them severe. Shellfish allergy status was unknown. The authors call for shellfish/atopy labelling [PMID:31597786].
- Potassium: serum K+ rose significantly with GS-KCl versus GS-NaCl (within normal range) [PMID:20649060]. The potassium load may equal about 20% of the daily potassium allowance in hemodialysis [PMID:20118652].
- Intraocular pressure: one RCT found a significant IOP rise after 3 months, more often in older patients [PMID:27768119]. A 6-month RCT of pCGS found no change [PMID:31104873]. UK Biobank, FAERS and FinnGen analyses linked use to higher IOP and glaucoma [PMID:38031296].
- Glucose: no consistent worsening of glycemia in humans, including a 24-month T2DM cohort [PMID:21251987] [PMID:42732213].
- Liver: no hepatotoxicity signal was found in the retrieved sources. In UK Biobank patients with T2DM and MASLD, non-users had more major adverse liver outcomes than regular users (HR 1.36, observational) [PMID:38842441].
- Alzheimer's disease: a 2026 EHR analysis associated glucosamine with faster AD progression and worse survival in patients with AD [PMID:42265388]. This contrasts with the UK Biobank's inverse association between glucosamine and type 2 diabetes in people without dementia [PMID:31988063].
- Cardiovascular: UK Biobank cohorts show lower CVD risk with use [PMID:31088786] [PMID:41712318]. A Chinese claims cohort of OA patients showed higher CVD risk with a dose-response by adherence (adherent HR 1.68) [PMID:36145069].

** Contraindications
- Hyperkalemia, advanced CKD or dialysis, and potassium-restricted diets: KCl-stabilized products add potassium that is often unlabelled [PMID:20118652] [PMID:20649060]. Prefer potassium-free forms or monitor.
- Known hypersensitivity, and caution in shellfish allergy or atopy given the hypersensitivity signal in pharmacovigilance [PMID:31597786] [PMID:40095377].
- Glaucoma or ocular hypertension: caution and monitor IOP [PMID:27768119] [PMID:38031296].
- Diagnosed Alzheimer's disease: caution, based on one 2026 EHR association plus animal data [PMID:42265388].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin: case report plus 20 FDA MedWatch and 21 WHO reports of raised INR, bleeding or bruising. Most WHO cases resolved after stopping glucosamine, and one MedWatch report described an intracranial bleed [PMID:18363538]. Glucosamine did not inhibit CYP2C9/2C19 in healthy volunteers, so the mechanism is unknown [PMID:20668444]. Monitor INR when starting or changing doses.
- Other anticoagulants/antiplatelets: no direct data. The warfarin bleeding reports [PMID:18363538] justify caution.
- Potassium-raising drugs (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements): additive potassium load from the KCl salt is plausible but unstudied. This is inferred from the potassium content data [PMID:20118652] [PMID:20649060].
- Antidiabetic drugs: human studies show no consistent glycemic effect, but data in diabetes are limited, so glucose monitoring is suggested [PMID:16569816] [PMID:21251987].
- NSAIDs/celecoxib: possible additive analgesia. In an NMA, glucosamine plus ibuprofen reduced pain (SMD -2.27, moderate quality) [PMID:39685902]. Celecoxib plus GS was synergistic in vitro [PMID:34445685]. No harmful interaction was reported.
*** Other supplements
- Chondroitin sulfate: the most common combination. Evidence for benefit beyond glucosamine alone is inconsistent [PMID:16495392] [PMID:24395557] [PMID:27477804] [PMID:38581640]. Warfarin INR reports often involved combined glucosamine-chondroitin products [PMID:18363538].
- EPA/DHA/DPA: glucosamine plus omega-3 ranked highest for pain reduction in an NMA (SMD -2.59, CI -4.42 to -0.75, moderate quality per CINeMA) [PMID:39685902].
- Potassium-containing supplements: additive potassium with the 2KCl form [PMID:20118652].
- No cited interaction with other supplements in this corpus.
*** Conditions
- Hyperkalemia/CKD, shellfish allergy/atopy, glaucoma, Alzheimer's disease: see Contraindications.
- Diabetes or insulin resistance: studies that included subjects with impaired glucose tolerance or insulin resistance were more likely to detect glycemic effects [PMID:21251987].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. Expect true physiological changes: INR rises in warfarin users [PMID:18363538], a small serum potassium rise with the KCl form [PMID:20649060], and a possible IOP rise [PMID:27768119]. Fasting glucose/HbA1c are mostly unchanged [PMID:42732213].

** Genotype notes
No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source found. (UK Biobank analyses found that polygenic risk scores for T2D and heart failure did not modify the associations [PMID:31988063] [PMID:41712318]. This is not a pharmacogenomic effect.)

** Latest research
- [2026-09] 24-month cohort of KOA plus T2DM (n=409): glucosamine did not change HbA1c (+0.06%) or FBG versus non-SYSADOA users [PMID:42732213].
- [2026-08] Umbrella review (19 SR/MAs): VAS pain SMD -0.36, JSN SMD -0.32, both at or below MCID. No function benefit; most reviews were low quality [PMID:42643227].
- [2026-08] UK Biobank prediabetes/diabetes cohort (n=54,096): CVD HR 0.94 (0.91-0.96) with habitual use [PMID:41712318].
- [2026-07] NMA of adjuncts for KOA: celecoxib ranked first for pain; CS+GH ranked second on WOMAC total; low/very low certainty [PMID:42558409].
- [2026-06] Hyperglycosylation drives AD in mice; oral glucosamine worsened cognition; EHR data linked use to faster AD progression [PMID:42265388].
- [2026-05] Glucosamine links hyperglycemia to mTORC1 activation in islets and kidney (mechanistic) [PMID:42171606].
- [2025-11] UK Biobank: users were less likely to have albuminuria (OR 0.81). Mendelian randomization found no causal effect [PMID:41271412].
- [2025-10] UK Biobank: habitual use associated with lower sepsis risk (HR 0.87) and lower 28-day post-sepsis mortality (HR 0.79) [PMID:40504933].
- [2025-08] Propensity-matched UK Biobank: lower risk of 7 NCDs, e.g. heart failure HR 0.88 and colorectal cancer HR 0.86 [PMID:40879960].
- [2025-07] Pilot crossover PK (n=10): no significant PK advantage of crystalline over KCl/NaCl-stabilized glucosamine sulfate [PMID:40806074].
- [2025-06] SR of 146 human studies: glucosamine/chondroitin generally well tolerated; 1500/1200 mg most common dose [PMID:40647198].
- [2025-04] Post-marketing SR: allergic reactions and GI disorders were the commonest AEs with non-pharmaceutical-grade glucosamine [PMID:40095377].
- [2025-04] UK Biobank T2DM cohort: microvascular complications HR 0.89, nephropathy HR 0.87 [PMID:40169583].
- [2025-02] SYSADOA MA (21 RCTs): pain SMD 0.38 within 3 months versus placebo; non-inferior to active drugs [PMID:40005447].
- [2024-12] NMA of glucosamine combinations (30 RCTs, n=5265): only combinations with omega-3, ibuprofen or CS+MSM exceeded the MCID [PMID:39685902].

** Open questions
- Whether 2KCl (regular) glucosamine sulfate performs like pCGS clinically. The only head-to-head PK data come from one n=10 pilot [PMID:40806074]. The pCGS-only benefit seen in Cochrane may reflect sponsor bias rather than formulation [PMID:15846645] [PMID:20847017].
- Whether the structural effect (JSN) is reproducible outside the two sponsor-linked 3-year trials [PMID:11214126] [PMID:12374520] [PMID:18821708].
- Whether the cohort associations with mortality, CVD and other outcomes are causal or reflect selection bias from prevalent-user designs [PMID:36029480]. New-user designs or RCTs are needed.
- Clinical significance of the potassium load in CKD or alongside RAAS inhibitors. No outcome studies exist [PMID:20118652].
- IOP and glaucoma risk, given conflicting small RCTs and biobank signals [PMID:27768119] [PMID:31104873] [PMID:38031296].
- Safety in Alzheimer's disease, given the conflict between EHR/animal harm signals and UK Biobank inverse associations [PMID:42265388].
- Guideline divergence: ESCEO recommends pharmaceutical-grade crystalline GS as background therapy, while OARSI strongly recommends against all glucosamine formulations [PMID:33116279] [PMID:31126594] [PMID:31278997]. ACR/AF 2019 lists no glucosamine recommendation among its endorsed therapies [PMID:31908149].
