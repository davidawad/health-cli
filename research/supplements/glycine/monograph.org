** Identity & forms
- Glycine is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: aminoacetic acid. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:37851316, PMID:39640489].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:37851316, PMID:39640489].

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small studies suggest subjective sleep improvements with bedtime glycine | Systematic review/meta-analysis or human trial synthesis | Mixed/possible benefit; no established clinical disease prevention | See evidence summary below; pooled estimates vary by outcome and population | low | PMID:37851316, PMID:39640489 |

- Systematic review included 50 human studies (42 RCTs); evidence across outcomes was limited by small samples, short duration and high risk of bias [PMID:37851316].
- Small studies suggest subjective sleep improvements with bedtime glycine; evidence for prevention or treatment of disease is insufficient [PMID:37851316].

** Dosing in trials
- Sleep studies commonly used 3 g orally about 1 hour before bed; other clinical studies used varying multi-gram doses and schedules [PMID:37851316].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Reported tolerability was generally acceptable in small short studies; long-term safety at supplemental doses is not established [PMID:37851316].

** Contraindications
- No established contraindication identified in the systematic review; evidence is insufficient for pregnancy/lactation or severe organ disease [PMID:37851316].

** Interactions
- Potential additive effects with sedatives are theoretical; no established clinically quantified interaction found in the human systematic review [PMID:37851316].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2024 systematic review concludes evidence for broad physiological or preventive effects remains limited and calls for larger, longer trials [PMID:37851316].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
