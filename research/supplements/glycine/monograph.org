** Identity & forms
- Glycine is the smallest proteinogenic amino acid. It is classed as non-essential but has been proposed as conditionally essential, because endogenous synthesis may not meet demand [PMID:31208147].
- It is a precursor for glutathione, porphyrins, purines, creatine, sarcosine and bile salts [PMID:29559876].
- Supplement forms used in human studies:
  - Free glycine powder or capsules: 3 g at bedtime for sleep [PMID:22529837]; 5 g/d in type 2 diabetes [PMID:18852529]; 15 g/d in metabolic syndrome [PMID:24144057]; up to 0.8 g/kg/d in schizophrenia trials [PMID:9892253].
  - Sublingual glycine at 0.5-2 g/d, used in a Russian acute-stroke trial [PMID:10629347].
  - GlyNAC: glycine combined with N-acetylcysteine (NAC, a cysteine donor) to supply both glutathione precursors [PMID:34587244] [PMID:35821844].
  - Collagen and collagen peptides are rich in glycine. Glycine- and collagen-containing supplements generally raise circulating glycine, with some evidence of dose dependence [PMID:42687500].
  - Glycine is also used as a 1.5% urological irrigating solution, a non-oral use with distinct risks (see Safety) [PMID:16317031].
- Plasma kinetics: high oral doses raise serum glycine about 8-fold [PMID:11806864]. 0.12 g/kg for 5 days raised serum glycine by 125% [PMID:41189445]. Enteral 0.4 g/kg/d raised plasma and muscle glycine dose-dependently in critically ill patients [PMID:41109034].

** Mechanisms
- NMDA receptor co-agonism: NMDA receptors need a co-agonist (glycine or D-serine) to activate. D-serine is the preferred co-agonist at some synapses, and glycine at others [PMID:26088787]. High-dose oral glycine crosses the blood-brain barrier [PMID:11806864]. This is the rationale for adjunctive use in schizophrenia, alongside D-serine, sarcosine and D-cycloserine [PMID:9892253] [PMID:30738126].
- Inhibitory neurotransmission: glycine is an agonist at glycine-gated chloride channels (glycine receptors) [PMID:29559876] [PMID:22293292].
- Sleep:
  - In rats, oral glycine lowered core temperature by causing peripheral vasodilation. This effect ran through NMDA receptors in the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) and was abolished by SCN ablation [PMID:25533534] [PMID:22293292].
  - Glycine did not change plasma melatonin or clock-gene expression, but it did raise SCN vasopressin and VIP [PMID:22529837].
- Glutathione (GSH) synthesis:
  - Tissue glycine sits below the glutathione synthetase Km. Higher urinary 5-oxoproline on low-protein diets suggests glycine can limit GSH synthesis [PMID:29559876].
  - Older adults had lower red-cell glycine, cysteine and GSH and slower GSH synthesis. Cysteine plus glycine restored these [PMID:21795440].
- Glucose metabolism:
  - Oral glycine raised glucagon and slightly raised insulin. Taken with glucose, it cut the glucose area response by >50%, possibly through a gut hormone [PMID:12450897].
  - Circulating glycine is consistently low in type 2 diabetes and obesity. Glycine receptors on islets and NMDA co-agonism in the brain are proposed links [PMID:28323968] [PMID:31208147].
- Anti-inflammatory: glycine is proposed to act by modulating NF-kB in immune and other cells. The evidence is mostly preclinical [PMID:37510995]. In type 2 diabetes it lowered TNF-receptor I [PMID:18852529].

** Dosing in trials
- Sleep: 3 g orally before bedtime [PMID:22529837] [PMID:42687500].
- Metabolic: 5 g/d (divided three times daily) for 3 months in type 2 diabetes [PMID:18852529]. 15 g/d for 3 months in metabolic syndrome [PMID:24144057].
- GlyNAC:
  - Lizzo trial: 2.4, 4.8 or 7.2 g/d total (glycine:NAC 1:1) for 2 weeks [PMID:35821844].
  - Sekhar/Kumar trials: 16-24 weeks in older adults and 12 weeks in people with HIV [PMID:35975308] [PMID:33783984] [PMID:33007928]. The weight-based doses are not stated in these abstracts.
- Schizophrenia:
  - 0.8 g/kg/d added to antipsychotics in 6-week crossover trials [PMID:9892253] [PMID:14732596].
  - 30 g/d and 60 g/d added to clozapine [PMID:9892314] [PMID:10784481].
- OCD: titrated to 60 g/d [PMID:19046587].
- Stroke: 0.5-2 g/d sublingually for 5 days. 1-2 g/d gave the favourable trends [PMID:10629347].
- Other: alcohol use disorder 0.12 g/kg/d for 5 days [PMID:41189445]. ICU enteral 0.2-0.4 g/kg/d, median about 16-30 g/d [PMID:41109034]. Hemodialysis 2 x 7 g/d for 4 months [PMID:34519439].
- Trial length: diseased populations were studied for up to 4 months and healthy populations for up to 14 days [PMID:37851316].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Generally well tolerated:
  - 0.8 g/kg/d was well tolerated in schizophrenia trials, with laboratory values monitored [PMID:9892253] [PMID:14732596].
  - GlyNAC was safe and well tolerated over 2-24 weeks [PMID:35821844] [PMID:35975308] [PMID:33783984].
  - 14 g/d for 4 months in hemodialysis did not change blood chemistry [PMID:34519439].
- Gastrointestinal effects and palatability: at 60 g/d in OCD, complaints about taste and/or nausea caused high non-adherence (only 14 of 24 participants were evaluable) [PMID:19046587].
- Sedation: slight sedation in 4.5% of stroke patients on sublingual glycine [PMID:10629347].
- Very high doses: a sports-nutrition review warns that doses above 500 mg/kg might cause cytotoxicity or acute glutamate toxicity. This is a theoretical, mainly preclinical concern [PMID:39453231].
- Psychiatric: in clozapine-treated patients, added glycine gave no benefit and was linked to worse positive symptoms in pooled data [PMID:9892314] [PMID:21936588].
- Glycine irrigating solution (not oral use): absorption during transurethral or endoscopic surgery causes TUR syndrome, through irrigant-solute toxicity, dilutional hyponatremia and brain edema. Experts advise avoiding glycine irrigation [PMID:16317031].
- Long-term (>4 months) safety of supplemental doses is not established [PMID:37851316].

** Contraindications
- Nonketotic hyperglycinemia (glycine encephalopathy, GLDC/AMT mutations): glycine builds up in the brain, and treatment aims to lower glycine to limit NMDA overstimulation. Supplementation runs counter to this [PMID:38949693] [PMID:27362913].
- Schizophrenia treated with clozapine: avoid adding high-dose glycine. RCTs and meta-analysis show no benefit and possible worsening of positive symptoms [PMID:9892314] [PMID:10784481] [PMID:21936588].
- Pregnancy, lactation and children: no trials were identified. Available reviews cover adults only [PMID:37851316].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Clozapine: lack of benefit and possible worsening of positive symptoms with 30-60 g/d glycine [PMID:9892314] [PMID:10784481] [PMID:21936588]. Trial authors suggest olanzapine/risperidone and clozapine affect NMDA receptor neurotransmission differently [PMID:14732596].
- Other antipsychotics (typical, olanzapine, risperidone): additive benefit on negative and total symptoms in small trials [PMID:14732596] [PMID:21936588]. Serum antipsychotic levels did not change with high-dose glycine, so no PK interaction was seen [PMID:11806864].
- Antidiabetic drugs: glycine lowered HbA1c in type 2 diabetes [PMID:18852529], lowered insulin resistance as GlyNAC [PMID:35052658] and blunted post-glucose glycemia [PMID:12450897]. Additive glucose lowering is possible but has not been quantified. No hypoglycemia reports were found.
- Sedatives and hypnotics: glycine has sleep-promoting and hypothermic effects [PMID:25533534], and mild sedation was reported in a stroke RCT [PMID:10629347]. Additive effects are possible; no interaction study exists.
- Antihypertensives: modest SBP reduction in men with MetS [PMID:24144057]. Genetically higher glycine was linked to lower SBP [PMID:39796632]. Clinically small.
*** Other supplements
- N-Acetyl-L-Cysteine (NAC): the GlyNAC combination supplies both glutathione precursors.
  - Reported benefits: corrected GSH deficiency in older adults, uncontrolled diabetes and HIV [PMID:21795440] [PMID:20929994] [PMID:24081740] [PMID:35975308].
  - Rodent data: the combination extended lifespan by 24% in mice [PMID:35268089].
  - Caveat: in a larger RCT of healthy older adults the primary GSH endpoint was null [PMID:35821844].
- Trimethylglycine (TMG, betaine): a methyl derivative of glycine, metabolized to dimethylglycine and sarcosine [PMID:24760587]. 3 g/d betaine raised plasma dimethylglycine and sarcosine [PMID:40944155]. Betaine remethylates homocysteine [PMID:16197300]. The pathways overlap; no combined trial was found.
- Collagen: glycine-rich. Collagen supplements raise circulating glycine, and glycine is a candidate mediator of reported collagen sleep effects. Mediation has not been tested [PMID:42687500].
- Melatonin: combining sleep aids may add sedation. Glycine did not alter plasma melatonin [PMID:22529837], and mild sedation was reported with glycine [PMID:10629347]. No combination study was found.
- Tryptophan (not in corpus): 3 g glycine + 0.2 g tryptophan lowered serum uric acid and triglycerides [PMID:30845731].
- Leucine (not in corpus): co-ingestion with glycine reduced the glucose response by 66%, a less-than-additive effect [PMID:25120925].
*** Conditions
- Nonketotic hyperglycinemia: contraindicated [PMID:38949693].
- Schizophrenia on clozapine: avoid [PMID:21936588].
- Type 2 diabetes: possible glycemic benefit; monitor glucose if on glucose-lowering drugs [PMID:18852529].
- Chronic kidney disease on hemodialysis: 14 g/d was tolerated without change in blood chemistry [PMID:34519439].
- Alcohol use disorder: glycine did not reduce craving or intake [PMID:41189445].
*** Lab-test interference
- Plasma/serum amino acids: supplementation raises serum glycine (about 8-fold at high dose) and serine [PMID:11806864] [PMID:9892253]. This matters when metabolomic glycine is read as a risk biomarker, e.g. for CHD, T2D, pancreatic cancer or lacunar stroke [PMID:39796632] [PMID:41421942] [PMID:42721413].
- Glucagon rises after oral glycine and insulin rises slightly [PMID:12450897]. HbA1c may fall [PMID:18852529]. These are real physiological changes, not assay artifacts.
- Uric acid and triglycerides fell with glycine + tryptophan [PMID:30845731].
- No direct assay interference was found.

** Genotype notes
- GLDC and AMT: biallelic mutations cause classic nonketotic hyperglycinemia (about 1:76,000), in which glycine accumulates. 80% of patients carry GLDC mutations [PMID:27362913]. Glycine supplementation is contraindicated on mechanistic grounds [PMID:38949693].
- Serine-glycine-one-carbon (SGOC) enzyme variants (e.g. PHGDH haploinsufficiency) in macular telangiectasia: in carriers, circulating serine falls further. In mice, dietary serine/glycine restriction accelerated retinal defects, which serine supplementation reversed [PMID:39191258]. This is preclinical with human genetic association only.
- Glycine GWAS: 27 loci in 80,003 people [PMID:30837465] and 61 loci in 230,947 people [PMID:39796632] determine circulating glycine. They are used as MR instruments, not as guides to supplementation.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Lacunar stroke MR: genetically higher glycine was inversely associated with isolated lacunar infarcts (OR 0.704, 95% CI 0.585-0.848) [PMID:42721413].
- [2026-09] Scoping review (22 trials): glycine and collagen supplements raise circulating glycine; all 3 glycine sleep trials positive; collagen-sleep mediation untested [PMID:42687500].
- [2026-05] Review of GlyNAC with or without exercise for sarcopenia/frailty: proof-of-concept only; NAC effects depend on context [PMID:42232577].
- [2026-04] UK Biobank MR: genetically higher glycine was associated with lower BMD in women (beta -0.022, 95% CI -0.037 to -0.007) [PMID:41971710].
- [2026-02] Social-cognition NMA (60 RCTs): no agent effective; glycine uptake inhibitors had a non-significant SMD of 0.46 [PMID:41684115].
- [2026-01] UK Biobank (n=205,208) with MR: glycine inversely associated with T2D; MR suggests a protective factor [PMID:41531546].
- [2025-12] UK Biobank cohort: high plasma glycine associated with lower pancreatic cancer risk (HR 0.786, 95% CI 0.657-0.940) [PMID:41421942].
- [2025-12] MA of dietary amino acids: higher glycine intake associated with lower hypertension risk (OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64-0.89) [PMID:41327095].
- [2025-11] Human lab RCT in AUD (n=48, 0.12 g/kg x 5 d): serum glycine +125%; no effect on craving or drinking [PMID:41189445].
- [2025-10] ICU RCT (n=29): enteral glycine dose-dependently raised plasma and muscle glycine; no effect on myofiber size; shorter stays [PMID:41109034].
- [2025-10] TRS MA: glycine-site agonist augmentation improved negative symptoms (g -1.18, GRADE low), but no non-clozapine strategy is recommended [PMID:41044402].
- [2025-08] Betaine 3 g/d RCT: raised plasma dimethylglycine and sarcosine, showing glycine-pathway flux from TMG [PMID:40944155].
- [2025-01] UK Biobank and MR: observational glycine-CAD link (HR 0.70) not supported by MR; glycine feeding did not reduce atherosclerosis in ApoE-/- mice [PMID:39796632].
- [2024-10] SGOC metabolism study: genetic and dietary serine/glycine deficits impair retinal and peripheral nerve function in mice, reversible with serine [PMID:39191258].

** Open questions
- Sleep: only 3 small glycine sleep trials exist, with high risk of bias. Independent, preregistered polysomnography trials are needed [PMID:37851316] [PMID:42687500].
- GlyNAC: positive results come mainly from one group's small trials. A larger RCT by a different group was null on its primary endpoint. Replication with hard functional endpoints is needed [PMID:35975308] [PMID:35821844].
- Is low glycine a cause or a consequence of insulin resistance? MR suggests insulin resistance lowers glycine [PMID:30837465].
- Why do MR signals for CHD differ across instruments and cohorts [PMID:30837465] [PMID:39796632]?
- Safety of chronic multi-gram dosing beyond 4 months, and in pregnancy, children and kidney disease.
- Mechanism of the clozapine-glycine interaction, and whether low-dose (3-5 g) glycine matters in clozapine-treated patients.
