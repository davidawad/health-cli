** Identity & forms
- Galacto-oligosaccharides are fermentable oligosaccharides, commonly β-GOS derived enzymatically from lactose; chain length and linkage composition vary by source and manufacturing [PMID:41263347]. They resist digestion in the small intestine and can be fermented by gut bacteria, often increasing bifidobacteria [PMID:38787732].

** Mechanisms
- GOS escape digestion and are selectively fermented by gut microbes; fermentation products and shifts in taxa depend on chain structure and baseline microbiome. A constipation RCT observed increased Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus [PMID:38787732; PMID:41263347].

** Dosing in trials
- Human trials commonly use approximately 2.5–15 g/day, often divided; the 2024 constipation RCT used a 4-week GOS intervention (dose per article protocol) [PMID:38787732]. Begin-dose and tolerability depend on product purity and degree of polymerization.

** Safety & adverse effects
- Fermentation can cause gas, bloating, abdominal discomfort and loose stool, especially at higher doses or in people sensitive to FODMAPs [PMID:38787732; PMID:41263347]. In a small 2024 RCT, no significant adverse drug reactions were found apart from pulse-rate signal; sample size limits rare-event detection [PMID:38787732].

** Contraindications
- Known galactosemia is a reason to avoid galactose-containing products unless specialist-directed. GOS can worsen symptoms in people with marked fermentable-carbohydrate sensitivity; this is intolerance rather than a universal contraindication [PMID:41263347].

** Interactions
- No established drug interactions or laboratory assay interference identified. Fermentable fiber may change timing/absorption of oral medicines in theory; direct GOS interaction trials are lacking [PMID:41263347].

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source specific to GOS was identified in this search; no genotype claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2024 placebo-controlled RCT in Rome IV functional constipation (n=63) reported improved bowel movement measures and constipation satisfaction after 4 weeks; microbiota Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus increased [PMID:38787732]. A 2025 review describes structural diversity and prebiotic applications but is not itself a clinical efficacy trial [PMID:41263347].

** Open questions
- Larger, better reported human studies with standardized products, clinically meaningful endpoints and longer follow-up are needed; current gaps are noted above.
