** Identity & forms
- Galacto-oligosaccharides are chains of mainly galactose units (degree of polymerization 2-10) that differ in chain length, branching and glycosidic linkage. The most studied and widely used form is beta-GOS made enzymatically from lactose. alpha-GOS and fructo-GOS also have prebiotic activity [PMID:41263347].
- Commercial GOS ingredients are mixtures, not pure oligosaccharide. In one FDA GRAS notice (GRN 000620, infant formula use), the powder is >=46% GOS on a dry-weight basis, with typical lactose 20-40%, glucose <=10% and galactose <=5% [URL:https://www.fda.gov/food/gras-notice-inventory/agency-response-letter-gras-notice-no-grn-000620].
- Branded or trial forms:
  - Bimuno (B-GOS), a trans-GOS mixture used at 2.7-5.5 g/d in elderly, traveller and mood trials [PMID:18996881] [PMID:28931149] [PMID:25449699].
  - Biotis GOS: 5.5-11 g/d in constipation [PMID:35057489]. A 21.6 g/d dose of the product supplied 15 g/d GOS [PMID:33509667].
  - Elix'or TOS: 9-20 g/d [PMID:9895419] [PMID:11110850].
  - RP-G28: an ultra-purified (>95%) short-chain GOS developed as a drug candidate for lactose intolerance [PMID:28049818] [PMID:32290344].
  - scGOS/lcFOS 9:1: the infant-formula prebiotic mixture, usually 4-8 g/L [PMID:16873437] [PMID:22237884].
- Dietary "GOS" in the FODMAP sense mostly means the alpha-linked raffinose-family sugars in legumes, which differ from beta-GOS supplements. Both are counted as oligosaccharide FODMAPs [PMID:28244665] [PMID:28809383].

** Mechanisms
- Non-digestible in the small intestine and selectively fermented in the colon. Prebiotic specificity comes from species-specific saccharolytic gene clusters, mainly in bifidobacteria [PMID:28244671].
- Bifidogenic effect:
  - An MA of fiber RCTs (64 studies, n=2099) found fructans and GOS gave significantly greater increases in Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus than comparators [PMID:29757343].
  - The effect is dose-dependent: 5-10 g/d was bifidogenic and 2.5 g/d was not. About half of subjects were non-responders [PMID:21059476]. A clear dose-response on fecal Bifidobacterium was seen with 5.5 vs 11 g/d [PMID:35057489].
  - An ex vivo study found bifidogenic and SCFA effects from 0.5 g with only mild gas production. This was not a clinical trial [PMID:40863149].
- Lactose adaptation: RP-G28 increased lactose-fermenting Bifidobacterium, Faecalibacterium and Lactobacillus in 90% of subjects [PMID:28049818].
- Mineral absorption: proposed lower-gut calcium absorption, mediated by bifidobacteria [PMID:23507173]. Iron absorption from ferrous fumarate correlates with lower fecal pH [PMID:28814396].
- Immune: in elderly volunteers, raised NK-cell activity, phagocytosis and IL-10, and lowered IL-6/IL-1beta/TNF-alpha [PMID:18996881]. A systematic review proposes SCFA-GPCR signalling and direct pattern-recognition-receptor effects [PMID:40516031].
- Gut-brain axis: lower salivary cortisol awakening response and less negative attentional bias after B-GOS [PMID:25449699]. Trend-level changes in brain GABA on 1H-MRS were seen in 2025 [PMID:40532960].

** Dosing in trials
- Bifidogenic threshold in healthy adults was about 5 g/d; 2.5 g/d had no significant effect [PMID:21059476]. A 2025 ex vivo model suggests lower doses may work, but this is unproven in humans [PMID:40863149].
- Constipation: 5.5-11 g/d for 3 weeks [PMID:35057489], 10 g/d gummies for 30 days [PMID:37751049], 9 g/d in yogurt in elderly [PMID:9895419].
- IBS: 3.5 g/d (better symptoms) vs 7 g/d (less benefit on symptoms) trans-GOS [PMID:19053980]. 1.4 g/d B-GOS alongside a low-FODMAP diet [PMID:32433273].
- Lactose intolerance: RP-G28 dose-escalation for 30-35 days, then dairy reintroduction [PMID:32290344] [PMID:24330605].
- Immune/elderly: 5.5 g/d B-GOS for 10 weeks [PMID:18996881]. Students: 2.5-5 g/d for 8 weeks [PMID:21525194]. Travellers: 2.7 g/d starting 5 days before travel [PMID:28931149].
- Mood: 5.5 g/d for 3-4 weeks [PMID:40025494] [PMID:25449699].
- Minerals: 5-10 g/d (calcium, adolescents) [PMID:23507173]; 20 g/d TOS (postmenopausal) [PMID:11110850]; 15 g GOS taken with ferrous fumarate (iron) [PMID:31004135] [PMID:35015879]; 7.5 g/d in infant micronutrient powders [PMID:28774885].
- Infant formula: scGOS/lcFOS 9:1 at 4-8 g/L [PMID:16873437] [PMID:22237884]. GRAS use up to 7.8 g GOS/L [URL:https://www.fda.gov/food/gras-notice-inventory/agency-response-letter-gras-notice-no-grn-000620].
- Pregnancy: 14.2 g/d GOS/FOS from <21 weeks' gestation to 6 months postpartum [PMID:39173718].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects are dose-related and caused by fermentation:
  - Flatulence improved at prebiotic doses <=6 g/d. Inulin-type fructans worsened flatulence (SMD 0.85) [PMID:30949662].
  - In IBS, 3.5 g/d improved flatulence, bloating and the composite symptom score; 7 g/d improved global assessment and anxiety but not the composite score [PMID:19053980].
  - Larger doses may have neutral or negative effects on IBS symptoms [PMID:28244671].
- Short trials report tolerability similar to placebo:
  - Elderly: GI symptoms similar to control [PMID:9895419].
  - Constipation RCT: no significant adverse reactions apart from a pulse-rate signal [PMID:38787732].
  - RP-G28 (n=377): safe and well tolerated [PMID:32290344].
  - Travellers' study: no adverse effects [PMID:28931149].
  - Endurance athletes: 16 g/d did not worsen exercise GI symptoms [PMID:40010361].
- Infants: prebiotic formulas were well tolerated, with no excess adverse effects (RR 1.01) [PMID:29035013] [PMID:40114538].
- IgE-mediated anaphylaxis to scGOS: reported in atopic, cow's-milk-tolerant patients in South-East Asia, triggered by oligosaccharides with >=3 sugar units [PMID:23102546]. Estimated prevalence of allergy to one GOS product was up to 3.5% in an atopic Singapore cohort [PMID:25951913]. A 2026 review lists scGOS among supplements causing immediate hypersensitivity [PMID:42437383].
- Microbial diversity: 15 g/d decreased diversity while increasing bifidobacteria [PMID:33509667]. The clinical meaning of this is unknown.

** Contraindications
- Classical galactosemia: lifelong lactose restriction is standard [PMID:27858262] [PMID:25873073]. Lactose-derived GOS ingredients carry residual lactose (20-40%) and free galactose (<=5%) [URL:https://www.fda.gov/food/gras-notice-inventory/agency-response-letter-gras-notice-no-grn-000620]. Colonic release of galactose from galactosides may also be absorbed [PMID:7671975]. Avoid unless a metabolic team directs otherwise.
- Known GOS allergy or positive scGOS skin-prick/basophil test: avoid, because of anaphylaxis risk [PMID:23102546] [PMID:25951913].
- Relative: FODMAP-sensitive IBS, especially during the elimination phase of a low-FODMAP diet [PMID:38401741] [PMID:38729390].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Oral non-heme iron (ferrous fumarate): GOS taken with it increases fractional absorption by about 30-60% in iron-depleted women and infants [PMID:31004135] [PMID:35015879] [PMID:28814396]. In iron-deficient children with HIV, adding GOS gave a 39% greater ferritin rise (p=0.053) [PMID:41233303]. No effect was seen when added to ferrous-sulfate-fortified follow-up formula with L. reuteri [PMID:39179207]. In infants, GOS offset the adverse effects of iron fortification on the gut microbiome [PMID:28774885].
- Antibiotics (amoxicillin): GOS taken during and after treatment supported bifidobacterial recovery and butyrate production (n=12) [PMID:24925303]. No effect on drug exposure is known.
- Antidiabetic agents: GOS alone did not change insulin sensitivity [PMID:28396144]. A GOS + B. lactis synbiotic lowered FBG by 0.69 mmol/L versus placebo in T2DM [PMID:39719724]. Additive glucose lowering is minor and only shown for synbiotics.
- No pharmacokinetic (CYP/transporter) interaction study was found.
*** Other supplements
- Probiotics (synbiotic use):
  - GOS + B. lactis MN-Gup beat the probiotic alone on FBG [PMID:39719724].
  - GOS + L. reuteri in toddler formula improved bone and muscle outcomes [PMID:41387706]. In mice it synergistically reduced colitis [PMID:39468026].
  - Bifidobacteria and GOS have each improved lactose tolerance, but the combination is untested [PMID:37630749].
- Inulin/FOS: shared bifidogenic action [PMID:29757343]. GOS/FOS and GOS/inulin mixtures are standard in infant and pregnancy trials [PMID:39173718] [PMID:41250898]. Fermentation is additive, and inulin-type fructans worsen flatulence [PMID:30949662]. Both fructans and GOS are leading FODMAP triggers [PMID:38401741].
- Calcium: GOS increased fractional calcium absorption [PMID:23507173] [PMID:11110850].
- alpha-galactosidase enzyme: reduced symptoms from high-GOS (alpha-linked, legume) foods in GOS-sensitive IBS [PMID:28809383]. It is not shown to act on beta-GOS supplements.
*** Conditions
- Galactosemia and GOS allergy: see Contraindications.
- IBS / low-FODMAP diet: GOS is a FODMAP trigger in about a third of responders [PMID:38401741]. However, 1.4 g/d B-GOS with a low-FODMAP diet gave more symptom relief than sham diet (67% vs 30%) [PMID:32433273]. A simplified diet restricting only fructans and GOS is being tested [PMID:38729393].
- Lactose intolerance: RP-G28 aims at colonic adaptation [PMID:32290344] [PMID:28049818]. Commercial GOS powders may contain substantial lactose [URL:https://www.fda.gov/food/gras-notice-inventory/agency-response-letter-gras-notice-no-grn-000620].
- Pregnancy: 14.2 g/d GOS/FOS showed no safety signals in 652 women [PMID:39173718]. It lowered GDM only in women with BMI >=25 [PMID:41987240].
- Atopy (South-East Asia): see Safety & adverse effects [PMID:25951913].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. True physiological changes that can shift results:
  - Stool microbiome tests: higher Bifidobacterium and lower diversity [PMID:29757343] [PMID:33509667].
  - Lower fecal pH in infants [PMID:22237884].
  - Iron indices: higher absorption, and ferritin in women [PMID:31004135] [PMID:39920867].

** Genotype notes
- Lactase non-persistence (genetic post-weaning lactase deficiency, 65-75% of people worldwide) is the target population for GOS-based lactose-intolerance strategies. Diet cannot change lactase non-persistence, but GOS may shift the microbiome and mitigate symptoms [PMID:38613035].
- Classical galactosemia (GALT deficiency) is a genetic contraindication, managed by galactose/lactose restriction [PMID:27858262].
- No cited pharmacogenomic source found.

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Fiber-in-IBS MA (30 RCTs, n=1904): fiber response RR 1.21. Integrative GI scores were not improved overall, except with beta-GOS (2 RCTs) [PMID:42600900].
- [2026-06] Review lists scGOS among supplements causing immediate hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis [PMID:42437383].
- [2026-04] Pregnancy GOS/FOS RCT secondary analysis: GDM aRR 0.50 only in BMI >=25 [PMID:41987240].
- [2026-02] PREGRALL RCT (n=376): antenatal GOS/inulin did not prevent infant atopic dermatitis (OR 1.01) [PMID:41250898].
- [2026-01] ESPGHAN technical review: synbiotic infant formulas (16 RCTs) well tolerated, with no difference in growth or GI outcomes [PMID:40114538].
- [2026] SR in healthy humans: GOS raised NK activity; prebiotics reduced infant infections. Effects on vaccines and systemic inflammation were inconsistent [PMID:40516031].
- [2025-12] Toddler formula with L. reuteri + GOS (n=182) improved tibia speed of sound and muscle strength [PMID:41387706].
- [2025-11] Iron + GOS in children with HIV: 39% greater ferritin rise (p=0.053) and fewer infection symptoms [PMID:41233303].
- [2025-10] RCT in young females (n=83): GOS did not reduce trait anxiety. Trend-level GABA changes and a transient Bifidobacterium rise [PMID:40532960].
- [2025-10] B-GOS 12-week pilot in athletes (n=18): no group benefit on GI comfort or wellbeing [PMID:41228463].
- [2025-08] Ex vivo bifidogenic effect at 0.5-3.65 g [PMID:40863149].
- [2025-05] Infant prebiotic-formula growth MA (24 studies): no overall effect [PMID:40215676].
- [2025-03] Children 6-14 y (n=53): no significant effect of GOS on primary anxiety and emotion outcomes; trends only [PMID:40025494].
- [2025-02] Probiotic/prebiotic iron MA: fractional iron absorption +0.74% (low certainty); ferritin +2.45 ng/mL in women [PMID:39920867].
- [2025-02] Blinded FODMAP reintroduction: GOS worsened pain (P=.04) and bloating [PMID:38729390].
- [2025-01] SYMBA RCT (n=652): maternal GOS/FOS did not reduce infant eczema (aRR 0.98) [PMID:39173718].
- [2025-01] Synbiotic (B. lactis + GOS) beat the probiotic alone on FBG in T2DM (n=120) [PMID:39719724].
- [2024-10] Synbiotic follow-up formula (GOS + L. reuteri) did not change iron absorption in Thai children [PMID:39179207].

** Open questions
- Whether beta-GOS has a durable, clinically meaningful benefit in IBS and constipation. Current RCTs are small, short and product-specific.
- Optimal dose: the window between bifidogenic benefit and fermentation symptoms, and why some people are bifidogenic non-responders [PMID:21059476].
- Whether RP-G28-type lactose adaptation persists, and whether regulatory approval will follow. No 2024-2026 trial was found.
- Mood/anxiety effects: early positive signals were not confirmed on primary endpoints in the 2025 trial.
- The prevalence of GOS anaphylaxis outside South-East Asia, and which product-specific allergens are involved.
- Long-term significance of lower microbial diversity at high doses.
