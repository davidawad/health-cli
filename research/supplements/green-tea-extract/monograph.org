** Identity & forms
- Concentrated extracts of Camellia sinensis leaves contain catechins, especially epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG); caffeine content and catechin composition vary by product and processing. [PMID:32140423]

** Mechanisms
- Catechins have antioxidant and signaling effects in experimental systems; human mechanism and effective dose for clinical outcomes are not settled. [PMID:32140423]

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weight loss | Systematic reviews of RCTs | small/inconsistent | Average effect small and not reliably clinically important | low | PMID:28927846 |
| Liver injury | Case series, pharmacovigilance, RCT safety review | harm in susceptible users | Idiosyncratic hepatocellular injury; fasting/high EGCG exposure increases concern | moderate | PMID:32140423 |
| Cancer/cardiovascular prevention | Clinical trials and reviews | unproven | No established outcome benefit | low | PMID:32140423 |

** Dosing in trials
- Human trials vary widely by extract and catechin dose. EFSA noted transaminase elevations at supplemental EGCG doses ≥800 mg/day; no universally established safe supplemental dose. [PMID:28927846, PMID:32140423]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Rare acute hepatitis and liver failure have been associated with concentrated extracts. Fasting increases EGCG exposure; take with food in safety studies. Nausea and caffeine-related effects may occur. [PMID:32140423, URL:https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547925/]

** Contraindications
- Avoid or use particular caution with active liver disease, prior supplement-associated liver injury, pregnancy, and concentrated high-EGCG products. Stop and assess if jaundice, dark urine, pruritus or abdominal symptoms occur. [PMID:32140423]

** Interactions
- May reduce nadolol exposure via intestinal transporter effects; can affect iron absorption. Caffeine-containing extracts may add stimulant effects. Liver injury risk may compound with hepatotoxic drugs, though direct interactions are not well quantified. [PMID:32140423]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- USP systematic review documents variable extract composition and a credible association with idiosyncratic liver injury; EFSA review links ≥800 mg/day supplemental EGCG to raised liver enzymes. [PMID:32140423]

** Open questions
- Identify product characteristics and genetic/clinical susceptibility associated with liver injury; establish a safe dose threshold and meaningful clinical efficacy.
