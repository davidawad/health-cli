** Identity & forms
- Green tea is made from unfermented leaves of Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze. Most of its polyphenols are catechins, and (-)-epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) is the most relevant one [PMID:32625874]. EGCG makes up about 50% of green tea polyphenols [PMID:39942757].
- Exposure from tea vs supplements: EU mean EGCG intake from green tea infusions is 90-300 mg/day (high consumers up to 866 mg/day). Food supplements provide 5-1,000 mg EGCG/day [PMID:32625874].
- Composition varies widely: Catechin profiles of extracts vary with the manufacturing process [PMID:32140423]. In 19 US green tea supplements, caffeine, EGCG and total polyphenols ranged 28-183 mg, 12-143 mg and 14-36% of tablet/capsule weight. Labels often did not match content, and only 7 products declared caffeine [PMID:16506807].
- Forms used in trials: decaffeinated extract with 843 mg EGCG/day (1,315 mg total catechins; Minnesota Green Tea Trial, MGTT) [PMID:28904061]; Polyphenon E with 400 mg EGCG/day [PMID:25873370]; extract standardized to 150 mg EGCG twice daily (MIRACLE) [PMID:35213393]; purified EGCG (Teavigo) [PMID:32438698].
- Pharmacokinetics: With 150 mg EGCG twice daily, EGCG half-life was about 2.6 h and peak levels varied more than 6-fold between people. MRP2 and OATP1B1 variants partly explained the variability [PMID:29466429]. Fasting raises exposure. Purified EGCG taken after an overnight fast gave the highest plasma AUC and Cmax, compared with taking it with breakfast [PMID:32438698]. Repeated bolus doses taken fasting markedly increase catechin bioavailability, possibly by saturating first-pass elimination [PMID:32140423]. Co-ingested caffeine (40 mg) raised EGCG AUC from 857 to 1,370 ng*h/mL [PMID:19734672].
- A prescription green tea catechin ointment is FDA-approved for genital warts. Oral supplements are not FDA-approved before sale [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/green-tea].

** Mechanisms
- EGCG has antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and cell-signaling effects (oxidative stress, apoptosis, lipid metabolism), mostly shown in preclinical work [PMID:39942757].
- Weight and energy metabolism: catechins and caffeine are each proposed to raise energy expenditure [PMID:23235664]. In long-term trials, decaffeinated extracts did not change adiposity. This suggests caffeine drives part of the effect [PMID:26701796] [PMID:29228755].
- Drug transporters: EGCG competitively inhibits intestinal OATP1A2 (Ki about 19-22 uM for nadolol) [PMID:24419562] [PMID:29480324]. In vitro, green tea inhibits OATP1B1/1B3, OCT1/2, MATE1/2-K and P-gp transport [PMID:26426900]. Catechins may also reduce drug solubility or enhance P-gp efflux [PMID:39748104].
- Folate pathway: EGCG inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) in vitro at 0.1-1.0 umol/L [PMID:15781612]. It also inhibits folic acid uptake in Caco-2 cells (IC50 34.8 umol/L) [PMID:17117344].
- Iron: tea polyphenols inhibit non-heme iron absorption [PMID:11029010] [PMID:29046302]. In Caco-2 cells, EGCG and green tea extract also inhibit heme-iron absorption, dose-dependently [PMID:22417433].
- Proteasome inhibitors: EGCG's 1,2-benzenediol groups react directly with boronic-acid proteasome inhibitors such as bortezomib and block their activity [PMID:19190249].
- Hepatotoxicity mechanisms: Proposed: immune-mediated idiosyncrasy linked to HLA-B*35:01 [PMID:32892374]. Dietary restriction potentiates EGCG liver injury in mice [PMID:33584288].

** Dosing in trials
- Weight: 12-13 week trials of mixed preparations [PMID:23235664].
- Breast-cancer biomarkers: 843 mg EGCG/day (4 capsules) for 12 months in MGTT [PMID:28904061].
- Prostate: Polyphenon E 200 mg EGCG twice daily with food for 1 year was well tolerated with monthly liver panels [PMID:28053292].
- Colorectal: 150 mg EGCG twice daily for 3 years after a 4-week safety run-in [PMID:35213393]. 0.9 g extract/day for 12 months in the Korean trial [PMID:28209333].
- Fibroids/fertility: 720-800 mg EGCG/day for 30 days in reproductive-age women [PMID:36678191] [PMID:40077973].
- Safety-derived limits: EFSA: >=800 mg EGCG/day as a supplement raises transaminases [PMID:32625874]. Industry-authored SR: safe level 338 mg EGCG/day for solid bolus doses; observed safe level 704 mg/day in beverage form [PMID:29580974]. USP label caution: "Do not take on an empty stomach. Take with food." [PMID:32140423].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Hepatotoxicity (key risk), EFSA 2018: supplements at >=800 mg EGCG/day significantly raised serum transaminases vs control in trials. Traditional infusions are generally safe, though rare idiosyncratic cases occur [PMID:32625874].
- USP reviews: hepatocellular injury pattern at EGCG intakes of 140 to ~1,000 mg/day, with large between-person variability; solvent, pesticide and pyrrolizidine contaminants were not implicated [PMID:32140423]. The 2008 USP review found 34 liver reports (7 probable) and greater risk on an empty stomach [PMID:18484782].
- MGTT (n=1,075, 843 mg EGCG/day, 12 months): ALT elevation in 6.7% vs 0.7% on placebo, with ALT-related serious AEs in 1.3%; nausea and skin AEs were more frequent [PMID:26051348]. In 1,021 women with normal baseline enzymes, ALT rose 5.4 U/L and AST 3.8 U/L; OR for moderate or worse liver abnormality 7.0 (CI 2.4-20.3), with positive dechallenge/rechallenge [PMID:28765194].
- SR of 34 RCTs (to 2013): liver AEs OR 2.1 (CI 0.5-9.8), mostly mild [PMID:27188915].
- US DILIN: green tea caused 40 of 1,414 adjudicated cases (3%). Median latency was 72 days, 95% were hepatocellular and 83% jaundiced; 35% were severe and 8% needed transplantation [PMID:32892374].
- SLIMQUICK products: 6 DILIN cases with ALT >1,000 U/L and one transplant [PMID:27189593]. Garcinia cambogia plus green tea injury is clinically indistinguishable from green tea alone [PMID:34400337].
- HDS cause 20% of US DILI, and green tea extract is one of the major implicated agents [PMID:39136211]. 2025-2026 reviews still list it among the main HDS hepatotoxins and report worsening outcomes in HDS-related acute liver failure [PMID:42504634] [PMID:42475234].
- LiverTox: extract, and rarely large amounts of tea, cause acute liver injury, including liver failure needing transplant or causing death [PMID:31643260].
- Fasting/bolus dosing: Toxicology links hepatotoxicity to bolus dosing and fasting [PMID:29580974].
- Other AEs: GI disorders, liver-enzyme elevation, and rarely insomnia, raised BP and skin reactions [PMID:32118296]. Nausea, constipation and abdominal discomfort are also reported [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/green-tea].
- Caffeine: no adverse effects expected up to 400 mg/day in healthy adults and 300 mg/day in pregnancy [PMID:28438661]. Caffeine content of extracts is often undeclared [PMID:16506807].
- Iron: severe iron-deficiency anemia has been reported after moderate green tea intake in a susceptible woman [PMID:39263133].

** Contraindications
- Liver disease, or prior supplement-induced liver injury: USP labeling advises against use [PMID:32140423]. Re-exposure caused recurrence with shorter latency [PMID:32892374].
- Concentrated extracts on an empty stomach or during calorie restriction: exposure and toxicity rise [PMID:32438698] [PMID:18484782] [PMID:33584288].
- Bortezomib therapy: avoid supplemental EGCG [PMID:19190249] [PMID:39119616].
- Pregnancy: keep caffeine moderate [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/green-tea] [PMID:28438661]. No adequate safety data for high-dose extract.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Nadolol (OATP1A2 substrate): avoid co-ingestion. Green tea 700 mL/day lowered nadolol Cmax and AUC by about 85% and blunted its BP effect [PMID:24419562]. A single extract dose cut AUC to 0.60-0.72 of control [PMID:29480324]. One cup of brewed tea cut it to 0.37, and taking tea 1 h before still gave 0.54 [PMID:32320490]. A case of therapeutic failure has been reported [PMID:37951784].
- Lisinopril: extract with ~300 mg EGCG lowered lisinopril AUC to 0.34 and Cmax to 0.29 of control [PMID:33048477]. This could reduce antihypertensive effect.
- Atorvastatin: 300-600 mg extract lowered AUC by 22-24% [PMID:31997084]. Green tea inhibits OATP1B1/1B3 uptake of atorvastatin in vitro [PMID:26426900].
- Rosuvastatin: Extract with 800 mg EGCG for 14 days lowered AUC by about 20%, independent of ABCG2 genotype [PMID:35399656]. A single 300 mg EGCG dose lowered AUC by 19%, but after 10 days of pretreatment there was no effect [PMID:28533679].
- Other OATP/transport substrates: extract lowered fexofenadine exposure by 70% [PMID:35678032] and raloxifene AUC by about 40% [PMID:37639334]. Clinical studies reported 18-99% reductions for 10 drugs, and a 50% increase for sildenafil [PMID:39748104]. Cardiovascular-drug evidence was reviewed in 2026 [PMID:41943479].
- Warfarin: tea is a vitamin K source. A 0.5-1 gallon/day green tea habit lowered INR from 3.79 to 1.14, and INR recovered after stopping [PMID:10332534]. A systematic review lists green tea among warfarin-interacting foods/herbs [PMID:32478963].
- Bortezomib: EGCG neutralizes bortezomib and other boronic-acid proteasome inhibitors in vitro and in xenografts [PMID:19190249]. Gallated catechins antagonized bortezomib at tea-attainable concentrations in long-term assays [PMID:39119616]. Antagonism in mice needed EGCG plasma levels above 200 uM, far above supplement levels (0.6-3 uM) [PMID:21400028]. Conservative advice is to avoid supplements during therapy.
- Tacrolimus: one case of a supratherapeutic trough (18.3 ng/mL) with heavy home-made green tea, which normalized after stopping [PMID:42226872].
- Metformin: green tea and EGCG inhibit OCT1/2 and MATE1/2-K metformin uptake in vitro. No human data [PMID:26426900].
- Stimulants (dextroamphetamine): caffeine in extracts adds sympathetic and BP effects. How caffeine-containing products interact with amphetamines is unresolved [PMID:32033367] [PMID:16506807].
- Tamoxifen, simvastatin, fluvastatin and pseudoephedrine pharmacokinetics were not affected in clinical studies [PMID:39748104] [PMID:35678032].
*** Other supplements
- Iron: Tea taken with a meal reduced non-heme iron absorption by 37%. Waiting 1 h halved the inhibition [PMID:29046302]. Tea can block response to oral ferrous salts but has little effect on heme iron in vivo [PMID:8647516]. Heme iron intake is the recommended counter-strategy [PMID:11029010]. In vitro, EGCG still inhibits heme-iron transfer dose-dependently [PMID:22417433]. Separate doses of any iron product.
- Folate: Green tea extract (0.3 g) reduced folic acid AUC by 27% (0.4 mg dose) and 40% (5 mg dose) [PMID:18551467]. 720-800 mg EGCG/day for 30 days did not lower serum folate, regardless of MTHFR/DHFR genotype [PMID:40077973] [PMID:36678191]. No data exist for 5-MTHF (Quatrefolic). The evidence covers folic acid uptake and DHFR inhibition only [PMID:15781612] [PMID:17117344].
- Caffeine: additive with caffeine in extracts. Caffeine also raises EGCG exposure [PMID:19734672] [PMID:28438661].
- L-Theanine: theanine plus caffeine gave small acute cognition/mood benefits [PMID:40314930].
- Hepatotoxic botanicals: Turmeric/curcumin, green tea extract, Garcinia cambogia, black cohosh, red yeast rice and ashwagandha are the most reported hepatotoxic botanicals in the US [PMID:39102266]. Turmeric injury also clusters with HLA-B*35:01 (allele frequency 0.45 vs about 0.06) [PMID:36252717].
*** Conditions
- Liver disease, HLA-B*35:01 carriage, fasting or weight-loss diets: see Safety and Genotype notes [PMID:32892374] [PMID:33584288].
- Iron deficiency or anemia: separate tea/extract from iron-containing meals and supplements [PMID:29046302] [PMID:39263133].
- Hypertension: a small BP-lowering effect [PMID:40497293]. However, extracts may lower lisinopril and nadolol levels [PMID:33048477] [PMID:24419562].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. True changes include ALT/AST rises with high-dose extract [PMID:28765194], small LDL/total cholesterol falls [PMID:27806972] and PSA decreases [PMID:25873370]. These can confound monitoring.
- INR can fall in warfarin users [PMID:10332534]. Serum folate can fall after co-ingestion with folic acid [PMID:18551467].

** Genotype notes
- HLA-B*35:01: present in 72% of DILIN green tea liver-injury cases, vs 12% of drug-DILI cases and 11% of population controls [PMID:32892374]. NCCIH notes that 5-15% of Americans carry the variant [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/green-tea]. The 2026 DILI review lists it as a risk factor [PMID:42092331]. The allele is also over-represented in Garcinia-plus-green-tea injury (55%) [PMID:34400337].
- UGT1A4 rs6755571 A/C: ALT rose +78-82% at months 6-9 vs +28-30% in C/C on 843 mg EGCG/day (MGTT) [PMID:36178169].
- COMT rs4680: small AST:ALT ratio difference [PMID:36178169]. COMT genotype did not modify effects on adiposity, cytokines or mammographic density [PMID:26701796] [PMID:41515260] [PMID:28904061].
- SLCO1A2 (OATP1A2): in MIRACLE, non-carriers on extract had fewer adenomas (RR 0.82, CI 0.69-0.97). Carriers had no benefit [PMID:40433816].
- MRP2 and OATP1B1 polymorphisms partly explain variability in catechin pharmacokinetics [PMID:29466429].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] DILI review names HLA-B*35:01 as a risk factor for green tea extract injury [PMID:42092331]. A second review lists green tea extract among leading HDS causes in the US [PMID:42475234].
- [2026-07] Review of 2025 DILI causes includes green tea extract among newly recognized hepatotoxic HDS [PMID:42504634].
- [2026-06] Postmenopausal MA (7 RCTs): total cholesterol -7.03 mg/dL; no other effects; advises liver monitoring with high doses [PMID:42228178].
- [2026-04] Review of green tea pharmacokinetic interactions with cardiovascular drugs [PMID:41943479].
- [2026-01] MGTT secondary analysis: 843 mg EGCG/day for 1 year did not lower CRP, IL-6 or TNF-a [PMID:41515260].
- [2025-12] GRADE dose-response BP MA (36 RCTs): SBP -1.08 / DBP -1.09 mmHg [PMID:40497293].
- [2025-10] EGCG glycemic MA (41 RCTs): HbA1c -0.18%, judged not clinically meaningful [PMID:40885603]. Tea/theanine cognition MA [PMID:40314930].
- [2025-08] Cancer MA (7 RCTs, 36 cohorts): green tea RR 0.91, EGCG RR 0.72. Heterogeneity is high [PMID:40832777].
- [2025-07] Review: 72% of clinical PK analyses showed 18-99% lower drug exposure with green tea [PMID:39748104].
- [2025-06] Obesity dose-response MA: body weight -0.74 kg, BMI -0.29 [PMID:40326418].
- [2025-04] MIRACLE GWAS: SLCO1A2 variants modify adenoma prevention [PMID:40433816].
- [2025-03] EGCG 720 mg/day did not lower folate in reproductive-age women [PMID:40077973]. HILI review names green tea extract among the main agents [PMID:39136211].
- [2024-12] Exercise plus green tea MA: weight SMD -0.30; no lipid benefit [PMID:39350601].
- [2024-11] Tea/coffee NMA: green tea -1.23 kg vs placebo, low certainty [PMID:39497467]. T2DM MA: improved FBG, HbA1c and HOMA-IR [PMID:39809182].

** Open questions
- A safe EGCG dose for bolus supplements. EFSA reported transaminase rises at >=800 mg/day, and the industry-derived 338 mg/day figure needs independent confirmation [PMID:32625874] [PMID:29580974].
- Whether HLA-B*35:01 or UGT1A4 screening could identify at-risk users before high-dose trials [PMID:32892374] [PMID:36178169].
- Whether the 2012 Cochrane weight conclusion still holds against newer, lower-quality positive MAs [PMID:23235664] [PMID:40326418].
- Clinical consequences of transporter interactions with chronic dosing. Rosuvastatin data conflict [PMID:28533679] [PMID:35399656].
- Hard cardiovascular and cancer outcomes. No adequately powered RCTs exist [PMID:32118296].
