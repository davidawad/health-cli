** Identity & forms
- Heme iron polypeptide (HIP) is partially proteolyzed hemoglobin, about 93% heme iron and 7% non-heme iron [PMID:41984398]. Colorado Biolabs makes it from USDA-edible bovine red blood cells by enzymatic digestion and ultrafiltration [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/].
- Products: Proferrin ES (tablet) and Proferrin Clear (capsule) each contain 10.5 mg elemental iron as HIP. Proferrin Forte contains 12 mg iron as HIP plus 1 mg folic acid [URL:https://www.proferrin.com/] [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/].
- Proferrin was the HIP product in a bariatric-surgery RCT [PMID:37801789]. Heme plus non-heme combinations (e.g. Hemofer, 18 mg/day) [PMID:8145004] and heme-bound iron (Optifer) [PMID:30613538] are related products, and they dominate the heme-vs-non-heme trial base [PMID:39708071].
- Porcine heme-iron concentrates are used as food fortificants [PMID:27314581]. The bovine origin matters to people avoiding animal products [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/].

** Mechanisms
- Separate uptake pathways:
  - Heme is probably absorbed intact (receptor-mediated endocytosis or HCP1; no receptor proven) and degraded by heme oxygenase in the enterocyte [PMID:41984398] [PMID:40647237].
  - Non-heme iron uses DMT1. Both forms exit through ferroportin, which hepcidin controls [PMID:24994858].
- Food heme iron utilization was not affected by iron status or detectable hepcidin; ferrous sulfate utilization was [PMID:20980658]. Twelve weeks of ferrous sulfate down-regulated non-heme but not heme absorption [PMID:11010941].
- Ferrous sulfate did not compete with heme absorption. The fraction of heme absorbed fell as the dose rose from 0.5 to 50 mg [PMID:22935997].
- Tea, bran and ascorbic acid had no appreciable effect on heme absorption [PMID:4038429]. Calcium inhibits both heme and non-heme forms [PMID:1984335] [PMID:21795430].
- Polyphenols inhibited heme transport dose-dependently in Caco-2 cells, and ascorbic acid offset this only at low concentrations [PMID:22417433].
- Heme absorption is proposed to resist inflammation-driven hepcidin, which was the rationale for an infant trial in a high-inflammation setting [PMID:38641845]. In rodents, HIP corrected anemia as well as ferrous sulfate [PMID:41984398].
- In the gut lumen, 8 mg heme iron raised fecal N-nitroso compounds, while 35 mg ferrous iron did not [PMID:12750250]. N-nitrosation and lipoperoxidation are the leading proposed colorectal mechanisms [PMID:21209396].

** Dosing in trials
- HIP trials:
  - CKD: 11 mg three times daily [PMID:23514036], two capsules daily for 6 months [PMID:22962411], or HIP replacing IV iron in hemodialysis [PMID:12900815].
  - Gastric bypass: 31.5-94.5 mg/d [PMID:28779269] or 60.4 mg/d [PMID:37801789] for 8 weeks.
  - Infants: 10 mg/d for 84 days [PMID:40803493].
- Manufacturer guidance: 1-3 Proferrin ES tablets daily in pregnancy. It claims one tablet equals 2-3 ionic-iron tablets, but no trial confirms this ratio [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/].
- Hepcidin-based scheduling (ferrous sulfate data; untested for HIP):
  - Doses >=60 mg raise hepcidin for 24 h and cut next-day absorption by 35-45% [PMID:26289639]. Alternate-day dosing raises fractional absorption [PMID:29032957] [PMID:31413088].
  - A 6-month RCT found equal ferritin with fewer GI effects on alternate days [PMID:38021373]. A 2025 MA found no Hb difference (MD 0.28 g/dL) [PMID:40841680], and an LMIC pilot RCT favored daily dosing [PMID:41354563].
  - Whether these schedules matter for low-dose, hepcidin-insensitive heme iron is unknown [PMID:20980658].
- EFSA: safe level of 40 mg/day total iron for adults (no UL could be set) [PMID:38868106].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects:
  - Ferrous sulfate raises GI side effects vs placebo (OR 2.32) [PMID:25700159]. Heme formulations caused fewer total side effects (RR 0.62, very low certainty) [PMID:39708071], including less constipation in blood donors [PMID:8145004].
  - CKD trials found adverse events similar to comparators [PMID:22962411] [PMID:23514036].
  - Neither HIP nor ferrous sulfate increased systemic oxidative-stress markers after bariatric surgery [PMID:37801789].
- Iron overload: haemochromatosis loads the liver, pancreas and heart [PMID:29620054]. C282Y homozygous or compound heterozygous postmenopausal women with high heme intake had geometric mean ferritin 183.9 ug/L [PMID:16399493].
- Chronic-disease signals from dietary heme (T2D, colorectal cancer, CVD) come from cohorts [PMID:35107626] [PMID:23568532] [PMID:25439662]. Their relevance to 10-12 mg/d HIP in iron-deficient people is unstudied.
- Acute toxicity in children: iron poisoning is a major cause of unintentional poisoning death in young children. US deaths fell from 29 to 1 after unit-dose packaging [PMID:15939855]. Serious toxicity starts at about 28 mg/kg [PMID:30882955].
- Pregnancy: oral iron reduces maternal anemia (RR 0.30), with very uncertain adverse-effect data [PMID:39145520] [PMID:39834271]. HIP-specific pregnancy data are uncontrolled [PMID:30613538].
- Malaria-endemic settings: iron does not raise clinical malaria in children (RR 0.93, high certainty) [PMID:41510785].

** Contraindications
- Hereditary haemochromatosis and other iron-overload states [PMID:29620054] [PMID:16399493]. Iron-replete people should also avoid unmonitored long-term use, because stores rise and stay raised [PMID:11010941]. Only a safe level, not a UL, exists [PMID:38868106].
- Avoidance of bovine-derived products [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/].
- Post-Roux-en-Y iron deficiency: HIP failed where ferrous sulfate worked [PMID:28779269]. Heme absorption fell from 23.9% to 6.2% after surgery [PMID:22952172].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Levothyroxine:
  - Iron chelates levothyroxine and reduces its absorption [PMID:28153426]. Cases show raised dose needs on ferrous sulfate [PMID:9191742], with the interaction persisting despite 4-h separation [PMID:42438581].
  - Label: do not take within 4 h of iron [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/spls/8bc641d8-0185-49b2-9c1f-d886bf5ce098.xml].
- Fluoroquinolones: ferrous sulfate cut AUC by 73% (norfloxacin), 57% (ciprofloxacin) and 25% (ofloxacin) [PMID:8148225]. Label: ciprofloxacin 2 h before or 6 h after iron [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/spls/14c3bc33-201d-492e-9aee-a4d84c813a3d.xml].
- Tetracyclines/doxycycline: concurrent iron seriously impairs absorption [PMID:5483323] [PMID:2054263]. A 2026 case reported treatment failure [PMID:42148617].
- Levodopa/carbidopa: iron reduces levodopa absorption [PMID:2340448]. The label advises caution because of chelation [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/spls/9e3c796b-2e68-4bcc-b927-18a58bea4703.xml].
- Methyldopa: ferrous sulfate cut absorption from 29.1% to 7.9%, and BP rose in 4 of 5 patients [PMID:3356082].
- Mycophenolate: a 90% AUC drop in healthy volunteers [PMID:11180021] was not seen in transplant RCTs or crossover studies [PMID:14742982] [PMID:16995871].
- Bisphosphonates: multivalent cations impair alendronate absorption, so wait at least 30 min after the dose [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/spls/4323d3aa-cbb0-41e2-aba7-7a94a0516993.xml].
- PPIs: associated with iron deficiency anemia (RR 2.56) [PMID:35255801]. The effect on heme absorption is unknown.
- The manufacturer claims "no known interactions" [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/]. Chelation interactions apply to iron generally [PMID:2054263], and no HIP-specific PK studies exist.
*** Other supplements
- Calcium:
  - 165 mg reduced heme and non-heme absorption in single meals [PMID:1984335]. 800 mg cut heme absorption by 37.7% [PMID:21795430].
  - 600 mg/d for 34 days did not change heme bioavailability [PMID:24290597]. An MA found small acute effects (WMD -5.57%) and no Hb loss [PMID:33758936].
- Magnesium: magnesium oxide and calcium carbonate in prenatal multivitamins lowered iron absorption [PMID:6823378].
- Zinc: zinc inhibits non-heme iron at >=1:1 ratios [PMID:22297381], and iron lowers zinc absorption [PMID:14594790]. Heme iron did not inhibit zinc absorption [PMID:7223699].
- Curcumin and Turmeric: curcumin chelates iron, represses hepcidin and caused iron deficiency in mice [PMID:18815282]. A human case of IDA on high-dose turmeric resolved after stopping it [PMID:30899609]. A formulated curcumin increased iron uptake in vitro [PMID:40613696].
- Vitamin C: no effect on heme absorption in humans [PMID:4038429]. It offsets low-level polyphenol inhibition in vitro [PMID:22417433]. Tea and polyphenols: tea had little effect on heme absorption in humans [PMID:4038429]. High polyphenol levels inhibited heme transport in vitro [PMID:22417433], and a polyphenol supplement cut non-heme absorption by about 40% [PMID:35320401].
- Prebiotics: a prebiotic mix raised heme iron bioavailability by 56% [PMID:28485415]. Non-heme iron: does not compete with heme [PMID:22935997]. Combined heme plus non-heme iron increased uptake in vitro [PMID:41595579].
*** Conditions
- Haemochromatosis and HFE variants: see Contraindications and Genotype notes.
- CKD/dialysis: no advantage for HIP, and ferritin is lower [PMID:25953602] [PMID:22962411].
- Bariatric surgery: poor HIP response [PMID:28779269] [PMID:22952172].
- Young children in the home: accidental overdose risk [PMID:15939855].
- T2D risk: dietary heme intake tracks with insulinemia and inflammation markers [PMID:39138340].
*** Lab-test interference
- Guaiac fecal occult blood: the manufacturer warns of possible false positives on guaiac tests but not on FIT [URL:https://proferrin.com/faq/]. Ferrous sulfate did not make Hemoccult II tests positive in vivo [PMID:2909440].
- Black stools reflect unabsorbed iron [PMID:38868106].
- Serum ferritin: HIP gives lower ferritin than comparators at similar Hb, which complicates interpretation [PMID:25953602] [PMID:23514036].

** Genotype notes
- HFE: carriers of H63D or C282Y showed higher T2D odds with rising heme intake (P-interaction 0.029); wild-type women did not [PMID:16306377].
- C282Y heterozygotes did not absorb heme or non-heme iron more than controls; compound heterozygotes absorbed more non-heme (not heme) iron [PMID:15447900].
- In C282Y homozygotes, a polyphenol supplement reduced non-heme absorption by about 40% [PMID:35320401].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] HIP (Proferrin) corrected iron-deficiency anemia in rats and mice as well as ferrous sulfate [PMID:41984398].
- [2026-07] Case: iron-levothyroxine interaction despite 4-h separation, resolved with IV iron [PMID:42438581].
- [2026-06] Cochrane review, blood donors (38 RCTs): oral iron probably reduces low-Hb deferral (RR 0.39, moderate certainty); GI adverse events RR 1.69 [PMID:42240175].
- [2026-05] Case report: iron-doxycycline interaction with treatment failure in chronic Q fever [PMID:42148617].
- [2026-01] Cochrane, malaria-endemic children (40 trials): iron does not raise clinical malaria (RR 0.93) [PMID:41510785].
- [2026-01] Pilot RCT: daily 60 mg beat 120 mg alternate-day dosing over 14 days [PMID:41354563].
- [2025-10] Gambian infant RCT (n=208): HIP not superior on Hb or ferritin, better on 5 secondary iron markers [PMID:40803493].
- [2025-09] RCT: a beef "meat factor" did not improve ferrous sulfate response [PMID:40633767].
- [2025-08] MA of daily vs alternate-day dosing (11 RCTs): no significant Hb difference; very low certainty [PMID:40841680].
- [2025-01] MA in non-anaemic pregnancy: oral iron halved anemia risk (RR 0.50) [PMID:39834271].
- [2024-12] First SR/MA of heme vs non-heme RCTs: side effects RR 0.62; Hb benefit only in anemic children; very low certainty [PMID:39708071].

** Open questions
- No adequately powered RCT has compared HIP with low-dose or alternate-day ferrous salts in iron-deficient adults.
- It is unclear why HIP lowers ferritin relative to comparators (absorption, storage or assay) [PMID:25953602].
- Does HIP keep its advantage under inflammation (CKD, obesity, infection), as the Gambian secondary outcomes suggest [PMID:40803493]?
- Long-term N-nitrosation and T2D risk from supplemental (not dietary) heme iron [PMID:12750250] [PMID:39138340].
- No HIP-specific drug-interaction PK studies exist. Several HIP trials are open-label, and the product costs more [PMID:22962411].
