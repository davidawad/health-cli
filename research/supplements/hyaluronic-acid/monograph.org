** Identity & forms
- Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a non-sulfated glycosaminoglycan made of repeating D-glucuronic acid / N-acetyl-D-glucosamine disaccharides. It is most concentrated in synovial fluid, and the skin holds about half of the body's HA [PMID:38234616] [PMID:25014997].
- Sources: rooster/chicken comb extracts (Hyal-Joint, Mobilee, Oralvisc) or bacterial fermentation, mainly Streptococcus zooepidemicus [PMID:18208600] [PMID:25024048] [PMID:35436410].
- Molecular weight (MW) varies widely across oral products: 2 kDa to 1.8 MDa in trials, plus "full-spectrum" blends [PMID:28761365] [PMID:41422283] [PMID:34933842]. MW changes the biology. High-MW HA tends to be anti-inflammatory, while small fragments can signal through CD44, RHAMM and TLRs [PMID:30724463].
- Routes with different evidence (*do not conflate them*): oral supplement (OTC, the subject of this monograph); intra-articular (IA) injection ("viscosupplementation", a medical device; context only); topical, eye-drop and dermal-filler products (context only).
- Many oral products combine HA with collagen, chondroitin, glucosamine, vitamin C or botanicals, so HA's own effect often cannot be isolated [PMID:29228816] [PMID:22956862] [PMID:33592868].

** Mechanisms
- Skin: HA's water-binding capacity is central to skin moisture, and skin HA content changes with age [PMID:23467280].
- Oral absorption: intact high-MW HA is poorly absorbed. Gut Bacteroides spp. cleave it into oligosaccharides (<3 kDa) that are partly absorbed or fermented to short-chain fatty acids (SCFA); germ-free mice absorbed almost none. Bioavailability of 13C-HA was about 0.2%, so the authors propose systemic signalling rather than direct delivery to skin or joints [PMID:37182970].
  - In rats, HA was degraded by caecal contents but not by gastric or intestinal juice. Oligosaccharides crossed the large intestine, and HA fragments were found in skin [PMID:27982756].
  - Earlier radiolabel studies in rats and dogs reported distribution to joints, skin and bone [PMID:18959406] [PMID:25383371]. A label is not proof that intact HA was absorbed [PMID:37182970].
  - In mice, 5 kDa HA at high oral doses raised plasma and tissue HA [PMID:37081790].
- Gut-epithelium signalling: oral 900 kDa HA bound colonic TLR4, raised SOCS3 and IL-10 and reduced autoimmune lymphoproliferation in mice [PMID:20504769]. 35 kDa fragments induced beta-defensin 2 [PMID:22761444] and protected tight junctions in alcohol-exposed mice [PMID:36574533]. Reviews: [PMID:31320101] [PMID:36876779].
- Gut microbiota (preclinical, size-dependent): high-MW HA shifted the microbiota and eased collagen-induced arthritis in rats, and faecal transplant reproduced the benefit [PMID:42678376]. Oral HA restored microbiota and SCFA in a Parkinson's mouse model [PMID:42589230]. In human faecal fermentation, 6.9-9.5 kDa HA gave more butyrate than 13-35 kDa HA [PMID:42197407].
- The only human microbiome data: a 7-day pilot of HA35 (140 mg/d, n=20) found no stool-microbiome change [PMID:31121841].
- Joints (oral): rat models show lower synovial cytokines and MMPs, more so with high-MW HA [PMID:41479667]. In humans, oral Mobilee changed blood-cell expression of genes related to cartilage metabolism [PMID:25024048].
- Photoaging models: oral HA reduced UV-induced HA/collagen breakdown in hairless mice. Adding collagen at a 12-fold higher dose gave no extra benefit [PMID:40716543]. 200-600 kDa HA performed best in D-galactose-aged mice [PMID:40857168].

** Dosing in trials
- Skin: 120 mg/d is the most common dose, for 6-12 weeks [PMID:25834304] [PMID:28761365] [PMID:34203487]. Other doses: 60-120 mg/d of 1.8 MDa sodium hyaluronate [PMID:41422283], 200 mg/d FS-HA for 28 days [PMID:34933842], and 60 mg/d of an HA matrix [PMID:40498387].
- MW in skin RCTs: 2 kDa and 300 kDa both reduced wrinkles, but only 300 kDa beat placebo at week 8 [PMID:28761365]. Both 300 kDa and 800 kDa improved moisture [PMID:25834304].
- Knee: oral doses of 30-300 mg/d over 4 weeks to 12 months [PMID:39886281]. Examples: 200 mg/d for 12 months with quadriceps exercise [PMID:23226979]; 80 mg/d rooster-comb extract (Hyal-Joint/Mobilee) [PMID:18208600] [PMID:26302034]; 111 mg/d in healthy knees [PMID:38234616]; 50 mg HA + 750 mg glucosamine + 250 mg chondroitin liquid [PMID:34622845].
- Oralvisc (3 months, n=40, obese knee OA) improved pain/function and lowered synovial cytokines [PMID:24899570]. An oral HA course gave similar 3-month gains to an IA series in an unblinded comparison [PMID:27681813].
- IA products (context): usually a series of 1-5 injections. Effects plateau at about 2 months [PMID:40222628]. High and ultra-high MW products ranked best in a network MA [PMID:39857759].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Oral: trials of up to 12 months report few AEs and no product-related serious AEs [PMID:23226979] [PMID:25834304] [PMID:40498387]. Liver enzymes, CK and CRP were unchanged at 12 weeks [PMID:38234616]; blood/urine indices were unchanged in a 2025 trial [PMID:41479667]; HA35 140 mg/d caused no laboratory changes [PMID:31121841].
- A supplement MA found oral skin supplements safe over <=24 weeks [PMID:40761858]. Data beyond 1 year are lacking.
- Industry funding: most oral RCTs are run or funded by manufacturers (Kewpie, Contipro, Bioiberica, Bloomage) [PMID:34203487] [PMID:41422283] [PMID:40498387] [PMID:38009035].
- IA injection (context): transient local reactions in about 8.5% [PMID:27492466]; more serious AEs than placebo in large-trial MAs [PMID:36333100] [PMID:22868835] [PMID:31073925]; across 51,146 HA-treated patients, AE rate 10.5%, severe AEs 0.7%, infection 0.1% [PMID:41451630].
- Dermal fillers (context):
  - Vascular occlusion can cause blindness. In a systematic review of 34 reports, vision loss was the commonest ocular complication and 45% showed no improvement after treatment [PMID:35091771]. Consensus advice for managing filler-induced vision loss: [PMID:35096260].
  - Delayed inflammatory reactions: about 1 per 2000 patient-years, varying by crosslinking technology [PMID:42781409]; an earlier estimate was 1.1%/year [PMID:31410442]. Proposed triggers: low-MW HA, BDDE crosslinker residues, impurities, biofilm, and immune triggers such as infections and vaccines [PMID:38907876] [PMID:38425859].
- Allergy: rooster-comb-derived products may contain avian protein, while fermentation-derived HA avoids this [PMID:18208600] [PMID:35436410]. No oral HA anaphylaxis reports were found.

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindication to oral HA has been established. Pregnancy and lactation data are absent: no trials were found.
- Known hypersensitivity to the product or its source (avian) [PMID:18208600].
- Active cancer: no human data show harm from oral HA. However, the HA/CD44 axis promotes tumour stemness and progression [PMID:37958796] [PMID:35649255], so caution is a theoretical precaution. By contrast, high-MW HA underlies cancer resistance in naked mole-rats and reduced cancer in transgenic mice [PMID:23783513] [PMID:37612507].
- Fillers (context): extra caution with active autoimmune disease, recent dental work or infection [PMID:38907876]. A systematic review of systemic sclerosis/morphea reported mild AEs and no flares [PMID:41730404].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- No pharmacokinetic drug interaction for oral HA was found in the literature.
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets: no data for oral HA. For injections, joint procedures at INR >=2 had 0.2% significant bleeding [PMID:22340924]. No clinically significant bleeding occurred in 237 people on anticoagulants or antiplatelets [PMID:36806477]. Filler bruising can be delayed and HA-related [PMID:30789524].
- Hyaluronidase: dissolves HA fillers and is the main rescue for complications. Allergy is possible, especially in people allergic to bee or wasp stings [PMID:34840662] [PMID:37771016]. Retrobulbar use is controversial [PMID:35096260].
- Antibiotics (theoretical): absorption depends on Bacteroides, and germ-free mice did not absorb HA [PMID:37182970] [PMID:27982756]. Antibiotics could therefore blunt uptake. No human data.
- NSAIDs/analgesics: oral FS-HA reduced rescue NSAID use in knee OA [PMID:32650511].
- Proton-pump inhibitors: an HA-chondroitin bioadhesive (Esoxx) plus PPI improved non-erosive reflux symptoms (52.6% vs 32.1% responders) [PMID:28116754].
- IA corticosteroids (context): HA plus corticosteroid had the largest modelled effect in a model-based MA [PMID:40222628].
- Vaccines and zoledronic acid (filler context): case reports of delayed filler reactions after mRNA COVID-19 vaccines [PMID:35384252] [PMID:34174156] and after IV zoledronic acid [PMID:38895606].
*** Other supplements
- Collagen: often co-formulated. Chicken-cartilage matrices (collagen II + HA + chondroitin) improved OA symptoms (2 g/d, n=80) [PMID:22486722] and facial dryness (open-label) [PMID:22956862]. In mice, adding collagen gave no benefit beyond HA [PMID:40716543]. Collagen alone has its own skin MA (hydration SMD 1.25) [PMID:40826844].
- Glucosamine Sulphate 2KCL: glucosamine is a substrate for HA synthesis [PMID:17716251] [PMID:14651444]. An HA + glucosamine + chondroitin liquid helped mild knee pain [PMID:33592868] but not moderate pain [PMID:34622845]. No trial isolates an additive effect.
- Vitamin C: claimed synergy for collagen/HA. The only human evidence is an uncontrolled 40-day study of a product with HA, vitamin C, biotin, copper and zinc [PMID:29228816], plus a rat wound study of topical HA with oral vitamin C [PMID:42501143]. This is weak.
- Green tea extract/EGCG: an uncontrolled study of an EGCG + folate + B12 + HA (50 mg) tablet for HPV clearance [PMID:40508059]. HA's contribution is unknown.
*** Conditions
- Knee OA: oral evidence is low certainty [PMID:39886281]; an industry-linked review is more positive [PMID:26818459]. Guidelines address only IA HA: OARSI rates it Level 1B/2 for knee OA depending on comorbidity, and not recommended for hip OA [PMID:31278997].
- Cancer or CD44-high tumours: theoretical concern; see Contraindications [PMID:37958796] [PMID:30724463].
- Obesity: circulating low-MW HA fragments are raised and pro-inflammatory via TLR [PMID:35896710]. It is unknown whether low-MW supplements add to this.
- Chronic liver disease: serum HA is elevated, so HA cannot be used as a marker without considering intake [PMID:9711881] [PMID:28579340].
*** Lab-test interference
- Serum HA is a liver-fibrosis marker: pooled sensitivity/specificity for cirrhosis 83%/89% in post-HCV disease [PMID:28579340]. It is one of three components (HA, PIIINP, TIMP-1) of the Enhanced Liver Fibrosis (ELF) test [PMID:40838567], which predicts liver outcomes [PMID:26104018] and had AUROC 0.81 across chronic liver disease [PMID:36650720]. 2026 MASLD MA cut-offs: <9.00 rules out >=F2; >=9.80 suggests >=F3 [PMID:42638152].
- Serum HA rises after meals, especially in cirrhosis and type 2 diabetes, so it should be measured fasting [PMID:9711881] [PMID:22592631].
- Oral supplements: no study tested whether oral HA raises serum HA or ELF in humans. Oral bioavailability of about 0.2% suggests little effect [PMID:37182970], but mouse data show plasma HA rises with high-dose low-MW HA [PMID:37081790]. A cautious approach is to stop it before testing.
- Serum HA is also a knee-OA progression marker [PMID:26453426]. Effects of supplements on it are untested.

- [2026-09] Rat arthritis: high-MW oral HA acts via the microbiota-gut-joint axis, and faecal transplant reproduces the benefit [PMID:42678376].
- [2026-09] Filler DIR MA (242 studies): about 0.0005 events per patient-year [PMID:42781409].
- [2026-07] Oral sodium hyaluronate remodels microbiota and is neuroprotective in MPTP mice [PMID:42589230].
- [2026-04] Fermentation model: 6.9-9.5 kDa HA yields more butyrate [PMID:42197407].
- [2026-03] HA fillers in scleroderma: mild AEs, no flares [PMID:41730404].
- [2026-03] Dry-eye drop MA (39 RCTs): 0.15-0.18% concentrations work best [PMID:41887128].
- [2025-12] Largest oral skin RCT (n=150, 1.8 MDa, 120 mg/d): better hydration, TEWL, elasticity and wrinkle depth [PMID:41422283].
- [2025-12] Oral HA + wheat-oil extract RCT (n=63): fewer crow's-feet wrinkles [PMID:41071523].
- [2025-12] Rat MW comparison plus clinical trial: oral HA lowered WOMAC [PMID:41479667].
- [2025-12] Umbrella review of IA HA: 20/22 reviews positive, but only 5 were of high or moderate quality [PMID:41240470]. Safety MA: [PMID:41451630].
- [2025-09] MA of 7 oral HA skin RCTs: hydration, elasticity and wrinkle depth improved [PMID:40911749].
- [2025-09] Mouse studies: oral HA counters photoaging [PMID:40716543], and 200-600 kDa is best against D-galactose aging [PMID:40857168].
- [2025-07] Supplement photoaging MA: HA not effective for MED/R2 [PMID:40761858].
- [2025-06] HA matrix RCT (60 mg/d, n=60): better wrinkles, smoothness and roughness [PMID:40498387].
- [2025-04] IA HA model-based MA (168 studies): plateau at about 2 months; greater benefit in age <65 and KL <=2 [PMID:40222628].
- [2025-01] Large-trial NMA: IA HA SMD -0.04 for pain; serious AEs OR 1.86 [PMID:39265924]. MW NMA: [PMID:39857759].
- [2024-12] Oral HA in OA/low-back-pain SR (11 studies) [PMID:39886281]. IA HA vs placebo MA: [PMID:39598466].

** Open questions
- Whether the oral effects are real and clinically meaningful. Trials are small, mostly manufacturer-funded, use different MWs, and few are independent or preregistered [PMID:40911749].
- The mechanism, given about 0.2% bioavailability: gut TLR4 signalling, SCFA, or absorbed oligosaccharides [PMID:37182970] [PMID:20504769].
- The optimal MW for skin vs joints, and whether low-MW fragments are pro-inflammatory in vivo [PMID:30724463] [PMID:35896710].
- How microbiome composition and antibiotics affect response, which is untested in humans [PMID:37182970] [PMID:31121841].
- Effect of oral HA on serum HA and ELF results.
- Long-term (>1 year) safety, pregnancy, and use in cancer.
- Oral knee trials beyond 12 months with structural endpoints.
