** Identity & forms
- Inulin is a chain of fructose units linked by beta(2-1) bonds. It is readily fermented by intestinal bacteria into large amounts of short-chain fatty acids (SCFA) [PMID:31707507].
- "Inulin-type fructans" (ITF) is the umbrella term for short-chain fructooligosaccharides (scFOS), oligofructose and inulin. They differ in chain length (degree of polymerization, DP) [PMID:34555168].
- Commercial supplements are mostly chicory-root extracts:
  - native or long-chain inulin;
  - oligofructose (short-chain, DP < 10);
  - oligofructose-enriched inulin (e.g. Synergy1, a mix of short and long chains) [PMID:17298707] [PMID:16087995].
  - Jerusalem artichoke inulin has also been used in pediatric obesity trials [PMID:39033197].
- Inulin is not digested in the small intestine. In ileostomy patients 88% of ingested inulin was recovered in the effluent [PMID:9023459].
- Chain length matters for some outcomes. Short-chain (DP < 10) fructans increased stool frequency, while long-chain (DP >= 10) fructans did not [PMID:30621208].
- Exogenous inulin (and sinistrin, Inutest) given intravenously is a separate use. Urinary inulin clearance is the reference ("gold standard") method for measuring GFR [PMID:32750458] [PMID:11853197].

** Mechanisms
- Bifidogenic effect: chicory ITF at 3-20 g/d increased Bifidobacterium abundance (SMD 0.83, 95% CI 0.58-1.08; 50 studies, n=2525) [PMID:35833477].
  - Human microbiome studies also show more Anaerostipes, Faecalibacterium and Lactobacillus, and less Bacteroides [PMID:31707507] [PMID:34555168].
  - The rise in Bifidobacterium is larger when baseline counts are low [PMID:17268410] [PMID:15585783].
  - In the Food4Gut trial, the large rise in Bifidobacterium was a signature of inulin intake rather than a driver of the clinical outcomes [PMID:32340903].
- SCFA production: a stable-isotope RCT (30 g/d for 7 d) found butyrate production up 44% in both young and older adults [PMID:40274191]. A systematic review found that microbiota shifts were not consistently matched by higher fecal SCFA [PMID:31707507].
- Laxation: fermentation increases stool frequency and softens stools [PMID:25208775] [PMID:35833477].
- Mineral absorption: ITF increase calcium absorption mainly in the colon. In responders, about 70% of the extra absorption occurred more than 7 h after dosing [PMID:17884999]. Proposed mechanisms include higher calcium solubility, more calbindin and a larger colonic absorptive surface [PMID:33611985].
- Gas and symptoms: inulin raises colonic gas and volume on MRI in both IBS patients and controls. Symptoms in IBS reflect colonic hypersensitivity to distension rather than excess gas production [PMID:27746233].
- Gut barrier: prebiotics lowered circulating lipopolysaccharide in 16 RCTs (SMD -0.88), with high heterogeneity [PMID:40378939].
- Liver (preclinical): in dysbiotic mice (TLR5-deficient and others), dietary inulin caused microbiota-dependent cholestasis and then hepatocellular carcinoma. Blocking fermentation or depleting fermenting bacteria prevented it [PMID:30340040]. Vancomycin prevented the cancer, but not the cholestatic liver injury, and the effect tracked loss of secondary bile acids [PMID:32223398].

** Dosing in trials
- Constipation: 12 g/d chicory inulin (3 x 4 g) for 4 weeks [PMID:27492975] [PMID:41233756]. IBS-C: 9.2 g/d inulin/oligofructose 50:50 for 8 weeks [PMID:41034384].
- Bifidogenic effect: 5 g/d was already bifidogenic; 8 g/d gave more responders [PMID:17268410]. 3 g/d and 7 g/d ITF in snack bars both raised Bifidobacterium [PMID:32320024]. The meta-analytic range is 3-20 g/d [PMID:35833477].
- Glycemia: about 10 g/d for at least 6 weeks was the dose suggested by dose-response modeling [PMID:31805963].
- Weight: 16 g/d native inulin plus diet for 3 months [PMID:32340903]. 8 g/d oligofructose-enriched inulin for 16 weeks in children [PMID:28596023]. Meta-regression found little evidence of a dose or duration effect [PMID:39313030].
- Calcium absorption: 8 g/d Synergy1-type ITF in adolescents [PMID:16087995]. 15 g/d oligofructose [PMID:10075343].
- High-dose research use: 30 g/d for 7 days [PMID:40274191]. 16-32 g/d in ICU patients [PMID:39806400].
- Tolerance-based dosing: native inulin up to 10 g/d and oligofructose up to 5 g/d were well tolerated after a single challenge [PMID:20497775]. Splitting the dose reduced early breath hydrogen but not 24-h totals [PMID:39732438].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects are dose-related: mainly flatulence, then bloating, rumbling and abdominal pain.
  - Over 2 weeks the rise in symptoms was mild and significant only at 20 g/d of one product [PMID:18608562].
  - 10 g of oligofructose in one challenge clearly increased symptoms [PMID:20497775].
  - 8 g/d was well tolerated in people with gas-related complaints [PMID:27680592].
- In IBS, ITF worsened flatulence (SMD +0.85) [PMID:30949662]. Fructans raised daily pain episodes in children with IBS (3.4 vs 2.4), and 52% of children qualified as fructan sensitive [PMID:28970147].
- FOS-fructans (2.1 g/d) caused more GI symptoms than gluten in self-reported non-celiac gluten/wheat sensitivity [PMID:39227818].
- Allergy: case reports describe IgE-mediated anaphylaxis to inulin in foods [PMID:10798950] [PMID:15650313] and to intravenous sinistrin used for GFR testing [PMID:11853197].
- Liver (animal signal only): fermentable-fiber-induced cholestatic HCC occurred in dysbiotic mice [PMID:30340040] [PMID:32223398]. No human signal was found, but the authors advise caution about enriching foods with fermentable fiber.
- Colorectal: a 2024 review notes limited clinical evidence that inulin prevents colorectal cancer and that improper use may harm GI health in some settings [PMID:38984069].
- Industry funding: several trials and the weight meta-analysis involved chicory-ITF manufacturers (BENEO, Sensus) [PMID:39313030] [PMID:41233756].

** Contraindications
- Known inulin or chicory/artichoke allergy (anaphylaxis reported) [PMID:15650313] [PMID:10798950].
- Fructan-sensitive IBS or a diagnosed fructan intolerance: symptoms can worsen. About 49% of IBS patients tested positive for fructan malabsorption on breath testing [PMID:38654193] [PMID:28970147].
- Hereditary fructose intolerance: management requires strict avoidance of fructose, sucrose and sorbitol [PMID:36052111]. Inulin itself mostly reaches the colon undigested [PMID:9023459]. Fructanase enzyme products, however, hydrolyze fructans to fructose [PMID:42580594], so they should not be combined with inulin in HFI.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Metformin (incl. Metformin ER): in Food4Gut, prior metformin use blunted most inulin-related microbiota and metabolic gains [PMID:32340903]. A prebiotic during metformin start-up was well tolerated in a 6-patient pilot [PMID:36909343]. A GI microbiome modulator improved metformin tolerance in a 10-patient crossover [PMID:25802471]. Additive GI effects are possible.
- Antidiabetic drugs and insulin: ITF lower FBG and HbA1c in T2D [PMID:31805963]. In type 1 diabetes, 6 g/d inulin reduced daily insulin need by 1.5 U vs control [PMID:41636295]. Glucose monitoring is reasonable; the size of the effect is small.
- Acarbose: acarbose sends undigested carbohydrate to the colon and causes fermentation-related flatulence [PMID:9663365]. ITF also increase flatulence [PMID:30949662]. Additive gas and bloating are plausible; no direct study exists.
- Vancomycin and other gut-active antibiotics: in mice, vancomycin abolished fiber-induced HCC by depleting fermenting bacteria [PMID:32223398]. The human ICU trial with broad-spectrum antibiotics showed no inulin effect on the microbiome [PMID:39806400].
*** Other supplements
- GOS: also bifidogenic [PMID:15585783]. Unlike ITF, beta-GOS improved IBS symptom scores [PMID:42600900], and non-ITF prebiotics reduced flatulence [PMID:30949662]. Stacking adds fermentable load.
- Psyllium or methylcellulose gels: reduced inulin-induced colonic gas in IBS and in healthy volunteers [PMID:34353864] [PMID:42728685] [PMID:39732438].
- Fructanase: reduced abdominal pain, bloating and flatulence after a 25 g inulin challenge (industry-funded) [PMID:42580594].
- Butyrate products (ProButyrate, Tributryn): inulin increases endogenous butyrate production [PMID:40274191]. Sodium butyrate plus inulin was tested together in T2D without safety issues [PMID:32315958].
- Green Tea Extract: catechins 400 mg plus inulin 2.3 g/d improved HOMA-IR but not visceral fat (Kao-funded) [PMID:41830022].
- Calcium and Magnesium: ITF raise fractional absorption [PMID:16087995] [PMID:17298707]. An ileostomy study found no effect on small-intestinal mineral excretion [PMID:9023459].
- Vitamin D-3: oligofructose-enriched inulin raised 25(OH)D by 42% in children with celiac disease [PMID:30445682].
*** Conditions
- IBS, fructan sensitivity, non-celiac gluten/wheat sensitivity: see Safety and Contraindications [PMID:28970147] [PMID:39227818].
- IBD: no remission benefit, and more flatulence and bloating [PMID:38781004].
- Dysbiosis or cholestatic liver disease: preclinical HCC signal only [PMID:30340040].
- Constipation, prediabetes/T2D, overweight: likely benefit populations (see Evidence summary).
*** Lab-test interference
- Hydrogen breath tests: inulin raises breath hydrogen within hours [PMID:27746233] [PMID:28970147]. Breath tests define maldigestion as a rise of >= 20 ppm [PMID:28323273], so recent inulin intake could confound fructan or fructose breath testing (inferred).
- Inulin-clearance GFR: no source shows that oral inulin interferes with exogenous inulin or sinistrin GFR measurement. Oral inulin is largely unabsorbed [PMID:9023459]. Anaphylaxis to IV sinistrin has been reported, which matters for people with inulin allergy [PMID:11853197].
- True physiological changes (not assay artifacts): lower FBG/HbA1c [PMID:31805963], LDL/TG [PMID:38309832] and higher 25(OH)D [PMID:30445682].

** Genotype notes
- Vitamin D receptor (VDR) FokI polymorphism: in a 1-year RCT in adolescents, those with the ff genotype had the smallest initial calcium-absorption response to ITF [PMID:16087995]. No pharmacogenomic sources were found.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] RCT (n=22): methylcellulose and psyllium gels reduced colonic gas from 15 g inulin once the gels reached the colon [PMID:42728685].
- [2026-09] Review: rapidly fermentable prebiotics act as both "beneficial prebiotics" and "symptom-provoking FODMAPs" depending on the host [PMID:42762890].
- [2026-08] Fiber-in-IBS MA (30 RCTs): fiber response RR 1.21, driven by psyllium; ITF did not improve symptom scores [PMID:42600900].
- [2026-08] Fructanase crossover RCT (n=30): fewer pain, bloating and flatulence symptoms after a 25 g inulin challenge [PMID:42580594].
- [2026-08] IBS-C network MA: inulin-based composite foods improved QoL (MD 5.40) [PMID:41217200].
- [2026-06] INGUTSKIN RCT in psoriasis (15 g/d, 8 wk): stable fasting glucose vs a rise on placebo; no body-composition change [PMID:42356231].
- [2026-04] RCT in students (4.9 g/d FOS-inulin): no effect on GAD-7 anxiety [PMID:41989194].
- [2026-03] Catechin plus inulin RCT: HOMA-IR -0.32 vs +0.18; no visceral-fat change [PMID:41830022].
- [2026-01] Type 1 diabetes pilot RCT: inulin 6 g/d lowered weight (-2 kg) and insulin need, with no HbA1c change [PMID:41636295].
- [2025-11] Functional constipation crossover RCT (12 g/d, n=39): higher stool frequency and QoL; carry-over bias noted [PMID:41233756].
- [2025-10] IBS-C RCT (9.2 g/d, n=34): IBS-SSS and QoL improved [PMID:41034384].
- [2025-07] Inulin vs FOS RCT (n=131): inulin improved OGTT glucose only in overweight/obese adults [PMID:40598275].
- [2025-07] Prebiotics and BP MA: SBP -8.5 mmHg in hypertensives [PMID:40806090].
- [2025-05] Corrigendum published for the 2024 chicory ITF weight MA [PMID:40414749].
- [2025-04] MASLD RCT (16 g/d, 24 wk): no change in liver fat; greater trunk-fat loss and lower hunger [PMID:40172664].
- [2025-04] Isotope RCT: inulin raised butyrate production 44% in young and older adults [PMID:40274191].
- [2025-01] ICU sepsis phase 2 RCT: no microbiome or clinical benefit [PMID:39806400].
- [2024-12] Divided-dose inulin vs psyllium RCT: psyllium delays inulin fermentation rather than reducing it [PMID:39732438].
- [2024-11] Chicory ITF weight MA (32 studies): -0.97 kg [PMID:39313030].

** Open questions
- Clinically meaningful constipation benefit: ACG grading remains "insufficient". Larger, independent RCTs in diagnosed chronic constipation are needed.
- Many positive trials and meta-analyses are funded by industry. Independent replication of the weight and glycemic effects is limited.
- Who benefits: responses depend on baseline microbiota, Bifidobacterium counts and possibly VDR genotype.
- Whether the mouse cholestatic HCC signal is relevant to humans with dysbiosis or cholestatic liver disease.
- Interaction with metformin: does metformin blunt the benefit in humans, or does inulin improve metformin tolerance?
- Long-term (> 1 year) safety and efficacy data at supplement doses above 15 g/d.
