** Identity & forms
- Iodine is an essential component of thyroid hormones T4 and T3. Potassium iodide (KI) supplies iodide; supplement dose is expressed as micrograms iodine. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]

** Mechanisms
- Proposed biological activity and the best-established physiological context are described above; clinical mechanisms remain uncertain where outcome evidence is limited. ["URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/"]

** Dosing in trials
- Adult RDA 150 mcg/day; trials/clinical use depend on deficiency and life stage. Adult UL 1,100 mcg/day. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Excess may cause goiter, thyroiditis, hypo- or hyperthyroidism; susceptible thyroid disease increases risk. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]

** Contraindications
- Thyroid disorders and pregnancy require avoiding excess and considering iodine status. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- KI with ACE inhibitors or potassium-sparing diuretics can cause hyperkalemia; antithyroid drugs may interact. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed; no genotype-specific claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2025–2026 literature: 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis evaluated thyroid function under mild-to-moderate iodine deficiency. [PMID:40633808]

** Open questions
- Whether findings translate into durable patient-important outcomes, optimal formulation/dose, and long-term safety remains uncertain. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/; PMID:40633808]
