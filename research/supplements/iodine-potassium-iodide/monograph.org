** Identity & forms
- Iodine is an essential trace element and a structural part of the thyroid hormones thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3). Its only known function in humans is as a component of thyroid hormone [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:40633808].
- Supplement forms: potassium iodide or sodium iodide. Kelp (seaweed) products are also sold. Potassium iodide is almost completely absorbed (96.4% in a small study). Many multivitamins contain 150 mcg, and some iodine-only products exceed the UL [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/].
- US product survey (2016-2017): 74.2% of the top-selling adult multivitamins contained iodine, all as potassium iodide, at a median 150 mcg/day. Only 57.6% of prenatal multivitamins contained iodine (median 150 mcg; range 25-290 mcg). Of these, 73.5% used KI and 23.5% used kelp [PMID:30266075].
- Label accuracy: in a 2025 analysis of 47 prenatal products, iodine was declared on 25. Of those, 80% contained more than 20% less iodine than labeled [PMID:40252729].
- Reference intakes (US FNB): RDA 150 mcg/day for adults, 220 mcg in pregnancy and 290 mcg in lactation. WHO/UNICEF/ICCIDD recommend 250 mcg/day in pregnancy. The adult UL is 1,100 mcg/day (900 mcg at 14-18 years, 600 at 9-13, 300 at 4-8 and 200 at 1-3). The UL does not apply to medical use under a physician's care [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/].
- Pharmacological KI (FDA-approved, OTC) is sold for thyroid blocking in radiation emergencies:
  - iOSAT: 130 mg or 65 mg tablets [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92].
  - ThyroShield: oral solution, 65 mg/mL [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=186aadd3-2cb6-4de4-99b1-2371613e248d].
- Saturated solution of KI (SSKI) is also used in dermatology (sporotrichosis and inflammatory panniculitides such as erythema nodosum) [PMID:40487487].

** Mechanisms
- Hormone synthesis: iodide is taken up by the sodium-iodide symporter (NIS), then oxidized, organified onto thyroglobulin and coupled to form T4/T3. TSH drives uptake. In deficiency, persistently high TSH causes goiter [PMID:38870258] [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/].
- Wolff-Chaikoff effect: an acute iodide load transiently inhibits organification. Normally the thyroid "escapes" within days by down-regulating NIS. Failure to escape causes iodine-induced hypothyroidism. Groups at risk include fetuses and newborns, people with autoimmune thyroiditis, people with Graves' disease previously treated with radioiodine, surgery or antithyroid drugs, and people with prior postpartum or subacute thyroiditis. It usually resolves 2-3 weeks after iodide is stopped [PMID:11396709] [PMID:38870258].
- Jod-Basedow phenomenon (iodine-induced hyperthyroidism): occurs mainly in autonomous nodular goiter from long-standing deficiency, but also in iodine-sufficient regions and in people with prior Graves' disease [PMID:11396708] [PMID:39510448].
- Radioprotection: a saturating stable-iodide dose competitively blocks thyroidal uptake of radioactive 131I. KI protects only the thyroid, not against other radionuclides or external radiation [PMID:38989660] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92].
- Large iodide doses also selectively inhibit thyroglobulin synthesis and hormone release. This is the basis for using iodide in Graves' disease and thyroid storm. In rats, the effect was synergistic with lithium [PMID:946343] [PMID:39510448].
- The relationship between iodine status and thyroid disease is U-shaped. Thyroid nodules were more likely in deficiency (OR 1.28) and showed a non-linear rise at excessive intakes [PMID:41675569]. Population iodine excess is linked to thyroid autoimmunity [PMID:33914231].

** Dosing in trials
- Pregnancy: 150-250 mcg/day as KI is typical, for example 200 mcg/day from about 11 weeks to delivery in the India/Thailand RCT [PMID:29030199] and 225 mcg/day KI in an Italian RCT [PMID:31689890]. The largest TSH reductions were seen with 200-300 mcg/day KI [PMID:42359139].
- Preconception: the American Thyroid Association advises 150 mcg/day starting 3 months before conception. In a Québec cohort, only 36% started before conception, at a median dose of 220 mcg/day [PMID:42567091].
- Lactation: 150 or 300 mcg/day did not change infant iodine sufficiency or growth where salt iodization works well [PMID:30272528].
- Children in deficient settings: single oral doses of 100 mg (infants) or 400-490 mg (ages 6-17), or 150 mcg/day for 28 weeks [PMID:42720098].
- Radiation emergency (FDA label): one dose every 24 hours, only when public officials advise. Doses are 130 mg for adults and adolescents weighing at least 150 lb, 65 mg for ages 3-18, 32.5 mg for 1 month to 3 years, and 16.25 mg from birth to 1 month. Taking more does not add protection [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=186aadd3-2cb6-4de4-99b1-2371613e248d].
- Graves' disease adjunct: KI tablets were added to methimazole until FT4 normalized [PMID:19912243].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Excess intake can cause the same problems as deficiency (goiter, raised TSH, hypothyroidism), as well as hyperthyroidism and thyroiditis. People with autoimmune thyroid disease or prior deficiency may react at intakes that are safe for others [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:38870258].
- Iodine-induced hypothyroidism: usually transient, but many affected people later develop permanent hypothyroidism [PMID:11396709]. In 2 patients with Hashimoto's thyroiditis, OTC high-dose iodine (Iodoral) caused severe hypothyroidism [PMID:23529350].
- Iodine-induced hyperthyroidism:
  - After Danish salt fortification, hyperthyroidism incidence rose from 102.8 to 138.7 per 100,000/year. The largest rise (+160%) was in ages 20-39 [PMID:16849408].
  - Kelp tablets caused transient hyperthyroidism in an older adult with no thyroid history [PMID:31517826].
  - OTC "Tri-iodine" worsened thyrotoxicosis in a patient taking methimazole [PMID:23529350].
- Pregnancy and fetus:
  - Maternal intake of 12.5 mg/day caused congenital hypothyroidism in 3 infants [PMID:22841183].
  - A supplement providing 62.5 times the pregnancy requirement caused fetal goiter, neonatal hypothyroidism and hearing loss [PMID:27006474].
  - In a pooled analysis, 52% of pregnant women had excessive intake, which was associated with hypothyroxinemia and hypo- or hyperthyroidism (observational) [PMID:36018544].
  - High water iodine (>100 ug/L) was associated with subclinical hypothyroidism in pregnancy (OR 10.8, 95% CI 1.4-84.0) [PMID:39736330].
- Iodinated contrast media (a common medical source of excess iodine [PMID:38870258]):
  - In a prospective cohort, 11.5% developed thyroid dysfunction (4.9% hyperthyroidism, 6.6% subclinical hypothyroidism) [PMID:38700099].
  - Neonates and preterm infants are the most vulnerable [PMID:42340805] [PMID:33112287].
- KI label adverse effects: salivary gland swelling, GI upset, metallic taste and allergic reactions (rash, angioedema, fever with joint pain). Rarely it causes hypo- or hyperthyroidism or goiter, which are more likely with thyroid disease or in infants under 1 month [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92].
- Population experience: a 2024 review judged KI generally well tolerated, with scarce data on adverse effects [PMID:38989660]. Poland's mass KI prophylaxis after Chernobyl was not followed by a substantial rise in childhood thyroid cancer [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:8498398].
- Acute poisoning is rare and usually needs doses of many grams [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/].

** Contraindications
- KI labels say not to use with:
  - known iodine allergy
  - nodular thyroid disease with heart disease
  - dermatitis herpetiformis
  - hypocomplementemic vasculitis
  [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=186aadd3-2cb6-4de4-99b1-2371613e248d].
- Supplemental doses above the UL in autoimmune thyroiditis, autonomous nodules, prior Graves' disease, or prior postpartum, subacute or amiodarone-related thyroiditis. These groups fail to escape Wolff-Chaikoff or develop Jod-Basedow [PMID:11396709] [PMID:11396708] [PMID:1331165].
- High-dose iodine in pregnancy because of fetal thyroid suppression [PMID:22841183] [PMID:27006474]. Repeat KI dosing in pregnancy, breastfeeding and neonates should be avoided [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Antithyroid drugs (methimazole, PTU): high-dose iodine has an additive effect and could cause hypothyroidism [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/].
  - Excess iodine may also reduce antithyroid-drug effectiveness in hyperthyroid Graves' disease [PMID:11396708].
  - In a controlled setting, KI plus methimazole sped FT4 control without causing thionamide resistance [PMID:19912243].
- Lithium (see the "Lithium" entry): both inhibit thyroid hormone release.
  - In 10 patients on chronic lithium, KI 250 mg four times daily raised mean TSH by 8.9 uU/mL. Two became hypothyroid, and one developed Jod-Basedow hyperthyroidism [PMID:576345].
  - In rats, iodide potentiated lithium's effect on thyroglobulin [PMID:946343].
  - Iodine status modifies thyroid risk with lithium [PMID:16968542].
- Amiodarone: contains 37% iodine by weight. Its deiodination releases large iodine loads, causing hypothyroidism or thyrotoxicosis in about 20% of patients [PMID:33159436]. Pooled prevalence of amiodarone-induced hypothyroidism is 14% [PMID:35026394]. Further iodide after amiodarone thyrotoxicosis induced subclinical hypothyroidism in 6 of 10 patients [PMID:1331165].
- ACE inhibitors and potassium-sparing diuretics (spironolactone, amiloride): KI adds potassium and raises the risk of hyperkalemia [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]. ARBs are not named by ODS, but share the same potassium-retaining mechanism (extrapolation). This matters mainly at pharmacological KI doses; 150 mcg of iodine supplies negligible potassium.
- Levothyroxine: in levothyroxine-treated hypothyroid pregnant women, 150 mcg/day iodine did not improve maternal FT4/FT3. Neonatal TSH was 33% higher [PMID:27387243]. Iodine does not replace levothyroxine [PMID:40900603].
- Iodinated contrast media: add a large iodine load. Extra iodine should be avoided around imaging in at-risk people [PMID:38870258] [PMID:38700099].
*** Other supplements
- Genistein / soy isoflavones: genistein inactivates thyroid peroxidase. Iodine deficiency greatly increases soy's antithyroid effects, whereas iodine supplementation is protective [PMID:12060828]. In an RCT meta-analysis, soy raised TSH by 0.248 mIU/L, with no change in FT3/FT4 [PMID:30850697].
- Lithium: see Drugs.
- Kelp/seaweed and other iodine-containing products (multivitamins, prenatal vitamins): stacking raises total intake. Kelp iodine content varies widely [PMID:30266075] [PMID:31517826] [PMID:39510448].
- Dairy: higher dairy intake is associated with lower odds of iodine deficiency in pregnancy (OR 0.58) [PMID:41374057]. Vegan diets without supplements or fortification may not supply enough iodine [PMID:37622183].
*** Conditions
- Autoimmune thyroiditis (Hashimoto's) and thyroid antibody positivity: risk of iodine-induced hypothyroidism [PMID:11396709] [PMID:23529350]. Antibody positivity is the strongest risk factor for hypothyroidism in pregnancy [PMID:40296843].
- Nodular goiter or prior Graves' disease: Jod-Basedow risk [PMID:11396708] [PMID:39510448].
- Iodine deficiency: increases radiation-related thyroid cancer risk (RR 3.2) and soy goitrogenicity [PMID:15900042] [PMID:12060828].
- Pregnancy: needs rise. US pregnant women are again iodine-insufficient (median UIC below 150 ug/L in NHANES 2011-2020) [PMID:40096706]. Excess is also harmful (see Safety).
- Neonates: prone to hypothyroidism after iodine loads [PMID:42340805] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ea98aef8-93b6-4fac-be35-eb17a1a04a92].
*** Lab-test interference
- Radioactive iodine uptake scans and 131I therapy: stable iodine from supplements, kelp or contrast reduces thyroidal radioiodine uptake. A low-iodine diet (no more than 50 mcg/day for 1-2 weeks) is used before RAI [PMID:26118628] [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/]. Four days was sufficient in an iodine-sufficient area (24-h urinary iodine below 50 mcg in 72%) [PMID:34534327].
- Thyroid function tests (TSH, FT4, thyroglobulin): iodine changes them physiologically rather than through assay artifact. Supplementation lowers thyroglobulin and TSH in deficient women [PMID:42359139]. Excess can raise TSH [PMID:38870258].
- Urinary iodine reflects recent intake and is used for population status. Thyroglobulin is a longer-term marker [PMID:42567091] [PMID:38870258].

** Genotype notes
- DUOX2: a case report described delayed-onset congenital hypothyroidism in an infant with biallelic DUOX2 mutations whose mother ate large amounts of seaweed in pregnancy. Newborn screening was negative. This is a gene-iodine interaction that can distort screening results [PMID:23239635]. This is case-level evidence only; no population pharmacogenomic data were found.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Cochrane update in children (8 RCTs, n=2528): iodine slightly improves cognition (SMD 0.19) and likely reduces hypothyroidism and infant mortality; goitre effect very uncertain [PMID:42720098].
- [2026-08] Québec cohort: women starting iodine after conception had a larger thyroglobulin decline; first-trimester Tg suggested insufficiency [PMID:42567091].
- [2026-08] SR in children: iodinated contrast causes mainly transient subclinical hypothyroidism, worst in neonates [PMID:42340805].
- [2026-06] MA in iodine-deficient pregnancy: supplementation lowers TSH (SMD -0.14) and Tg (SMD -0.22), most with 200-300 mcg/day KI [PMID:42359139].
- [2026-05] Dose-response MA of 8 cohorts: suboptimal maternal iodine linked to lower child cognition (g -0.22); exploratory optimum about 150-300 mcg/day [PMID:42124075].
- [2026-03] ALSPAC: first-trimester iodine below 50 ug/g linked to 3.3-point lower full-scale IQ at age 15 [PMID:41915209].
- [2026-01] Dose-response MA (25 studies, n=54,621): U-shaped link between iodine status and thyroid nodules [PMID:41675569].
- [2025-11] Dairy MA: dairy intake lowers odds of iodine deficiency in pregnancy and lactation (OR 0.58) [PMID:41374057].
- [2025-11] JAMA review: hypothyroidism prevalence 0.3-12% depending on iodine intake [PMID:40900603].
- [2025-10] Review: iodine deficiency resurging among US pregnant women; 23-59% inadequate [PMID:40096706].
- [2025-09] SR/MA (72 studies): no clear association of mild-to-moderate deficiency with TSH/FT4/FT3 [PMID:40633808].
- [2025-07] Shandong: water iodine >100 ug/L associated with subclinical hypothyroidism, nodules and goiter in pregnancy [PMID:39736330].
- [2025-06] Prenatal product analysis: 80% of labeled products contained more than 20% less iodine than declared [PMID:40252729].
- [2025-05] PoppiE RCT statistical analysis plan: 20 vs 200 mcg/day in iodine-replete pregnant women; data collection to July 2026 [PMID:40390018].
- [2025-05] Thyroid SR for ATA guideline: iodine deficiency and excess both linked to subclinical hypothyroidism in pregnancy [PMID:40296843].
- [2025-05] Review of KI in dermatology (indications, adverse effects) [PMID:40487487].
- [2025-03] Prospective contrast cohort: 11.5% developed thyroid dysfunction [PMID:38700099].
- [2025-03] Endocrine Practice review: iodine-induced hyperthyroidism from supplements, seaweed, contrast and amiodarone [PMID:39510448].
- [2024-11] Endocrine Reviews: risks of iodine excess; safe ULs in high-risk groups need better definition [PMID:38870258].

** Open questions
- Whether routine iodine supplementation in mildly deficient or replete pregnancies benefits or harms child neurodevelopment. RCTs are null, but cohorts show associations. PoppiE results are pending [PMID:32320029] [PMID:40390018] [PMID:42124075].
- Optimal timing (preconception vs early pregnancy) and dose. Whether thyroidal iodine stores before conception determine benefit [PMID:42567091] [PMID:32320029].
- Safe upper limits for susceptible groups: autoimmune thyroiditis, nodular goiter, pregnancy, neonates [PMID:38870258].
- Long-term thyroid autoimmunity effects of population iodine increases [PMID:33914231] [PMID:16849408].
- Accuracy and regulation of iodine content in supplements, especially kelp-based ones [PMID:40252729] [PMID:30266075].
- KI prophylaxis in iodine-deficient regions, after dirty-bomb events, and formulation choice (granules vs tablets) remain debated [PMID:38989660].
