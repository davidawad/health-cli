** Identity & forms
- Phylloquinone (vitamin K1) is the plant form of vitamin K, a 2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone with a phytyl side chain. Menaquinones (K2, MK-n) carry polyisoprenyl side chains and are mostly bacterial. Green leafy vegetables supply most dietary vitamin K in North America [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7].
- Reference intakes (IOM 2001): Adequate Intake 120 ug/day for men and 90 ug/day for women. No Tolerable Upper Intake Level was set, because no adverse effects had been reported at higher intakes [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7].
- OTC supplements: synthetic K1 in multivitamins and stand-alone products, typically in microgram doses. Prescription phytonadione comes as 5 mg oral tablets and as an injectable emulsion (1 mg/0.5 mL and 10 mg/mL) for IV, IM or SC use [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e8808230-2c44-44c6-8cab-8f29b6b34051].
- Absorption: K1 is absorbed in the jejunum and ileum. This depends on bile and pancreatic juice and is enhanced by dietary fat [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7]. The tablet label says oral phytonadione is adequately absorbed only if bile salts are present [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe]. A 500 ug tablet gave an AUC about 6 times higher than the same amount of K1 eaten as raw spinach (27.55 vs 4.79 nmol/L.h) [PMID:10356087].
- K1 vs K2 (separate monographs exist for K2 MK-7 and K2-MK4): K1 and MK-7 both peak in serum about 4 h after intake. MK-7 has a much longer half-life and accumulates 7- to 8-fold with daily dosing [PMID:17158229]. Body stores of vitamin K are low and turn over quickly; K1 is side-chain cleaved and converted to tissue MK-4 via menadione and UBIAD1 [PMID:24489112]. Maternal K1 supplementation raised breast-milk MK-4 [PMID:12064330].

** Mechanisms
- Cofactor for gamma-glutamyl carboxylation of vitamin K-dependent proteins (VKDPs): coagulation factors II, VII, IX and X (liver), osteocalcin (bone) and matrix Gla protein (MGP; vessel wall) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe] [PMID:29749440]. 17 VKDPs have been identified [PMID:31862867].
- Vitamin K cycle: vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR, gene VKORC1) regenerates the active hydroquinone. Warfarin and other VKAs inhibit VKOR, and large K1 doses act as an antidote [PMID:35748323] [PMID:24489112].
- Catabolism: CYP4F2 is the hepatic vitamin K1 omega-hydroxylase. The V433M (rs2108622) variant lowers enzyme levels and K1 oxidation [PMID:19297519]. CYP4F2 and CYP4F11 also hydroxylate MK-4 [PMID:24138531].
- In replete people, phytonadione has almost no pharmacodynamic activity on clotting. Its action shows only in vitamin K deficiency or antagonism, with an onset of 6-10 h orally and an INR improvement within 1-8 h [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
- Extrahepatic markers respond to supplementation. 500 ug/day lowered %ucOC [PMID:18252784]. 1000 ug/day suppressed %ucOC by 81% in Crohn's disease, with no further suppression at 2000 ug [PMID:25181575]. 10 mg thrice weekly cut dp-ucMGP by 86% in hemodialysis [PMID:35641194].

** Dosing in trials
- Diet-level supplement: 500 ug/day for 3 years in a multivitamin (with calcium 600 mg and vitamin D 400 IU) [PMID:18252784]. 200 ug/day for 2 years [PMID:17243866].
- Higher doses: 1 mg/day for 3 years (with minerals and vitamin D) [PMID:14506950], 1 mg/day for 12 months [PMID:20881072] [PMID:25181575], and 5 mg/day for 2-4 years (ECKO) [PMID:18922041]. In the bisphosphonate add-on trial, 1 mg/day for 18 months gave no BMD difference [PMID:37338608].
- Vascular/CKD: 5 mg orally thrice weekly [PMID:37216675], 10 mg orally thrice weekly [PMID:35641194], 10 mg IV after each hemodialysis [PMID:39680321], and 10 mg/day orally for 3 months in diabetes [PMID:34637494]. The ongoing PASSPORT aortic stenosis trial uses 10 mg/day [PMID:41425110].
- Warfarin stabilization: 100 ug/day [PMID:17666020], 150 ug/day [PMID:27346552], 200 ug/day [PMID:23953594], or 500 ug/day (open label, needing 6-95% warfarin dose increases) [PMID:17323135].
- Over-anticoagulation: oral 1-2.5 mg [PMID:12186515] [PMID:11034038]. Dosing tailored to INR and body surface area outperformed fixed doses (68.9% vs 52.8% in range) [PMID:28771671]. A systematic review favored low-dose oral K for asymptomatic warfarin over-anticoagulation, but found no added benefit over simply withholding acenocoumarol [PMID:15257939]. Label doses are 2.5-10 mg, up to 25-50 mg [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
- Newborn prophylaxis: 0.5-1 mg IM within 1 hour of birth [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e8808230-2c44-44c6-8cab-8f29b6b34051] [PMID:35190810].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Oral K1: no UL, and no adverse effects have been reported at high intakes [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7]. ECKO (5 mg/day for up to 4 years) found no excess adverse effects and fewer cancers (3 vs 12) [PMID:18922041]. Dialysis meta-analysis judged vitamin K safe but noted a small serum calcium rise (0.18) [PMID:38046003]. VitaVasK reported no treatment-related adverse events [PMID:37216675].
- BOXED WARNING (injection): fatal hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, occur during and right after IV and IM injection, even with dilution and on first dose. The label says to use SC unless that route is not feasible, and to give IV no faster than 1 mg/min [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e8808230-2c44-44c6-8cab-8f29b6b34051].
- Incidence: 3 per 10,000 IV doses (95% CI 0.04-11) [PMID:12392385]. Reactions are mostly non-IgE anaphylactoid and linked to the polyethoxylated castor oil or polysorbate solubilizer [PMID:28301903] [PMID:24594861]. Reactions appear dose-related in animal models [PMID:28407450].
- Other injection risks: benzyl alcohol "gasping syndrome" in neonates; cutaneous reactions (eczematous, scleroderma-like) up to a year later [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e8808230-2c44-44c6-8cab-8f29b6b34051]. Hyperbilirubinemia occurs in newborns at doses above those recommended [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
- Over-reversal: high-dose K1 can overshoot INR and cause temporary warfarin resistance. 1 mg oral K1 with acenocoumarol increased sub-therapeutic INRs (RR 1.83) [PMID:12152676] [PMID:9801181] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].

** Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to phytonadione or any formulation component (label contraindication) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e8808230-2c44-44c6-8cab-8f29b6b34051].
- Unsupervised supplement use while taking warfarin or other VKAs: this antagonizes anticoagulation. Any use should be at a fixed daily dose with INR monitoring [PMID:17323135] [PMID:24832594].
- Benzyl alcohol-preserved injection in neonates and infants: use preservative-free products if available [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e8808230-2c44-44c6-8cab-8f29b6b34051].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin and other coumarins: K1 directly antagonizes VKAs, and larger doses can cause temporary resistance [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe]. Daily low doses (100-200 ug) slightly improved stability (TTR +3.5%), with no proven effect on bleeding or thrombosis [PMID:23744743] [PMID:17666020] [PMID:23953594]. Dietary K1 effects appear mainly above ~150 ug/day, and a stable diet is advised rather than restriction [PMID:26962786]. Plasma K1 varies widely in warfarin users [PMID:25828628].
- DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran) inhibit factor Xa or IIa directly rather than the vitamin K cycle. No vitamin K-mediated interaction is expected, and K1 is not a DOAC reversal agent. INR is unreliable on DOACs [PMID:24861793] [PMID:23023509] [PMID:28476405].
- Heparin: phytonadione does not counteract heparin [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
- Antibiotics (N-methylthiotetrazole cephalosporins, cefazolin): these inhibit hepatic K epoxide reductase. Low vitamin K status (TPN) predisposes to hypoprothrombinemia, which 1 mg IV K1 reverses [PMID:3350995] [PMID:3306622] [PMID:2276882]. Antibiotic-related hypoprothrombinemia is a labeled phytonadione indication [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
- Orlistat: vitamin K fell (non-significantly) in adolescents [PMID:12126214]. One case report describes a raised INR on warfarin, attributed partly to reduced vitamin K absorption [PMID:12659605].
- Bile acid sequestrants (cholestyramine): there are case reports of vitamin K-deficiency bleeding, including one refractory to concurrent oral K [PMID:12688565] [PMID:40878026].
- Mineral oil: concern about fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption, but clinical evidence is limited [PMID:17041175].
- Salicylates: listed on the label as a cause of drug-induced hypoprothrombinemia treatable with phytonadione [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin E: 1000 IU/day for 12 weeks raised PIVKA-II (1.8 to 5.3 ng/mL in healthy men), which suggests vitamin K antagonism [PMID:15213041]. The IOM calls the E-K antagonism "clearly established" [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7].
- K2 MK-7: additive vitamin K load. MK-7 accumulates, and 50 ug/day or more may affect VKA therapy [PMID:17158229].
- K2-MK4: K1 is a precursor of tissue MK-4 [PMID:24489112] [PMID:12064330], and both are CYP4F2 substrates [PMID:24138531].
- Vitamin D-3 (with calcium): modest bone synergy. Benefits were seen only for the combinations [PMID:17243866] [PMID:14506950]. In a network MA, vitamin D plus K ranked highest for P1NP and osteocalcin [PMID:40949733].
*** Conditions
- Fat malabsorption (obstructive jaundice, celiac disease, IBD, cystic fibrosis, intestinal resection): these raise deficiency risk and are labeled indications [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe]. In Crohn's disease, 1 mg/day for 12 months corrected %ucOC but did not change spine or hip BMD [PMID:25181575].
- Hemodialysis/CKD: low intake and status are common [PMID:22648294]. K1 lowers dp-ucMGP more than K2 [PMID:38046003], but calcification and outcome benefit are unproven [PMID:42851346] [PMID:36704782].
- Liver disease: the label says repeated large doses are not warranted if the response is poor [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6c992275-b040-42d0-9eff-570dce4272fe].
- Older inpatients: 36% of hip-fracture patients were vitamin K deficient on admission, rising to 64% after surgical fasting [PMID:30618350].
- Newborns: risk of VKDB, prevented by IM prophylaxis [PMID:35190810] [PMID:27109090].
*** Lab-test interference
- PT/INR is insensitive as a vitamin K status marker. Serum K1 below 0.15 ug/L indicates deficiency but reflects recent intake. PIVKA-II reflects hepatic status, VKA exposure, and is also an HCC marker [PMID:31862867]. K1 intake therefore lowers PIVKA-II, while high-dose vitamin E raises it [PMID:15213041].
- Supplementing lowers %ucOC and dp-ucMGP. These are status markers, not outcomes [PMID:18252784] [PMID:35641194]. In CKD, PIVKA-II but not dp-ucMGP was weakly associated with aortic calcification [PMID:39604863].
- DOACs falsely raise INR and PT [PMID:28476405] [PMID:24861793].

** Genotype notes
- CYP4F2 rs2108622 (V433M): carriers metabolize K1 less [PMID:19297519] and need 8.3% (5.6-11.1%) higher coumarin doses [PMID:23132553]. Higher plasma K1 was seen in carriers [PMID:27462768].
- VKORC1 -1639G>A confers warfarin sensitivity across vitamin K tertiles [PMID:27462768]. VKORC1 risk alleles were modestly associated with osteopenia/osteoporosis (OR 1.16, 1.01-1.35) [PMID:41597466].
- No trial has tested genotype-guided K1 supplementation.

** Latest research
- [2026-10] A CKD review concludes that K1 has more consistent (still heterogeneous) imaging evidence than K2, but neither reduces MACE or mortality [PMID:42851346].
- [2026-09] Swedish cohort of 2.0 million births: no IM vitamin K was associated with intracranial bleeding (aOR 2.91), and non-receipt is rising [PMID:42440325].
- [2026-08] UK Biobank: higher dietary K1 was associated with lower ASCVD risk (HR Q3 vs Q1 0.90). Higher K2 intake was associated with higher risk, possibly food confounding [PMID:42485745].
- [2026-08] VitaK-CAC (K2 MK-7 360 ug/day, 2 years): CAC progression slowed (P=.02). This is relevant only as a K2 contrast [PMID:42268593].
- [2026-01] Glycemic MA of 8 RCTs: FBG SMD -0.22, HbA1c -1.00%, HOMA-IR -0.63 [PMID:41599883].
- [2026-01] VKORC1 polymorphisms and osteoporosis MA: OR 1.16 [PMID:41597466].
- [2025-11] PASSPORT RCT design: K1 10 mg/day vs placebo in non-severe calcific aortic stenosis. It has met its recruitment target of 108 [PMID:41425110].
- [2025-09] Dietary vitamin K and fracture dose-response MA: RR 0.83 (0.68-1.01), with benefit up to 120 ug/day [PMID:40947710].
- [2025-09] Network MA of supplements on bone turnover markers: vitamin D plus K ranked highest for P1NP [PMID:40949733].
- [2025-07] ViKCoVaC bone post hoc: K1 10 mg/day had no effect on vertebral 18F-NaF uptake [PMID:40258481].
- [2025-07] Case of cholestyramine-induced vitamin K deficiency refractory to oral K [PMID:40878026].
- [2025-03] IV K1 10 mg thrice weekly in hemodialysis: CAC progression 20.8% vs 44% (RR 0.45, NS CI) [PMID:39680321].
- [2024-12] BMD MA: vitamin K raised lumbar spine BMD and cOC, mainly with K2 [PMID:39657786].
- [2024-11] PIVKA-II, but not dp-ucMGP, was weakly associated with aortic calcification in CKD [PMID:39604863].

** Open questions
- Does high-dose K1 (5-10 mg) prevent fractures? ECKO's fracture signal was small and not a primary outcome [PMID:18922041] [PMID:19818211].
- Will K1 slow aortic valve or vascular calcification in adequately powered trials with clinical endpoints [PMID:41425110] [PMID:42851346]?
- Is the association of low K1 status with mortality causal [PMID:32359159]?
- Which patients with unstable INR benefit from low-dose K1 [PMID:23744743] [PMID:24832594]?
- Is the kidney-transplant mortality association real? A meta-analysis pooling "normal vitamin K status or supplementation" against deficiency or no supplementation gave RR 0.72 (0.60-0.86) [PMID:38125342].
