** Identity & forms
- NIH ODS identifies phylloquinone as the dietary K1 form, a cofactor for gamma-carboxylation of coagulation and bone proteins [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/].
** Mechanisms
- Vitamin K participates in vitamin K-dependent carboxylation of prothrombin, osteocalcin, and matrix Gla protein [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/].

** Dosing in trials
- Adult adequate intake: 120 mcg/day men, 90 mcg/day women; trials used 500 mcg/day phylloquinone for 3 years, or 1 mg/day for 12 months [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/].
** Safety & adverse effects
- Dietary quantities have low toxicity; no UL established. Vitamin K can seriously antagonize warfarin and related vitamin K antagonists [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/].
** Contraindications
- Warfarin and other VKAs; keep intake consistent and monitor INR.
** Interactions
- NIH ODS reports warfarin/VKA antagonism; no specific genotype evidence found [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/].

** Latest research
- 2024-12-11: The 2024 RCT meta-analysis on vitamin K and BMD found heterogeneous, site-dependent effects; it does not establish fracture prevention [DOI:10.1302/2046-3758.1312.BJR-2024-0053.R1].
