** Identity & forms
- Menaquinone-7 is a long-chain vitamin K2 (2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone with a 7-isoprenyl side chain). Long-chain menaquinones (MK-n) are bacterial products, while MK-4 is made in tissues from menadione/phylloquinone [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7] [PMID:18841274].
- Sources: natto (Bacillus subtilis-fermented soybean) is the richest natural source. Supplements are natto-fermentation-derived or synthetic [PMID:41393956] [PMID:21781006] [PMID:38443482]. Only trans MK-7 is fully active; cis MK-7 showed only slight carboxylative activity in vitro [PMID:35583412].
- MK-7 vs K1 (phylloquinone): both peak about 4 h after intake, but MK-7 has a very long half-life, giving stable serum levels and 7- to 8-fold accumulation with daily use. It carboxylated osteocalcin more completely than K1 [PMID:17158229]. A 1 mg single-dose PK study confirmed Tmax about 6 h and a very long half-life, with no circadian rhythm [PMID:37132123].
- MK-7 vs MK-4: 420 ug MK-7 was detectable in serum for up to 48 h, while MK-4 at the same dose was undetectable. 60 ug/day MK-7 for 7 days raised serum MK-7, but MK-4 did not raise MK-4 [PMID:23140417]. K1 and MK-4 are covered in separate monographs.
- Matrix affects delivery: MK-7 in a yogurt also containing vitamins D3/C, magnesium and n-3 PUFA raised plasma MK-7 more than capsules [PMID:26908424]. Co-ingestion with non-fat fermented milk increased MK-7 AUC [PMID:41765411].
- Long-chain MKs travel mainly in LDL, and K1 in triglyceride-rich lipoproteins [PMID:18841274]. In dialysis patients, little MK-7 was incorporated into lipoproteins, and uremic HDL carried MK-7 poorly [PMID:34774554].
- Reference intakes (US IOM 2001, all vitamin K forms): AI 120 ug/day for men and 90 ug/day for women. No UL was set because no adverse effects were reported at higher intakes [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7]. Some authors argue K2 needs its own intake recommendation (opinion) [PMID:32575901].

** Mechanisms
- Cofactor for gamma-glutamyl carboxylase (GGCX). It activates vitamin K-dependent proteins: hepatic clotting factors II, VII, IX and X, osteocalcin (bone) and matrix Gla protein (MGP, a vascular calcification inhibitor) [PMID:18841274] [PMID:35583412]. Warfarin blocks VKORC1 recycling of vitamin K [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdbbc317-35d3-42ba-94f0-c83891358529].
- Extrahepatic status markers: dp-ucMGP (inactive MGP) and ucOC/ucOC:cOC ratio. MK-7 at about 90-360 ug/day lowers both, while coagulation-factor carboxylation is already near-complete in healthy people [PMID:22289649] [PMID:26875489] [PMID:23062766].
- Under vitamin K antagonist (VKA) blockade, vitamin K can bypass inhibition in the liver but not in bone or the vessel wall. MK-7 is more effective than K1 at carboxylating both liver and bone Gla proteins [PMID:18841274].
- In observational data, elevated dp-ucMGP was associated with all-cause mortality (HR 1.84, 1.48-2.28) and CVD mortality (HR 1.96). Dietary menaquinone intake was associated with lower CHD risk (HR 0.70, 0.53-0.93; 2 studies). These associations are not causal [PMID:31119401].
- Proposed anti-inflammatory, OPG/RANKL and insulin-sensitivity pathways come mainly from preclinical and narrative reviews [PMID:35774605] [PMID:42654291]. In an RCT, MK-7 lowered ucOC by 70% but did not change HOMA-IR [PMID:33664429].

** Dosing in trials
- Status/biomarker trials: 10-360 ug/day in healthy adults [PMID:22289649]. At least 100 ug/day improved osteocalcin carboxylation in Japanese adults [PMID:26875489]. 45 ug/day was used in children [PMID:19450370].
- Bone: 180 ug/day for 3 y [PMID:23525894], 375 ug/day for 1-3 y with calcium 800 mg and D3 38 ug [PMID:27625301] [PMID:33030563], and 90 ug/day for 1 y [PMID:32060566].
- Vascular: 180 ug/day (healthy women) [PMID:25694037] [PMID:40077685]; 360 ug/day (CAD, diabetes, dialysis, transplant) [PMID:42268593] [PMID:31387121] [PMID:34476095] [PMID:36695702]; 400 ug/day (CKD 3b-4) [PMID:32817311]; 720 ug/day + 25 ug D3 (AVADEC) [PMID:35465686].
- Dialysis intermittent dosing: 360-1080 ug thrice weekly (dose-finding) [PMID:24285428]; 360 ug thrice weekly (Trevasc-HDK) [PMID:37705910]; 2000 ug thrice weekly with rivaroxaban (Valkyrie) [PMID:31704740].
- Glycemia: 360 ug/day for 12 wk [PMID:33159574]. Cramps: 180 ug/day [PMID:39466236]; 360 ug/day in HD [PMID:35203045]. Highest short-term dose: 999 ug/day for up to 14 days in COVID-19 [PMID:38930004].
- Take with food or a fat-containing matrix (fat-soluble; matrix effects above) [PMID:26908424] [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7].

** Safety & adverse effects
- No UL exists for vitamin K [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7]. Toxicology: rat 90-day NOAEL 4500 mg/kg/day (the highest dose tested; fermented MK-7) and no genotoxicity [PMID:38443482]. Synthetic MK-7 NOAEL 10 mg/kg/day (highest tested) [PMID:21781006]. No toxicity at 2000 mg/kg acute or 90-day doses in Wistar rats [PMID:28911451].
- Coagulation in people not on anticoagulants: 90 ug/day for 30 days did not change PT, APTT or factor II/VII/IX/X activity [PMID:34115006]. 10-360 ug/day did not change thrombin generation [PMID:22289649]. In dialysis, 360 ug/day for 1 y showed no coagulation activation and no excess vascular events [PMID:38128853].
- Trials report adverse events similar to placebo: vascular MA RR 0.92 [PMID:37252246]; K2 bone MA RR 1.03 [PMID:36033779]. One long-term K2 osteoporosis MA (largely MK-4) found more adverse reactions (RR 1.33, 1.11-1.59) but none serious [PMID:35711002]. Menatetrenone (MK-4) at pharmacologic doses had RR 1.47 for AEs [PMID:30734066]. That is a separate compound.
- GI intolerance: dropouts in an HD dose-finding study were mainly from GI effects related to the tablets' unpleasant smell [PMID:24285428]. One GI complaint occurred in the HD cramp trial [PMID:35203045].
- Possible site-specific bone signal in dialysis: 360 ug/day for 2 y accelerated 1/3 distal radius BMD loss (-0.023 g/cm2) while preventing lumbar spine loss [PMID:36460034].
- PET signal: in T2D, 18F-NaF uptake tended to increase with MK-7 (P=0.06) with no CT change. The meaning is unclear [PMID:31387121].

** Contraindications
- Vitamin K antagonists (warfarin, acenocoumarol, phenprocoumon): avoid unless supervised with INR monitoring. As little as 10-20 ug/day MK-7 caused clinically relevant INR lowering in 40-60% of acenocoumarol-treated volunteers. 45 ug/day lowered INR by about 40% [PMID:23530987]. Doses of 50 ug/day or more may interfere in a clinically relevant way [PMID:17158229]. The warfarin label lists increased vitamin K intake as a cause of decreased INR response [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdbbc317-35d3-42ba-94f0-c83891358529].
- Trials commonly excluded VKA users and coagulation disorders (e.g., AVADEC) [PMID:30139903].
- No other absolute contraindication was found in retrieved sources.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin/VKAs: antagonism, with MK-7 more potent and longer-acting than K1 (see Contraindications) [PMID:23530987] [PMID:17158229] [PMID:15825811]. Low-MK-7 natto (46 ug MK-7/day) did not change PT-INR in 10 warfarin patients [PMID:34406879].
- DOACs (rivaroxaban): no vitamin K dependence. In Valkyrie, rivaroxaban plus 2000 ug MK-7 thrice weekly was used in HD patients. Bleeding did not differ significantly between arms, except for fewer major bleeds with rivaroxaban than with VKA [PMID:31704740].
- Antibiotics: bleeding in antibiotic-treated patients has been attributed to suppressed gut menaquinone synthesis, with confounding [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7]. The warfarin label flags antibiotics for INR monitoring [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdbbc317-35d3-42ba-94f0-c83891358529].
- Glucose-lowering drugs: small HbA1c/FPG reductions in T2D [PMID:33159574] [PMID:40054729]. Additive effect is minor and not studied as an interaction.
- Teriparatide (rhPTH 1-34): combination with vitamin K2 (form not specified) improved BMD more than K2 alone [PMID:39197463].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin D-3: frequently co-formulated. A K+D MA found total BMD SMD 0.316 and lower ucOC [PMID:32219282]. AVADEC (K2+D3) was null for valve calcification [PMID:35465686]. Baseline K status did not modify D3 effects [PMID:42453673].
- K1 and K2-MK4: all forms add to total vitamin K exposure relevant to VKA therapy [PMID:18841274] [PMID:15825811]. In dialysis, K1 lowered dp-ucMGP more than K2 (SMD -1.64 vs -0.56) [PMID:38046003].
- Vitamin E: 1000 IU/day raised PIVKA-II (poorer hepatic K status) [PMID:15213041]. The IOM notes established vitamin E antagonism of vitamin K action [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7].
- Calcium: adding Ca 500 mg + D3 10 ug to 90 ug K2 gave no extra BMD effect [PMID:32060566].
- EPA/DHA: a multi-nutrient yogurt matrix (including n-3 PUFA) increased MK-7 uptake versus capsules. This is not isolated to fish oil [PMID:26908424].
*** Conditions
- CKD/dialysis: functional deficiency is common (dp-ucMGP 4.5-fold higher) [PMID:22169620]. Supplementation improves status but not calcification, stiffness or mortality [PMID:34476095] [PMID:37705910] [PMID:32817311] [PMID:38046003]. Dialysis MA reported a modest calcium rise (0.18) [PMID:38046003].
- Kidney transplant: prevented PWV progression in a 12-week RCT [PMID:36695702].
- Fat malabsorption: vitamin K is poorly absorbed in lipid malabsorption syndromes [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10026/chapter/7].
- Hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance: see Lab-test interference. HCC outcome MA (mostly menatetrenone) is not MK-7 evidence [PMID:41388930].
*** Lab-test interference
- PIVKA-II/DCP (HCC tumor marker): vitamin K administration lowers PIVKA-II positivity (28.5% vs 54.5%), and PIVKA-II "should be measured without vitamin K administration" [PMID:1705389] [PMID:1309308]. MK-7 lowers PIVKA-II in T2D, dialysis and COVID-19 [PMID:35365594] [PMID:38128853] [PMID:38930004].
- INR on VKAs falls with MK-7. This is a true pharmacodynamic effect [PMID:23530987].
- dp-ucMGP and ucOC fall with supplementation. Interpret them as intake markers, not outcomes [PMID:25694037] [PMID:33030563].

** Genotype notes
- VKORC1 rs8050894: in a 40-person T2D RCT, CC carriers had higher ucOC after K2 or K2+D3 than other genotypes, and CG carriers had glycemic responses [PMID:42257367].
- GGCX polymorphism modified ucOC response to menatetrenone (MK-4, 45 mg/day) in women aged 65 and over. Extrapolation to MK-7 is unverified [PMID:21344298].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] VitaK-CAC (JAMA Cardiol): 360 ug/day for 2 y slowed CAC progression in symptomatic CAD (P=.02), especially in noncalcified plaques that calcified. No significant AEs [PMID:42268593].
- [2026-08] Narrative review of the "heart-brain-bone" axis (omega-3, D3, MK-7) explicitly frames it as a conceptual, unvalidated framework [PMID:42654291].
- [2026-08] CKJ review: K1 and K2 improve status, but MK-7 has not produced reproducible CT calcification benefits. Neither form reduces MACE or mortality [PMID:42851346].
- [2026-06] Post hoc analysis of a D3 RCT (n=200): baseline K1/MK-4/MK-7 status did not modify D3 effects on bone or CV markers [PMID:42453673].
- [2026-06] VKORC1 rs8050894 genotype and ucOC response to K2+/-D3 in T2D (n=40) [PMID:42257367].
- [2026-04] TAKEOVER RCT (n=71, 240 ug/day x 12 wk): no effect on strength, soreness or inflammation after muscle-damaging exercise. Age-interaction signals were exploratory [PMID:41843412].
- [2026-04] RCT of MK-producing probiotics (n=20): dp-ucMGP -59.89 pmol/L over 12 wk; serum MK-7 up [PMID:42000184].
- [2026-01] Glycemia MA (8 RCTs, all K forms): FBG SMD -0.22; HbA1c -1.00%; HOMA-IR -0.63; no insulin effect [PMID:41599883].
- [2025-11] AVADEC substudy: 720 ug K2 + D3 lowered dp-ucMGP but had no effect on epicardial/pericoronary fat inflammation or systemic markers [PMID:41100911].
- [2025-11] Bone turnover MA (9 RCTs, n=2570): ucOC -1.54, OC +1.86, CTX -0.09 [PMID:41268154].
- [2025-11] Natto MA (6 observational/quasi-experimental studies, n=2327): higher serum MK-7, lower ucOC, modestly higher BMD; low or very low certainty for bone [PMID:41393956].
- [2025-07] JAMA Intern Med retraction-and-replacement notice for the nocturnal leg cramp RCT [PMID:40314950].
- [2025-03] MK-7 MA (6 RCTs): HbA1c SMD -0.32 and HOMA-IR -0.56 with high heterogeneity [PMID:40054729].
- [2025-02] Post hoc 1-y RCT (n=165): 180 ug/day attenuated stiffness in post-menopausal women [PMID:40077685].
- [2025-01] Long COVID open-label RCT (K2 240 ug + D3 2000 IU, n=151): improved LC Index and symptom count [PMID:39861434].
- [2024-12] Nocturnal leg cramp RCT (n=199): weekly cramps reduced by 2.67 vs placebo [PMID:39466236]. Bone/BMD MA in middle-aged and older adults: LS BMD up (P=0.035), driven by K2 [PMID:39657786].

** Open questions
- Does the VitaK-CAC CAC signal replicate and translate into fewer events, given null AVADEC, dialysis and diabetes trials? [PMID:42268593] [PMID:35465686] [PMID:42851346]
- MK-7-specific fracture trials are absent. Bone MAs pool MK-4 (45 mg) with microgram-dose MK-7 [PMID:36033779] [PMID:31076817].
- Why do K1 and K2 differ in dialysis (K1 lowers dp-ucMGP more), and does impaired lipoprotein transport of MK-7 in uremia explain it? [PMID:38046003] [PMID:34774554]
- Is the cramp effect robust after the retraction/replacement, and does it extend to neuropathic or dialysis cramps in larger trials? [PMID:40314950] [PMID:35203045]
- What is the safe MK-7 dose, if any, for stably anticoagulated VKA patients? [PMID:23530987] [PMID:34406879]
