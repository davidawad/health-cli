** Identity & forms
- Vitamin K2 includes menaquinones; MK-4 (menatetrenone) is a short-chain form distinct from MK-7. Prescription high-dose menatetrenone has been used for osteoporosis in Japan. [PMID:30734066]

** Mechanisms
- Proposed biological activity and the best-established physiological context are described above; clinical mechanisms remain uncertain where outcome evidence is limited. ["PMID:30734066"]

** Dosing in trials
- Osteoporosis trials often used 45 mg/day menatetrenone, far above nutritional supplement doses; not interchangeable with low-dose products. [PMID:30734066]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Generally tolerated in trials; high-dose safety and long-term benefit evidence remain uncertain. [PMID:30734066]

** Contraindications
- Vitamin K antagonists are contraindicated with inconsistent vitamin K intake because anticoagulation can be reduced. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- Warfarin: antagonizes anticoagulant effect; maintain consistent vitamin K intake and monitor INR. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/]

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed; no genotype-specific claim is made.

** Latest research
- 2025–2026 literature: 2024/2025 systematic reviews continue to assess vitamin K forms and bone outcomes, while high-quality fracture evidence remains limited. [PMID:16801507]

** Open questions
- Whether findings translate into durable patient-important outcomes, optimal formulation/dose, and long-term safety remains uncertain. [PMID:30734066; PMID:16801507]
