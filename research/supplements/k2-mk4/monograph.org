** Identity & forms
- Menaquinone-4 (MK-4) is a short-chain vitamin K2 with four isoprene units. Menatetrenone is the synthetic drug form, chemically identical to MK-4 [PMID:16801507] [PMID:23140417].
- Regulatory status differs by country. In Japan, menatetrenone is a prescription osteoporosis drug given at 45 mg/day (15 mg capsules three times daily). The same 45 mg/day regimen has been trialled in China [PMID:19082528] [PMID:24426779] [PMID:23702931]. Elsewhere MK-4 is sold over the counter as a supplement, usually at microgram to low-milligram doses. RX_STATUS is recorded as otc for the supplement; the 45 mg/day regimen is prescription-only in Japan.
- Dietary MK-4 comes mostly from animal foods. The body also makes it from phylloquinone (K1): K1 is cleaved to menadione in the intestine, and the prenyltransferase UBIAD1 then converts menadione to MK-4 in tissues [PMID:20953171] [PMID:24085302]. MK-4 is the main vitamin K form in the human brain [PMID:40339909].
- Pharmacokinetics compared with MK-7: a nutritional single dose of MK-4 (420 ug) was undetectable in serum, and 60 ug/day for 7 days did not raise serum MK-4. MK-7 at the same doses was well absorbed and accumulated [PMID:23140417]. MK-7 has a much longer half-life than K1 [PMID:17158229]. This is why MK-4 trials use milligram doses.
- Absorption depends on fat. For a 15 mg menatetrenone dose, AUC0-24 roughly tripled when meal fat rose from 8.8 g to 34.9 g, with a plateau above about 35 g [PMID:8877895].
- Reference intakes: EFSA sets adequate intakes for phylloquinone only (70 ug/day for adults). It judged the evidence on menaquinones insufficient to set any value for them [PMID:32625486]. The US ODS says no normal ranges for menaquinones are available, and vitamin K has no tolerable upper intake level [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/] [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/HealthInformation/ODS_Frequently_Asked_Questions.aspx].

** Mechanisms
- MK-4 is a cofactor for gamma-glutamyl carboxylation of vitamin K-dependent proteins. These include clotting factors, osteocalcin (OC) and matrix Gla protein (MGP) [PMID:39125301] [PMID:37252246].
- Osteocalcin carboxylation: 45 mg/day lowered serum undercarboxylated OC (ucOC) within 2 weeks while total OC stayed stable [PMID:12720050]. A dose of 1.5 mg/day also lowered ucOC [PMID:23702931]. In meta-analyses the effect on ucOC is the most consistent finding [PMID:30734066] [PMID:41268154].
- Nuclear receptor signalling: MK-4 activates the steroid and xenobiotic receptor (SXR/PXR) more strongly than phylloquinone, in a reporter assay. This is a proposed non-carboxylation pathway in bone [PMID:14585304] [PMID:39125301].
- Vascular: carboxylated MGP inhibits arterial calcification. Vitamin K supplementation lowers dp-ucMGP (inactive MGP) [PMID:37252246] [PMID:30514729].
- Endogenous MK-4 synthesis by UBIAD1 is not inhibited by warfarin [PMID:20953171]. It needs intermediates of cholesterol synthesis, and atorvastatin lowered kidney MK-4 formation by about 45% in mice [PMID:30753659].
- Proposed anti-HCC activity includes suppression of VEGF-mediated angiogenesis. Combined with an ACE inhibitor, MK-4 lowered serum VEGF in a small clinical study [PMID:19501932].

** Dosing in trials
- Japanese osteoporosis regimen: 45 mg/day menatetrenone (15 mg three times daily after meals), often with calcium, for 1-3 years [PMID:10750566] [PMID:19082528] [PMID:17287908].
- Combination regimens: 45 mg/day with alfacalcidol 0.75 ug/day [PMID:11180916], with oral bisphosphonate plus calcium/vitamin D [PMID:37338608], and with risedronate (JOINT-03 design) [PMID:23828145].
- Low-dose nutritional MK-4: 1.5 mg/day for 12 months lowered ucOC and prevented forearm BMD loss in a 48-woman RCT [PMID:23702931]. Doses of 60-420 ug did not raise serum MK-4 [PMID:23140417].
- HCC trials: 45 mg/day after resection or ablation [PMID:16400650]. The phase III trial tested 45 and 90 mg/day [PMID:21574174]. With sorafenib, 45 mg/day was used [PMID:33481328].
- Other settings: 45 mg/day in cirrhosis-related bone loss [PMID:12003435], after pelvic radiotherapy [PMID:39849242], and in an open-label CAC study [PMID:27175730].
- Taking doses with fat-containing meals increases absorption [PMID:8877895].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Pharmacological doses are generally well tolerated, but adverse events are more frequent than with placebo. Su 2019 found AEs RR 1.47 (1.07-2.02) and adverse drug reactions RR 1.29 (1.07-1.56), with no difference in serious AEs [PMID:30734066]. Zhou 2022 found an adverse-reaction RR of 1.33 (1.11-1.59) and no serious events [PMID:35711002]. Ma 2022 found no difference (RR 1.03) [PMID:36033779].
- In the OF study (n=4378), skin and skin-appendage lesions were significantly more common with menatetrenone [PMID:19818211] [PMID:19082528].
- Coagulation: MK-4 is a functional vitamin K and reverses warfarin anticoagulation. In Japan, vitamin K2 is used to reverse warfarin-related coagulopathy [PMID:25636520].
- No tolerable upper intake level has been set for vitamin K [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/HealthInformation/ODS_Frequently_Asked_Questions.aspx]. MK-4-specific long-term safety data beyond 3 years are sparse [PMID:17287908].
- Research integrity: some menatetrenone fracture trials and a meta-analysis by one research group were retracted in 2018 [PMID:19499964] [PMID:12110423]. A 2019 update noted that the integrity of some earlier evidence had been questioned [PMID:31076817].

** Contraindications
- Vitamin K antagonist therapy (warfarin, acenocoumarol, phenprocoumon): MK-4 antagonises the anticoagulant effect. Prior warfarin use was an exclusion criterion in JOINT-03 [PMID:23828145]. Even microgram doses of MK-7 lowered INR [PMID:23530987]. Anticoagulated patients are advised to keep vitamin K intake consistent [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/].
- HCC surveillance that relies on PIVKA-II/DCP: vitamin K lowers DCP and can mask this tumour marker (see Lab-test interference) [PMID:1309308].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin and other VKAs (major): vitamin K reverses VKA-induced coagulopathy [PMID:25636520]. Low-dose MK-7 (10-45 ug) lowered INR in a dose-dependent way in healthy volunteers taking acenocoumarol [PMID:23530987]. Supplements supplying 50 ug/day or more of MK-7 may interfere with anticoagulation [PMID:17158229]. No equivalent MK-4 dose-response study was found, so the size of the effect at supplement doses is inferred. Pharmacological 45 mg/day doses would be expected to antagonise warfarin strongly.
- Low-dose vitamin K for INR stability: Cochrane found insufficient data (2 trials, 100 participants, using vitamin K1 150-175 ug) [PMID:24832594]. This does not apply to MK-4.
- Warfarin depletes tissue MK-4: users had 68-79% lower postmortem brain MK-4 [PMID:40339909].
- Bisphosphonates: an add-on to bisphosphonate gave no extra BMD benefit in an RCT [PMID:37338608]. JOINT-03 was designed to test MK-4 plus risedronate [PMID:23828145].
- Glucocorticoids: MK-4 prevented prednisolone-induced lumbar BMD loss over 8 weeks, comparable to alfacalcidol [PMID:14712427].
- Statins: atorvastatin lowered renal MK-4 synthesis in mice [PMID:30753659]. In dialysis patients, the highest PIVKA-II was seen with a statin plus a proton pump inhibitor [PMID:34774554]. Clinical relevance is unknown.
- Sorafenib: adding 45 mg/day MK-4 raised objective response (27.3% vs 4.5%) and PFS (HR 0.44) in a small phase II HCC trial [PMID:33481328].
- ACE inhibitors (perindopril): combining with MK-4 reduced cumulative HCC recurrence. Neither drug alone did [PMID:19501932].
- Orlistat and fat malabsorption: orlistat produced a non-significant fall in vitamin K [PMID:12126214]. MK-4 absorption depends on dietary fat [PMID:8877895].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin E: 1000 IU/day RRR-alpha-tocopherol for 12 weeks raised PIVKA-II (for example from 1.7 to 11.9 ng/mL) in adults not taking anticoagulants. This indicates vitamin K antagonism [PMID:15213041]. High-vitamin E diets lowered extrahepatic MK-4 and phylloquinone in rats [PMID:16857056]. The two vitamins share omega-hydroxylation and PXR pathways [PMID:14585304].
- Vitamin D-3 and active vitamin D: combining menatetrenone with alfacalcidol raised lumbar BMD more than either alone in a small RCT [PMID:11180916]. A review describes possible synergy [PMID:39125301]. However, MK-4 added to calcium plus vitamin D3 had no BMD effect in North American women [PMID:19113922]. The calcium rise seen with alfacalcidol was attenuated when MK-4 was added [PMID:14712427].
- K1: phylloquinone is a precursor of tissue MK-4 via menadione and UBIAD1 [PMID:20953171] [PMID:24085302]. K1 1 mg/day and MK-4 45 mg/day gave similar ucOC lowering and null BMD results [PMID:19113922]. Both add to the total vitamin K load in VKA users.
- K2 MK-7: MK-7 has much higher bioavailability and a longer half-life at nutritional doses [PMID:23140417] [PMID:17158229]. In dialysis, uptake of MK-4 into lipoproteins was better preserved than uptake of MK-7 [PMID:34774554].
- Calcium: most menatetrenone fracture trials co-administered calcium [PMID:19082528] [PMID:24426779].
*** Conditions
- Chronic kidney disease/dialysis: vitamin K transport and MK-4 kidney synthesis are disturbed [PMID:34774554].
- Liver cirrhosis/HCC: MK-4 prevented lumbar BMD loss in women with viral cirrhosis [PMID:12003435]. HCC chemoprevention evidence is conflicting [PMID:21574174] [PMID:41388930].
- Fat malabsorption: reduced absorption is expected [PMID:8877895].
*** Lab-test interference
- PIVKA-II/DCP (HCC marker): intravenous MK-4 lowers DCP in HCC patients with the same kinetics as in vitamin K deficiency, then DCP rebounds within 7-10 days [PMID:1309308]. DCP should be interpreted with knowledge of vitamin K intake.
- ucOC and the ucOC/OC ratio: these fall markedly with MK-4 (by 82.3% in one RCT), so they reflect intake rather than bone status [PMID:24426779] [PMID:12720050].
- INR/PT in VKA users: falls with vitamin K intake (a true pharmacodynamic effect) [PMID:23530987].
- Total osteocalcin: rises with MK-4 [PMID:41268154] [PMID:10750566].

** Genotype notes
- VKORC1 risk alleles (rs9923231, rs9934438) were modestly associated with osteopenia/osteoporosis (OR 1.16, 1.01-1.35; 6 studies, n=7335) [PMID:41597466]. No study tested whether VKORC1 genotype modifies the response to MK-4.

** Latest research
- [2026-08] VitaK-CAC RCT (MK-7 360 ug/day, 2 y, n=180): slower CAC progression than placebo (p=0.02). This is a comparator for MK-4, which lacks such data [PMID:42268593].
- [2026-03] HCC MA (11 studies, n=688): vitamin K improved PFS (HR 0.62) and recurrence (HR 0.26) but not overall survival (HR 0.77, 0.58-1.01) [PMID:41930200].
- [2026-01] Glycaemia MA (8 RCTs): FBG SMD -0.22, HbA1c MD -1.00%, HOMA-IR MD -0.63. Quality was moderate [PMID:41599883].
- [2026-01] VKORC1 polymorphism MA: OR 1.16 for osteopenia/osteoporosis [PMID:41597466].
- [2026-01] Dietary vitamin K and fracture MA (6 cohorts, n=93,776): RR 0.83 (0.68-1.01); risk fell up to 120 ug/day [PMID:40947710].
- [2025-12] HCC MA with trial sequential analysis (11 studies, n=1030): lower recurrence and mortality at 1-3 y. The large phase III trial was null [PMID:41388930] [PMID:21574174].
- [2025-11] Bone turnover MA (9 RCTs, n=2570): OC MD +1.86, ucOC WMD -1.54, CTX -0.09; NTX unchanged [PMID:41268154].
- [2025-05] Rush MAP autopsy study: warfarin users had 68-79% lower brain MK-4. Cognitive diagnosis did not differ [PMID:40339909].
- [2025-01] Uterine cancer radiotherapy cohort (n=34): 45 mg/day menatetrenone prevented BMD loss in irradiated vertebrae [PMID:39849242].
- [2024-12] Vitamin K BMD MA: lumbar spine BMD increased (p=0.035), driven by carboxylation changes rather than total OC [PMID:39657786].

** Open questions
- Whether the Japanese fracture benefit is real after excluding retracted or high-risk-of-bias trials. The largest trial (OF study) was null in its full analysis set [PMID:19082528] [PMID:31076817].
- Whether nutritional (microgram) MK-4 doses have any clinical effect, given that they do not raise serum MK-4 [PMID:23140417].
- Dose-response for warfarin interaction with MK-4 specifically (data mostly MK-7/K1).
- Whether MK-4 slows vascular calcification. Existing positive data are for mixed vitamin K forms or MK-7 [PMID:37252246] [PMID:42268593].
- Whether HCC recurrence benefit in small trials survives in high-quality RCTs [PMID:21574174].
- Clinical significance of the vitamin E-vitamin K antagonism at common supplement doses [PMID:15213041].
