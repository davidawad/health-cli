** Identity & forms
- L-Theanine is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: theanine, gamma-glutamylethylamide. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:41227106, PMID:39633316, PMID:42410082].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:41227106, PMID:39633316, PMID:42410082].

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Possible small short-term improvements in selected attention tasks | Systematic review/meta-analysis or human trial synthesis | Mixed/possible benefit; no established clinical disease prevention | See evidence summary below; pooled estimates vary by outcome and population | low | PMID:41227106, PMID:39633316, PMID:42410082 |

- 2025 meta-analysis of five RCTs (148 healthy adults) found a small improvement in selected visual processing/reaction-time outcomes, but not all cognitive tests [PMID:41227106]. A systematic review in mental disorders found limited heterogeneous adjunctive RCT evidence [PMID:39633316].
- Possible small short-term improvements in selected attention tasks; subjective stress/sleep findings remain preliminary and are not consistent across outcomes [PMID:41227106, PMID:39633316].

** Dosing in trials
- Trials often use 100–400 mg/day; acute cognition trials use around 200 mg before testing; tea-derived doses are lower [PMID:41227106].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short-term studies suggest reasonable tolerability; adverse-event and long-term data remain limited [PMID:41227106].

** Contraindications
- Insufficient evidence in pregnancy, lactation and children [PMID:41227106].

** Interactions
- Possible additive sedation or blood-pressure lowering is theoretical; robust clinical interaction studies are lacking [PMID:41227106].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis pooled 31 RCTs (1,168 participants), reinforcing that outcomes vary across cognitive and affective domains [PMID:42410082].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
