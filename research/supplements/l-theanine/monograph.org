** Identity & forms
- Non-proteinogenic amino acid (N-ethyl-L-glutamine, gamma-glutamylethylamide) found in the tea plant Camellia sinensis and some mushrooms [PMID:42761839] [PMID:17182482] [PMID:26192072].
- Dietary exposure from tea is low: about 20 mg per cup of black tea [PMID:18296328]. Supplement doses (100-900 mg) are far higher.
- Commercial forms used in trials:
  - Suntheanine (Taiyo Kagaku). Used in the pediatric ADHD sleep RCT [PMID:22214254] and in the 13-week rat toxicology study [PMID:16759779].
  - AlphaWave L-theanine, 200 mg single dose and 400 mg/d for 28 days [PMID:34562208] [PMID:38758503].
  - Tea-extracted, chemically synthesized and E. coli-fermented L-theanine. Each has its own US GRAS notice [URL:https://www.hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?set=GRASNotices&type=basic&search=theanine].
- Usually sold alone or combined with caffeine (energy drinks/shots, "nootropic" stacks) [PMID:36862943], or in sleep blends with GABA, magnesium, glycine or melatonin-related ingredients [PMID:41636292] [PMID:42742055] [PMID:36094342].
- Many sleep and stress trials tested multi-ingredient products, not pure theanine. This makes it hard to attribute effects to theanine [PMID:40056718].

** Mechanisms
- Glutamatergic: in animal neurochemistry, theanine has micromolar affinity for AMPA, kainate and NMDA receptors and may antagonize group I metabotropic glutamate receptors. It raises brain serotonin, dopamine and GABA [PMID:17182482]. It also inhibits glutamate transporters, which is shown in tumor cells [PMID:14643924]. Reviews describe it as a glutamate reuptake inhibitor and a low-affinity glutamate receptor antagonist [PMID:35111479].
- In schizophrenia, 8 weeks of theanine (250 mg/d) did not change brain Glx on 1H-MRS, but the change correlated with baseline Glx. The authors called this "stabilising" [PMID:25896423].
- EEG alpha activity:
  - 50 mg raised resting alpha activity versus placebo [PMID:18296328].
  - 200 mg AlphaWave raised frontal alpha power 3 h after dosing during a stress challenge [PMID:34562208].
  - Theanine alone (100 mg) did not change alpha activity during a sustained-attention task [PMID:22326943]. Alpha effects therefore depend on the task and dose.
- Autonomic/stress physiology: theanine reduced heart rate and salivary IgA responses to an arithmetic stressor, which HRV analysis attributed to lower sympathetic activation [PMID:16930802]. It lowered salivary alpha-amylase during a stressful practicum [PMID:24051231].
- Sensorimotor gating: 200 and 400 mg, but not 600 mg, increased prepulse inhibition in healthy adults (inverted-U dose response) [PMID:24372613].
- Blood pressure: high intraperitoneal doses (1500-2000 mg/kg) lowered BP in spontaneously hypertensive rats, not normotensive rats, and reduced brain 5-hydroxyindoles [PMID:7539642]. Cardiovascular mechanisms (Nrf2, NF-kB, eNOS, AMPK) are reviewed but are mostly preclinical [PMID:41796839].
- Metabolism: theanine is hydrolyzed to ethylamine and glutamic acid, so its effects may come partly from these metabolites [PMID:23096008].

** Dosing in trials
- Acute cognition: 200 mg taken 30-60 min before testing [PMID:42410082]. The effect appeared at 50 mg on EEG [PMID:18296328]. 200-400 mg, but not 600 mg, increased PPI [PMID:24372613].
- Caffeine combinations (theanine + caffeine): 97 + 40 mg [PMID:21040626]; 100 + 50 mg [PMID:18681988]; 250 + 150 mg [PMID:18006208]; 200 + 160 mg in sleep-deprived adults [PMID:40789769]; 3 mg/kg each in athletes [PMID:40977612]; 2.5 + 2.0 mg/kg in children with ADHD [PMID:32753637].
- Stress/anxiety: 200-400 mg/d [PMID:31758301] [PMID:31412272]. Chronic regimens include 200 mg/d for 4 wk [PMID:31623400] and 400 mg/d for 28 d [PMID:38758503].
- Sleep: 200-450 mg/d. Trials used 50-900 mg/d [PMID:41176609]. 200 mg about 2 h before bed for insomnia in cancer patients [PMID:38846134].
- Psychiatric adjunct:
  - Schizophrenia: 400 mg/d for 8 wk [PMID:21208586] [PMID:37697164], or 250 mg/d [PMID:25896423].
  - MDD: 200 mg/d with sertraline [PMID:37084960].
  - GAD: 450-900 mg/d [PMID:30580081].
- Pediatric ADHD: 400 mg/d (2 x 100 mg chewables twice daily) for 6 wk in boys aged 8-12 [PMID:22214254].
- PK: 100 mg from capsules or green tea gives similar kinetics. Plasma Cmax is about 24-26 umol/L at 0.8 h, and AUC rises with dose from 50 to 200 mg [PMID:23096008]. Half-life is about 1 h (LactMed) [PMID:40460226]. In mice, oral bioavailability is about 70% and serum levels peak at 15 min [PMID:38583116]. Theanine crosses the blood-brain barrier [PMID:39854799]. In rats, piperine-containing formulations increased absorption by raising intestinal blood flow [PMID:34115818].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Tolerability in trials:
  - The 31-RCT MA found no serious adverse events and compared dropouts between theanine and placebo [PMID:42410082].
  - 400 mg/d for 28 d produced no clinically relevant lab or vital-sign changes [PMID:38758503].
  - 400 mg/d for 8 wk in schizophrenia and 400 mg/d in boys with ADHD were well tolerated [PMID:21208586] [PMID:22214254].
- Reported effects in single studies:
  - 250 mg increased "headache" ratings and reduced serial-subtraction accuracy [PMID:18006208].
  - 200 mg slowed overall reaction time on a visual probe task [PMID:17891480].
  - 200 mg alone reduced maximal isometric leg/back strength in athletes [PMID:41668720].
- Toxicology: a 13-week OECD-408/GLP rat study found a NOAEL of 4000 mg/kg bw/day, the highest dose tested. Renal tubular adenomas in high-dose females were judged not treatment-related [PMID:16759779]. Reviews describe a favorable toxicology profile but limited human long-term data [PMID:26192072] [PMID:39854799].
- Regulatory: FDA issued "no questions" letters for L-theanine GRAS notices GRN 209 (2007), GRN 338 (tea-extracted, 2010), GRN 501 (chemically synthesized, 2014) and GRN 1311 (E. coli-produced, Sep 2026). GRN 1344 is pending [URL:https://www.hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?set=GRASNotices&type=basic&search=theanine].
- Critical view: a 2025 review urges caution with pharmacologic doses because claims outpace rigorous human trials [PMID:39854799].
- Green tea extracts (not pure theanine) have caused dose-dependent hepatotoxicity linked to catechin/EGCG content [PMID:29580974]. A theanine review flags the need to separate the two [PMID:31412272].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindications have been established.
- Breastfeeding: there are no data. LactMed advises it is probably best to avoid supplemental theanine while nursing a newborn or preterm infant. Tea-level intake is likely acceptable [PMID:40460226].
- Pregnancy and children: data are limited to small pediatric trials [PMID:22214254] [PMID:35215501]. There are no human pregnancy safety data. Only one mouse gestational-obesity study was found [PMID:41016812].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- CNS depressants/hypnotics:
  - In mice, theanine lowered the hypnotic ED50 of pentobarbital and enhanced its anticonvulsant effect [PMID:26330182]. In rats it acted additively or synergistically with midazolam on anxiety and motor activity [PMID:20108732].
  - In humans, 200 mg had no acute anxiolytic effect in an anticipatory-anxiety model, and neither did alprazolam 1 mg [PMID:15378679].
  - Additive sedation is possible but has not been shown in humans.
- Antihypertensives: theanine blunted stress-induced BP rises in high responders [PMID:23107346] and lowered BP in hypertensive rats at very high doses [PMID:7539642]. Any additive effect is theoretical and likely minor.
- Stimulants (caffeine, methylphenidate):
  - Theanine antagonized the caffeine-induced BP rise (250 mg caffeine + 200 mg theanine) [PMID:17891480].
  - It reduced caffeine-associated anxiety and tachycardia in wrestlers [PMID:40977612].
  - In boys with ADHD, the RCT stratified randomization by stimulant use and reported no significant AEs [PMID:22214254]. Theanine+caffeine was compared with, not combined with, methylphenidate [PMID:42096377].
- Antipsychotics (risperidone and others): used as an add-on in RCTs with no increase in side effects or extrapyramidal symptoms [PMID:21208586] [PMID:37697164].
- Antidepressants (sertraline): adjunct 200 mg/d gave better HDRS response, with side effects comparable to placebo [PMID:37084960]. Adjunct use with stable antidepressants in GAD caused no safety signal [PMID:30580081].
- Chemotherapy (animal data only):
  - In tumor-bearing mice, theanine increased doxorubicin and idarubicin concentrations in tumors and their antitumor effect, without raising concentrations in normal tissue. It reduced myelosuppression, possibly via inhibition of glutamate transporters and the GS-X pump [PMID:14643924] [PMID:10960760].
  - In mice it reduced cisplatin nephrotoxicity [PMID:39607236].
  - Anticancer effects are in vitro/in vivo only [PMID:33891786]. No human interaction data exist.
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine: the best-characterized combination, with additive or synergistic effects on attention and alertness. Theanine blunts caffeine's BP rise and jitteriness [PMID:24946991] [PMID:40314930] [PMID:18006208] [PMID:17891480].
- Melatonin: in a head-to-head RCT, melatonin 3 mg was better than theanine 200 mg for insomnia, and both beat placebo [PMID:38846134]. No combination study was found. Theanine is described as relaxing without sedation [PMID:41176609].
- Glycine: a 5-ingredient sleep blend (tryptophan, glycine, magnesium, tart cherry, theanine) shortened sleep latency by 24 min and increased sleep efficiency [PMID:36094342]. In mice, theanine increased a serum peak overlapping glycine [PMID:38583116] and is reported to induce glycine release [PMID:26330182].
- GABA: synergistic sleep effects in rodents [PMID:30707852]. A single-arm human study (GABA 700 mg + theanine 200 mg) improved PSQI (MD -3.15, 95% CI -4.01 to -2.31) [PMID:41636292].
- Magnesium: a theanine+GABA+magnesium+SAMe supplement improved menopausal sleep disturbance (PROMIS SD -11.1, CI -13.6 to -8.6) [PMID:42742055]. An evening MgT+theanine+apigenin blend showed minimal effects over 2 weeks [PMID:42829807].
- Green Tea Extract: supplies theanine plus catechins. Hepatotoxicity relates to EGCG dose, not theanine [PMID:29580974].
- Piperine-containing formulations increased theanine absorption in rats [PMID:34115818].
*** Conditions
- Hypotension or BP-lowering therapy: see Antihypertensives. The effect in humans is small and stress-dependent [PMID:23107346].
- Breastfeeding: see Contraindications [PMID:40460226].
- Schizophrenia, MDD, GAD and ADHD: theanine was studied only as an adjunct to standard treatment [PMID:39633316].
*** Lab-test interference
- No clinical assay interference has been reported. In mice, theanine produced a chromatographic peak overlapping glycine in PITC-derivatized amino-acid analysis [PMID:38583116]. Plasma amino-acid profiles could theoretically be affected. Theanine is metabolized to glutamate and ethylamine, which rise transiently in plasma [PMID:23096008].

** Open questions
- Whether sleep and stress benefits hold for pure theanine. Most sleep RCTs used blends [PMID:40056718].
- Dose-response. PPI data suggest an inverted U [PMID:24372613], and optimal doses for each outcome are undefined.
- Long-term (>8-12 week) human safety, and safety in pregnancy, lactation and children.
- Human relevance of animal findings: potentiation of barbiturates and benzodiazepines, and modulation of anthracycline chemotherapy.
- Whether the caffeine "synergy" exceeds caffeine's own effect. Moderator analysis suggests caffeine dose dominates [PMID:24946991], and some trials found no synergy [PMID:22326943] [PMID:41668720].
- Replication of schizophrenia, MDD and ADHD adjunct findings in larger, independent, preregistered trials.
- Industry funding is common (tea and supplement makers) [PMID:40314930] [PMID:42410082].
