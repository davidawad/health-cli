** Identity & forms
- Aromatic, conditionally dispensable amino acid. It is made in the liver from phenylalanine by phenylalanine hydroxylase, and becomes essential in phenylketonuria (PKU) [PMID:39427751] [PMID:42123950].
- It is the precursor of catecholamines (dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine), thyroid hormones and melanin [PMID:35201733] [PMID:17513421] [PMID:21834848].
- Sold as free-form L-tyrosine powder/capsules. It is also a standard ingredient of PKU protein substitutes [PMID:11157309], energy drinks [PMID:36862943] and multi-ingredient "focus" products [PMID:31771598].
- Normal serum tyrosine is about 30-120 umol/L. Symptoms of hypertyrosinemia generally do not appear below about 500 umol/L [PMID:35201733].

** Mechanisms
- Tyrosine hydroxylase is the rate-limiting enzyme of catecholamine synthesis and is not normally saturated. Raising brain tyrosine increases catecholamine synthesis only in actively firing neurons, and Tyr is preferred over Phe as substrate [PMID:17513421].
- Brain entry is via the LAT1 (SLC7A5) transporter at the blood-brain barrier. Tyrosine competes there with the other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs), including levodopa [PMID:41067390] [PMID:6694694]. Trials therefore report the plasma Tyr:LNAA ratio: 150 mg/kg raised it 2.5-2.9-fold; 300-400 mg/kg raised it about 8-fold [PMID:21437603] [PMID:24389518] [PMID:31834099].
- Depletion model (converse evidence): tyrosine/phenylalanine-free amino-acid drinks lower dopamine function in humans. They raise prolactin and impair spatial working memory [PMID:11291999], reduce amphetamine-induced striatal dopamine release on PET [PMID:14583741], and blunt stimulant effects and mania ratings [PMID:11581118].
- "Depletion-repletion" hypothesis: tyrosine helps cognition mainly when catecholamines are temporarily depleted by acute stress or high demand and neurotransmitter function is intact [PMID:26424423] [PMID:25797188]. Benefit may also depend on baseline dopamine tone, i.e. an inverted-U [PMID:25339925] [PMID:27403851].
- Thyroid hormones are formed by thyroid peroxidase iodinating tyrosyl residues within thyroglobulin, not free tyrosine. Synthesis is driven by TSH, iodide uptake through NIS, and H2O2 [PMID:28153798] [PMID:36834802]. Iodine supply, not tyrosine, is the nutritional limit [PMID:34960019].
- Melanogenesis: L-tyrosine and L-DOPA are substrates of melanogenesis and also act as hormone-like regulators of melanocytes [PMID:21834848].

** Dosing in trials
- Acute cognitive/stress protocols: 100-150 mg/kg as a single dose about 60-90 min before testing [PMID:10548261] [PMID:8029265]. Some protocols gave two 150 mg/kg doses, 300 mg/kg in total [PMID:17585971] [PMID:25220844]. Fixed doses of 2.0 g were also used [PMID:31521870] [PMID:38975711].
- A single 150 mg/kg dose raises serum tyrosine as much as two doses [PMID:26285023]. Doses of 300 and 400 mg/kg raise the Tyr:LNAA ratio similarly, so there is a plateau [PMID:31834099].
- Repeated dosing: 2 g five times daily in a drink during combat training [PMID:10230711]; 100 mg/kg/d for 4 weeks in depression and PKU trials [PMID:2142699] [PMID:9579151] [PMID:8523192]; 2.5 g three times daily in hypertension [PMID:3891616]; 500 mg twice daily in Parkinson's disease on levodopa [PMID:30363894].
- Older adults reach higher plasma tyrosine than young adults on the same mg/kg dose [PMID:29168741]. Maternal PKU: a single 1.3 g dose normalized plasma tyrosine for at least 3 h [PMID:9497192].

** Safety & adverse effects
- 2026 crossover RCT (30 healthy men, 1-4 g/d for 4 weeks each): no dose-related changes in hematology, biochemistry or electrolytes, and AEs similar to placebo. Authors propose 4 g/d as a 4-week NOAEL. The study was industry-sponsored (Ajinomoto) [PMID:42356406].
- Acute 150-300 mg/kg doses in healthy adults: heart rate and blood pressure rose during a multitask battery, not clearly attributable to tyrosine [PMID:10548261]. Anger increased under severe stress [PMID:25220844]. No side effects at 6 g/d in cocaine dependence [PMID:8895116]. In depression, adverse effects did not differ from placebo [PMID:2142699]. A 12-week pilot RCT in adolescents with anorexia nervosa reported no side effects [PMID:39548569].
- Neurocognitive harm signals: dose-dependent working-memory decline in older adults [PMID:29168741], worse proactive inhibition with age [PMID:30094335], and worse cognitive flexibility under load in young adults [PMID:31521870].
- Chronically high plasma tyrosine is harmful in some settings: corneal lesions and palmoplantar hyperkeratosis in tyrosinemia type II [PMID:39446998]; keratopathy in nitisinone-induced tyrosinemia, controlled below about 600 umol/L [PMID:28879639].
- PKU: experts warn that extra free tyrosine and high-tyrosine protein substitutes cause large daytime swings in blood tyrosine and may not be safe for the fetus in maternal PKU [PMID:11157309].
- No regulator-set tolerable upper intake level was found. Long-term (>4 weeks) controlled safety data are absent.

** Contraindications
- Hereditary tyrosinemia types I-III: treatment requires lifelong tyrosine/phenylalanine restriction [PMID:39446998] [PMID:28771246] [PMID:35201733].
- Nitisinone therapy (HT-1, alkaptonuria): already causes hypertyrosinemia, and is managed with Tyr/Phe restriction [PMID:31503358] [PMID:34736252] [PMID:28879639].
- Phenelzine: the US label says patients should not take L-tyrosine [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=513a41d0-37d4-4355-8a6d-a2c643bce6fa].
- Relative contraindications (caution): mania/bipolar disorder (inferred from depletion studies) [PMID:11581118]; melanoma (mechanistic only) [PMID:34869032]; older adults using high acute doses for cognition [PMID:29168741].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- MAO inhibitors (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid, high-dose selegiline):
  - The phenelzine (Nardil) label groups L-tyrosine with L-dopa, L-tryptophan and phenylalanine as "related compounds" that may cause hypertensive crisis [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=513a41d0-37d4-4355-8a6d-a2c643bce6fa]. Be precise: the classic MAOI dietary reaction comes from tyramine, a decarboxylated amine that MAO normally clears in the gut and liver. Tyrosine is the amino acid, not tyramine [PMID:30255284] [PMID:35721816].
  - No published case of tyrosine-MAOI hypertensive crisis was found. The only review claiming none exist has been retracted [PMID:25092999]. Treat the combination as caution based on the label, not as a proven reaction.
- Levodopa (carbidopa/levodopa):
  - Levodopa shares intestinal transporters with dietary amino acids [PMID:25073474] and competes with LNAAs at LAT1 [PMID:41067390].
  - Oral LNAAs at 100 mg/kg reversed the effect of infused levodopa without lowering plasma levodopa [PMID:6694694]. The Sinemet label warns that high-protein diets may impair absorption [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9b17b028-964a-473c-823d-81423535bd66]. Tyrosine itself was not tested in that study. A 7-day RCT of 1 g/d in levodopa-treated PD found no BP benefit and no harm [PMID:30363894].
- Amphetamines (Dexedrine) and methylphenidate:
  - Tyrosine availability sets the size of stimulant-evoked dopamine release. Depletion reduced d-amphetamine dopamine release on PET [PMID:14583741] and subjective effects [PMID:10475719]. In rats, exogenous tyrosine potentiated methylphenidate-evoked accumbens dopamine [PMID:1760749]. An additive effect in humans is plausible but unstudied.
- Nitisinone: additive hypertyrosinemia (see Contraindications) [PMID:31503358].
- Thyroid hormone (levothyroxine) and antithyroid drugs: no interaction study was found. Free tyrosine is not rate-limiting for hormone synthesis [PMID:28153798]. Twelve months of tyrosine in YARS2 disease caused no thyroid toxicity [PMID:41404429].
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine: often co-formulated. Caffeine plus theanine plus tyrosine improved movement accuracy in athletes (industry-funded RCT) [PMID:31771598]. Caffeine and L-tyrosine are the lead military nootropic candidates [PMID:38851246]. ISSN says caffeine is the main active ingredient in energy drinks [PMID:36862943].
- L-Theanine: in a 3-arm RCT (n=80), neither changed salivary stress markers. Only tyrosine (2 g) reduced Stroop misses [PMID:38975711]. The combination has only been tested within a multi-ingredient product [PMID:31771598].
- Iodine (potassium iodide): complementary substrates. Iodine, not tyrosine, limits thyroid hormone synthesis, so no additive thyroid effect is expected [PMID:34960019] [PMID:36834802].
- Lithium: lithium did not correct reduced fibroblast tyrosine uptake in bipolar disorder, whereas clozapine did [PMID:37318540]. Tyrosine depletion is antimanic [PMID:11581118]. Loading tyrosine in mania is theoretically counterproductive (no clinical data).
- Other LNAA products (BCAAs, LNAA tablets in PKU) compete for the same transporter and lower brain Tyr uptake (LAT1 competition) [PMID:6694694] [PMID:41067390].
- No cited interaction was found with the other supplements in this batch.
*** Conditions
- Phenylketonuria: tyrosine is already in Phe-free protein substitutes. Extra supplementation is not routinely recommended [PMID:33427303] [PMID:11157309]. European guideline targets concern Phe [PMID:28082082] [PMID:42520342].
- Hereditary tyrosinemia, alkaptonuria on nitisinone: avoid [PMID:39446998] [PMID:34736252].
- Liver cirrhosis: endogenous tyrosine is already raised [PMID:18653725]. A low Fischer (BCAA:aromatic) ratio tracks encephalopathy grade [PMID:30568347].
- Mania/bipolar disorder: see Lithium [PMID:11581118].
- Melanoma (mechanistic caution only): high tyrosine pushed primary melanoma cultures toward MAPK-inhibitor-resistant phenotypes in vitro [PMID:34869032], and Tyr/Phe restriction blocked metastasis in rodents [PMID:3470551]. Human restriction diets produced no tumor response and were poorly tolerated [PMID:3966427] [PMID:12028515].
- Older adults: possible cognitive worsening with high acute doses [PMID:29168741] [PMID:30094335]. Paradoxically, low endogenous tyrosine is associated with postoperative neurocognitive delay and ICU delirium (observational) [PMID:40518008].
- Parkinson's disease: the serum Tyr/Phe ratio is low in PD [PMID:27061063]. Separate tyrosine from levodopa doses (see Drugs).
*** Lab-test interference
- Plasma amino acids: supplementation raises plasma tyrosine and Tyr:LNAA. The rise is modest at 4 g/d fasting [PMID:42356406] and about 2-fold 2 h after 2.5 g [PMID:3891616]. Take a dosing history when interpreting plasma amino acid profiles or Phe:Tyr ratios in PKU monitoring [PMID:20882350].
- Tyrosinemia screening: newborn screening uses blood-spot succinylacetone, which is more specific than tyrosine [PMID:28771246] [PMID:28274233]. Adding Tyr and Tyr/Phe improved PPV from 9% to 72% [PMID:40407518]. Elevated tyrosine on expanded screening can detect type II [PMID:18577048].
- Catecholamine metabolites: tyrosine increased urinary MHPG in a depression RCT [PMID:2142699]. Plasma epinephrine, norepinephrine and dopamine rose after 6.5 g under heat [PMID:24141022]. Biochemical catecholamine testing could be affected; no assay study was found.

** Genotype notes
- DRD2 C957T (rs6277): T/T homozygotes benefited more than C/C on stop-signal and N-back tasks (RCT; single lab) [PMID:27403851].
- Inborn errors: biallelic TAT variants (tyrosinemia II), FAH (type I) and PAH (PKU) change tyrosine handling. In these patients, supplementation is either contraindicated or specialist-directed [PMID:39446998] [PMID:28771246] [PMID:33427303].
- YARS1/YARS2 (tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase) deficiencies: tyrosine raised in vitro TyrRS activity, and supplementation was associated with clinical gains in case series [PMID:41518087] [PMID:41404429].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] European PKU guideline infographic summary published [PMID:42520342].
- [2026-08] Football RCT (n=24, crossover): tyrosine improved Stroop performance; combined with carnosine, incongruent RT was 15% faster [PMID:42676602].
- [2026-06] 4-week graded-dose safety RCT: up to 4 g/d well tolerated; proposed NOAEL 4 g/d [PMID:42356406].
- [2026-04] Mentally fatigued cyclists: 300 mg/kg increased time to exhaustion by 16% [PMID:41818465].
- [2026-04] YARS1 deficiency (n=9): tyrosine plus high-protein diet improved motor skills in 8/9 [PMID:41518087].
- [2026-04] Pediatric PKU narrative review: diet and protein substitutes remain the cornerstone [PMID:42123950].
- [2025-12] YARS2 case reports: 12 months of tyrosine well tolerated with no thyroid or cardiac toxicity [PMID:41404429].
- [2025-11] Tyrosine hydroxylase deficiency guideline: L-dopa, not tyrosine, is first-line treatment [PMID:41215497].
- [2025-08] Low preoperative serum tyrosine associated with delayed neurocognitive recovery in older surgical patients (AUC 0.73) [PMID:40518008].
- [2025-07] Military review: caffeine and L-tyrosine are the leading dietary nootropic candidates, but the evidence is weak [PMID:38851246].
- [2025-05] Dutch HT-1 screening: adding tyrosine-based markers raised PPV to 72% [PMID:40407518].
- [2025-01] Soccer players in heat (150 mg/kg): no effect on power or decision-making [PMID:39820592].
- [2024-11] Anorexia nervosa pilot RCT: not feasible to recruit; tyrosine raised blood levels with no side effects [PMID:39548569].

** Open questions
- No quantitative meta-analysis of cognitive outcomes exists. Trials are small, acute, heterogeneous, and concentrated in a few labs. A tDCS-tyrosine study from one group was retracted [PMID:35659065].
- Whether chronic dosing gives any benefit, and whether tolerance develops as seen in adult ADD [PMID:3300376].
- Dose-response and the threshold for harm in older adults [PMID:29168741].
- Whether the phenelzine label caution reflects any real pressor risk (no data) [PMID:25092999].
- Long-term safety above 4 g/d [PMID:42356406], and whether high-dose supplementation affects melanoma biology in humans [PMID:34869032].
