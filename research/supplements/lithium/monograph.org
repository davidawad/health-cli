** Identity & forms
- Lithium (Li+) is an alkali-metal cation. Two very different dose domains need to be kept apart: background/trace intake (micrograms per day or per litre of water) and therapeutic lithium (serum mmol/L) [PMID:42693365].
- Prescription form: lithium carbonate is an Rx mood stabilizer for bipolar I disorder. It carries a boxed warning that toxicity can occur at doses close to therapeutic concentrations. The therapeutic range is 0.8-1.2 mEq/L and toxicity starts at >=1.5 mEq/L. Elimination is renal, with a half-life of about 18-36 h [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Supplement form: lithium orotate (OTC) has been sold for decades as a supplemental lithium source. Lithium-equivalent therapeutic doses are 5.5-67 times higher than typical supplemental orotate doses [PMID:34146638]. Elemental content: 3.83 mg Li per 100 mg orotate vs 18.8 mg Li per 100 mg carbonate [PMID:18072162]. 5 mg/day lithium from orotate gives 2-7% of the lithium in a typical therapeutic carbonate dose [PMID:38810780].
- Diet and water: grains and vegetables are the main food sources, and drinking water can contribute substantially [PMID:11838882]. A meta-analysis of 76 papers found a pooled water lithium of 5.4 ug/L, with groundwater 14.5 times higher than surface water (40.4 vs 2.8 ug/L) and very large differences between countries [PMID:37261955].
- Essentiality is disputed: Schrauzer (2002) argued lithium is essential and proposed a provisional RDA of 1000 ug/day for a 70 kg adult, based mainly on animal deprivation studies [PMID:11838882]. A 2026 review concludes lithium is not recognized as essential. It finds no defined deficiency syndrome and no validated status biomarker; at most lithium is a candidate "beneficial trace element" [PMID:42693365].

** Mechanisms
- GSK-3beta inhibition is the leading proposed mechanism for effects on amyloid and tau. Trace lithium is also proposed to raise BDNF and lower IL-6/IL-8 (qualitative review) [PMID:39598397].
- Aron et al. 2025 (Nature) [PMID:40770094]: In human tissue, lithium was the only metal measured that was significantly reduced in the brain in MCI. Amyloid deposits bound lithium, further lowering its availability in AD. In mice, a diet that cut cortical lithium by about 50% increased amyloid and phospho-tau, caused microglial activation, synapse/axon/myelin loss and faster cognitive decline, partly via GSK3beta. Lithium orotate, a salt that binds less to amyloid, prevented pathology and memory loss in AD-model and aged wild-type mice.
- Other proposed pathways: Bcl-2 induction, BDNF signalling, mitochondrial stabilization and reduced oxidative stress. In humans, lithium raised N-acetylaspartate and preserved grey matter in imaging studies (narrative review) [PMID:42268602].
- Amyloid/tau evidence: a systematic review found inconsistent effects on amyloid and tau, with mostly preclinical support [PMID:41260370].
- Orotate vs carbonate pharmacology is contested: Mouse data suggest orotate is more potent and less toxic. Orotate at 1.5 mg/kg nearly fully blocked amphetamine hyperlocomotion, while carbonate at 15-20 mg/kg only partly blocked it. Carbonate, but not orotate, caused polydipsia and raised creatinine and TSH [PMID:37356352]. A 2026 critique argues orotate dissociates in the stomach and is absorbed as Li+. It notes no stable orotate complex has been shown in body fluids and that pharmacokinetics are comparable after oral dosing [PMID:42312809].
- Brain uptake at low dose: 7Li-MRI detected brain lithium after 5 mg/day orotate in 9 healthy men [PMID:38810780].
- Telomeres: in bipolar cohorts, telomere length correlated with lithium treatment duration (>2 years: R=0.17, p=0.037) [PMID:27084304]. Chronic users had longer telomeres (n=384, p=0.03) [PMID:30559463]. UK Biobank (n=591 users): duration of use showed no association with telomere length, frailty, metabolomic age or mortality [PMID:38539016].
- Kidney: lithium enters collecting-duct cells through ENaC and causes vasopressin insensitivity, which leads to polyuria and nephrogenic DI [PMID:40449743].
- Thyroid: lithium concentrates in the thyroid and inhibits hormone synthesis and release [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].

** Dosing in trials
- Microdose: 300 ug/day lithium for 15 months in AD patients (small trial; MMSE stable vs decline in controls) [PMID:22746245].
- Supplement-level: lithium orotate providing 5 mg/day lithium for up to 28 days (7Li-MRI study) [PMID:38810780].
- Subtherapeutic carbonate in amnestic MCI: Target serum 0.25-0.5 mmol/L for 12 months (n=45) [PMID:21525519]. Same target for 2 years double-blind plus 2 years single-blind (n=61) [PMID:30947755].
- LATTICE (2026): daily low-dose lithium carbonate vs placebo for 2 years in adults >=60 with MCI [PMID:41770546].
- FTD agitation pilot: up to 600 mg/day carbonate, median serum 0.42 mmol/L [PMID:41236362].
- Low-dose cohort definition: all serum levels <=0.40 mmol/L in a real-world comparison with valproate [PMID:42372694].
- Proposed future dose: low-dose orotate trials targeting about 0.3 mM have been proposed but not done [PMID:42268602].
- Therapeutic (Rx) dosing: serum 0.8-1.2 mEq/L, with levels checked 12 h post-dose [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec]. Lower targets apply in older adults: levels of 0.82-1.00 mmol/L were linked to more side effects [PMID:42460615].
- Water exposure ranges in ecological studies: Denmark: median about 12 ug/L [PMID:28832877]. Japan: >=30 ug/L was associated with lower suicide SMRs [PMID:32780508].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Low-dose and orotate data: Orotate was not genotoxic. A 28-day rat study found a NOAEL of 400 mg/kg/day, and the authors cite no postmarket safety signal [PMID:34146638]. Overdose case: an 18-year-old took 18 tablets of an internet orotate product (120 mg each) and had serum Li 0.40 mEq/L with nausea and tremor. The authors note chronic toxicity is a theoretical concern [PMID:18072162].
- Low-dose carbonate in older adults: 4-year MCI trial: no eGFR change, but higher TSH, neutrophils and weight. There were more adverse events, more diabetes (P=.037) and more arrhythmia (P=.028) [PMID:25093483]. LATTICE: serious AEs 29% vs 23% (none definitely related). Diarrhea 29% vs 15%, tiredness 29% vs 15%, tremor 24% vs 15% [PMID:41770546]. Pooled RCTs in AD: no excess serious AEs, but drug-related AEs were more frequent [PMID:41177743].
- Therapeutic-dose toxicity profile [PMID:22265699]: Urinary concentrating ability falls by about 15% of normal maximum. Clinical hypothyroidism OR 5.78. Renal replacement therapy in 0.5%. No significant excess of congenital malformations, alopecia or skin disorders in that analysis.
- Kidney: CKD3+ in 10.4% of lithium users vs 3.0% of controls (HR 1.90). Risk rises with mean level 0.60-0.79 (HR 2.93) and 0.80-0.99 mmol/L (HR 4.31) [PMID:39572104]. Lithium serum cut-offs of about 0.50-0.59 mEq/L were linked to thyroid dysfunction and CKD3+ [PMID:39932712]. Polyuria responds to amiloride, but evidence is low-quality (25 patients) [PMID:40449743].
- Thyroid: Hypothyroidism is common. Hyperthyroidism is uncommon but reported [PMID:30841715]. 41% stopped thyroid replacement after lithium was discontinued [PMID:31670617].
- Parathyroid: lithium-associated hyperparathyroidism is often multiglandular [PMID:39192574] [PMID:42606582].
- Cardiac: in 135 bradycardia cases, 32% occurred at therapeutic levels and 5 died; it recurred in 52% on re-challenge [PMID:42425479]. The label warns of Brugada syndrome unmasking [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Other label warnings: serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia, and toxicity made worse by dehydration from sweating, diarrhea or fever [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Pregnancy [PMID:28591541] [PMID:31623458]: Cardiac malformations: 2.41% exposed vs 1.15% unexposed. Right ventricular outflow tract defects (including Ebstein anomaly): aRR 2.66. First-trimester spontaneous abortion OR 3.77. Neonatal readmission: aOR 1.62 [PMID:29929874].

** Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min): avoid. Use lower doses and slow titration at CrCl 30-89 mL/min [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Brugada syndrome or suspected Brugada syndrome: generally avoid [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Pregnancy (especially the first trimester) and breastfeeding: Fetal and neonatal harm are possible [PMID:28591541] [PMID:31623458]. Breastfeeding is not recommended during maternal therapy; infant plasma levels reach 30-40% of maternal [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Dehydration, sodium depletion and significant cardiovascular disease raise toxicity risk. Self-directed OTC lithium is inappropriate in people already taking prescription lithium, because it adds to the same serum Li+ [PMID:42312809] [PMID:18072162].

** Interactions
Most interaction data come from therapeutic lithium. Their size at 1-5 mg/day supplemental doses is unstudied, but the mechanisms (reduced renal Li+ clearance) are the same.
*** Drugs
- NSAIDs, including COX-2 inhibitors: they cut renal blood flow and raise serum lithium. Case reports describe rises of up to 99% with celecoxib and 448% with rofecoxib [PMID:14658947] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec]. In an Ontario case-control study NSAIDs were not independently associated with toxicity admissions [PMID:15086664].
- ACE inhibitors: levels rose 36% and clearance fell 25.5% after starting an ACE inhibitor [PMID:8834421]. Risk of admission for toxicity was RR 7.6 in the first month in the elderly [PMID:15086664].
- ARBs: the label groups ARBs with ACE inhibitors as renin-angiotensin antagonists that raise steady-state lithium [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec]. In 30 intoxication reports, RAS inhibitors and some diuretics predicted toxicity [PMID:26630207].
- Thiazide diuretics raise lithium by 25-40% after starting [PMID:8521679].
- Loop diuretics: RR 5.5 for admission with toxicity within a month in the elderly [PMID:15086664]. Furosemide lowers lithium clearance during volume contraction [PMID:3690979].
- SSRIs, SNRIs and other serotonergic drugs: risk of serotonin syndrome [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec]. A probable case was reported with venlafaxine [PMID:16882112].
- Theophylline and other methylxanthines raise lithium clearance by about 30%, which lowers levels [PMID:6741601] [PMID:8521679].
- Antipsychotics (neurotoxicity) and SGLT2 inhibitors (lower lithium) also have label warnings [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].
- Older adults: diuretics, ACE inhibitors and NSAIDs call for closer monitoring [PMID:42460615].
*** Other supplements
- Iodine (potassium iodide): iodide preparations can cause hypothyroidism together with lithium (label). In 10 lithium-treated patients, KI raised TSH by 8.9 uU/mL; 2 became hypothyroid and 1 developed Jod-Basedow hyperthyroidism [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec] [PMID:576345].
- Aspirin: reports conflict. Some show no effect or lower lithium, while others show increases [PMID:14658947].
- Magnesium: oral magnesium sulfate did not change lithium carbonate absorption in 10 volunteers [DOI:10.1007/BF00353312].
- Vitamin D-3 and calcium: lithium raises calcium and PTH [PMID:22265699] [PMID:39192574]. Adding calcium-raising supplements is untested but could mask or worsen hypercalcemia. This is an inference only.
- Caffeine (food): cutting coffee raised lithium levels and worsened tremor in 2 cases [PMID:3338980].
*** Conditions
- Kidney disease, dehydration and low sodium intake: a low-salt diet reduces lithium clearance [PMID:3690979]. An add-on 1 g/day NaCl reduced fluctuation in lithium levels (26.7% vs 63.3%; RCT, n=60) [PMID:36409058].
- Thyroid disease, hyperparathyroidism, Brugada syndrome, pregnancy, older age: see Safety and Contraindications.
- Pre-existing bipolar disorder on Rx lithium: OTC orotate adds to the total lithium load [PMID:42312809].
*** Lab-test interference
- Lithium heparin collection tubes cause spurious lithium levels. Neonatal samples read as high as 4.19 mmol/L, and levels above 3 mmol/L are possible in underfilled tubes [PMID:19057375]. Underfilled lithium-heparin tubes also falsely raise creatinine on the Abbott enzymatic assay (+40.6%) [PMID:34463152].
- True physiological lab changes (not assay artifacts): TSH rises [PMID:22265699]. Neutrophilic leukocytosis: neutrophilia in 18% of patients [PMID:11105780] [PMID:25093483]. Calcium and PTH rise [PMID:39192574].
- Pseudo-hypercalcemia from volume depletion in nephrogenic DI should be excluded [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b839ff4b-f62d-41ab-a823-550a756d58ec].

** Genotype notes
- Response to therapeutic lithium in bipolar disorder: a ConLiGen GWAS (n=2563) found a chromosome 21 locus (lncRNA genes AL157359.3/4). Carriers of the alternate alleles relapsed more often (HR 3.8) [PMID:26806518].
- Telomere length: polygenic scores explained more of the variance in chronic lithium users (8.9%) [PMID:30559463].
- No pharmacogenomic data exist for low-dose or supplemental lithium.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] JAD SR/MA (23 studies): cohort HR 0.63 for dementia with lithium exposure; 3 RCTs SMD -0.41 [PMID:42803710].
- [2026-09] JAMA Psychiatry narrative review proposes an MCI trial of low-dose lithium orotate (~0.3 mM) [PMID:42268602].
- [2026-09] Narrative review: no human lithium-deficiency syndrome or validated biomarker exists [PMID:42693365].
- [2026-08] ISBD task force: levels of 0.82-1.00 mmol/L linked to side effects in older adults; 1.20-1.25 mmol/L overtly toxic [PMID:42460615].
- [2026-08] Lithium-associated HPT SR (125 studies): hypercalcemia 7.2%, HPT 4.8%, multiglandular in 25-83% [PMID:42606582].
- [2026-07] SR of 135 bradycardia cases: 32% occurred at therapeutic levels [PMID:42425479].
- [2026-06] Bayesian reanalysis of LATTICE: 96.5% probability of benefit for verbal delayed recall; overall inconclusive [PMID:42521537].
- [2026-06] Low-dose lithium vs valproate (TriNetX, n=853 per arm): dementia HR 0.38. The authors flag bias and call it hypothesis-generating [PMID:42372694].
- [2026-06] Critique: lithium orotate is probably absorbed as Li+; superiority claims are unproven [PMID:42312809].
- [2026-04] LATTICE pilot RCT (n=80, 2 years): no coprimary outcome met; CVLT slope +0.69/yr [PMID:41770546].
- [2026-02] US county study (UCMR 5): higher water lithium linked to lower dementia prevalence (PR 0.965) and incidence (IRR 0.958) [PMID:41643982].
- [2025-11] SR: lithium effects on amyloid and tau are inconsistent and mostly preclinical [PMID:41260370].
- [2025-11] Low-dose lithium in FTD agitation: feasible and tolerated; no efficacy signal (n=16) [PMID:41236362].
- [2025-10] AD RCT MA (6 RCTs, n=394): no consistent cognitive benefit; no excess serious AEs [PMID:41177743].
- [2025-08] Aron et al., Nature: brain lithium is low in MCI; lithium orotate replacement prevented AD pathology in mice [PMID:40770094].
- [2025-05] Amiloride (ENaC inhibitor) for lithium polyuria: likely helpful, but low-quality evidence [PMID:40449743].
- [2025-05] Suicide RCT MA update: nonsignificant reductions in attempts (OR 0.73) and suicide (OR 0.61) [PMID:40441661].
- [2025-02] Hong Kong cohort: hypothyroidism aHR 2.00, CKD3+ aHR 1.35 with lithium [PMID:39932712].
- [2024-12] Iceland cohort: CKD3+ HR 1.90; no excess at 0.30-0.59 mmol/L [PMID:39572104].
- [2024-11] Network MA in pregnancy: congenital malformations OR 1.61 for lithium [PMID:39528870].
- [2024-11] Qualitative review of trace lithium for suicide and dementia prevention [PMID:39598397].
- [2024-10] HPT/hypercalcemia MA: pooled prevalence of lithium-associated hypercalcemia 3.17-4.23% [PMID:39192574].

** Open questions
- Whether trace or supplemental lithium causes any of the ecological associations. These studies are prone to ecological fallacy and confounding, and some (Scotland, autism) point the other way [PMID:36747488] [PMID:37010840].
- Whether lithium orotate differs from carbonate in humans. Mouse data and chemical arguments conflict [PMID:37356352] [PMID:42312809].
- What dose is needed for neuroprotection in MCI. The ~0.3 mM target or microgram doses have not been tested in an adequately powered RCT [PMID:41770546] [PMID:42268602].
- Long-term renal, thyroid and parathyroid safety of 1-5 mg/day OTC lithium. Data at serum <0.3 mmol/L are sparse.
- Whether lithium status can be measured. No validated biomarker exists [PMID:42693365].
