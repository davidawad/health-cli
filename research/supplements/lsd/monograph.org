** Identity & forms
- Lysergic acid diethylamide is a semisynthetic ergoline and classic serotonergic psychedelic. It is listed in US Schedule I (21 CFR 1308.11, hallucinogenic substances) [URL:https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-1308/section-1308.11].
- Research forms:
  - LSD base (ethanolic solution or rapidly dissolving tablet) and LSD tartrate (aqueous solution). All oral forms were bioequivalent, with an absolute oral bioavailability of 80% at about 80 ug freebase equivalent [PMID:40418105].
  - MM120/DT120: lysergide D-tartrate, developed by MindMed (now Definium Therapeutics). DT120 is an orally disintegrating tablet (ODT) given as a single 100 ug dose for generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and major depressive disorder (MDD) [PMID:42848417] [URL:https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/247/definium-therapeutics-receives-breakthrough-therapy-designation-for-dt120-lysergide-orally-disintegrating-tablet-odt-in-major-depressive-disorder-mdd-following-positive-phase-3-results].
  - Sublingual microdose formulations (e.g. MB-22001, titratable 4-20 ug) are used in microdosing trials [PMID:39182140] [PMID:40251818].
- Dose ranges in trials: "full" psychedelic doses are 100-200 ug [PMID:36266118] [PMID:33059356], and "microdoses" are about 5-20 ug. A 2026 drug review stresses that 100 ug is a perceptible psychedelic dose, not microdosing [PMID:42848417] [PMID:42365488].
- Regulatory status: investigational, not approved anywhere as a medicine. FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation for MM120 in GAD came in March 2024 [URL:https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/137/mindmed-receives-fda-breakthrough-therapy-designation-and-announces-positive-12-week-durability-data-from-phase-2b-study-of-mm120-for-generalized-anxiety-disorder], and a second, for MDD, in September 2026 [URL:https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/247/definium-therapeutics-receives-breakthrough-therapy-designation-for-dt120-lysergide-orally-disintegrating-tablet-odt-in-major-depressive-disorder-mdd-following-positive-phase-3-results].

** Mechanisms
- Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonism mediates the psychedelic effects. The sponsor describes DT120 as a 5-HT2A partial agonist [URL:https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/247/definium-therapeutics-receives-breakthrough-therapy-designation-for-dt120-lysergide-orally-disintegrating-tablet-odt-in-major-depressive-disorder-mdd-following-positive-phase-3-results]. Ketanserin (40 mg) pretreatment prevented the response to 200 ug LSD [PMID:33059356]. Ketanserin given 1 h after LSD shortened subjective effects from 8.5 h to 3.5 h, without changing LSD pharmacokinetics [PMID:36342343].
- Broad receptor profile: LSD and nor-LSD are active at 5-HT1A, 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B and 5-HT2C receptors [PMID:30981875]. The crystal structure with 5-HT2B shows very slow dissociation from 5-HT2B and 5-HT2A, explained by an extracellular-loop "lid" [PMID:28129538].
- Autonomic effects: LSD mainly increases sympathetic activity, and ketanserin counteracts this [PMID:33772794]. It moderately raises blood pressure and heart rate [PMID:33059356] [PMID:34515824].
- Neuroplasticity hypotheses: in an MDD trial, the high-dose LSD arm showed increased white-matter fractional anisotropy that correlated with symptom improvement [PMID:42102823].
- 5-HT2B agonism is the proposed basis for a valvulopathy concern with chronic or repeated exposure (see Safety) [PMID:41643722] [PMID:42209433].
- Compared with other psychedelics, effects of the serotonergic psychedelics differed little apart from duration [PMID:38301886]. Doses of 100-200 ug LSD and 30 mg psilocybin gave comparable subjective effects. LSD raised heart rate more, and psilocybin raised blood pressure more [PMID:35217796].

** Dosing in trials
- GAD (MM120): a single oral dose of 25, 50, 100 or 200 ug freebase equivalent. 100 ug was taken forward to Phase 3 [PMID:40906494]. Phase 3 Voyage and Panorama used DT120 ODT 100 ug with at least 8 h of monitoring on dosing day [URL:https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/251/definium-therapeutics-announces-positive-topline-results-from-phase-3-panorama-study-of-dt120-odt-in-generalized-anxiety-disorder].
- LSD-assisted therapy for anxiety: two sessions of 200 ug with psychotherapy [PMID:36266118] [PMID:24594678].
- MDD: 100 ug then 200 ug, versus 25 ug + 25 ug, in two sessions with supportive psychotherapy [PMID:40482648].
- Alcoholism (historical): a single dose within various treatment programs [PMID:22406913].
- Microdosing regimens: 10 ug every 3 days for 6 weeks (14 doses), mostly self-administered at home [PMID:36997080]; 5, 10 or 20 ug every 4 days (6 doses) in older adults [PMID:31853557]; 13 or 26 ug at 3-4 day intervals [PMID:35106880].
- Clinical microdosing: 20 ug twice weekly for 6 weeks for ADHD [PMID:40105807]; 6-20 ug sublingual twice weekly for 8 weeks for MDD [PMID:41202873].
- Dose thresholds: 5 ug gave no significant subjective effects; 10 ug was the threshold for psychotropic effects [PMID:32975835]. Other studies found measurable mood or attention effects at 5 ug [PMID:33082016] and orderly dose-related effects from 6.5 ug [PMID:31331617].
- Pharmacokinetics (full doses): 200 ug oral gives Cmax 4.5 ng/mL at 1.5 h, half-life 3.6 h and terminal half-life 8.9 h, with about 1% excreted unchanged in urine and 13% as 2-oxo-3-hydroxy-LSD [PMID:26108222]. At 100 ug, Cmax is 1.3 ng/mL, plasma half-life 2.6 h and effects last 8.2 h (11.6 h at 200 ug) [PMID:28197931]. At 85/170 ug, half-life is 3.7-4.0 h [PMID:37596682].
- Pharmacokinetics (microdoses): Cmax 151, 279 and 500 pg/mL after 5, 10 and 20 ug, half-life about 3 h [PMID:32975835]. Sublingual 10 ug: Cmax 0.20 ug/L, half-life 3.08 h [PMID:40251818]. No tolerance or sensitisation was found over 16 twice-weekly microdoses [PMID:41713673].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Acute effects in controlled settings: in a pooled analysis (83 healthy subjects, 131 administrations), no participant had systolic BP >180 mmHg. Heart rate >100/min occurred in 0%, 6%, 20% and 25% at 25, 50, 100 and 200 ug, and temperature >38 C in up to 34% at 200 ug. Liver and kidney function were unchanged, and 6 subjects reported transient flashbacks [PMID:34515824]. The 200 ug dose induced significant anxiety and more ego dissolution than 100 ug [PMID:33059356].
- MM120 Phase 2b: dose-related visual perceptual changes (46.2% at 25 ug up to 100% at 200 ug, vs 10.3% on placebo), nausea (up to 60.0%) and headache [PMID:40906494]. In Phase 3 Voyage, adverse events were mostly mild to moderate and occurred on dosing day, with no suicidality signal reported [URL:https://ir.definiumtx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/243/definium-therapeutics-announces-positive-topline-results-from-phase-3-voyage-study-of-dt120-odt-in-generalized-anxiety-disorder].
- LSD-assisted therapy for anxiety: transient mild acute adverse effects in 19% and one serious adverse event (acute transient anxiety, 2%) [PMID:36266118].
- Classic psychedelics across trials (SR/MA, 3504 participants): serious adverse events occurred in none in healthy participants and about 4% in participants with neuropsychiatric disorders. These included worsening depression, suicidal behavior, psychosis and convulsions. There was no persistent psychosis or HPPD after high doses in contemporary trials. However, only 23.5% of studies since 2005 assessed adverse events systematically [PMID:39230883].
- Psychosis: incidence of psychedelic-induced psychosis was 0.002% in population studies and 0.6% in RCTs. It was 3.8% in uncontrolled trials that included people with schizophrenia, and 13.1% of those with induced psychosis later developed schizophrenia [PMID:39592825]. Case reports describe schizophrenia-spectrum and affective disorders after even single use [PMID:39564729].
- Mania: psychedelic-associated (hypo)mania ranged from 5.8% in controlled trials to 30% in naturalistic studies of people with bipolar disorder. Risk was higher with bipolar I disorder and familial vulnerability [PMID:42215638]. In a population cohort, acute care involving hallucinogens was linked to incident mania (weighted HR 5.97) [PMID:41329646]. In adolescents, the association of psychedelic use with manic symptoms interacted with genetic vulnerability to schizophrenia or bipolar I disorder [PMID:38477889].
- HPPD/flashbacks: an SR of 31 treatment studies (87 patients) found 28% full and 61% partial recovery within a year. Benzodiazepines were ineffective for LSD-triggered HPPD, and risperidone aggravated symptoms in several reports [PMID:40938689]. Flashbacks resolved within 12 months in about half of case reports [PMID:39564729].
- Cardiovascular/valvular (5-HT2B): LSD has high 5-HT2B affinity, and preclinical data support fibrotic signalling. No clinical cases of LSD valvulopathy have been reported [PMID:41643722]. In mice, chronic low-dose LSD caused no ventricular or valvular remodelling. The authors note that 5-HT2B activation is substantial but short-lived [PMID:41789300].
- A 2026 review says cumulative exposure risks remain insufficiently characterized [PMID:42209433]. The first human microdosing echocardiography (open-label, n=19, 8 weeks) found no clinical alterations [PMID:41202873].
- Microdosing tolerability: treatment-related anxiety led 4 of 40 participants to withdraw in the home-dosing RCT [PMID:36997080]. In older adults, adverse events were no more frequent than on placebo, with no impairment of balance or cognition [PMID:31853557]. Microdosing RCTs show adverse event risks similar to control [PMID:42593636].

** Contraindications
- Personal history of psychotic disorder, or family history/genetic vulnerability to psychosis [PMID:39592825] [PMID:38477889] [PMID:39230883].
- Bipolar I disorder or familial bipolar vulnerability: risk of (hypo)mania [PMID:42215638] [PMID:41329646].
- Concurrent lithium: seizures reported (see Interactions) [PMID:34348413].
- Uncontrolled hypertension or significant cardiovascular disease: LSD raises BP, heart rate and temperature dose-dependently [PMID:34515824]. Trial meta-analyses note BP/HR increases [PMID:38111216].
- Valvular heart disease: theoretical 5-HT2B concern with repeated dosing [PMID:41643722].
- Pregnancy: human data are sparse and confounded (11 LSD sources from 1968-2020), and absence of data is not evidence of safety [PMID:41930230]. In an early rat study, injection early in pregnancy caused abortions and stillbirths [PMID:6028038].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Lithium: in 62 online reports of lithium plus classic psychedelics, 47% involved seizures and 39% medical attention, versus none with lamotrigine [PMID:34348413]. An adolescent case had three generalized seizures after LSD on low-dose lithium [PMID:38986146]. Chronic lithium also subjectively increased LSD responses [PMID:8788508].
- SSRIs: chronic SSRIs/trazodone attenuated or eliminated LSD responses in 28 of 32 users (88%) [PMID:8726753]. In a controlled trial, paroxetine (20 mg, 42 days) left pleasant effects intact but reduced bad drug effects, anxiety and nausea. It raised LSD Cmax and AUC (GMR 1.4 and 1.5) through CYP2D6 inhibition, with no QTc change [PMID:40022427]. SSRI initiation worsened LSD flashbacks in two adolescents [PMID:7965440].
- Tricyclic antidepressants: chronic use subjectively increased physical, hallucinatory and psychological responses to LSD [PMID:8788508].
- MAO inhibitors: chronic use subjectively decreased LSD effects [PMID:8788508]. A systematic review of 52 interaction studies found both attenuated and potentiated effects across drug classes [PMID:37982394].
- Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors: paroxetine raised LSD exposure, and other strong CYP2D6 inhibitors are expected to act similarly (inference) [PMID:40022427]. CYP2D6, 2E1 and 3A4 form nor-LSD, and CYP1A2, 2C9, 2E1 and 3A4 form 2-oxo-3-hydroxy-LSD. Rifampicin induction increased metabolite formation in vitro [PMID:30981875].
- 5-HT2A antagonists (ketanserin): block or reverse LSD effects [PMID:33059356] [PMID:36342343].
- Antipsychotics: risperidone aggravated HPPD in several reports [PMID:40938689]. Antipsychotic interactions are summarized in a systematic review [PMID:37982394].
- Tramadol and other serotonergic or seizure-threshold-lowering drugs: no LSD-specific human interaction data were found. The DDI review reports no serious adverse drug events except in a few case reports [PMID:37982394].
*** Other supplements
- Lithium (corpus item): see Drugs. Seizure reports occurred even at subtherapeutic serum levels [PMID:38986146] [PMID:34348413].
- No cited interaction with other supplements in this batch.
*** Conditions
- Psychosis, bipolar disorder, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, pregnancy: see Contraindications.
- Seizure risk: convulsive episodes were among the serious adverse events in patient trials [PMID:39230883]. Lithium co-use is the main reported trigger [PMID:34348413].
- Prior HPPD or flashbacks: may recur, and SSRIs or risperidone may aggravate them [PMID:7965440] [PMID:40938689].
- Recreational co-use: 56.3% of psychedelic users report typical co-use, most often cannabis and alcohol [PMID:39468747]. No controlled LSD-cannabis interaction data were found.
*** Lab-test interference
- Urine LSD immunoassays (CEDIA) give false positives with sertraline [PMID:22511703], ambroxol [PMID:9737335] and bupropion [PMID:17529897]. Positives are presumptive until confirmed chromatographically [PMID:24986836].
- Standard drug screens: LSD is extensively metabolized, with only about 1% excreted unchanged in urine [PMID:26108222] [PMID:37596682].

** Genotype notes
- CYP2D6: in a pooled analysis of 81 subjects, poor metabolizers had longer half-lives, about 75% higher LSD and 2-oxo-3-hydroxy-LSD AUC, and stronger, longer subjective effects. Other CYPs (1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2B6) had no effect [PMID:34035391].
- With microdoses, 2 of 4 participants who withdrew for anxiety had intermediate or weak CYP2D6 activity [PMID:40251818]. Paroxetine's CYP2D6 inhibition of LSD clearance was nominally greatest in normal metabolizers [PMID:40022427].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] DT120 drug profile: a credible signal from a single 100 ug dose without mandated psychotherapy. Phase 3 Voyage and Panorama were positive; durability, retreatment and functional unblinding remain open [PMID:42848417].

- [2026-06] DTI study of the MDD trial: white-matter FA increases correlated with antidepressant response [PMID:42102823].
- [2026-03] SR of LSD/MDMA and 5-HT2B valvulopathy: no clinical LSD cases, but preclinical fibrotic signalling [PMID:41643722].
- [2026-02/03] Open-label microdosing for MDD (n=19): MADRS -59.5%; normal echocardiography; no tolerance over 16 doses [PMID:41202873] [PMID:41713673].
- [2026-01] PAT depression MA: Hedges g -0.84; more preparation therapy hours were associated with larger effects [PMID:41563753].
- [2026-01] Pregnancy scoping review: human prenatal psychedelic evidence is sparse [PMID:41930230].
- [2025-12] Living MA (30 RCTs): psychedelics for MDD, SMD -0.62 (very low certainty); LSD did not help ADHD [PMID:41205366].
- [2025-12] Ontario cohort: hallucinogen acute care was linked to incident mania (HR 5.97) [PMID:41329646].
- [2025-11] Mouse study: chronic low-dose LSD caused no valvular remodelling [PMID:41789300].
- [2025-10] MM120 Phase 2b in GAD published (JAMA) [PMID:40906494].
- [2025-09] MDD RCT of high- vs low-dose LSD therapy (n=61): IDS-C -9.2 [PMID:40482648]. LSD base and tartrate are bioequivalent, F=80% [PMID:40418105]. HPPD treatment SR [PMID:40938689].
- [2025-06] Paroxetine-LSD interaction RCT: CYP2D6 inhibition raised exposure about 1.5-fold and reduced negative effects [PMID:40022427]. ADHD microdose RCT: null [PMID:40105807].

- [2024-12] Adverse events SR/MA of classic psychedelics [PMID:39230883].

** Open questions
- Durability, retreatment intervals and long-term safety of single-dose DT120. Phase 3 results so far are sponsor topline releases, not peer-reviewed.
- Functional unblinding and expectancy effects in both full-dose and microdose trials [PMID:41563753] [PMID:33648632].
- Whether microdosing has any effect beyond placebo in clinical populations. Observational studies report more benefit than experiments. The LSDDEP2 RCT is pending [PMID:39182140] [PMID:38877715] [PMID:40834796].
- Cardiac valve safety of chronic or frequent microdosing in humans (5-HT2B).
- Safety in people with bipolar spectrum or psychosis vulnerability, and the mechanism of the lithium-seizure signal.
- Interactions with non-CYP2D6-inhibiting SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol and antipsychotics under controlled conditions.
- Pregnancy and lactation safety.
