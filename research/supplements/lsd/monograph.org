** Identity & forms
- Lysergic acid diethylamide is a potent serotonergic psychedelic and Schedule I controlled substance in the United States; research administration uses measured pharmaceutical doses in supervised settings [PMID:39230883].

** Mechanisms
- LSD is a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist with additional serotonergic receptor activity; mechanistic claims do not establish treatment efficacy [PMID:38574699].

** Dosing in trials
- Clinical studies used widely varying measured oral doses in monitored sessions; no unsupervised dose can be inferred. Contemporary reviews stress setting and safety-monitoring limitations [PMID:39230883].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Acute anxiety, nausea, headache, perceptual changes, and cardiovascular effects occur; review found SAEs including worsening depression, suicidality, psychosis, and convulsions in vulnerable participants. AE capture in studies was incomplete [PMID:39230883].

** Contraindications
- Clinical trials generally exclude individuals with psychotic disorders, bipolar mania risk, unstable cardiovascular disease, or interacting medications; exact exclusions vary. Legal status is controlled and varies by jurisdiction [PMID:39230883].

** Interactions
- Drugs, supplements, conditions, and relevant laboratory interference are summarized in the structured interaction object. Lithium co-use has been associated with seizure reports in case literature; serotonergic medicines and stimulants can alter effects, but controlled interaction data are sparse. Research-only setting matters [PMID:39230883]. No genotype-specific claim is made because no directly relevant pharmacogenomic source was identified.

** Genotype notes
- No directly relevant, clinically validated pharmacogenomic/nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed.

** Latest research
- 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis included psychedelic studies searched through Feb 2024; LSD had fewer trials than psilocybin, with pooled symptom estimate Hedges g −0.65 across indications [PMID:38574699]. 2024 adverse-event review found SAE reports in about 4% of participants with preexisting neuropsychiatric disorders across classic psychedelics and highlighted poor AE reporting [PMID:39230883].

** Open questions
- Larger replicated trials, long-term follow-up, standardized adverse-event capture, and direct LSD-specific interaction studies are needed [PMID:39230883; PMID:38574699].
