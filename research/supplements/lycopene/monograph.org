** Identity & forms
- Lycopene is a dietary, fat-soluble carotenoid abundant in tomatoes; supplements include synthetic and tomato-derived forms [PMID:37496241].
** Mechanisms
- Carotenoid antioxidant activity and effects on oxidative biomarkers are proposed mechanisms; biomarker changes do not prove disease prevention [PMID:37496241].

** Dosing in trials
- Trials used variable tomato foods/extracts or supplements, often 5–30 mg/day for weeks to months; no standardized evidence-based dose [PMID:37496241].
** Safety & adverse effects
- Food exposure is customary; supplement trials report few short-term harms, but long-term high-dose safety is less characterized.
** Contraindications
- No established formal contraindications; caution with products containing other active ingredients.
** Interactions
- Possible additive BP-lowering is theoretical; clinical interaction data limited.
- Structured high-priority interactions are represented in interactions.json; absence of an edge is not proof of no interaction.
** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic/nutrigenomic source was identified for a supplement-specific genotype effect; no genotype claim made.
** Latest research
- 2025-11: 2025 umbrella review reports possible BP effects in tomato-derived intervention evidence, contrasting with the null pooled BP result in the 2023 GRADE review; composition and intervention heterogeneity remain substantial [PMID:41369612] [PMID:37496241].
** Open questions
- Which formulations, populations, and durations produce clinically meaningful outcomes rather than biomarker-only changes?
- Are there long-term benefits or harms beyond the relatively short human trials available?
