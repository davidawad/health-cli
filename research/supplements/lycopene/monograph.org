** Identity & forms
- Lycopene is a red, linear hydrocarbon carotenoid with no provitamin A activity [PMID:12424334] [PMID:17707976]. Tomatoes and tomato products are the main dietary source [PMID:33668703] [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/prostate-supplements-pdq].
- Supplement forms: synthetic crystalline lycopene [PMID:12176084], tomato oleoresin extracts (e.g. Lyc-O-Mato) [PMID:17571945] and whey-protein-bound lactolycopene [PMID:31591650]. Trials also use tomato paste, juice, sauce and puree. The NCI PDQ notes that US FDA has accepted GRAS determinations for lycopene-containing products [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/prostate-supplements-pdq].
- Isomers: foods contain mostly all-trans-lycopene, but human plasma and tissues contain a large share of cis-isomers [PMID:26561629] [PMID:12424334]. A 14C dose given as 92% all-trans was 50% trans and 38% 5-cis within 24 h [PMID:21543537].
- Cis-rich sources absorb better: tetra-cis-rich tangerine tomato juice gave 47.7% vs 5.0% fractional absorption from red juice (8.5-fold) [PMID:25620547]. A heat-induced cis-rich sauce also beat an all-trans-rich sauce [PMID:17391568].
- Pharmacokinetics: about 23% of a 10 mg 13C dose was absorbed; plasma half-life about 5.3 d (all-trans) and 8.8 d (cis) [PMID:26561629]. Lycopene or its metabolites persisted in skin for up to 42 d [PMID:21543537]. Adipose tissue holds about two-thirds of body stores [PMID:41848742].
- Supplement vs food: 20 mg/d synthetic tablets gave plasma responses comparable to tomato soup and higher than tomato juice, with effective half-lives of about 5 d (all-E) and 9 d (5-Z) [PMID:15309451].
- Fat raises bioavailability: avocado added to salsa increased lycopene absorption 4.4-fold [PMID:15735074], and cooking tomatoes with olive oil raised plasma trans-lycopene 82% [PMID:15927929]. In an RCT meta-analysis, more fat improved carotenoid bioavailability, with unsaturated fats (SMD 0.90) beating saturated fats (SMD 0.27) [PMID:34897461].

** Mechanisms
- Singlet-oxygen quenching and antioxidant activity [PMID:36615780]. In RCT meta-analyses lycopene lowered MDA [PMID:37496241].
- Metabolism: cleaved mainly eccentrically by BCO2. Whether native lycopene or its lycopenals, lycopenols and lycopenoic acids mediate effects is unknown [PMID:33668703].
- Androgen axis: preclinical studies show null or inhibitory effects on androgen metabolism and signaling in prostate cancer models [PMID:30875962]. Lycopene also affects IGF signaling and cell-cycle progression in vitro [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/prostate-supplements-pdq].
- Vascular: lycopene improved endothelium-dependent vasodilation in statin-treated patients without changing endothelium-independent or basal NO responses [PMID:24911964]. FMD gains in a 2025 RCT occurred without changes in oxidative-stress or NO markers [PMID:40936452].
- Photoprotection: carotenoid deposition in skin [PMID:21543537] [PMID:35938228] raises minimal erythema dose (MED) and lowers MMP-1 and ICAM-1 [PMID:36606553].
- Plasma microRNAs: in a 2026 RCT, lycopene in tomato juice changed 5 miRNAs compared with lycopene-depleted juice [PMID:42765324].

** Dosing in trials
- Cardiovascular: 1.44-75 mg/d (43 studies) [PMID:32652029]. Effects on SBP were larger above 12 mg/d [PMID:24051501], and on LDL only at >=25 mg/d [PMID:21163596]. The umbrella review proposes 5-30 mg/d [PMID:41369612].
- Endothelial: 6 or 15 mg/d for 8 wk [PMID:21194693]; 7 mg/d for 2 mo [PMID:24911964]; 15-26.7 mg/d as juice for 12 wk [PMID:40936452].
- Prostate: 15 mg twice daily for 3 wk before prostatectomy [PMID:11489752]; 30 mg/d for 4 mo [PMID:17571945]; 15 mg/d for 6 mo [PMID:18156403] and 25 mg/d for 8 wk [PMID:36407517] in BPH. Across prostate trials, 8-45 mg/d for 3 wk to 2 y was reported safe [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/prostate-supplements-pdq].
- Sperm: 14 mg/d lactolycopene for 12 wk [PMID:31591650]; 25 mg/d for 12 wk [PMID:31468596].
- Skin: about 16 mg/d (40 g tomato paste with olive oil) for 10 wk; benefit seen at week 10 but not week 4 [PMID:11340098].
- Other: 15 mg/d with periodontal therapy [PMID:40670371].
- Plasma levels plateau after about 4 wk of 15 mg/d [PMID:11914107]. Take with a fat-containing meal [PMID:34897461].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Generally well tolerated. In prostate trials, adverse effects were mostly mild GI symptoms, which in one study resolved when lycopene was taken with meals [URL:https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/prostate-supplements-pdq]. No adverse effects were attributed to 30 mg/d for 4 mo [PMID:17571945]. All supplements in a photoaging MA were safe over <=24 wk [PMID:40761858].
- Carotenodermia/lycopenemia: benign orange-red skin discoloration, mainly on palms and soles, that reverses when intake falls [PMID:35938228]. It was reported during 15 mg/d supplementation [PMID:11914107]. In a 42-day feeding study, definite carotenodermia appeared only in the 30 mg/d beta-carotene arm, not with 180 g/d tomato juice [PMID:3421202].
- Pregnancy: an RCT of 2 mg/d from early pregnancy found no reduction in pre-eclampsia (18.2% vs 18.3%). Preterm labor (10.4% vs 1.2%) and low birthweight (22.1% vs 9.8%) were more common with lycopene [PMID:19527386]. An earlier RCT reported less pre-eclampsia (8.6% vs 17.7%) [PMID:12767566]. An antioxidant MA found no benefit for prevention [PMID:30111493].
- Toxicology: rat 13-week NOAEL for synthetic lycopene formulations was 3000 mg/kg/d [PMID:12176084]. EFSA's ADI is 0.5 mg/kg bw/d, based on an ALT rise in a 1-year rat study; JECFA's ADI is "not specified" [PMID:42005766]. In EFSA's 2026 exposure assessment the highest P95 (maximum-level scenario) was 2.52 mg/kg bw/d in toddlers, and novel-food use in supplements added substantially for supplement users [PMID:41972196].
- Causality: Mendelian randomization found no causal protection against CHD or ischemic stroke from genetically higher lycopene [PMID:33413940] [PMID:34796734].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindications have been established.
- Pregnancy: avoid supplemental lycopene. The RCT signal for preterm labor and low birthweight is unreplicated but unresolved [PMID:19527386] [PMID:30111493].
- Known tomato allergy, if the product is tomato-derived: the umbrella review flags allergy [PMID:33131949].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Orlistat: plasma lycopene fell, then partly rebounded, over 4.5 mo (n=6). Alpha- and beta-carotene fell 45% and 32% [PMID:17707976]. Orlistat cut beta-carotene absorption by about one-third [PMID:8852391]. Clinical relevance for lycopene is unquantified.
- Bile acid sequestrants (cholestyramine): lowered serum lycopene 30% and beta-carotene 40% over 3 y [PMID:7627696].
- Probucol: lowered carotenoids 30-40% [PMID:7627696].
- Statins: simvastatin lowered crude plasma carotenoids by lowering LDL. Lipid-adjusted lycopene rose [PMID:20678906] or did not change [PMID:17005272]. Interpret raw levels relative to lipids.
- Ezetimibe: reduced lycopene transport about 20% in Caco-2 cells (in vitro only) [PMID:16177187].
- Antihypertensives: an additive SBP reduction is possible but small and inconsistent [PMID:28129549] [PMID:37496241].
- Antiplatelets/aspirin: a water-soluble tomato extract (Fruitflow) inhibits platelet aggregation, with about one-third of the effect of 75 mg aspirin and no additive effect [PMID:27876806] [PMID:16960170]. This is not shown for lycopene itself.
*** Other supplements
- Beta-carotene and lutein (Zeaxanthin + Lutein): adding a second carotenoid lowered the chylomicron response to the first, but 3-wk plasma status was unaffected [PMID:11864859]. Competition occurs in micelles and lipoproteins [PMID:10047699].
- EVOO and other dietary fats: increase absorption [PMID:15927929] [PMID:34897461] [PMID:15735074].
- Plant sterol esters: serum lycopene fell 14.6% in adults [PMID:15199384]. Olestra lowered serum lycopene below the reference range, and a multivitamin did not prevent it [PMID:15930452]. Customary olestra snack use was linked to 4.3-22.4% lower carotenoids [PMID:16522910].
- Curcumin: synergistic in a rat BPH model (preclinical only) [PMID:37446563].
- Saw palmetto and selenium combinations: studied in BPH [PMID:40777606]. A post-hoc analysis found no excess prostate cancer with selenium plus lycopene [PMID:28899491].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy: see Contraindications [PMID:19527386].
- Fat malabsorption or very-low-fat diets: absorption depends on dietary fat [PMID:34897461]. This is inferred; no condition-specific trials were found.
- Prostate cancer or raised PSA: lycopene does not reliably lower PSA [PMID:35031434], and early PSA falls may be transient and non-specific [PMID:17571945].
*** Lab-test interference
- PSA: a real but inconsistent decrease is possible in BPH or high-baseline-PSA patients [PMID:18156403] [PMID:33355018]. Early falls may be transient [PMID:17571945].
- Skin carotenoid scores (Veggie Meter, Vegecheck) rise with intake [PMID:38797482] [PMID:35938228].
- Serum carotenoids track lipids, so adjust for cholesterol on lipid-lowering therapy [PMID:20678906].
- ALT: rose in rats at high doses; null in human carotenoid MA [PMID:42005766] [PMID:41437050].

** Genotype notes
- Bioavailability: 28 SNPs in 16 genes explained 72% of the variance in postprandial chylomicron lycopene (CV 70%) [PMID:25772008]. Each single variant explains only a few percent; ELOVL2 is shared across tomato carotenoids [PMID:30277929].
- BCO1: rs12934922 and rs6564851 predicted plasma lycopene response to tomato-soy juice in men with prostate cancer [PMID:30801647]. BCO1 variants separate strong from weak responders [PMID:23517913]. BCO1 rs7500996 and CD36 rs1527479 predicted skin carotenoids [PMID:38797482]. No BCO2- or SCARB1-specific lycopene outcome data were found.
- Adipose lycopene: 17 SNPs in 11 genes (incl. ABCA1, CD36, ISX, SOD2) explained 55% of variability [PMID:41848742].
- APOE e4: carriers with higher plasma lycopene had slower cognitive decline (observational) [PMID:40876538].
- None of this is validated for dosing decisions.

** Latest research
- [2026-10] RCT (n=22): lycopene in tomato juice changed 5 plasma miRNAs compared with lycopene-depleted juice [PMID:42765324].
- [2026-08] SR/MA (4 studies, n=182): FMD +1.36%, fragile in sensitivity analysis [PMID:42588128].
- [2026-07] POMOSANO RCT (n=79, MASLD): tomatoes reduced CAP steatosis [PMID:42588099].
- [2026-07] RCT (n=98, MASLD): carotenoid-rich tomato sauce reduced liver fat [PMID:42397564].
- [2026-06] SR/MA: tomato products reduced waist circumference by 1.15 cm, with no lycopene dose response [PMID:42300210].
- [2026-05] OSF umbrella review: lycopene improves mouth opening; certainty low or very low [PMID:42216734].
- [2026-04] EFSA lycopene exposure assessment [PMID:41972196].
- [2026-04] Adipose lycopene genetics: 17 SNPs explained 55% of variance [PMID:41848742].
- [2026-03] Circulating lycopene vs carotid IMT, MA (n=9131): OR 0.70 [PMID:41978094].
- [2026-01] Umbrella review (9 SRs): tomato-derived lycopene lowers BP (GRADE high) [PMID:41369612].
- [2025-12] Carotenoid liver-enzyme GRADE MA: null [PMID:41437050].
- [2025-11] PREDIMED cohort: highest lycopene quartile had prostate cancer HR 0.46 [PMID:41214650].
- [2025-11] MIND trial: lycopene associated with slower decline in APOE e4 carriers [PMID:40876538].
- [2025-09] RCT (n=75): lycopene-rich juice raised FMD [PMID:40936452].
- [2025-07] Male reproduction MA: sperm concentration SMD 0.33 [PMID:40806357].
- [2025-07] Photoaging MA: lycopene showed no significant benefit [PMID:40761858].
- [2025-06] Metabolic MA: lowest serum lycopene associated with MAFLD, OR 1.39 [PMID:40566597].
- [2025-05] Mental-health SR: no effect [PMID:40507061].
- [2025-04] COMP study: Serenoa/selenium/lycopene vs dutasteride, similar LUTS effects [PMID:40777606].
- [2025-02] Cancer MA (121 cohorts): blood lycopene RR 0.89 for overall cancer [PMID:40013157].

** Open questions
- Is lycopene itself causal, or a marker of tomato and diet quality? Standardized tomato extract, but not synthetic lycopene, lowered BP [PMID:33676812]; lycopene-free tomato matrices also showed effects [PMID:42300210]; MR is null [PMID:34796734].
- Are isolated-lycopene trials large and long enough for hard outcomes? Prostate cancer incidence RCT data are small [PMID:22633187].
- Is the preterm-birth signal real [PMID:19527386]? Can BCO1 genotype guide dosing [PMID:30801647]?
