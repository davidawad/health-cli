** Identity & forms
- L-lysine is an essential (indispensable) amino acid; mammals cannot synthesize it. Its main role is protein synthesis, and liver catabolism dominates. First-pass metabolism of dietary lysine is about 30-42% [PMID:17513440].
- Requirements: about 30 mg/kg/d in adults and 64 mg/kg/d in 0.5-year infants. Western diets supply 40-180 mg/kg/d [PMID:17513440]. An IAAO study in adults >60 y estimated 32.2 mg/kg/d for men. For women the estimate was 32.9 mg/kg/d at 60-69 y and 46.2 mg/kg/d at >70 y [PMID:38735574].
- Lysine is the first limiting amino acid in wheat- and cereal-based diets [PMID:15214256] [PMID:15159538].
- Supplements are mostly L-lysine monohydrochloride (LMH), sold over the counter mainly to suppress recurrent herpes simplex [PMID:9013429].
- Commercial lysine is made by microbial fermentation (Corynebacterium glutamicum), with over 1 million t/yr produced. Supplement absorption is reported as comparable to dietary protein sources [PMID:40626529].
- Other forms in the literature: lysine-fortified wheat flour (3 g lysine/kg) [PMID:15214257]; combined lysine + arginine products (anxiety trials) [PMID:17510493]; and IV lysine/arginine solutions for kidney protection during radionuclide therapy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72d1a024-00b7-418a-b36e-b2cb48f2ab55].
- Recent comprehensive reviews cover metabolism, post-translational modifications (collagen hydroxylation, elastin desmosine), carnitine and homoarginine synthesis, and supplement use [PMID:41009362] [PMID:40626529].

** Mechanisms
- Arginine-lysine antagonism (HSV rationale): in cell culture, arginine deficiency suppressed HSV replication, and lysine antagonized arginine's growth-promoting effect [PMID:6262023] [PMID:640102].
  - In healthy men, rising oral arginine doses linearly lowered whole-body lysine oxidation and plasma lysine. This is the first human evidence of the antagonism [PMID:32187681].
  - Lysine competes with arginine for cellular transport [PMID:17513444].
  - A systematic review notes lysine has no direct antiviral activity. It is thought to act by lowering arginine, and that does not hold in cats [PMID:26573523].
- Calcium handling: lysine 400 mg blunted the calciuric response to an oral calcium load in healthy women, and 800 mg/d raised fractional 47Ca absorption in osteoporotic patients (valine and tryptophan did not) [PMID:1486246]. In rats, lysine stimulated GLP-1 secretion via the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) [PMID:40899781].
- Glucose and hormones:
  - Oral lysine raises glucagon and modestly raises insulin, and lowers the glucose response to co-ingested glucose [PMID:19553295] [PMID:28592515].
  - In β-cells, lysine potentiates glucose-stimulated insulin secretion through AASS-dependent catabolism and GABA (in vitro and islet data) [PMID:41167553].
  - Ingested lysine is catabolized to 2-aminoadipic acid (2-AAA) and also raises insulin, C-peptide, glucagon and GLP-1 [PMID:41721626].
- Kidney: lysine shares an apical proximal-tubule uptake mechanism with gentamicin: 0.5 mmol/L lysine cut gentamicin uptake 38.9% in vitro [PMID:8429116]. High-dose lysine (IV) inhibits tubular uptake of radiolabelled peptides and albumin [PMID:12483404] [PMID:35835746].
- Stress axis: in rats, lysine deficiency raised stress-induced anxiety and changed amygdala serotonin release [PMID:12468617]. Lysine + arginine reduced restraint-stress anxiety and corticosterone [PMID:12722988].
- Nitric oxide: lysine interferes with NO production, which was the rationale for a schizophrenia pilot trial [PMID:21501494].
- Collagen: specific collagen lysines are hydroxylated to hydroxylysine, glycosylated and oxidatively deaminated to form covalent cross-links [PMID:22708567]. Lysyl hydroxylase 2-derived hydroxyallysine cross-links rise in fibrosis [PMID:31324706]. Ascorbate is a cofactor for collagen lysyl hydroxylases [PMID:41980618] [PMID:6265920].

** Dosing in trials
- HSV: 3 g/d (1 g three times daily) for 6 months gave benefit [PMID:3115841]. 1.2 g/d (400 mg three times daily) [PMID:6419679] and 1 g/d [PMID:6153847] failed. Reviews conclude <1 g/d is ineffective without a low-arginine diet, and >3 g/d may help subjectively [PMID:30881246].
- Anxiety: lysine 2.64 g/d + arginine 2.64 g/d for 1 week [PMID:17510493]. Lysine 3 g + arginine 3 g/d for 10 days [PMID:16117182].
- Fortification: 3 g lysine/kg wheat flour for 3 months [PMID:15214257].
- Calcium absorption: 400 mg acutely, or 800 mg/d short term [PMID:1486246].
- Glycemia (acute): 1 mmol/kg lean body mass [PMID:19553295], 5 or 10 g [PMID:28592515], and 2 or 5 g [PMID:25324894].
- Schizophrenia: 6 g/d for 4 weeks [PMID:21501494]. Hypertriglyceridemia: 1 g/d for 12 weeks [PMID:22818869].
- LPI: individualized small mealtime doses. 0.05 mmol/kg three times daily was tolerated, while 0.55-1.1 mmol/kg caused profuse diarrhea [PMID:12870174] [PMID:17224331].
- Upper levels: provisional NOAEL of 6.0 g/d for supplemental lysine in healthy adults, based on GI symptoms [PMID:30661148] [PMID:33000161] [PMID:33000163]. An older review suggested an upper limit of 300-400 mg/kg/d [PMID:17513440]. Trial doses ranged from 16.8 mg/d to 17.5 g/d, given for 1-1095 days [PMID:30661148].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Two systematic reviews of 71 studies (3357 subjects) found mainly subjective GI events (nausea, stomachache, diarrhea). The pooled risk ratio for GI symptoms was 1.02 (95% CI 0.96-1.07; p=0.49) [PMID:30661148] [PMID:33000161].
- Children: lysine supplements are generally considered safe, with side effects limited to GI symptoms. Excess intake can cause amino-acid imbalance [PMID:38740261].
- Kidney: one case report describes Fanconi syndrome and tubulointerstitial nephritis after oral lysine, progressing to chronic renal failure [PMID:8840955]. In rats, high-dose IV lysine reduced GFR and caused tubular necrosis and lysosomal inclusions [PMID:6425441].
- Lipids/gallbladder (animal and theoretical):
  - Lysine + methionine-enriched diets caused hypercholesterolemia in rabbits [PMID:15539285].
  - Raising the lysine/arginine ratio raised serum cholesterol in almond-fed rabbits [PMID:3155291]. Another study found the ratio is not the main determinant [PMID:6411098].
  - In rats on a low-protein (7% casein) diet, >=1.5% dietary lysine raised LDL-C and lowered albumin; arginine normalized many endpoints [PMID:30488372].
  - A review advises cautioning people with cardiovascular or gallbladder disease about the theoretical risk [PMID:30881246].
- Minerals: 1.5% dietary lysine did not change copper or iron bioavailability in rats [PMID:26968817].
- IV lysine/arginine (radionuclide therapy) is not the same as oral supplements. 75 g IV lysine caused potassium levels of 6.3-6.8 mmol/L in 3 of 6 patients [PMID:12483404]. Amino-acid co-infusion caused metabolic acidosis with hyperkalemia [PMID:29488080]. A 2026 post-authorisation study (n=41) found mean K+ +0.60 mmol/L at 4 h and treatment-related hyperkalemia in 12.2% [PMID:41978231].
- Lactation: LactMed says high-dose supplementation during breastfeeding has not been studied. It judges supplements probably low risk, but an alternative may be preferred for newborn or preterm infants [PMID:40587650].
- Cats: supplementation in cats may worsen feline herpesvirus disease. Cats cannot synthesize arginine, and arginine deficiency causes hyperammonemia [PMID:26573523].

** Contraindications
- Glutaric aciduria type 1: treatment is a low-lysine diet, so supplementing lysine runs directly against therapy [PMID:36221165] [PMID:33000154].
- Pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy (ALDH7A1/antiquitin deficiency): managed with lysine-restriction diet ± arginine. Supplements would oppose this [PMID:40680317] [PMID:26026794].
- Familial hyperlysinemia (AASS defects) [PMID:38991296].
- Pre-existing kidney disease: caution, based on a human case of Fanconi syndrome/TIN and animal nephrotoxicity [PMID:8840955] [PMID:6425441].
- Lysinuric protein intolerance: use only under specialist direction at low doses, because high doses cause diarrhea and IV loads occasionally raised ammonia [PMID:12870174] [PMID:10831173].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Aminoglycosides (gentamicin, tobramycin): in rats, lysine and aminoglycosides were each nephrotoxic, and toxicity was additive when combined [PMID:6425441]. Lysine did not prevent gentamicin nephrotoxicity in another rat study [PMID:6798129]. In vitro, lysine competes with gentamicin for tubular uptake [PMID:8429116]. No human data.
- Lutetium Lu 177 dotatate (and other radioligands): the label requires IV L-lysine HCl + L-arginine HCl (18-25 g each) to lower kidney radiation dose [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72d1a024-00b7-418a-b36e-b2cb48f2ab55]. Watch potassium and acid-base status [PMID:41978231] [PMID:29488080]. Oral lysine reduced renal peptide uptake in rats [PMID:16330570], but oral supplements are not a substitute.
- Antidiabetic agents: lysine lowers postprandial glucose acutely [PMID:19553295] [PMID:28592515]. Any additive effect is likely minor and unstudied.
- Antihypertensives: possible additive BP reduction based on one small RCT [PMID:32153847].
- Antivirals: no interaction data. Reviews place lysine as an alternative symptomatic option; it is not a replacement for nucleoside antivirals [PMID:37376614].
*** Other supplements
- L-arginine: has opposing effects (mutual transport competition) [PMID:32187681] [PMID:17513444]. It undermines the HSV rationale [PMID:6262023]. Yet it is combined deliberately in anxiety products [PMID:17510493], and in rats it reversed adverse effects of excess lysine [PMID:30488372].
- Calcium: lysine raises intestinal calcium absorption and lowers urinary calcium losses [PMID:1486246]. In rats, lysine + calcium strongly increased GLP-1 [PMID:40899781].
- Zinc: an open-label lysine/zinc/herbal topical product for cold sores [PMID:15989381]. Zinc and lysine are both listed as promising HSV remedies [PMID:37376614].
- Vitamin C: cofactor for lysyl hydroxylase in collagen hydroxylysine formation [PMID:41980618] [PMID:6265920].
- Collagen: hydroxylysine-derived cross-links are central to collagen structure [PMID:22708567] [PMID:41009362]. No trial combines lysine with collagen peptides.
- Vitamin D-3: a narrative review proposes combining lysine, calcium and vitamin D for muscle/bone in cereal-based diets. This is unproven [PMID:33290094].
- Iron/copper: no change in bioavailability (rat) [PMID:26968817].
- Vitamin B6/carnitine: lysine 1 g/d ± B6 had no lipid effect [PMID:22818869].
*** Conditions
- HSV recurrence: see the evidence table [PMID:30881246].
- Cereal-dominated, lysine-poor diets: benefits are clearest here [PMID:15214256] [PMID:38740261].
- Low-protein diets: adverse lysine effects in rats were confined mainly to low-protein diets [PMID:30488372].
- Salt-sensitive hypertension/CKD: lysine protected the kidney in rats. In at-risk patients it inhibited tubular albumin uptake [PMID:35835746] [PMID:38639736].
- Pregnancy: no specific supplement safety data were found. For lactation, see LactMed [PMID:40587650].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. Supplementation causes real changes: plasma lysine rises (about 3-fold after an oral load) [PMID:19553295] [PMID:21501494], plasma and urine 2-AAA rise [PMID:41721626], glucagon rises [PMID:28592515], and arginine/lysine plasma ratios shift [PMID:32187681]. These can confound plasma amino acid profiles and 2-AAA biomarker studies.
- IV lysine/arginine raises serum potassium and lowers bicarbonate/pH [PMID:29488080] [PMID:41978231].

** Genotype notes
- Inborn errors (Mendelian, not common pharmacogenomic variants) change lysine tolerance: SLC7A7 (LPI) [PMID:31213652] [PMID:15050971], GCDH (glutaric aciduria type 1) [PMID:36221165], ALDH7A1 (pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy) [PMID:40680317] and AASS (hyperlysinemia) [PMID:38991296].
- AASS, ALDH7A1 and DHTKD1 expression is lower in type 2 diabetic islets (in vitro relevance only) [PMID:41167553].
- No common-variant nutrigenomic source was identified.

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Rat study: severe (60%) dietary lysine deficiency impaired hippocampal neurogenesis, altered anxiety-like and cognitive behavior, and shifted gut microbiota [PMID:42848007].
- [2026-04] LysaKare (2.5% lysine-arginine IV) post-authorisation safety study (n=41): K+ +0.60 mmol/L at 4 h; 12.2% treatment-related hyperkalemia; no serious AEs [PMID:41978231].
- [2026-03] 13C-lysine challenge in healthy adults (n=16, 5 g): converted to 2-AAA, which peaks around 2 h. Insulin, C-peptide, glucagon and GLP-1 rose [PMID:41721626].
- [2026-01] AASS-dependent lysine catabolism supports insulin secretion in human islets; AASS pathway genes are lower in T2D islets [PMID:41167553].
- [2025-12] CKD cross-sectional study: urinary lysine higher than controls; links to megalin and tubular injury [PMID:41441627].
- [2025-11] Mouse study: a lysine-restricted diet reduced obesity-related anxiety-like behavior via gut microbiota (Oscillibacter, tryptophol) [PMID:41316428]. A companion study found a lysine-restricted diet ameliorated obesity, and blood lysine was higher in children with obesity [PMID:41224764].
- [2025-10] Rat study: oral lysine strongly stimulated GLP-1 via CaSR. Lysine + calcium lowered glycemia in an OGTT [PMID:40899781].
- [2025-09] Review of lysine sources, metabolism and supplement use. It flags osteoporosis and kidney resorption as priority research areas [PMID:41009362].
- [2025-08] Pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy cohort (n=17): a lysine-restricted diet lowered plasma lysine and urine α-AASA; no added seizure benefit [PMID:40680317].
- [2025-04] Review of lysine for child/adolescent growth: improves anthropometry in cereal-based diets; GI symptoms only; caution about amino-acid imbalance [PMID:38740261].
- No new human HSV-lysine RCTs were identified after 2018 in PubMed searches.

** Open questions
- Does lysine prevent HSV recurrence at >=3 g/d? This needs an adequately powered, preregistered RCT with diet (arginine) control; dietary lysine and arginine intake vary widely [PMID:3117869] [PMID:30881246].
- Long-term (>1 year) renal and lipid safety at 3-6 g/d in humans, given one human nephritis case and animal cholesterol data.
- Is lysine's calcium-absorption effect clinically meaningful for bone density? No fracture or BMD trials were found.
- Can lysine-arginine anxiety effects be reproduced independently of the manufacturer (Ajinomoto-affiliated trials)?
- Whether higher circulating 2-AAA from lysine intake carries cardiometabolic risk [PMID:41721626] [PMID:37582714].
- Kidney-protective potential in salt-sensitive hypertension and CKD, which so far rests on animal and pilot human data [PMID:38639736].
