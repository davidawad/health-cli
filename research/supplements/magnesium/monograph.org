** Identity & forms
- Magnesium is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: magnesium citrate, chloride, oxide, glycinate, lactate, sulfate. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/, PMID:39519450, PMID:41245414].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/, PMID:39519450, PMID:41245414].

- NIH ODS identifies magnesium as essential nutrient with variable bioavailability by salt; 2025 meta-analysis found modest systolic BP lowering in normotensive adults at ≤360 mg/day and longer duration [PMID:39519450]. 2025 meta-analysis found CRP reduction in metabolic syndrome but not consistent effects on other inflammatory markers [PMID:41245414].
- Deficiency correction is established; for BP and metabolic markers, effects are small and context-dependent; cognitive RCT evidence is insufficient [PMID:39519450, PMID:41245414, PMID:39009081].

** Dosing in trials
- Supplement trials often use about 250–400 mg/day elemental magnesium for 12–16 weeks; salts differ in absorption and GI tolerance [PMID:41245414, URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Diarrhea and abdominal cramping are common dose-limiting effects; hypermagnesemia risk rises with impaired renal clearance [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].

** Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment increases toxicity risk; distinguish elemental magnesium from compound mass [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].

** Interactions
- Chelates tetracycline/quinolone antibiotics and oral bisphosphonates, reducing absorption; separate dosing. Diuretics and proton-pump inhibitors can alter magnesium status [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2025 RCT meta-analysis in metabolic syndrome found lower CRP after prolonged supplementation, while other inflammatory markers were not consistently changed [PMID:41245414].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
