** Identity & forms
- Essential mineral. An adult body holds about 25 g, 50-60% in bone and most of the rest in soft tissue. Less than 1% is in serum, which is kept between about 0.75 and 0.95 mmol/L. Hypomagnesemia is serum Mg <0.75 mmol/L [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Reference intakes (US FNB, via NIH ODS fact sheet updated 2026-01-06): adult RDA 400-420 mg/day for men and 310-320 mg/day for women. The Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) for adults is 350 mg/day of *supplemental* magnesium (supplements and medications only, not food) [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Labels declare elemental magnesium, not the weight of the whole salt. About 30-40% of dietary magnesium is absorbed [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Absorption: paracellular in the small intestine (claudin-2/-12) and transcellular in the colon via TRPM6/7 and CNNM4. The kidney fine-tunes balance in the distal convoluted tubule [PMID:38871680].
- Forms and bioavailability:
  - General rule: soluble forms are absorbed better. Small studies show aspartate, citrate, lactate and chloride are more bioavailable than oxide and sulfate [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
  - Citrate vs oxide: in a 60-day double-blind RCT (n=46, 300 mg/day elemental), citrate and amino-acid chelate raised 24-h urinary Mg more than oxide. Citrate gave the highest serum Mg, and oxide did not differ from placebo [PMID:14596323]. In Mg-saturated men, a single 400 mg dose of citrate raised urinary and plasma Mg while oxide did not [PMID:32162607].
  - Oxide: high elemental content but poorly soluble. Serum exposure was not dose-proportional between 750 and 2000 mg single doses [PMID:42091979].
  - Glycinate (bisglycinate/diglycinate): in ileal-resection patients, 26Mg absorption was similar to oxide overall (23.5% vs 22.8%). It was higher (23.5% vs 11.8%) in the 4 patients who absorbed oxide worst, and it was better tolerated [PMID:7815675]. In a 2024 acute crossover study (n=40), plasma Mg rose after oxide and citrate but not significantly after bisglycinate within 6 h [PMID:39770988].
  - L-threonate (Magtein, MMFS-01): in neurons, threonate raised intraneuronal Mg and synapse density, and other Mg anions did not (preclinical) [PMID:27178134]. Human data are limited to small RCTs on cognition and sleep (see Evidence).
  - Chloride: used in several trials (depression, exercise, prediabetes) [PMID:28654669] [PMID:40077784] [PMID:41641401].
- Laxatives/antacids: magnesium hydroxide (e.g. 500 mg elemental Mg per tablespoon of milk of magnesia) is a common hidden source [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].

** Mechanisms
- Cofactor in more than 600 enzymatic reactions, mostly as Mg-ATP. Needed for energy metabolism, muscle contraction and neurotransmission [PMID:38871680].
- The free ion gates the NMDA receptor and opposes calcium in vascular smooth muscle. These actions are the proposed basis for effects on BP, arrhythmia, cramps, migraine and sleep (narrative review) [PMID:42647115].
- Vitamin D: enzymes that make and break down vitamin D metabolites are Mg-dependent. In an RCT, the effect of Mg on 25(OH)D depended on baseline 25(OH)D [PMID:30541089].
- Glucose metabolism: deficiency is linked to insulin resistance. In diabetes, renal insulin resistance reduces Mg reabsorption [PMID:41641401] [PMID:40613016].
- Inflammation: a meta-analysis in metabolic syndrome shows lower CRP [PMID:41245414].

** Dosing in trials
- BP: median 365-368 mg/day elemental for about 3 months. 300 mg/day for 1 month was enough to raise serum Mg and lower BP [PMID:27402922] [PMID:41000008]. In normotensives, <=360 mg/day and >3 months gave the largest SBP drop [PMID:39519450].
- T2D: 300-600 mg/day elemental. Example: magnesium oxide 500 mg (302 mg elemental) for 12 months [PMID:42534812]. HbA1c effect modeled at 500 mg/day [PMID:35045911].
- Sleep: bisglycinate 250 mg/day elemental for 4 weeks [PMID:40918053]. Magnesium L-threonate 1-2 g/day of salt [PMID:39252819] [PMID:41601871].
- Migraine: up to 600 mg/day. 300 mg twice daily has been suggested. These doses exceed the UL and should be used only under clinician supervision [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Depression: 248 mg/day elemental as chloride for 6 weeks [PMID:28654669]. 250 mg/day added to fluoxetine [PMID:41916937].
- Most RCTs in the 2026 cardiometabolic MA used >=300 mg/day for >=12 weeks. Data at >=500 mg/day or >=25 weeks are sparse [PMID:42588058].
- Many trial doses sit at or above the 350 mg/day supplemental UL [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects are the main dose-limiting problem: diarrhea, nausea and cramping from the osmotic effect of unabsorbed salt. Carbonate, chloride, gluconate and oxide are most often reported to cause diarrhea [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/]. In the Cochrane cramp review, minor (mostly GI) AEs were more common with magnesium (RR 1.51, 0.98-2.33), affecting 11-37% of users [PMID:32956536].
- Hypermagnesemia:
  - Toxicity usually appears above serum 1.74-2.61 mmol/L: hypotension, nausea, flushing, urinary retention, ileus, lethargy, then weakness, breathing difficulty, arrhythmia and cardiac arrest. It is mostly linked to very large laxative/antacid doses (>5000 mg/day). Risk rises with impaired kidney function [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
  - In 320 inpatients prescribed MgO, 23% developed hypermagnesemia. Independent risk factors were eGFR <=55.4 (OR 3.1), BUN >=22.4 (OR 3.5), dose >=1650 mg/day and use >=36 days [PMID:30805197].
  - Case series: 4 older adults with renal dysfunction and constipation had symptomatic hypermagnesemia on MgO, and 1 died [PMID:30136128]. A fatal case with normal kidney function suggests retained laxative in a constipated bowel can act as an absorption reservoir [PMID:29661354].
- CKD trial: in MAGiCAL-CKD (eGFR 15-45, magnesium hydroxide 15 mmol twice daily for 12 months), GI AEs occurred in 35 vs 9 patients. There were 5 deaths and 6 CV events on magnesium vs 2 and 0 on placebo [PMID:36749131].
- Exercise: short-term MgCl2 600 mg/day lowered VO2max and sprint power in replete exercisers (small crossover) [PMID:40077784].

** Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment or kidney failure: excess cannot be cleared. Avoid unsupervised supplementation [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:30805197] [PMID:30136128].
- Chronic constipation, ileus or bowel dysmotility combined with Mg laxatives or high doses: risk of toxic accumulation [PMID:29661354] [PMID:30136128].
- Doses above the 350 mg/day supplemental UL without medical supervision [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Tetracyclines (doxycycline, demeclocycline): magnesium forms insoluble complexes. Take the antibiotic >=2 h before or 4-6 h after magnesium [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/]. An aluminum-magnesium antacid cut doxycycline AUC from 38.6 to 6.0 mg.h/L, giving subtherapeutic levels [PMID:2610502].
- Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin): same timing advice [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/]. Maalox given 5-10 min, 2 h and 4 h before ciprofloxacin left 15%, 23% and 70% relative bioavailability. Giving it 6 h before or 2 h after had no effect [PMID:2598571].
- Oral bisphosphonates (alendronate): magnesium reduces absorption. Separate by >=2 h [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Levothyroxine: in a crossover PK trial, magnesium aspartate cut T4 AUC by 12% (GMR 0.88, 0.81-0.95). Citrate cut it by 7% (GMR 0.93, 0.86-1.01, NS). The authors advise separating doses [PMID:41221788].
- Gabapentin: 1 g MgO lowered gabapentin Cmax by 33%, AUC by 43% and bioavailability by 32% [PMID:22240839].
- Proton pump inhibitors: long-term use (typically >1 year) can cause hypomagnesemia. In FDA-reviewed cases, supplements failed to correct it in 25%, and the PPI had to be stopped. FDA advises checking serum Mg before and during long-term PPI use [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/]. Meta-analyses: adjusted OR 1.71 (1.33-2.19), higher with high-dose PPIs (OR 2.13) [PMID:31689852]. A case report describes PPI hypomagnesemia with delirium, hypocalcemia and hypokalemia that resolved after stopping the PPI and repleting [PMID:39465769].
- Diuretics:
  - Loop and thiazide diuretics increase urinary Mg loss, while potassium-sparing diuretics (amiloride, spironolactone) reduce it [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
  - In the Rotterdam Study (n=9820), thiazides were linked to hypomagnesemia (OR 2.74-3.14), but loop diuretics and thiazide plus potassium-sparing combinations were not [PMID:30095199].
- Calcineurin inhibitors: listed among common drug causes of hypomagnesemia [PMID:38871680].
- SGLT2 inhibitors raise serum Mg and have been proposed as a treatment for hypomagnesemia [PMID:38871680] [PMID:40565121].
- Antihypertensives: the larger BP drop seen in treated hypertensive patients (SBP -7.68 mmHg) suggests an additive effect [PMID:41000008].
- Metformin: linked to hypomagnesemia, possibly through GI loss and TRPM6 suppression (case and narrative review) [PMID:40613016]. Magnesium itself slightly lowers FBG and HbA1c [PMID:42588058].
- SSRIs: in a small non-blinded RCT, magnesium added to fluoxetine gave a larger HDRS drop (-6.35 vs -2.80) [PMID:41916937].
*** Other supplements
- Zinc: very high supplemental zinc (142 mg/day) reduced Mg absorption and balance in metabolic-ward studies [PMID:7836627] [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Calcium: in adolescent girls, raising calcium intake from 800 to 1800 mg/day did not significantly change Mg absorption, kinetics or balance (n=5) [PMID:9277559].
- Vitamin D3: Mg supplementation changed 25(OH)D3 depending on baseline 25(OH)D (raised near 30 ng/mL, lowered at 30-50 ng/mL) [PMID:30541089]. Mg glycinate plus D3 raised 25(OH)D more than D3 alone in overweight/obese adults (+6.3 ng/mL) [PMID:35576873].
- Heme Iron (Proferrin): no human study of magnesium with heme iron polypeptide was found.
- Mg-containing laxatives and antacids add to the supplement dose and are the main source of toxic exposure [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:29661354].
*** Conditions
- CKD/renal impairment, older age with constipation: see Contraindications [PMID:30805197] [PMID:30136128].
- Type 2 diabetes, alcohol use disorder and older age raise the risk of depletion through urinary loss, poor intake or reduced absorption [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/].
- Ileal resection: absorption is impaired, and glycinate may be better tolerated [PMID:7815675].
- Kidney transplant recipients have lower circulating Mg, partly from immunosuppressants [PMID:40565121].
*** Lab-test interference
- Serum Mg is a poor marker of status. It holds less than 1% of body Mg, is defended by kidney and bone, and correlates poorly with tissue levels. A normal value does not exclude depletion. Alternatives (erythrocyte, ionized, urinary Mg, loading test) each have limits, and no single test is satisfactory [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:42336500] [PMID:42647115].
- The reference change value for serum Mg is about 12%, similar to or larger than the change with oral repletion. Serum endpoints in trials are therefore underpowered [PMID:42647115]. In a 6-month T2D trial, citrate raised 24-h urinary Mg but not serum Mg [PMID:41903889].
- Hemolysis falsely raises measured magnesium, roughly in proportion to the amount of lysate [PMID:16519604].
- Magnesium is not part of routine electrolyte panels, so deficiency (for example from PPIs) is easily missed [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/] [PMID:39465769].

** Genotype notes
- TRPM7: in a precision-based RCT randomized by TRPM7 genotype, Mg supplementation increased vitamin-D-synthesizing gut bacteria (C. maltaromaticum, F. prausnitzii) only in people without the TRPM7 missense variant. With the variant, C. maltaromaticum decreased (not significant after FDR correction) [PMID:40946805].
- No clinically actionable genotype-guided dosing exists.

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Sleep SR (12 RCTs): inconsistent findings, low to very low certainty; not supported as routine insomnia treatment [PMID:42661485].
- [2026-08] Narrative review: serum Mg fails as a trial endpoint (RCV about 12%) [PMID:42647115].
- [2026-07] Cardiometabolic dose-response MA (78 RCTs): SBP -2.50, DBP -1.58 mmHg, FBG -3.60 mg/dL, HbA1c -0.15%, HDL +1.45 mg/dL; modest effects [PMID:42588058].
- [2026-07] Insulin-resistance MA (15 RCTs): no significant HOMA-IR effect [PMID:42426860].
- [2026-07] 12-month RCT of MgO in T2D (n=247): HbA1c -0.30% vs -0.05% (NS); more patients reached HbA1c <7% (14% vs 6%) [PMID:42534812].
- [2026-07] Liver-enzyme MA (7 trials): ALT -4.76 and AST -6.28 U/L [PMID:42580984].
- [2026-06] Clinical-chemistry review: no single biomarker reflects whole-body Mg [PMID:42336500].
- [2026-05] RCT of IV MgSO4 in cardiac surgery (n=265): no POAF reduction (RR 1.29); stopped for futility [PMID:42206948].
- [2026-05] MgO single-dose PK: non-dose-proportional exposure from 750 to 2000 mg [PMID:42091979].
- [2026-03] RCT of Mg citrate 350 mg/d in T2D with arterial calcification: no effect on calcification propensity or PWV [PMID:41903889].
- [2026-03] Mg 250 mg/d added to fluoxetine improved HDRS (small, non-blinded) [PMID:41916937].
- [2026-02] Regression-discontinuity study of 171,727 ICU admissions: repleting borderline-low Mg did not prevent tachyarrhythmia [PMID:41359319].
- [2026-02] Three-arm leg-cramp RCT: compression stockings worked, magnesium did not [PMID:41680812].
- [2026-01] Magtein RCT (n=100): better NIH cognition composite and subjective sleep impairment; no change in objective sleep [PMID:41601871].
- [2025-11] ThyroMag crossover trial: Mg aspartate lowers levothyroxine AUC by 12% [PMID:41221788].
- [2025-09] BP MA (38 RCTs): SBP -2.81 mmHg; larger effects in treated hypertension and hypomagnesemia [PMID:41000008].
- [2025-09] TRPM7-stratified RCT: microbiome effects of Mg depend on genotype [PMID:40946805].
- [2025-08] Bisglycinate 250 mg/d RCT (n=155): small ISI improvement (d=0.2) [PMID:40918053].
- [2025-03] MgCl2 600 mg/d lowered VO2max in replete exercisers [PMID:40077784].
- [2024-10] Supplement migraine MA: Mg reduced attacks (MD -2.51) and migraine days (MD -1.66) [PMID:39404918].

** Open questions
- Whether benefits are confined to people who start out depleted. Status markers to select them are poor, and many subgroup signals are post hoc [PMID:41000008] [PMID:42647115].
- How different salts compare on clinical outcomes, not just short-term absorption markers. Glycinate and threonate claims rest on small or industry-linked studies.
- Migraine: pooled benefits rest on small trials, and an earlier MA found no significant effect on severity [PMID:30764890]. Optimal dose relative to the UL is unclear.
- The excess deaths and CV events in MAGiCAL-CKD were small in number but unexplained. The safety of chronic supplementation in moderate CKD needs clarification [PMID:36749131].
- Depression trials are small, often open-label, and highly heterogeneous.
- The size and timing of chelation interactions with modern supplement doses (as opposed to antacid doses).
