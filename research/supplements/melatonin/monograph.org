** Identity & forms
- Endogenous pineal indoleamine hormone (N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine), secreted at night. It acts through the G-protein-coupled MT1 and MT2 receptors [PMID:26514204].
- Regulatory status: OTC dietary supplement in the US and Canada; regulated as a medicine in the UK and EU [PMID:36562403].
  - UK: Circadin 2 mg prolonged-release is prescription-only (POM) for short-term primary insomnia in adults aged 55 or over [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc].
  - Australia: Schedule 4 (prescription). Schedule 3 (pharmacist-only) covers modified-release tablets of 2 mg or less, for adults 55 or over, in packs of no more than 30 [URL:https://www.tga.gov.au/scheduling-decision-final/notice-final-decision-amend-current-poisons-standard-relation-melatonin].
- Forms: immediate-release tablets, capsules, sublingual products and gummies; adult prolonged-release (Circadin) [PMID:26514204]; pediatric prolonged-release minitablets (PedPRM), studied for 2 years in autism [PMID:31982581].
- US adult use rose from 0.4% (1999-2000) to 2.1% (2017-2018), and use of more than 5 mg/d from 0.08% to 0.28% [PMID:35103775].
- Pharmacokinetics: oral bioavailability is about 15% (9-33%), Tmax about 50 min for immediate release, half-life about 45 min. Exposure is altered by age, caffeine, smoking, oral contraceptives, food and fluvoxamine [PMID:26008214].
- Label accuracy is poor. Canadian products: -83% to +478% of label; more than 71% outside ±10%; 26% contained serotonin [PMID:27855744]. US gummies: only 3 of 25 within ±10%, range 74-347% of label; one contained only CBD. These figures are from secondary reporting of the research letter [PMID:37097362] [URL:https://mdedge.com/content/shocking-data-whats-really-melatonin-gummies].
  - US children's products (n=110): 0-667% of label, up to 50 mg/serving [PMID:39482109]. EU: 12% of legal-supply-chain products non-compliant [PMID:40572610].

** Mechanisms
- Chronobiotic: MT1/MT2 signalling in the suprachiasmatic nucleus shifts circadian phase; synthetic agonists (ramelteon, tasimelteon, agomelatine) use the same receptors [PMID:26514204]. Melatonin advanced endogenous melatonin onset by 1.18 h in DSWPD [PMID:21120122].
- Soporific: effects peaked at about 4 mg in a dose-response MA. Dosing earlier (about 3 h before bed) predicted larger sleep onset latency (SOL) gains [PMID:38888087].
- Immune: modulates T-cell activation and differentiation (Th17, Treg, memory T cells), partly via calcineurin [PMID:28152213]. Inhibits NF-kB/NLRP3 and upregulates Nrf2/SIRT1, which interferes with mTOR signalling [PMID:30242884].
- Beta cell: suppresses first-phase insulin secretion. The effect is amplified in MTNR1B rs10830963 risk carriers [PMID:42346809] [PMID:26440713].
- Coagulation: a single dose lowered FVIII:C and fibrinogen in healthy men [PMID:18289163].
- Metabolism: 6-hydroxylation mainly by CYP1A2, with minor roles for CYP2C19 and CYP2C9 [PMID:11317475].

** Dosing in trials
- Sleep onset: 0.5-5 mg. Efficacy peaks at about 4 mg taken about 3 h before bed, compared with the usual 2 mg 30 min before bed [PMID:38888087]. Circadin: 2 mg 1-2 h before bed after food, for up to 13 weeks [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc].
- Jet lag: 0.5-5 mg at destination bedtime. 5 mg works faster than 0.5 mg; doses above 5 mg add nothing; 2 mg slow-release is less effective [PMID:12076414].
- DSWPD: 0.5 mg fast-release 1 h before desired bedtime plus behavioural scheduling [PMID:29912983]. Shift work: 1-10 mg after the night shift [PMID:25113164].
- Pediatrics: PedPRM 2/5/10 mg nightly for up to 104 weeks [PMID:31982581]. Neuropsychiatric pediatric MA optimum is 2-4 mg, 3 h before bed [PMID:40914024].
- High dose: 10 mg or more in 79 studies (n=3861) [PMID:34923676]. 10 mg nightly for 3 months in T2D [PMID:35619221].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short-term AEs are mild and transient: drowsiness, headache, dizziness, fatigue, mood swings, nightmares [PMID:31722088] [PMID:30670284]. High-dose MA: no increase in serious AEs (rate ratio 0.88) but more minor AEs (RR 1.40). Only 4 low-bias studies [PMID:34923676]. Somnolence 8.8% vs 1.4% with placebo over 12 weeks [PMID:35207411].
- Long-term data are scarce; reviewers warn "against complacency" [PMID:36562403]. 2-year PedPRM data show no effect on growth, BMI or Tanner stage [PMID:31982581].
- Heart failure (HF) signal (AHA Scientific Sessions Nov 2025 abstract, not peer reviewed):
  - TriNetX insomnia cohort, n=130,828; melatonin use for 12 months or more vs matched non-users. Incident HF 5% vs 3% (HR 1.89); HF hospitalization 19% vs 7%; death 8% vs 4% (HR 2.09) [URL:https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Articles/2025/11/03/16/19/mon-melatonin-aha-2025].
  - Exposure came from prescription records in mostly-OTC markets; the study cannot show causation [URL:https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-conference-abstract-on-association-between-long-term-melatonin-supplementation-and-incidence-of-heart-failure-in-patients-with-insomnia/].
- Later HF analyses: NHANES found no significant association with CVD or HF [PMID:42167796]. A letter critiques confounding [PMID:41764369]. A speculative prolactin mechanism has been proposed [PMID:42394777]. RCTs in cardiac patients show no harm and a possible LVEF gain [PMID:42123177].
- Glucose: worsened glucose tolerance in MTNR1B carriers [PMID:42346809]; 10 mg/d reduced insulin sensitivity 12% in T2D [PMID:35619221]. Blood pressure: raised 24-h BP (+6.5/+4.9 mmHg) in patients on nifedipine [PMID:10792199].
- Pediatric overdose surge:
  - 260,435 pediatric ingestions reported to US poison centers in 2012-2021 (+530%); 5 children ventilated, 2 deaths [PMID:35653284].
  - About 10,930 ED visits for unsupervised ingestion at age 5 or under in 2019-2022 (7.1% of such medication visits); gummies were the main form [PMID:38451863].
  - 2026 SR: rising prescribing, extended use and overdoses in young children, with no efficacy data in typical development [PMID:41481289].
- Daytime dosing causes sleepiness and can delay adaptation [PMID:12076414]. Pregnancy/lactation: no insomnia RCTs; no major signals in trials for other indications [PMID:34730672].

** Contraindications
- Fluvoxamine co-use: avoid (AUC up 17-fold) [PMID:10668847] [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc].
- Autoimmune disease: SmPC does not recommend use (no data) [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc]. Mechanistically plausible given T-cell effects [PMID:28152213].
- Hepatic impairment, pregnancy and breastfeeding: not recommended per SmPC [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc]. Human pregnancy data are limited [PMID:34730672].
- Driving or machinery after dosing, especially with other sedatives [PMID:18763235].
- Dementia-related sleep disturbance: AASM recommends against [PMID:26414986]. Cochrane found no benefit [PMID:33189083].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Fluvoxamine (CYP1A2): AUC up 17-fold, Cmax up 12-fold [PMID:10668847]. 50-69% inhibition of 6-hydroxylation in vitro [PMID:11317475].
- Other CYP1A2 inhibitors: 5-methoxypsoralen impairs 6-hydroxylation at clinical concentrations [PMID:24732412]. The SmPC also lists cimetidine, oestrogens and quinolones [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc]. Inducers (carbamazepine, rifampicin) lower levels.
- Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs: PR-melatonin plus zolpidem worsened psychomotor, driving and memory impairment. There was no PK change, so the interaction is pharmacodynamic via GABA-A [PMID:18763235]. Melatonin does not help benzodiazepine taper [PMID:26547856].
- Warfarin: case reports of harm cited in the Cochrane jet-lag review [PMID:12076414]. Melatonin lowers FVIII/fibrinogen [PMID:18289163]. A bleeding review flags it with anticoagulants [PMID:36304597]. Monitor INR.
- Nifedipine and other calcium-channel blockers: BP and heart rate rose in a crossover RCT [PMID:10792199]. Reviews flag antihypertensive interactions [PMID:30670284].
- Immunosuppressants: T-cell and calcineurin modulation [PMID:28152213] could theoretically oppose therapy. No clinical study found.
- Antidiabetics: possible loss of glycemic control [PMID:35619221] [PMID:42346809]. One MA reports improvement [PMID:39428939]. Monitor glucose.
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine: 600 mg raised melatonin Cmax by 142% and AUC by 120% (CYP1A2 inhibition). Stronger in non-smokers and *1F/*1F carriers [PMID:14616429]. Caffeine taken 12-24 h earlier had no significant mean effect [PMID:17040215].
- Magnesium (+ zinc): melatonin-magnesium-zinc improved PSQI by 6.8 points in long-term-care residents (combination product, additive) [PMID:21226679].
- L-Theanine: 3 mg melatonin beat 200 mg theanine, and both beat placebo, for insomnia in cancer patients. No combination arm [PMID:38846134].
- Glycine: no effect on plasma melatonin; acts on SCN neuropeptides. Additive sleep effects are untested [PMID:22529837].
- Rapamycin: preclinical synergy (AKT/mTOR, mitophagy); melatonin reduced rapamycin toxicity to normal cells [PMID:29247557] [PMID:30242884]. No human data.
*** Conditions
- Smoking (CYP1A2 induction): exogenous melatonin AUC was about 3-fold higher after quitting (21.07 vs 7.34 nmol/L x h) [PMID:15824912].
- Alcohol: suppressed endogenous melatonin 15-19% [PMID:17612945]. The SmPC advises avoiding it [URL:https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2809/smpc].
- Diabetes / impaired glucose tolerance: see above and Genotype notes [PMID:35619221] [PMID:42346809].
- Epilepsy: Cochrane draws no conclusions on seizures [PMID:27513702]; a 2024 MA found better sleep latency and seizure severity [PMID:38417212]. Case reports of harm [PMID:12076414]. One case of mild generalized epilepsy in a pediatric MA [PMID:31982807].
- Heart failure: unresolved observational signal [PMID:42167796]. Young children: overdose and label risk [PMID:38451863] [PMID:37097362].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical interference found. Physiological effects: worse glucose tolerance on testing in MTNR1B carriers [PMID:26440713]; lower FVIII:C and fibrinogen [PMID:18289163]. Endogenous melatonin clearance is a candidate CYP1A2 phenotype marker [PMID:42063293].

** Genotype notes
- CYP1A2*1F: caffeine inhibition was greater in *1F/*1F carriers [PMID:14616429]. *1A/*1A carriers had higher 1.5-h levels [PMID:17040215].
- MTNR1B rs10830963: melatonin's harmful effect on glucose AUC was 6-fold larger in carriers [PMID:26440713]. In carriers, glucose tolerance worsened 11.7% and first-phase beta-cell responsivity fell 40%, with no effect in non-carriers [PMID:42346809].
- PER3 VNTR: PER3 4/4 DSWPD patients respond better than 5-allele carriers [PMID:32682347].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Diabetes Care: melatonin impairs glucose tolerance only in MTNR1B risk carriers [PMID:42346809]. Endogenous melatonin clearance correlates with CYP1A2 indices (r=0.57-0.67) [PMID:42063293].
- [2026-06] Speculative prolactin pathway proposed for the HF signal [PMID:42394777].
- [2026-05] NHANES: no significant association with CVD or HF [PMID:42167796].
- [2026-04] Cardiac RCT MA: LVEF change +3.95 points [PMID:42123177].
- [2026-02] BMJ NMA: melatonin receptor agonists OR 0.54 for postoperative delirium [PMID:41690769].
- [2026-02] Migraine MA (9 RCTs): -1.54 headache days/month [PMID:41627537].
- [2026-01] JAMA Netw Open SR: young-child use and overdoses rising [PMID:41481289]. Breast radiotherapy MA: fatigue SMD -0.43 [PMID:42726650].
- [2025-12] Cardiometabolic MA (63 RCTs): SBP -2.34 mmHg [PMID:41515249]. Pediatric dose MA: 2-4 mg [PMID:40914024].
- [2025-11] AHA abstract: HF HR 1.89 with long-term use [URL:https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Articles/2025/11/03/16/19/mon-melatonin-aha-2025].
- [2025-10] Migraine NMA: no difference from placebo [PMID:41139810].
- [2025-09] ICU MA: delirium RR 0.72 [PMID:40662882]. A 2025-08 survey of US children's products found 0-667% of label [PMID:39482109].
- [2025-07] MAGIC RCT favours midazolam [PMID:40622250]. Cancer-related fatigue MA SMD -0.23 [PMID:39733381].
- [2025-04] Cochrane cancer review: probably reduces fatigue [PMID:40304216]. Severe COVID MA OR 0.19 [PMID:40187234].
- [2024-11] Older-inpatient delirium MA: OR 0.59 [PMID:39587681].

** Open questions
- Whether the AHA HF association holds after peer review, active-comparator designs and adjustment for insomnia severity. Long-term cardiovascular RCT data are absent.
- Multi-year pediatric safety beyond 2 years. Clinical relevance of glucose effects in MTNR1B carriers.
- Optimal adult dose and timing (about 4 mg, 3 h before bed vs common practice).
- Warfarin and epilepsy signals rest on case reports; no controlled interaction studies.
- Product quality (label accuracy, serotonin contamination, child-resistant packaging), and replication of the cancer, COVID and migraine findings.
