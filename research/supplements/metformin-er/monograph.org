** Identity & forms
- Prescription biguanide (metformin hydrochloride) in once-daily extended-release (ER/XR) tablets. It is labeled as an adjunct to diet and exercise for glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes (T2D); longevity use is off-label [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
- ER products: generic ER tablets (500 mg), Glumetza (500/1000 mg), Fortamet, and Glucophage XR (GelShield diffusion system) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=fb832474-88d9-4e29-95cd-fbc446944cc4] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=8cbcee12-3eb1-4726-9212-5dddc39a3b0f] [PMID:15966755].
- Label PK: median Tmax 7 h; peak about 20% lower than IR with comparable AUC; food lowers Cmax about 40% and AUC about 25%. About 90% is renally eliminated (tubular secretion) within 24 h; plasma half-life about 6.2 h, blood half-life about 17.6 h [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
- XR 2000 mg once daily gave AUC equivalent to IR 1000 mg twice daily (Tmax 7 vs 3 h), with no accumulation [PMID:15966755]. ER is one way to moderate early GI effects [PMID:38784991].

** Mechanisms
- Lowers hepatic glucose production and intestinal glucose absorption and raises peripheral glucose uptake, without changing insulin secretion [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
- Molecular actions depend on concentration and tissue: AMPK-dependent and -independent pathways, complex I inhibition at millimolar gut levels, complex IV / mitochondrial glycerophosphate dehydrogenase inhibition in liver, lysosomal AMPK activation at about 10 uM, and a key role for the gut [PMID:28776086] [PMID:41819225].
- Aging hypothesis: effects on nutrient sensing, autophagy, mitochondria and senescence. This is mostly preclinical [PMID:32333835] [PMID:34421827].
- Muscle: raises AMPK signaling and tends to blunt mTORC1 signaling during resistance training [PMID:31557380]. It abolishes the exercise-induced rise in mitochondrial respiration [PMID:30548390] [PMID:40695624].
- B12 malabsorption reflects calcium-dependent ileal uptake of the B12-intrinsic factor complex [PMID:10977010]. Antiviral effect: proposed host mTOR/translation suppression [PMID:38690892].

** Dosing in trials
- Label (ER): 500 mg once daily with the evening meal, up 500 mg weekly to 2000 mg once daily, swallowed whole. IR users can switch at the same total dose [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]. Glumetza titrates every 1-2 weeks to the same maximum [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=fb832474-88d9-4e29-95cd-fbc446944cc4].
- Prevention: DPP/DPPOS used IR 850 mg twice daily [PMID:11832527] [PMID:40311647]. Oncology: MA.32 used 850 mg twice daily for 5 years [PMID:35608580]. Pregnancy: MiTy used 1000 mg twice daily with insulin [PMID:32946820].
- Aging/exercise: MASTERS 1700 mg/d for 14 weeks [PMID:31557380]; MET-PREVENT 500 mg three times daily for 4 months (poorly tolerated) [PMID:40147475]. Weight trials used 500-2550 mg/d [PMID:38468148].
- TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin) was proposed as the first trial of aging as an indication [PMID:27304507]. Its goals have been questioned [PMID:30746605].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Boxed warning, lactic acidosis: postmarketing cases include deaths, hypothermia, hypotension and resistant bradyarrhythmias. Risk factors are renal impairment, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, age 65+, contrast studies, surgery, hypoxic states, excess alcohol and hepatic impairment [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
- Measured lactic acidosis incidence is low: upper limit 4.3 per 100,000 patient-years vs 5.4 on other drugs [PMID:20393934]; about 3-10 per 100,000 person-years overall [PMID:25536258]. In 559 published cases, 97% had independent risk factors [PMID:31372935].
- GI (ER RCTs): diarrhea 10% vs 3% on placebo, nausea/vomiting 7% vs 2%; diarrhea led to stopping in 0.6% [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]. Observational GI rates are lower with XR than IR [PMID:39350158].
- Vitamin B12: about 7% fell to subnormal levels in 29-week trials. The label advises annual blood counts and B12 every 2-3 years [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]. Risk rises with dose and duration [PMID:30615306].
- B12, other data: long-term use (4+ years) carries 67% higher odds of deficiency than non-use [PMID:40850508]. In non-diabetic people (MA.32), B12 fell within 6 months without anemia [PMID:28447237].
- Hypoglycemia: low alone, higher with insulin or secretagogues [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529] [PMID:36129996]. Hypomagnesemia: OR 3.72 in a cohort [PMID:38599521] [PMID:40613016].
- Older adults: hospital admissions 34% vs 8% on placebo in MET-PREVENT [PMID:40147475]. Training adaptations are blunted [PMID:31557380] [PMID:30548390]. In MA.32, grade 3+ non-hematologic toxicity was 21.5% vs 17.5% [PMID:35608580].

** Contraindications
- eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, hypersensitivity, and acute or chronic metabolic acidosis including DKA. Starting at eGFR 30-45 is not recommended. Not recommended in hepatic impairment [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
- A cohort study found excess acidosis only below eGFR 30 [PMID:29868840].
- Iodinated contrast: stop at or before the procedure if eGFR is 30-60, with liver disease, alcoholism or heart failure, or with intra-arterial contrast; recheck eGFR at 48 h [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]. Guidelines conflict and rest on level IV-V evidence [PMID:20032157].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (topiramate, zonisamide, acetazolamide, dichlorphenamide): non-anion-gap acidosis may raise lactic acidosis risk; monitor more often [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
- OCT2/MATE inhibitors (label: ranolazine, vandetanib, dolutegravir, cimetidine) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]:
  - Dolutegravir 50 mg daily raised metformin AUC 79% (145% at 50 mg twice daily) [PMID:26974526]. In obese women with HIV, a 1000 mg cap may underdose [PMID:40468972].
  - Ranolazine 1000 mg twice daily raised AUC 1.79-fold; the authors advise capping metformin at 1700 mg/d [PMID:27128216].
  - Cimetidine raised AUC 24%, mainly via hepatic OCT1 [PMID:40098288]. It is a lactic acidosis signal in renal impairment [PMID:37948503].
- Insulin and sulfonylureas: additive hypoglycemia; the other drug may need a lower dose [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]. Severe hypoglycemia was 2.2% with glimepiride added to metformin [PMID:36129996].
- Alcohol potentiates metformin's effect on lactate; avoid excess [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529].
*** Other supplements
- B12 methylcobalamin: corrects deficiency. Oral B12 1000 ug/d normalized levels in 3 weeks (RCT, n=50; sucrosomial form, industry-linked) [PMID:41754978] [PMID:25502588].
- B Complex: without B vitamins or folic acid, metformin raised homocysteine (MD +2.02 umol/L); with them it fell (MD -0.74) [PMID:27941660]. Calcium reversed the holotranscobalamin fall in a small study [PMID:10977010].
- Berberine: inhibits OCT1/OCT2/MATE1 transport of metformin; exposure rose (IV) or fell (oral) in rats [PMID:25359200] [PMID:30428337]. Added to oral hypoglycemics it improved glycemia in low-quality RCTs [PMID:23118793]. No human PK study was found.
- Acarbose: the combination lowered FBG, 2-h glucose and HbA1c more than either drug alone [PMID:28588669]. HbA1c effects were similar head-to-head [PMID:25961824].
- Rapamycin: metformin reduced rapamycin-induced glucose intolerance in female HET3 mice. Mice on both lived at least as long as on rapamycin alone [PMID:29579736]. No human data.
- Magnesium: hypomagnesemia is associated with use [PMID:38599521] [PMID:40613016]. Heme Iron (Proferrin): no PK interaction known; evaluate anemia, since B12-related anemia is possible [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529] [PMID:40535375].
*** Conditions
- Renal or hepatic impairment, hypoxic states, surgery and contrast: see Contraindications.
- Resistance or aerobic training goals: hypertrophy and aerobic gains are blunted in older adults [PMID:31557380] [PMID:30548390] [PMID:41751275].
- Pregnancy: label data are insufficient to define birth-defect risk, and metformin may restore ovulation [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=7cb47c3e-6657-4c11-8aa2-ae172e5a8529]. MiTy showed excess SGA; offspring outcomes were reassuring at 24 months and 5-11 years [PMID:32946820] [PMID:36746160] [PMID:38876183].
*** Lab-test interference
- FDG-PET/CT: intense diffuse bowel (mainly colonic) uptake, OR 95.3 for colon [PMID:17786437]. A 48-h hold cut large-bowel SUVmax (2.63 vs 5.42) [PMID:30106348]. Distal colon uptake persists with holds under 72 h [PMID:32815120] [PMID:27392947].
- Serum B12 and homocysteine changes are physiological, not assay artifacts [PMID:30615306] [PMID:27941660].

** Genotype notes
- SLC22A1 (OCT1): R61C and 420del did not change HbA1c response in GoDARTS (n=1531) [PMID:19336679]. A SR links rs12208357, rs34059508 and G465R to higher exposure and several SNPs to HbA1c response [PMID:37550919]. OCT1/OCT2 variants moderately changed the cimetidine DDI [PMID:40098288].
- ATM rs11212617: treatment success OR 1.35 in a GWAS [PMID:21186350], replicated (OR 1.25) [PMID:22453232]. SLC2A2 rs8192675 C allele: larger fasting-glucose fall on metformin (2.4 mmol/L) [PMID:30413829]. None is used clinically for dosing.

** Latest research
- [2026-08] Lung-cancer MA (29 observational studies): HR 0.85 (0.77-0.94) in T2D [PMID:42581008].
- [2026-07] DEMFOS RCT (n=114): no added physical-function benefit on top of intensive lifestyle [PMID:42532077].
- [2026-07] 2-year RCT preprint (n=145, prediabetes): frailty index improved and two epigenetic clocks slowed. Fried score was unchanged once weight loss was excluded [PMID:42620010].
- [2026-07] COVID-19 MA (4 RCTs): modest, low-to-moderate certainty benefit on hospitalization, ED visits or death [PMID:42250263].
- [2026-06] SR of 27 cohorts: lower mortality, CVD and dementia; no RCTs [PMID:42310907]. Mechanism review on concentration-dependent actions [PMID:41819225].
- [2026-03] Pregnancy MA + Mendelian randomization: malformations RR 0.83 vs insulin [PMID:41628713].
- [2026-02] B12 RCT in metformin users: rapid normalization [PMID:41754978]. GDM MA: neonatal hypoglycemia OR 0.65 [PMID:41354637].
- [2026-01] Dementia NMA (67 studies): no significant metformin effect [PMID:41126557].
- [2025-12] PCOS MA: continuing through the first trimester raised clinical pregnancy (OR 1.57) [PMID:40473092]. Dolutegravir PK in obese women [PMID:40468972].
- [2025-10] All of Us: long-term use linked to B12 deficiency and neuropathy [PMID:40850508].
- [2025-09] Critical review: "emerging uncertainty" on anti-aging potential [PMID:40582648].
- [2025-08] Metformin suppresses exercise-adaptation transcription (BCL6B) [PMID:40695624]. Cimetidine DDI: AUC +24% [PMID:40098288].
- [2025-06] DPP/DPPOS 21 years: diabetes HR 0.83 [PMID:40311647]. WHI target-trial emulation: HR 0.70 for death before 90 vs sulfonylurea [PMID:40388602].
- [2025-05] WHO-guideline MA: long COVID RR 0.6 (low certainty, single trial) [PMID:40433308].
- [2025-03] MET-PREVENT: no walk-speed benefit; poorly tolerated [PMID:40147475].
- [2024-12] Dementia NMA: observational OR 0.89; RCTs neutral [PMID:39716328].
- [2024-10] Male monkeys (40 months): slowed multi-omic aging clocks [PMID:39270656].

** Open questions
- Whether metformin slows aging in people without diabetes. No completed aging-indication RCT was found; TAME was proposed in 2016 [PMID:27304507]. Cohort benefits may reflect bias [PMID:40582648] [PMID:33709434].
- How to reconcile metabolic benefits with blunted exercise adaptations, and whether timing or dose avoids this [PMID:41751275].
- Whether ER differs from IR in long-term efficacy, B12 loss or exercise effects. No head-to-head aging trials exist.
- Long-term outcomes after in utero exposure, including the SGA signal [PMID:32946820] [PMID:39042587]. Human relevance of berberine-metformin transporter interactions.
