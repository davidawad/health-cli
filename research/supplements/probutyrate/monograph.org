** Identity & forms
- ProButyrate is a Tesseract Medical Research product. It is sold through the Dr Hyman store and practitioner retailers. The matrix technology is licensed to Tesseract from Czap R&D [URL:https://drhyman.com/products/probutyrate] [URL:https://www.professionalsupplementcenter.com/products/probutyrate-by-tesseract-medical-research].
- Label: 2 capsules provide 600 mg "Natural Butyric Acid Proprietary Complex" (60 servings per bottle). The complex is described as free butyric acid held in "CyLoc", a dextrin fiber matrix, compounded with a "DexKey" release agent (patent pending, Al Czap) [URL:https://drhyman.com/products/probutyrate].
  - Other ingredients: hypromellose capsule, high- and low-viscosity hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (sustained release), a plant-derived enzyme and calcium laurate [URL:https://drhyman.com/products/probutyrate].
  - The amount of butyric acid inside the 600 mg complex is not disclosed. Retailer copy describes "strategic SCFAs" (butyric and acetic acid) trapped in the matrix [URL:https://www.professionalsupplementcenter.com/products/probutyrate-by-tesseract-medical-research].
  - Suggested use differs between sellers: 1-3 capsules twice daily on the Dr Hyman page, 2 capsules twice daily on retailer pages [URL:https://drhyman.com/products/probutyrate] [URL:https://www.professionalsupplementcenter.com/products/probutyrate-by-tesseract-medical-research].
- No published human study of ProButyrate itself was found in PubMed. Everything below is extrapolated from other butyrate forms used in trials.
- Butyrate forms in human research:
  - Sodium butyrate, plain or enteric-coated (e.g. 4 g/d pH-coated tablets) [PMID:10795763] [PMID:29799027].
  - Microencapsulated sodium butyrate (MSB, lipid-matrix "colonic delivery"), mostly at 300-600 mg/d [PMID:22738315] [PMID:41422374].
  - Calcium butyrate, including sustained-release forms [PMID:40635319] [PMID:41628665] [PMID:40565024].
  - Tributyrin, a butyrate triglyceride prodrug [PMID:12736763] [PMID:41271518].
  - Butyrylated high-amylose starch, which releases about 57% of its esterified butyrate in the colon [PMID:21940597].
  - Rectal sodium butyrate enemas [PMID:1612357] [PMID:8899080].
- Reviews stress that results depend on the delivery technology, release profile and dose. They advise judging each preparation on its own data [PMID:41238998].

** Mechanisms
- Butyrate is the main energy source for colonocytes. It inhibits NF-kB activation and histone deacetylases (HDAC), reinforces the epithelial barrier and reduces oxidative stress. Most of this comes from models, and human data are limited [PMID:17973645] [PMID:21472114].
- HDAC inhibition: butyrate is an HDAC inhibitor, as is valproic acid [PMID:24502598] [PMID:39276955].
- Colonic delivery problem: oral butyrate is absorbed fast in the upper gut. A pH/HPMC-coated sodium butyrate tablet delayed release by 2-3 h and reached the ileo-caecal region, with variable transit in Crohn's disease [PMID:17373743].
  - Plain oral butyrate (4 g/d) did not raise plasma or faecal butyrate in one study [PMID:29799027]. In another, plasma butyrate rose but faecal butyrate did not [PMID:39034917].
  - A colon-delivery capsule (5.28 g/d) raised serum butyrate without changing faecal SCFAs [PMID:38199487].
  - The liver takes up rectally delivered butyrate, so systemic levels do not rise [PMID:26156796].
- Microbiome: MSB increased SCFA-producing Lachnospiraceae in UC and Butyricicoccus in Crohn's disease [PMID:32476236]. Calcium butyrate increased Lachnospiraceae and lowered Ruminococcus gnavus in children with IBS [PMID:40635319].
- Barrier function: a 90-min colonic butyrate perfusion (100 mmol/L) reduced bile-acid-induced transcellular permeability in IBS biopsies [PMID:40810534]. Trials with 300-570 mg/d report lower plasma zonulin and LBP [PMID:38135194] [PMID:40167641].
- Immune effects: a sustained-release butyrate tablet lowered activated Th cells and the Th17/Treg ratio ex vivo but did not change serum inflammatory markers [PMID:39408351].

** Dosing in trials
- ProButyrate label: 600 mg complex per 2 capsules. The free-butyrate content is undisclosed, so trial doses cannot be matched to it directly [URL:https://drhyman.com/products/probutyrate].
- Microencapsulated sodium butyrate: 300 mg/d (diverticulosis, 12 months) [PMID:24343275]; 2 x 300 mg/d (UC, 8 weeks) [PMID:41422374]; 1.5 g/d (T2D with IBS, 12 weeks) [PMID:41974937].
- Sodium butyrate (plain or enteric-coated): 4 g/d for 4-8 weeks in Crohn's disease, UC, metabolic syndrome and type 1 diabetes [PMID:16225487] [PMID:10795763] [PMID:29799027] [PMID:31915895]. 1875 mg/d for 12 weeks in obesity [PMID:41880673]. 20 mg/kg/d in children [PMID:36469320]. 150 mg twice daily in pediatric IBD [PMID:36014789].
- Calcium butyrate: 500 mg/d in pediatric IBS [PMID:40635319]; 600 mg/d sustained release in hand OA [PMID:41628665]; 1000 mg/d in MASLD [PMID:40565024].
- Tributyrin: 500 mg three times daily in Parkinson's disease [PMID:41271518]. In oncology, 150-200 mg/kg three times daily reached a median plasma butyrate of 52 uM; escalation stopped because of capsule burden [PMID:12736763].
- Enemas: 80-100 mmol/L sodium butyrate [PMID:1612357] [PMID:8899080] [PMID:11072942].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short trials of oral sodium or calcium butyrate report few adverse events:
  - "No untoward side effects" with 4 g/d plus mesalazine [PMID:10795763].
  - None in pediatric IBD [PMID:36014789], diverticulosis [PMID:24343275] or travellers' diarrhoea [PMID:24063909].
  - Mild AEs comparable to placebo with sustained-release calcium butyrate. Two participants stopped, one of them for pre-existing liver impairment [PMID:41628665].
  - Transient mild nausea and headache in 2 of 54 children [PMID:36469320].
- Blood pressure: 4 weeks of oral sodium butyrate raised 24-h daytime BP in hypertensive patients. The authors advised caution [PMID:39034917]. A 6-week T2D trial found raised total and LDL cholesterol and insulin within the butyrate group, though between-group differences were not significant [PMID:35623879].
- Odour and taste: free butyric acid and butyrate salts are described as unpalatable, which drives encapsulation, prodrug (tributyrin) and matrix formulations [PMID:41974937] [PMID:23140283] [PMID:41238998]. The manufacturer claims its CyLoc matrix reduces odour and taste. This is unverified [URL:https://www.professionalsupplementcenter.com/products/probutyrate-by-tesseract-medical-research].
- Sodium load: sodium butyrate supplies sodium, and the hypertension trial used a sodium chloride placebo [PMID:39034917]. ProButyrate is labelled as free butyric acid, not a sodium salt [URL:https://drhyman.com/products/probutyrate].
- Tributyrin was well tolerated at high doses without dose-limiting toxicity [PMID:12736763]. A 30-day study in Parkinson's disease reported a "reassuring safety profile" [PMID:41271518].
- In peanut OIT, GI adverse events were common (73%) in both arms, with no difference between butyrate and placebo [PMID:40804708].
- Pregnancy: no human data. HDAC inhibitors including sodium butyrate and valproic acid induce congenital malformations in experimental embryo studies [PMID:24502598]. The label advises consulting a practitioner if pregnant or nursing [URL:https://www.professionalsupplementcenter.com/products/probutyrate-by-tesseract-medical-research].

** Contraindications
- No formal contraindications have been established.
- Uncontrolled hypertension: use with caution because of the BP rise in an RCT [PMID:39034917].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding: avoid unless advised by a clinician. There are no human data, and HDAC inhibitors including sodium butyrate caused malformations in experimental embryo studies [PMID:24502598] [URL:https://www.professionalsupplementcenter.com/products/probutyrate-by-tesseract-medical-research].
- Children: pediatric trials used sodium or calcium butyrate under supervision [PMID:36469320] [PMID:40635319]. They did not use ProButyrate.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Mesalazine (5-ASA): 4 g/d oral butyrate plus mesalazine gave greater improvement from baseline than mesalazine alone, though the between-arm difference was not significant [PMID:10795763]. MSB as add-on to standard UC therapy improved remission rates [PMID:41422374]. Possible additive benefit.
- Antihypertensives: oral butyrate raised BP in hypertensive patients (medication was withdrawn during that trial) [PMID:39034917]. Monitor BP. A systematic review found direct butyrate effects on BP inconsistent [PMID:42775036].
- Antidiabetic drugs (metformin, insulin, others): sodium butyrate improved CGM time-in-tight-range and HbA1c in some T2D trials [PMID:41880673] [PMID:41974937]. Additive glucose lowering is possible but small.
- Metformin: metformin itself enriches microbial butanoate-metabolism pathways [PMID:38871078]. No combination study exists.
- Acarbose: raises faecal butyrate (22-28 vs 18-19 mol/100 mol) [PMID:9164992] and serum butyrate (4.2 vs 2.6 uM) [PMID:10961161]. This is additive butyrate exposure; no harm is known.
- Valproate and other HDAC-inhibitor drugs (e.g. vorinostat): shared HDAC inhibition [PMID:24502598] [PMID:39276955]. Additive effects are theoretical. No clinical interaction study was found.
- Levodopa: in a Parkinson's RCT, SCFA (propionate + butyrate) supplementation coincided with lower levodopa requirements [PMID:41350593]. Clinicians may need to review dosing.
- Antibiotics: these deplete commensal and butyrate-producing bacteria. Butyrate supplementation is proposed as protection against antibiotic dysbiosis, but this is from a review only [PMID:33198506].
*** Other supplements
- Tributryn (tributyrin): the same active moiety, so stacking duplicates butyrate exposure [PMID:12736763] [PMID:41271518].
- inulin: butyrogenic fibre. Butyrate plus inulin lowered fasting glucose and waist-to-hip ratio within the group, and raised GLP-1 vs placebo, in T2D [PMID:28962046]. Combined use increased SOD and miR-146a [PMID:32315958]. Fibre interventions raise faecal butyrate (SMD 0.24) [PMID:29757343].
- GOS: bifidogenic, but in one crossover RCT (16 g/d) it lowered butyrate-producing Ruminococcus and raised fasting glucose [PMID:28924143]. A GOS-containing fibre blend increased butyrate producers in prediabetes [PMID:39064648]. Effects on butyrate are inconsistent.
- Vitamin B2: 50-100 mg/d riboflavin increased faecal butyrate production in an RCT [PMID:35943883].
- Berberine: RCTs show depletion of butyrate-producing taxa alongside cardiometabolic improvements [PMID:42356246]. This may oppose endogenous butyrate production. Clinical significance is unknown.
- EPA/DHA/DPA: fish oil plus soluble fibre reduced Roseburia butyrate-kinase genes without changing faecal butyrate [PMID:40754387].
*** Conditions
- Hypertension: see Contraindications [PMID:39034917].
- IBD: use only as an adjunct to standard therapy. Pediatric data are negative [PMID:36014789] [PMID:42646603].
- IBS: low butyrate is reported in IBS-C (SMD -0.53), but pooled butyrate does not differ from controls overall [PMID:30762787] [PMID:33407171].
- Liver impairment: the liver clears absorbed butyrate [PMID:26156796]. A participant with pre-existing liver impairment stopped sustained-release calcium butyrate [PMID:41628665].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact is known. True physiological changes have been reported:
  - Faecal calprotectin and hs-CRP fall in UC and IBD [PMID:39003477] [PMID:41422374] [PMID:41354580].
  - Fewer positive lactulose hydrogen breath tests [PMID:41974937].
  - Plasma and faecal SCFA profiles shift, with faecal butyrate sometimes unchanged [PMID:31915895] [PMID:29799027] [PMID:39034917].
  - Plasma zonulin and LBP fall [PMID:38135194].
- Stool SCFA "microbiome" tests taken during supplementation may therefore not reflect endogenous production.

** Open questions
- How much free butyric acid ProButyrate delivers, and where in the gut it is released. No dissolution, pharmacokinetic or clinical data were found for the CyLoc/DexKey matrix.
- Whether low-dose (300-600 mg/d) oral butyrate reaches the colon in amounts that matter, given rapid upper-gut absorption and hepatic clearance [PMID:17373743] [PMID:26156796].
- The BP rise with oral sodium butyrate needs replication, and it is unclear whether it applies to non-sodium forms [PMID:39034917].
- Many positive RCTs are small, single-centre or from a single research group. Independent replication with hard endpoints is needed (IBS, obesity, sarcopenia).
- Long-term (>12 months) safety, and use in pregnancy, are unknown.
- Whether fibre-driven endogenous butyrate (inulin and other fibres) is preferable to direct supplementation [PMID:29757343] [PMID:40557239].
