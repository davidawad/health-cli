** Identity & forms
- Quatrefolic is a branded glucosamine salt of the biologically active folate form (6S)-5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF). Other supplemental folate forms include folic acid and calcium L-methylfolate. [PMID:25914100]

** Mechanisms
- 5-MTHF donates a methyl group for methionine synthase-mediated homocysteine remethylation; folate coenzymes support nucleotide synthesis and one-carbon metabolism. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Folate status / homocysteine | RCTs and systematic reviews | improves biomarkers | 5-MTHF raises folate; comparative equivalence to folic acid for status is supported | moderate | PMID:25914100 |
| Neural tube defect prevention | Population trials / guideline evidence for folic acid | benefit for folic acid; 5-MTHF outcome evidence indirect | Folic acid 400 µg/day periconceptionally recommended; not proven interchangeable for NTD outcome | high | URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/ |
| Cardiovascular events via homocysteine lowering | Large RCTs/meta-analysis | no consistent benefit | Biomarker reduction does not reliably lower events | moderate | PMID:2409100 |
| MTHFR genotype-specific outcomes | Clinical evidence synthesis | uncertain | No robust genotype-tailored outcome effect demonstrated | low | URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/ |

** Dosing in trials
- Supplement and trial doses range from 400 µg to 15 mg/day depending on indication; compare folate equivalents and formulation. Standard periconceptional recommendations specify 400 µg/day folic acid, not necessarily branded 5-MTHF. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Safety & adverse effects
- High folate can correct megaloblastic anemia while B12 deficiency neurologic injury progresses; evaluate B12 when clinically relevant. UL of 1,000 µg/day applies to synthetic folic acid from supplements/fortified foods, not naturally occurring food folate. [URL:https://ods.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Contraindications
- Cancer treatment with methotrexate is a special context: folate can interfere with antifolate therapy, although prescribed folinic/folic acid is sometimes intentionally co-administered in other settings. Antiepileptic medicines may interact with folate status. [URL:https://ods.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- Methotrexate: interaction depends on indication and timing; oncology and rheumatology protocols differ. Phenytoin/carbamazepine/valproate can lower folate; folate can affect phenytoin levels. Check B12 status before prolonged high-dose folate. [URL:https://ods.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Genotype notes
- MTHFR C677T can reduce folate availability; NIH ODS says outcome evidence supporting genotype-specific supplementation with 5-MTHF remains inconclusive. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- NIH ODS current fact sheet reports MTHFR C677T may reduce available 5-MTHF but evidence that 5-MTHF supplementation improves outcomes in carriers remains inconclusive. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/]

** Open questions
- Direct trials of Quatrefolic on clinical outcomes are sparse; clarify bioavailability and equivalence across salts, doses, and pregnancy outcomes.
