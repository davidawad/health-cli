** Identity & forms
- Quatrefolic is the glucosamine salt of (6S)-5-methyltetrahydrofolic acid (5-MTHF), the main circulating folate form [PMID:27008238] [PMID:20608755]. Metafolin is the crystalline calcium salt (L-5-MTHF-Ca) [PMID:20608755]. A sodium salt also exists [PMID:41161371].
- Folic acid is a synthetic, oxidized form that has no coenzyme activity until dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) reduces it. 5-MTHF needs no reduction [PMID:20608755] [PMID:24494987].
- Glucosamine salt vs calcium salt: In a manufacturer-run rat PK study (n=6/group), Quatrefolic gave a 1.8x higher Cmax than the calcium salt and a 3.1x higher Cmax than folic acid. AUC(0-8h) was only 1.12x that of the calcium salt [PMID:27008238]. A second rat study found a new calcium crystal form ("form C") more stable than crystal form I or the amorphous glucosamine salt, with 218% relative bioavailability vs the glucosamine salt [PMID:34641555]. Neither study involved humans. EFSA (2022) judged that the cation's effect on bioavailability is "within the margin of error". It applies the same dietary folate equivalent (DFE) factors to both salts: 1.7 below 400 ug/day and 2.0 at or above 400 ug/day [PMID:36034319].
- EU upper limit: EFSA's 2023 adult UL of 1000 ug/day covers combined folic acid plus both 5-MTHF salts [PMID:37965303].
- Prescription "medical food": US L-methylfolate calcium 7.5 mg and 15 mg tablets (the Deplin-type products) are dispensed by prescription. The labeled use is adjunctive dietary management in major depressive disorder and schizophrenia. Usual adult dose is 7.5-15 mg/day [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
- Levomefolate calcium is also added to some ethinylestradiol/drospirenone oral contraceptives. In a 24-week RCT it raised RBC folate from 990 to 1406 nmol/L [PMID:22067790].

** Mechanisms
- 5-MTHF gives up its methyl group to homocysteine through B12-dependent methionine synthase. This regenerates tetrahydrofolate and methionine/SAM [PMID:3927946] [PMID:30228213].
- Without B12, folate builds up as 5-MTHF (the "methyl trap"). This explains the folate-B12 interdependence [PMID:3927946].
- MTHFR (an FAD-dependent enzyme) produces 5-MTHF. Supplemental 5-MTHF enters the cycle after this step, so it bypasses both MTHFR and DHFR [PMID:20608755] [PMID:24494987].
- Riboflavin raises SAM and cystathionine specifically in MTHFR 677TT individuals [PMID:32330571].
- Because 5-MTHF needs no DHFR reduction, reviews propose less interaction with DHFR-inhibiting drugs [PMID:20608755] [PMID:24126794].
- Reviews also propose less masking of B12-deficiency anemia, since 5-MTHF cannot enter the folate cycle without B12 [PMID:20608755] [PMID:14988450]. This is a theoretical argument, not tested in outcome trials.
- Folic acid at high intakes appears in plasma as unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA). Folic acid allocation in trials quadrupled plasma folic acid [PMID:23352552]. Replacing folic acid with 5-MTHF lowers UMFA [PMID:42739064] [PMID:41971363].

** Dosing in trials
- Bioavailability/status trials: 113-416 ug/day of 5-MTHF, or equimolar to 100-400 ug folic acid [PMID:12600857] [PMID:16825690]. RBC folate half-life is about 8 weeks, so steady state takes about 40 weeks [PMID:17991654].
- Pregnancy: 1100 ug 5-MTHF glucosamine salt is equimolar to 600 ug folic acid (1.35 umol) [PMID:42716224]. 1000 ug DFE 5-MTHF in a prenatal multivitamin was compared against 1330 ug DFE folic acid [PMID:41971363]. A 400 or 800 ug 1:1 5-MTHF/folic acid mix has also been tested [PMID:39466653].
- Depression: 15 mg/day L-methylfolate (7.5 mg/day was ineffective) [PMID:23212058] [PMID:24813065]. 800 ug/day was used in a small open-label trial [PMID:39844190].
- Schizophrenia: 15 mg/day for 12 weeks [PMID:28289280].
- Hemodialysis/resistant hypertension: 800 ug methylfolate plus 1000 ug methylcobalamin for 3 months [PMID:41580678].
- NTD prevention guidelines specify folic acid, not 5-MTHF: 0.4-0.8 mg/day [PMID:37526713], or at least 0.4 mg/day in people on antiseizure drugs [PMID:38748979].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short-term trials show tolerability similar to placebo. With 15 mg/day, adverse events did not differ from placebo [PMID:23212058]. Most nutraceuticals in a depression NMA were tolerated like placebo [PMID:40314175].
- Allergic reactions are the only adverse reaction listed on the L-methylfolate label [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
- Preclinical toxicology: Calcium L-methylfolate: rat NOAEL 400 mg/kg bw/day; not teratogenic [PMID:31673504]. Sodium salt: no adverse findings in a 90-day study [PMID:41161371].
- Mood elevation: the label warns that mood elevation is possible in bipolar-risk patients. An open bipolar depression registry (n=10) reported no YMRS rise [PMID:27794238].
- B12 deficiency masking: high folate can correct the anemia of B12 deficiency while neurological damage progresses [PMID:38987872] [PMID:7796091]. In older adults with low B12, high serum folate was associated with anemia (OR 3.1) and cognitive impairment (OR 2.6) [PMID:17209196], and with higher MMA/homocysteine [PMID:19726595]. Higher serum 5-MTHF was also associated with anemia in low-B12 seniors [PMID:20357042]. So the claim that 5-MTHF does not mask B12 deficiency [PMID:23482308] is not established.
- High-dose folic acid and cancer (observational, folic acid only): High maternal folic acid (>=1 mg) in mothers with epilepsy: childhood cancer HR 2.7 (1.2-6.3) [PMID:36156660]. Women who had given birth, using >1 mg/day: cancer HR 1.1 after a 6-month lag; non-Hodgkin lymphoma HR 2.0 [PMID:39540679]. Umbrella review: credibility is "highly suggestive" for some folate-cancer associations, but evidence is mostly weak [PMID:41718012]. Pre-fortification cohort: high total folate intake was associated with lower colorectal cancer mortality [PMID:40455102].
- UMFA: associated with lower NK-cell cytotoxicity (cross-sectional) [PMID:16365081]. It was not associated with colorectal cancer overall [PMID:26376686] [PMID:28600398]. A 2026 systematic review found no evidence that UMFA independently causes harm [PMID:42739064].

** Contraindications
- Cancer chemotherapy with methotrexate or capecitabine/5-FU, unless prescribed by the oncology team. Labels instruct patients to avoid folic acid or folate-analog products [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=aafd97c1-3a0a-42b2-845a-a9db9bb7d876] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0b59e98c-5b34-4b93-90f6-20526f4d4f88].
- Untreated or unevaluated B12 deficiency, including pernicious anemia: folate alone is inadequate and may delay diagnosis [PMID:38987872] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
- Hypersensitivity to any component [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
- Not a substitute for folic acid in NTD prevention. Guideline recommendations and outcome RCTs exist only for folic acid [PMID:37526713] [PMID:39339754] [PMID:41277701].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Methotrexate: In cancer, folate products may reduce methotrexate's efficacy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=aafd97c1-3a0a-42b2-845a-a9db9bb7d876]. In RA, psoriasis and pJIA, the label directs folic or folinic acid supplementation. A Cochrane review found it reduced liver-enzyme elevation (RR 0.23) and withdrawals (RR 0.39) without loss of efficacy [PMID:23728635]. No trial has tested 5-MTHF specifically.
- Capecitabine/5-FU: folates raise fluorouracil toxicity. In a CRC cohort, folic acid-containing supplements gave toxicity HR 1.81 and plasma folic acid HR 2.09-2.31 [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0b59e98c-5b34-4b93-90f6-20526f4d4f88] [PMID:38070682].
- Phenytoin: the drug lowers folate, and adding folate can lower phenytoin levels and change its kinetics. Starting both together is advised [PMID:1628589] [PMID:8520091] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
- Carbamazepine and phenobarbital: enzyme inducers lower serum folate. Valproate did not lower it [PMID:9073038]. Folate deficiency is common in women with epilepsy [PMID:41066307].
- Valproate: Guidelines advise avoiding valproate in people who could become pregnant. They also advise at least 0.4 mg/day folic acid with any antiseizure drug [PMID:38748979] [PMID:19398680]. A meta-analysis found no reduction in major malformations with periconceptional folic acid in women with epilepsy (OR 1.34) [PMID:39326214].
- Sulfasalazine inhibits folate absorption [PMID:6117796]. Trimethoprim 400 mg/day for 7 days lowered serum folate by 1.95 nmol/L vs placebo [PMID:26566149].
- Pyrimethamine: an antifolate that affects folate metabolism [PMID:5800521]. Supplementary folate at NTD-prevention doses did not reduce sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine efficacy, and 5-MTHF is proposed as non-antagonizing [PMID:24126794].
- Metformin: long-term use lowers B12, which raises homocysteine [PMID:20488910]. The L-methylfolate label also lists decreased serum folate with metformin [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
- SSRIs and other antidepressants: used as an intended adjunct (see evidence table) [PMID:34794190].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin B12 / B12 methylcobalamin: methionine synthase needs B12, so folate without B12 risks masking and the methyl trap [PMID:3927946] [PMID:38987872]. Combined methylfolate plus methylcobalamin lowered homocysteine and BP in hemodialysis patients [PMID:41580678].
- Vitamin B6 / B Complex: in an NMA, combinations near 800 ug folic acid with B6/B12 lowered homocysteine most [PMID:39960689]. In an RCT, methylfolate + P5P + methylcobalamin lowered homocysteine by 11.8% in homozygous carriers [PMID:38892484].
- Trimethylglycine (TMG/betaine): lowers homocysteine through the folate-independent BHMT route (-1.23 umol/L at >=4 g/day) [PMID:23997720]. Adding it to low-dose B vitamins lowered homocysteine further [PMID:36717385].
- Vitamin B2 (riboflavin): an MTHFR cofactor. It modifies one-carbon metabolites and BP in MTHFR 677TT individuals [PMID:32330571] [PMID:33172445].
*** Conditions
- B12 deficiency, pregnancy planning, epilepsy, active cancer chemotherapy: see above.
- History of colorectal adenoma: 1 mg/day folic acid increased advanced and multiple adenomas (RR 1.67) and SSA/P on extended use [PMID:17551129] [PMID:31401653]. Pooled trials were null [PMID:21170989].
- Bipolar disorder: label caution for mood elevation [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676].
*** Lab-test interference
- No direct assay artifact from 5-MTHF was found. Supplementation causes true rises in serum and RBC folate. Serum folate reflects recent intake, while RBC folate reflects about 4 months [PMID:30228213]. Labs differ: CLIA and microbiological assays give non-interchangeable values [PMID:23007069].
- High folate results should prompt B12 assessment [PMID:30228213].
- 5-MTHF degrades in stored samples and products, a known confounder in trials [PMID:41544303].

** Genotype notes
- MTHFR C677T: TT carriers show a smaller serum folate response to folic acid (-7.2 nmol/L vs CT/CC) with similar homocysteine change (MA) [PMID:26497154]. RBC folate rises with 400 ug in all genotypes [PMID:23456769]. The TT-related homocysteine excess disappears once plasma folate reaches about 15 ng/mL [PMID:29371246]. TT carriers respond more to higher folic acid doses [PMID:22230384]. Low-dose 5-MTHF lowered homocysteine as well as folic acid in both 677CC and TT subjects [PMID:12486865]. In recurrent miscarriage, 5-MTHF vs folic acid showed no benefit by C677T/A1298C genotype [PMID:26630680]. Folinic acid outperformed L-methylfolate for homocysteine in 677CT individuals [PMID:38056998]. ACMG advises against routine MTHFR polymorphism testing [PMID:23288205]. The L-methylfolate label states use regardless of C677T genotype [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13eb8bf0-5227-455c-9660-b02abee04676]. CSPPT stratified randomization by C677T genotype [PMID:25771069]. Riboflavin modifies BP risk in TT carriers [PMID:33172445].
- Depression pharmacogenomics: Folate-pathway markers may predict response to 15 mg L-methylfolate (exploratory) [PMID:24813065]. A 2026 SR links MTHFR variants to treatment-resistant depression, from inconsistent studies [PMID:41737528]. A retrospective genotype-guided cohort is hypothesis-generating only [PMID:42738962].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Prospective cohort with Mendelian randomization: RBC 5-MTHF >700 nmol/L in early pregnancy was associated with 18% fewer birth defects [PMID:42673811].
- [2026-09] Pregnancy RCT (n=110) of 5-MTHF glucosamine salt vs equimolar folic acid: RBC folate and homocysteine were similar; plasma 5-MTHF rose more with borderline significance [PMID:42716224].
- [2026-09] UMFA systematic review: 5-MTHF lowers UMFA (GRADE low); no equivalence claim is possible; no proof of UMFA harm [PMID:42739064].
- [2026-09] Clinical review: folic acid remains the only form with NTD outcome RCTs; 5-MTHF is suggested for high-risk cases with B12 [PMID:41277701].
- [2026-08] Retrospective cohort of MTHFR-guided nutraceuticals in mental health: hypothesis-generating [PMID:42738962].
- [2026-03] Prenatal multivitamin RCT (n=80): 5-MTHF vs folic acid gave equal folate status and less maternal/placental UMFA [PMID:41971363].
- [2026-02] Folate-cancer umbrella review (67 syntheses): highly suggestive associations for colorectal and oesophageal cancer [PMID:41718012].
- [2026-02] Recurrent pregnancy loss feasibility RCT: UMFA fell with 5-MTHF; 5-MTHF degraded in stored product [PMID:41544303].
- [2026-01] Hemodialysis RCT: methylfolate + methylcobalamin lowered homocysteine and BP [PMID:41580678].
- [2026-01] Sodium L-methylfolate preclinical safety: no adverse findings [PMID:41161371].
- [2026-01] SR of MTHFR variants and treatment-resistant depression [PMID:41737528].
- [2025-12] SR/MA (11 RCTs): active folate gave higher plasma/RBC folate and lower UMFA than folic acid; homocysteine did not differ [PMID:41398893].
- [2025-11] Scoping review: folate deficiency is common in women with epilepsy [PMID:41066307].
- [2025-07] Homocysteine NMA in healthy adults: folic acid combinations near 800 ug were most effective [PMID:39960689].
- [2025-05] Depression NMA (192 trials): folate + antidepressant SMD 0.64 [PMID:40314175].
- [2025-01] Nordic register study: high-dose folic acid linked to maternal cancer (non-Hodgkin lymphoma HR 2.0) [PMID:39540679].
- [2025-01] Open-label RCT: 800 ug methylfolate added to antidepressants improved PHQ-9 at day 28 [PMID:39844190].
- [2024-12] Epilepsy SR: periconceptional folic acid did not reduce malformations [PMID:39326214].
- [2024-11] 400 vs 800 ug 5-MTHF/folic acid mix: higher serum 5-MTHF at 800 ug without more detectable folic acid [PMID:39466653].
- [2024-10] Pregnancy RCT: removing folic acid after 12 weeks lowered UMFA [PMID:38783413].

** Open questions
- No RCT has tested 5-MTHF (either salt) for NTD prevention. Equivalence to folic acid rests on biomarkers [PMID:39339754] [PMID:42739064].
- Human head-to-head comparisons of the glucosamine and calcium salts are absent. The only data are rodent PK studies [PMID:27008238] [PMID:34641555].
- Whether 5-MTHF truly avoids masking B12 deficiency in humans [PMID:20357042].
- Long-term safety of 15 mg/day and the cancer risk of high-dose reduced folates; clinical meaning of UMFA [PMID:42739064]; stability of 5-MTHF in products [PMID:41544303].
