** Identity & forms
- Quercetin is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: quercetin dihydrate, isoquercetin, rutin (related glycoside). Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:42827146, PMID:41227106].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:42827146, PMID:41227106].

- A 2026 systematic review of pharmacokinetic interactions found clinically relevant human data fragmented and interaction risks uncertain, with much evidence preclinical [PMID:42827146].
- Human trials have evaluated inflammatory, cardiometabolic, allergy and exercise endpoints, but clinical effects remain inconsistent and formulation/bioavailability vary; certainty low to very low [PMID:42827146].

** Dosing in trials
- Human supplementation studies commonly use roughly 500–1,000 mg/day for several weeks; no dose-response or optimal formulation is established [PMID:42827146].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short-term studies commonly report mild GI symptoms or headache; long-term high-dose safety is inadequately characterized [PMID:42827146].

** Contraindications
- Avoid assuming food exposure and concentrated supplements are equivalent; limited safety evidence in pregnancy, lactation, and significant renal/hepatic disease [PMID:42827146].

** Interactions
- In vitro enzyme/transporter modulation raises possible interaction with CYP3A4/CYP2C9 substrates and transporters, but clinical relevance is uncertain [PMID:42827146]. No validated lab-test interference identified in reviewed clinical sources.
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2026 systematic review focused on CYP enzymes, phase II metabolism, transporters and clinical interactions; it concludes more human PK work is needed [PMID:42827146].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
