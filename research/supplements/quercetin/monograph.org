** Identity & forms
- Quercetin (3,3',4',5,7-pentahydroxyflavone) is a dietary flavonol found in onions, apples, tea and berries [PMID:26785820] [PMID:9816216]. Supplements mostly contain the free aglycone, often at doses up to 1000 mg/d, which is far above usual dietary intake [PMID:29127724].
- Oral bioavailability of the aglycone is poor, and absorbed quercetin circulates mainly as methyl, glucuronide and sulfate conjugates [PMID:31557798] [PMID:28135601].
- A 2025 SR/MA of 31 human bioavailability studies ranked the forms [PMID:40037045]:
  - Quercetin-3-O-oligoglucosides were about 2x more bioavailable than quercetin-3-O-glucoside (isoquercetin), about 10x more than rutin (quercetin-3-O-rutinoside) and about 20x more than the aglycone.
  - Relative to the aglycone, a quercetin-3-O-glucoside-gamma-cyclodextrin complex gave 10.8x exposure, a fenugreek galactomannan/lecithin self-emulsifying form 62x, and a lecithin phytosome 20.1x. Taking it with dietary fat or fibre roughly doubled exposure.
- Quercetin Phytosome (food-grade lecithin): in a 12-subject crossover PK study, plasma levels were up to 20x higher than with unformulated quercetin, with no notable side effects [PMID:30328058]. A 200 mg/d phytosome dose was used in an allergy RCT [PMID:35776034] [PMID:40362785].
- Isoquercetin (isoquercitrin, quercetin-3-O-glucoside): used at 500-1000 mg/d in antithrombotic phase II trials [PMID:30652973] [PMID:38157227]. A 150-300 mg quercetin-4'-O-glucoside dose gave plasma peaks of 4.7-9.7 uM at 30 min [PMID:15613018].
- EMIQ (enzymatically modified isoquercitrin; alpha-oligoglucosyl isoquercitrin) is made enzymatically from rutin, has much higher bioavailability, and is used as a food additive and supplement ingredient [PMID:36499113]. One EMIQ preparation contained 1-12 glucose units [PMID:38185856].
- Results from one form or dose cannot be assumed to apply to another, because systemic exposure differs widely between forms [PMID:40037045] [PMID:42827146].

** Mechanisms
- Senolytic activity: in transcriptomic and siRNA work, quercetin killed senescent human endothelial cells and mouse BM-MSCs, while dasatinib killed senescent preadipocytes. The two together (D+Q) cleared senescent cells in aged, irradiated and progeroid mice [PMID:25754370].
- Xanthine oxidoreductase (XOR) inhibition: quercetin, quercetin-3'-sulfate and the microbial metabolite 3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid inhibit XOR. This is the proposed mechanism for lower plasma uric acid in humans [PMID:33091574] [PMID:26785820]. In vivo, quercetin 500 mg/d raised a caffeine-derived XO activity index by 15% [PMID:19215233].
- Antiplatelet and antithrombotic effects:
  - Oral quercetin-4'-O-glucoside inhibited collagen-induced platelet aggregation and GPVI signalling (Syk, PLCgamma2) in humans [PMID:15613018].
  - The methylated metabolites isorhamnetin and tamarixetin have similar potency in vitro [PMID:31367693].
  - Isoquercetin inhibits extracellular protein disulfide isomerase (PDI) [PMID:30652973].
- Vascular effects: BP reductions in RCT meta-analyses [PMID:27405810] [PMID:31940027]. In a CABG RCT, endothelium-dependent relaxation improved in men, with reversal of vascular senescence transcriptomic signatures [PMID:40375481]. The BP fall in stage 1 hypertension occurred without changes in systemic oxidative-stress markers [PMID:17951477].
- Drug-metabolism and transporter modulation [PMID:42827146]:
  - CYP1A2 inhibition [PMID:19215233] [PMID:25025048].
  - CYP2C9 inhibition (diclofenac) [PMID:29168292].
  - CYP3A induction after repeated dosing [PMID:21680781] [PMID:25988261].
  - Variable P-gp effects: inhibition with fexofenadine [PMID:19221726], induction with talinolol [PMID:23422925].
  - OATP1B1/1B3 inhibition [PMID:22114872] [PMID:22394605].
- Thyroid: in vitro and in rats, quercetin lowers iodide uptake and NIS, TSH-receptor, TPO and thyroglobulin expression. It also inhibits type 1 5'-deiodinase [PMID:24447974] [PMID:39456456].
- Iron chelation: quercetin binds iron and lowers duodenal ferroportin in rats and Caco-2 cells [PMID:25058155].
- Antibacterial: binds the ATP pocket of E. coli gyrase B and inhibits supercoiling [PMID:12804597]. It competes only weakly with norfloxacin at the gyrase-DNA complex [PMID:8718602].

** Dosing in trials
- BP and cardiometabolic: 500-1000 mg/d aglycone for 4-12 weeks. BP effects were seen at >=500 mg/d [PMID:27405810], and 730 mg/d was used in stage 1 hypertension [PMID:17951477]. A review proposes 500 mg aglycone as the BP and anti-inflammatory dose [PMID:31557798].
- Uric acid: 500 mg/d for 4 weeks [PMID:26785820].
- Exercise: median 1000 mg/d [PMID:21606866] [PMID:37398956]. Quercetin glycosides at 200 mg/d were used in older adults [PMID:40063125].
- Phytosome: 200 mg/d [PMID:35776034]. Isoquercetin: 500-1000 mg/d for 56 days [PMID:30652973].
- Senolytic (D+Q), intermittent "hit-and-run" dosing with dasatinib 100 mg (a prescription kinase inhibitor):
  - 1000 mg/d quercetin for 3 days [PMID:31542391].
  - 1250 mg/d, 3 days/week for 3 weeks [PMID:30616998] [PMID:36857968].
  - 1000 mg/d, 3 days/week, in 7-week cycles [PMID:42823536].
- IV (investigational only): a phase I bolus trial escalated 60-1700 mg/m2. Dose-limiting nephrotoxicity occurred at 1700 mg/m2, and 1400 mg/m2 was recommended [PMID:9816216].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Oral supplements: adverse effects in human studies are rare and mild. Adequate safety data for long-term (>12 weeks) use of >=1000 mg/d are lacking [PMID:29127724].
- Possible risk groups identified from animal data: quercetin may worsen nephrotoxicity in an already damaged kidney and may promote estrogen-dependent tumors [PMID:29127724].
- Renal toxicity at IV doses: in the phase I bolus trial, nephrotoxicity was dose-limiting at 1700 mg/m2. At 945 mg/m2, 3 of 14 patients had clinically significant renal toxicity. Weekly dosing caused a 19% (+/- 8%) GFR fall within 24 h but no cumulative impairment [PMID:9816216].
- Thyroid: quercetin acts as a thyroid disruptor in vitro and in rats (lower radioiodine uptake after 14 days). Reviewers advise caution with high doses [PMID:24447974] [PMID:39456456]. No human thyroid-function data were found.
- Bleeding/haemostasis: isoquercetin lowered coagulation markers without major haemorrhage in cancer patients [PMID:30652973] and caused no off-target bleeding in sickle cell disease [PMID:38157227].
- Sex-specific signal: in a CABG RCT, quercetin had a pro-inflammatory effect on circulating proteins in women but benefits in men [PMID:40375481].
- D+Q regimens (adverse events partly reflect dasatinib): the IPF RCT had more non-serious AEs (65 vs 22), with sleep disturbance and anxiety in 4/6 vs 0/6 [PMID:36857968]. The MASH RCT had AEs in 82% vs 43%, all self-limiting [PMID:42823536]. The open-label IPF study saw respiratory, skin and GI events [PMID:30616998], and the bone RCT had no serious AEs [PMID:38956196]. A 2026 SR rates senolytics as investigational, with long-term safety uncharacterized [PMID:42834340].

** Contraindications
- Pre-existing kidney disease, or concurrent nephrotoxic exposure: there is an animal signal for potentiated nephrotoxicity [PMID:29127724], and IV doses were nephrotoxic [PMID:9816216].
- Estrogen-dependent cancer: a theoretical tumor-promotion signal from animal studies [PMID:29127724].
- Narrow-therapeutic-index drugs that are P-gp, CYP3A, CYP2C9 or OATP substrates (cyclosporine, digoxin, warfarin): see Interactions [PMID:21466223] [PMID:14697403] [PMID:36311308].
- Pregnancy and lactation: no safety data found in the retrieved sources. Long-term high-dose safety is not established [PMID:29127724].
- D+Q is not self-administered supplement use. Dasatinib is a prescription drug, and D+Q trials are investigational [PMID:42834340].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Cyclosporine: in rats, quercetin cut cyclosporine (Neoral) Cmax by 67.8% and AUC by 43.3% via P-gp/CYP3A4 activation. The authors advise transplant patients to avoid it because of rejection risk [PMID:21466223].
- Digoxin: in pigs, quercetin raised digoxin Cmax by 413% and AUC by 170%, and 2 of 3 pigs at 50 mg/kg died [PMID:14697403]. No human data were found, but P-gp inhibition is shown in humans [PMID:19221726].
- Fexofenadine (P-gp probe): quercetin 500 mg three times daily for 7 days raised AUC 55% and Cmax 68% in healthy volunteers [PMID:19221726].
- Talinolol (P-gp probe): the effect ran in the opposite direction. Short-term quercetin lowered talinolol AUC by 24-61% in 5 of 10 subjects, though the mean change was not significant [PMID:24472704]. 500 mg/d for 13 days lowered talinolol AUC significantly, most in ABCB1 3435 TT carriers [PMID:23422925].
- Pravastatin and OATP1B1 substrates: quercetin 500 mg/d for 14 days raised pravastatin AUC 24% (CI 15-32%) and Cmax 31% [PMID:22114872]. Quercetin inhibits OATP1B1/1B3 in vitro (Ki about 8 uM) [PMID:22394605].
- CYP2C9 substrates: 500 mg twice daily for 10 days raised diclofenac Cmax and AUC (GMR outside 0.80-1.25) and lowered 4-OH-diclofenac [PMID:29168292].
- CYP3A substrates (midazolam): 500 mg/d for 13 days lowered oral midazolam AUC (CYP3A induction), more in CYP3A5*1 carriers [PMID:21680781]. 1500 mg/d for 1 week gave AUC GMR 0.82 (90% CI 0.61-1.10) [PMID:25988261].
- Warfarin: a 2022 case report is titled "Enhanced Anticoagulant Effect of Warfarin When Co-administered With Quercetin" (no abstract available) [PMID:36311308]. Quercetin metabolites strongly displace warfarin from albumin in vitro but only weakly inhibit CYP2C9 [PMID:28135601]. Added antiplatelet activity is plausible [PMID:15613018]. Monitor INR.
- Antiplatelets and anticoagulants: quercetin inhibits platelet aggregation in humans [PMID:15613018]. Its metabolites potentiated aspirin's antiplatelet effect more than additively in vitro [PMID:31367693]. Isoquercetin lowers D-dimer and thrombin generation [PMID:30652973].
- Fluoroquinolones: quercetin binds DNA gyrase [PMID:12804597], but it competes for the quinolone binding site much more weakly than ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin [PMID:8718602]. In E. coli, high-dose quercetin partly reduced ciprofloxacin's bacteriostatic and bactericidal effect [PMID:33025172]. This is in vitro only, and clinical relevance is unknown.
- Antihypertensives: an additive BP fall is possible (SBP about -2 to -4 mmHg) [PMID:27405810] [PMID:37654199].
- Antidiabetics: a small insulin decrease and subgroup FPG reductions [PMID:37654199] [PMID:30848564]. Quercetin did not inhibit OCT1 (the metformin uptake transporter) in vitro [PMID:22394605].
- Dasatinib: combined deliberately as a senolytic (D+Q). AEs rise compared with placebo [PMID:36857968] [PMID:42823536].
- Overall: clinically demonstrated PK interactions are limited to a few substrates (CYP2C9, CYP2E1, P-gp), and most predicted interactions have not been confirmed in humans [PMID:42827146].
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine: quercetin 500 mg/d for 13 days reduced CYP1A2 activity by 10.4% (CI 1.1-29.8%) and changed paraxanthine kinetics [PMID:19215233]. A separate study confirmed significant inhibition of caffeine metabolism [PMID:25025048].
- Green tea extract (EGCG): quercetin inhibits COMT/MRP1 and raised tissue EGCG 2-3x in mice [PMID:22438067]. No human data were found.
- Aspirin: more-than-additive antiplatelet effect with quercetin metabolites in vitro [PMID:31367693].
- Iron (non-heme): quercetin chelates iron and lowers ferroportin, inhibiting iron absorption in rats [PMID:25058155].
*** Conditions
- Kidney disease and estrogen-dependent cancer: see Contraindications [PMID:29127724] [PMID:9816216].
- Hypothyroidism or borderline thyroid function: anti-thyroid effects are shown in vitro and in rats [PMID:24447974] [PMID:39456456].
- Hyperuricaemia: plasma urate may fall [PMID:26785820].
- Bleeding disorders or upcoming surgery: antiplatelet and anti-PDI activity [PMID:15613018] [PMID:30652973].
*** Lab-test interference
- Quercetin inhibits horseradish peroxidase (HRP) at nM concentrations and abolished the signal in Amplex-type HRP assays in vitro [PMID:40518027]. HRP-coupled assays may therefore be affected. This is shown in research assays only; no clinical-analyzer interference has been reported.
- Real physiological changes, not assay artifacts: lower plasma uric acid [PMID:26785820], and lower D-dimer/thrombin-generation markers with isoquercetin [PMID:30652973].

** Genotype notes
- ABCB1 (MDR1) C3435T: quercetin's lowering of talinolol AUC was most marked in 3435 TT carriers [PMID:23422925].
- CYP3A5: CYP3A induction (lower midazolam AUC) was more prominent in CYP3A5*1/*1 and *1/*3 carriers [PMID:21680781].
- CYP1A2*1C/*1F: inhibition of caffeine metabolism by quercetin did not differ by genotype [PMID:25025048].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Phase 2 RCT of D+Q in fibrotic MASH (n=31): fibrosis improvement 47% vs 7%; more AEs (82% vs 43%), all self-limiting [PMID:42823536].
- [2026-10] Systematic review of quercetin and xenobiotic metabolism: clinically shown interactions mainly involve CYP2C9, CYP2E1 and P-gp substrates [PMID:42827146].
- [2026-10] SR of human senolytic trials (6 studies): D+Q shows preliminary biomarker and functional effects. It should be considered investigational [PMID:42834340].
- [2026-07] RCT (n=110): 500 mg/d quercetin bar cut acute mountain sickness from 25% to 2% and lowered BP at altitude [PMID:42435069].
- [2026-04] RCT (n=50): 4 weeks of quercetin glycoside before an mRNA COVID booster did not change immunogenicity or reactogenicity [PMID:41759822].
- [2025-12] MASLD SR/MA (7 RCTs, n=540): lower ALT/AST/GGT and better lipids; GRADE very low to moderate [PMID:41404533].
- [2025-08] CABG RCT (n=97): improved endothelial function and lower post-operative AF (4% vs 18%) in men; pro-inflammatory signal in women [PMID:40375481].
- [2025-08] Quercetin is a potent HRP inhibitor, which interferes with HRP-based assays [PMID:40518027].
- [2025-06] Bioavailability SR/MA (31 studies): oligoglucosides about 20x the aglycone; phytosome 20.1x [PMID:40037045].
- [2025-04] Narrative review of the lecithin formulation for allergy: only 2 monotherapy clinical trials exist [PMID:40362785].
- [2025-03] RCT in older adults (n=26): quercetin glycosides plus resistance training gave larger strength gains [PMID:40063125].
- [2024-12] RCT in trained cyclists (n=48): 1000 mg/d quercetin, with or without citrulline, did not improve 20-km time trial [PMID:39417670].
- [2024-10] Review: quercetin acts as a thyroid disruptor in experimental models; caution advised with high doses [PMID:39456456].

** Open questions
- Whether the small SBP reduction (about 2-3 mmHg) is clinically meaningful, and whether it is larger in hypertension [PMID:37654199] [PMID:17951477].
- Whether high-bioavailability forms (phytosome, EMIQ, oligoglucosides) change efficacy or interaction risk. Most efficacy trials used the aglycone [PMID:40037045].
- Long-term (>12 weeks) safety of >=1000 mg/d, including kidney and thyroid effects in humans [PMID:29127724] [PMID:39456456].
- How much of D+Q's effect and adverse events comes from quercetin rather than dasatinib, and whether quercetin alone is senolytic in humans [PMID:25754370] [PMID:42834340].
- Clinical significance of the conflicting P-gp findings (inhibition with fexofenadine, induction with talinolol) [PMID:19221726] [PMID:23422925].
- Human data on the warfarin and digoxin interactions beyond case reports and animal studies [PMID:36311308] [PMID:14697403].
- Sex differences in response [PMID:40375481].
