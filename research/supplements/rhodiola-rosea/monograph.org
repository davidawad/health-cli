** Identity & forms
- Rhodiola rosea L. (Crassulaceae). The medicinal parts are the rhizome and root. About 140 compounds have been isolated, including monoterpene alcohols and glycosides, cyanogenic glycosides, aryl glycosides, phenylethanoids, phenylpropanoids and their glycosides, flavonoids, proanthocyanidins and gallic acid derivatives [PMID:20378318].
- Typical standardization is about 3% rosavins and 1% salidroside, e.g. ~200 mg doses in acute exercise studies [PMID:37641937]. Rosavins are the markers that distinguish R. rosea from related species. Salidroside also occurs in other Rhodiola species [PMID:26626192].
- EU regulatory form: a dry extract (DER 1.5-5:1, extraction solvent ethanol 67-70% V/V) in solid oral dosage forms. This is registered as a traditional herbal medicinal product, not under well-established use [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-european-union-herbal-monograph-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf]. Such extracts have been in medicinal use in the EU since 1987 [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf]. The EMA public summary describes the use as temporary relief of stress symptoms such as fatigue and a sensation of weakness, in adults only [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/rhodiolae-roseae-rhizoma-et-radix]. Rhodiola is the main adaptogen with an HMPC/EMA stress indication [PMID:29325481].
- Branded or standardized extracts in trials: SHR-5 (Swedish Herbal Institute; "Arctic Root"): DER 2.5-5:1, extracted with 70% ethanol and then water [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf]. Used in most older RCTs [PMID:19016404] [PMID:17990195] [PMID:11081987] and in the human CYP study [PMID:26613955]. WS 1375 (marketed as Rosalin [PMID:28367055] and Vitano [PMID:26502953]): an ethanolic dry extract, 200 mg twice daily, used in open-label stress, fatigue and burnout trials [PMID:22228617] [PMID:28219059] [PMID:28367055] [PMID:26502953]. Biosynthetic ("nature-identical") salidroside alone, 60 mg/d [PMID:39601362] [PMID:37686755]. Chinese "Rhodiola capsules" (e.g. Tongrentang, 2.4 g/d) in sports trials [PMID:42738995]. R. crenulata extracts are used for altitude illness [PMID:24176010]. These are different preparations, and in some cases different species.
- Adulteration and authenticity: About one-fifth of ~40 European market products labelled R. rosea contained no rosavin. About 80% of the rest had less rosavin than registered products and appeared to be adulterated with other Rhodiola species [PMID:26626192]. In a DNA-barcoding survey of UK roseroot products, only 5 of 10 products with Rhodiola DNA matched R. rosea. Three products contained only alfalfa and fenugreek [PMID:29154110]. The labelled amounts of salidroside and rosavins were confirmed in fewer than a quarter of products. The R. crenulata marker crenulatin was found in 2 of 4 products [PMID:32311178]. Only 40% of Chinese R. crenulata decoction pieces were authentic, and 10% were R. rosea [PMID:25661009]. A 2026 review of more than 500 constituents across the genus concludes that reliable chemical markers for identifying species are still lacking [PMID:42657013].

** Mechanisms
- Adaptogen/stress-response model: proposed modulation of the HPA axis (cortisol), molecular chaperones (Hsp70), stress-activated JNK, FoxO/DAF-16 and nitric oxide. This model comes mainly from in vitro and animal work [PMID:19500070] [PMID:20378318]. A 2026 review adds salidroside activation of PI3K/Akt [PMID:41901106].
- Cortisol: SHR-5 changed the cortisol awakening response compared with placebo in burnout patients with fatigue syndrome [PMID:19016404]. A systematic review of human trials found the overall HPA-axis effects of phytonutrients other than ashwagandha unclear [PMID:33650944].
- Monoamine oxidase: methanol and water root extracts inhibited MAO-A by 92.5% and 84.3%, and MAO-B by 81.8% and 88.9%, at 100 ug/mL in vitro. Rosiridin was the most active compound (MAO-B pIC50 5.38) [PMID:19168123]. Isolated salidroside did not inhibit MAO-A or MAO-B at relevant concentrations [PMID:37686755].
- Antidepressant-like activity across neuroendocrine, neurotransmitter and immune networks in preclinical models [PMID:27013349] [PMID:41750223].
- Proposed oestrogen-receptor (SERM-like) activity. This is a hypothesis paper, not human data [PMID:26776957].
- Pharmacokinetics: salidroside is rapidly metabolized and has low oral bioavailability. In rats it is excreted mainly as tyrosol, and hypobaric hypoxia roughly doubled its bioavailability (+108%) [PMID:41548618]. No human PK data on rosavins were found.

** Dosing in trials
- EU traditional use (adults): 144-200 mg dry extract 1-2 times daily (daily dose 144-400 mg). Consult a doctor if symptoms last more than 2 weeks. Not recommended under 18 years [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-european-union-herbal-monograph-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- WS 1375: 200 mg twice daily for 4-12 weeks [PMID:22228617] [PMID:28219059] [PMID:28367055].
- SHR-5: Depression: 340 or 680 mg/d for 6 weeks [PMID:17990195]. Fatigue syndrome: 576 mg/d for 28 days [PMID:19016404]. Low-dose fatigue regimens: 1 tablet/d [PMID:11081987]. Cadets: 2 vs 3 capsules once, with no difference between doses [PMID:12725561].
- GAD pilot: 340 mg/d for 10 weeks [PMID:18307390]. Nursing-student RCT: 364 mg, up to 2 capsules/d, for 42 days [PMID:25268730].
- Exercise: Acute: about 200 mg 60 min before exercise [PMID:37641937]. Chronic: higher doses of about 1500-2400 mg/d for 4-30 days [PMID:37641937], or 1200 mg/d for 4 days [PMID:40873122]. VO2max gains were greater at >600 mg/d in subgroup analysis [PMID:41080184].
- Salidroside alone: 60 mg/d for 16 days [PMID:39601362] or 150 mg/d for 4 weeks [PMID:40988719].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Short-term trials report few, mostly mild AEs: WS 1375: AE incidence of 0.015 events per observation day over 12 weeks [PMID:28367055]. In another study, mostly mild AEs and no serious AEs [PMID:22228617]. Mao 2015: AEs in 30.0% on Rhodiola vs 63.2% on sertraline and 16.7% on placebo [PMID:25837277]. GAD pilot: dizziness and dry mouth were the commonest AEs [PMID:18307390]. An exercise SR of 10 studies reported no obvious adverse reactions [PMID:35464040].
- EU monograph undesirable effects (frequency not known): headache, nervousness, insomnia, dizziness; nausea, vomiting; skin rash and itching. These were derived from trials and EudraVigilance reports [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- Toxicology gaps: tests for reproductive toxicity, genotoxicity and carcinogenicity are not available. Because genotoxicity data are missing, the HMPC did not create an EU list entry [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-european-union-herbal-monograph-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf] [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- Psychiatric activation: there is a case report of mania associated with Rhodiola rosea (title only; no abstract available) [PMID:35303403]. A paroxetine add-on case presented with symptoms interpreted as serotonergic syndrome [PMID:25413939].
- Liver: no Rhodiola-specific hepatotoxicity series was found. In one DILIN case, rhodiola was named as a possible co-causative agent alongside ashwagandha [PMID:31991029].
- Product quality is a safety issue because substitution and adulteration are common (see Identity & forms) [PMID:26626192] [PMID:29154110].
- Long-term safety beyond about 12 weeks has not been characterized [PMID:22643043] [PMID:41906501].

** Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance. This is the only formal contraindication in the EU monograph [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-european-union-herbal-monograph-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- Pregnancy and lactation: not recommended because safety has not been established, and there are no fertility data [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-european-union-herbal-monograph-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- Children and adolescents under 18: not recommended because data are lacking [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-european-union-herbal-monograph-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- Bipolar disorder: caution because of an antidepressant/activating profile and a mania case report [PMID:35303403] [PMID:27013349]. This is a precaution based on case-level evidence, not a formal contraindication.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- CYP2C9 substrates (warfarin, phenytoin, losartan): Human cocktail study (n=13, SHR-5 for 14 days): the EXP-3174/losartan ratio fell 21% (p=0.023), indicating CYP2C9 inhibition. The authors flag narrow-therapeutic-index substrates such as warfarin and phenytoin [PMID:26613955]. In vitro, the same product inhibited CYP2C9 noncompetitively (IC50 19.2 ug/mL) [PMID:28805981]. The HMPC judged the clinical relevance unclear [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf].
- Warfarin: in rats, SHR-5 did not alter warfarin PK or anticoagulant effect [PMID:18844284]. However, human CYP2C9 inhibition was later shown [PMID:26613955]. Monitoring INR when Rhodiola is started or stopped is reasonable.
- Losartan: Spanakis 2013 was a rabbit study, not a human one. Losartan AUC almost doubled and Cmax rose, with no change in EXP-3174 [PMID:23327826]. In humans, the metabolic ratio fell 21% [PMID:26613955].
- Theophylline: no PK change in rats [PMID:18844284]. In humans, CYP1A2 activity (caffeine probe) was not affected [PMID:26613955].
- CYP3A4 and P-gp substrates: Potent in vitro inhibition: CYP3A4 IC50 1.7-3.1 ug/mL; P-gp (digoxin transport) IC50 16.7-51.7 ug/mL [PMID:19790032]. In humans, however, midazolam (CYP3A4), omeprazole (CYP2C19) and dextromethorphan (CYP2D6) metabolism did not change [PMID:26613955]. Isolated salidroside showed no CYP, MAO or OATP inhibition risk [PMID:37686755].
- Antidepressants (SSRIs/SNRIs/TCAs): Case of a serotonergic-type syndrome with paroxetine [PMID:25413939]. EudraVigilance reports with sertraline, paroxetine (including serotonin syndrome), duloxetine and amitriptyline [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf]. A pharmacovigilance series names Rhodiola among herbs with significant interaction risk with psychotropics [PMID:30659561]. Siwek 2023 chart review: adaptogens were involved in 9% of AEs with concomitant antidepressants, and the authors propose CYP/P-gp and pharmacodynamic mechanisms. The abstract does not list Rhodiola-specific cases [PMID:37829299]. An RCT of Rhodiola capsule plus sertraline reports efficacy, but its abstract gives no safety data [PMID:32090788].
- MAO inhibitors: root extracts inhibit MAO-A and MAO-B in vitro [PMID:19168123]. Additive MAO inhibition is a theoretical risk only; salidroside alone does not inhibit MAO [PMID:37686755].
- Antipsychotics and benzodiazepines: case series list haloperidol (myoclonus, hypoglycaemia, sedation), risperidone and diazepam (excessive sedation) combined with Rhodiola [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf] [PMID:30659561].
- Antidiabetics: glucose-lowering effects via AMPK in preclinical models [PMID:31069710]. There are no human interaction data, so the risk is mechanistic only.
- Antihypertensives: an aqueous extract lowered SBP in hypertensive rats via beta-endorphin [PMID:23192943]. R. crenulata had no BP effect in altitude RCTs [PMID:40584626]. Any additive effect is speculative.
- Stimulants (dextroamphetamine): CNS-stimulating activity is reported [PMID:20378318], and nervousness and insomnia are listed AEs [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf]. Additive activation is an inference only.
*** Other supplements
- Caffeine: several RCTs combined caffeine (3 mg/kg) with Rhodiola for sport performance: The combination improved jump and punch power [PMID:40005009] [PMID:40278391] and aerial-duel performance [PMID:42123940]. A 2026 trial found no significant CAF x RHO interaction [PMID:42738995]. Rhodiola did not change CYP1A2 caffeine metabolism [PMID:26613955].
- Ashwagandha: rhodiola was a possible co-causative agent in one ashwagandha liver-injury case [PMID:31991029].
- Ginseng (Panax): both are classed as stimulating adaptogens [PMID:34445021]. Ginseng and Rhodiola are both among the herbs most often implicated in adverse interactions with psychotropic drugs [PMID:30659561]. Additive effects are inferred; no combination study exists.
- Fixed combinations studied: magnesium/B vitamins/L-theanine (stress DASS-42 ES 0.29) [PMID:35565828]; ADAPT-232 with Eleutherococcus and Schisandra in long COVID [PMID:35337143]; saffron in mild-moderate depression (open-label) [PMID:30034237]; black cohosh for menopause [PMID:32455817].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy and lactation, age under 18, and bipolar disorder: see Contraindications.
- Major depressive disorder: Rhodiola is less effective than sertraline [PMID:25837277] and not recommended by WFSBP/CANMAT [PMID:35311615]. It should not replace established treatment.
- High-altitude travel: R. crenulata did not prevent AMS [PMID:24176010] [PMID:31012483].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference reported. The EU assessment notes "no data available" for laboratory findings [URL:https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf]. Physiological shifts include salivary cortisol awakening response [PMID:19016404] and CK/lactate after exercise [PMID:41080184]. INR may shift through the CYP2C9 effect on warfarin [PMID:26613955].

** Genotype notes
- CYP2C9: the 21% fall in the losartan metabolic ratio was more pronounced in CYP2C9 extensive metabolizers than in intermediate or poor metabolizers [PMID:26613955]. No other pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source was found.

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Bayesian NMA of supplements on jump performance (119 studies): the caffeine + Rhodiola CMJ credible interval excluded zero, but most comparisons were very low certainty [PMID:42821324].
- [2026-08] Caffeine/Rhodiola DanceSport RCT (n=48, 2.4 g/d): no significant CAF x RHO interaction; synergy not established [PMID:42738995].
- [2026-08] Narrative review of adaptogens in athletes: evidence for Rhodiola is limited and preliminary [PMID:42588175].
- [2026-08] Systematic review of Rhodiola constituents (>500 compounds) aimed at preventing adulteration: no reliable species markers yet [PMID:42657013].
- [2026-04] Soccer RCT (n=96): Rhodiola mainly lowered perceived exertion; combination with caffeine gave the broadest benefits [PMID:42123940].
- [2026-04] Rat PK: hypoxia increased salidroside bioavailability by ~108% [PMID:41548618].
- [2026-03] Systematic review of RCTs of ashwagandha and Rhodiola (5 Rhodiola RCTs, 290-1500 mg/d, 3-16 weeks): benefits for stress, mood and cognition, limited by small and short trials [PMID:41906501].
- [2026-03] Mechanistic review: salidroside acts via PI3K/Akt; adaptogens modulate HPA, Hsp70, JNK and FOXO [PMID:41901106].
- [2026-02] Football RCT (n=24, 4 weeks): better Yo-Yo IR2, repeated sprint and decision-making under fatigue [PMID:41829897].
- [2026-02] Review of medicinal plants for MDD, including Rhodiola, highlights herb-drug interaction and standardization risks [PMID:41750223].
- [2025-11] Dose-response crossover RCT (n=27): resistance performance and Stroop improved vs no-capsule control [PMID:41374026].
- [2025-11] Basketball RCT (n=48, 28 days): VO2max, 5 km run and simulated-game time improved [PMID:41373984].
- [2025-11] Digital eye strain RCT (1200 mg/d for 4 days): reduced accommodation variability [PMID:40873122].
- [2025-09] Endurance MA (26 RCTs, n=668): VO2max ES 0.32, TTE ES 0.38, CK ES -0.84 [PMID:41080184].
- [2025-09] Salidroside 150 mg/d plus eccentric training: added late-stage reactive-strength benefit [PMID:40988719].
- [2025-06] MA of R. crenulata for acute high-altitude disease (19 RCTs): better SaO2/PaO2 vs placebo [PMID:40584626].
- [2025-04] Boxing RCT (n=96): caffeine + Rhodiola improved punch power [PMID:40278391].
- [2025-03] Bench-press/pull crossover RCT: small strength gains, no effect on mental fatigue [PMID:40289957].
- [2025-02] SR of drugs and supplements for mild depression: Rhodiola better than placebo in a single RCT [PMID:40014460].
- [2025-02] Volleyball RCT (n=48): caffeine + Rhodiola gave the largest jump gains [PMID:40005009].
- [2024-12] Salidroside 60 mg/d RCT (n=50): maintained TTE and less myoglobin rise [PMID:39601362].

** Open questions
- Whether CYP2C9 inhibition is clinically meaningful with warfarin, phenytoin or sulfonylureas. No human warfarin interaction study exists.
- Whether the potent in vitro CYP3A4/P-gp inhibition matters clinically for digoxin or other P-gp substrates, given the null human midazolam result.
- Serotonergic and manic risk when combined with antidepressants, and in bipolar disorder. Evidence is limited to case reports and pharmacovigilance.
- Efficacy in burnout and depression in adequately powered, placebo-controlled, independent RCTs using authenticated, standardized extracts.
- Genotoxicity and reproductive toxicity data, which the HMPC has identified as missing.
- How results transfer across preparations (SHR-5, WS 1375, Chinese Rhodiola capsules, R. crenulata, pure salidroside), and how widespread market adulteration is.
