** Identity & forms
- Essential trace element incorporated as selenocysteine in selenoproteins. Common supplemental forms are selenomethionine, selenium-enriched yeast, sodium selenite and sodium selenate; absorption and retention differ by form. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Mechanisms
- Selenoproteins include glutathione peroxidases, thioredoxin reductases and iodothyronine deiodinases, supporting redox reactions and thyroid hormone metabolism. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Dosing in trials
- Trials used approximately 100–800 µg/day for weeks to years; SELECT used 200 µg/day selenomethionine. Dietary RDA is 55 µg/day; U.S. adult UL is 400 µg/day. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Excess causes selenosis: hair/nail brittleness or loss, garlic breath, metallic taste, rash, nausea, fatigue and neurologic effects. Chronic high intake can cause toxicity; total diet and multiple products matter. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Contraindications
- Avoid supplementation in known selenium excess/selenosis. Pregnancy and lactation require attention to total intake; deficiency correction and research dosing are distinct contexts. UL is not a target. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- Warfarin: inconsistent reports of altered anticoagulant response; monitor INR when intake changes. Other antioxidant supplements: randomized trials do not establish additive clinical benefit. Thyroid disease: physiologic relevance does not establish treatment efficacy. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- 2025 NIH ODS fact sheet refresh reiterates lack of support for cardiovascular prevention in selenium-replete adults and summarizes thyroid antibody research. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/]

** Open questions
- Identify baseline selenium status and whether any subgroup has patient-important benefit; establish dose-response safety at chronic low supplemental doses.
