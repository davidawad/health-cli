** Identity & forms
- Spermidine is a natural polyamine (with putrescine and spermine). Cells make it, and it also comes from the diet and gut bacteria. Polyamines are needed for normal cell growth [PMID:30923709] [PMID:32634331].
- Dietary sources are plant-based and fermented foods. Among patients with coronary disease the median intake was 11.5 mg/day [PMID:42000692]. In NHANES, the main dietary sources were vegetables, cereals, legumes, nuts and cheese [PMID:36249217].
- Supplement forms used in trials:
  - Spermidine-rich wheat germ extract (TLL The Longevity Labs, spermidineLIFE): 0.9-1.2 mg/day in the Berlin SCD trials [PMID:29315079] [PMID:35616942]. In the EU it is a listed novel food, and its specification was amended by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/443 [URL:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2020/443/oj].
  - High-purity synthetic spermidine trihydrochloride: 40 mg/day [PMID:39405978]. A 24 mg/day dose is being tested in the POLYCAD trial [PMID:41168834].
  - Rice germ extract (Miricell): 1.5-3.3 mg/day [PMID:40862848].
  - Combination "fasting mimetic" products with nicotinamide, PEA and OEA [PMID:39549554] [PMID:41720867].
  - A topical analogue, N1-methylspermidine, is sold in hair lotions [PMID:29766701].
- Wheat germ products contain gluten. A retailer listing for spermidineLIFE states it "contains gluten and is not safe for people with gluten intolerances or allergies" [URL:https://www.vitalabo.com/longevity-labs/spermidine-life].

** Mechanisms
- Autophagy induction. Spermidine extended lifespan in yeast, flies and worms by inhibiting histone acetyltransferases, which induced autophagy [PMID:19801973]. It inhibits the acetyltransferase EP300 and acts as an EP300 inhibitor in human cells [PMID:25526088].
- Spermidine and resveratrol induce autophagy through different routes: spermidine works independently of SIRT1, while resveratrol needs it. Both converge on the acetylproteome, and they acted synergistically at subthreshold doses in cells [PMID:21339330] [PMID:20157579].
- eIF5A hypusination. Spermidine is the substrate for hypusination of eIF5A, which is needed to translate the autophagy transcription factor TFEB. This pathway is depleted in old human B cells and restored ex vivo by spermidine [PMID:31474573].
- In model organisms, fasting- and caloric-restriction-induced autophagy and longevity needed spermidine synthesis and eIF5A hypusination. Fasting raised spermidine levels in human volunteers [PMID:39117797]. Rapamycin-induced autophagy and longevity likewise depended on a surge in spermidine [PMID:39212197].
- Cardiovascular effects (animal data): oral spermidine extended mouse lifespan, reduced cardiac hypertrophy and preserved diastolic function. It also lowered blood pressure in Dahl salt-sensitive rats. These effects needed cardiomyocyte Atg5 [PMID:27841876] [PMID:28118075].
- Brain (mouse data): dietary spermidine crossed the blood-brain barrier in mice, increased hippocampal eIF5A hypusination and improved spatial learning [PMID:33852843].
- Spermine produced from spermidine chelates Fe2+ and inhibits ferroptosis. Polyamine catabolism also produces H2O2 and acrolein [PMID:42444672].
- Cancer biology: polyamines are needed for tumour growth, and polyamine depletion (ODC inhibition with DFMO) is an established anticancer strategy [PMID:30181570] [PMID:35477776] [PMID:38749882].

** Dosing in trials
- Wheat germ extract:
  - 1.2 mg/day for 3 months (safety and pilot memory trials) [PMID:29315079] [PMID:30388439].
  - 0.9 mg/day for 12 months (SmartAge) [PMID:35616942] [PMID:31039826].
- Higher-dose supplements:
  - 15 mg/day for 5 days (PK crossover) [PMID:37111071].
  - 40 mg/day high-purity spermidine for 7-28 days [PMID:39405978].
  - 6 mg/day for 13 weeks (vaccine pilot) [PMID:42169618].
  - 24 mg/day for 48 weeks in older coronary-disease patients (POLYCAD; n=187; completion expected August 2026) [PMID:41168834].
- Rice germ extract: 1.5 or 3.3 mg/day for 56 days; changes were seen only at 3.3 mg [PMID:40862848].
- Senekowitsch et al. concluded that supplements below 15 mg/d are unlikely to have short-term effects, because ingested spermidine appears to be converted to spermine before reaching the circulation [PMID:37111071]. Plasma spermidine tracks habitual dietary intake only weakly (+18% per doubling of intake) [PMID:42000692].
- SmartAge authors called for future trials at higher doses [PMID:35616942].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Wheat germ extract (1.2 mg/d, 3 months, n=30): no differences from placebo in vital signs, clinical chemistry or haematology. A 28-day mouse study found no tumorigenic or fibrotic changes [PMID:29315079].
- SmartAge (0.9 mg/d, 12 months, n=100): adverse events balanced between groups [PMID:35616942].
- High-purity spermidine (40 mg/d, 28 days, n=37 men aged 50-70): no changes in clinical, lipid, chemistry or haematology measures, and no product-related AEs. The trial was industry-funded [PMID:39405978].
- Vaccine pilot (6 mg/d, 13 weeks) and the rice germ pilot: well tolerated [PMID:42169618] [PMID:40862848].
- Rat toxicology: acute oral toxicity about 600 mg/kg. The 6-week dietary NOAEL was 1000 ppm (83 mg/kg/day); spermine was more toxic [PMID:9207896].
- Theoretical cancer concern:
  - Tumour-derived spermidine suppresses CD8+ T-cell function in mouse models. Giving spermidine made glioblastoma more aggressive in mice [PMID:37276392] [PMID:39561012].
  - In the Takayama cohort, women showed a suggestive positive association between spermidine intake and cancer mortality (HR 1.38, 0.99-1.93) [PMID:37964604].
  - Large cohorts found no rise in prostate or colorectal cancer [PMID:42591195] [PMID:26135350].
- Observational biomarker caveat: higher plasma spermidine was associated with worse brain-aging markers, post-stroke cognitive impairment and post-stroke depression [PMID:36321615] [PMID:39817544] [PMID:40043446]. These are endogenous levels, not supplementation.
- Conflicts of interest: several of the trial teams (SmartAge, the vaccine pilot and the 40 mg study) have ties to manufacturers [PMID:35616942] [PMID:42169618] [PMID:39405978].

** Contraindications
- Wheat allergy or coeliac disease: wheat germ products contain gluten [URL:https://www.vitalabo.com/longevity-labs/spermidine-life]. Synthetic or rice-germ forms avoid wheat [PMID:39405978] [PMID:40862848].
- Active cancer, or treatment that depletes polyamines (eflornithine/DFMO): supplementation works against the therapeutic aim of polyamine depletion [PMID:30181570] [PMID:38749882] [PMID:23340449]. A small trial of a polyamine-reduced diet in men with metastatic prostate cancer was well tolerated [PMID:12653644].
- Advanced CKD (relative): putrescine and the spermine-derived toxin acrolein accumulate in renal failure [PMID:16554974] [PMID:27758122]. No supplementation safety data exist for CKD.
- Pregnancy, lactation and children: no human data were found.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Eflornithine (DFMO, Iwilfin): this irreversible ODC inhibitor lowers polyamines and is FDA-approved as maintenance therapy for high-risk neuroblastoma [PMID:39612452] [PMID:37883734]. In the DFMO+sulindac adenoma trial, the drug combination worked in the lower three quartiles of dietary polyamine intake (RR 0.19) but not the top quartile (RR 1.51, CI 0.53-4.29) [PMID:23340449] [PMID:18841250]. Avoid supplements during DFMO therapy.
- Immune checkpoint inhibitors: mixed signals.
  - In aged mice, spermidine improved anti-PD-L1 efficacy [PMID:36387057] [PMID:37723019].
  - Tumour-derived spermidine also acts as a T-cell checkpoint, and blocking polyamine synthesis improved anti-PD-1 responses [PMID:37276392].
  - There are no human data.
- Antihypertensives: dietary spermidine is linked to lower BP in humans (observational), and it lowered BP in rats [PMID:27841876] [PMID:28118075]. Any additive effect is theoretical and minor.
- Rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor): rapamycin-induced autophagy needs endogenous spermidine synthesis (preclinical) [PMID:39212197]. No human combination data exist.
*** Other supplements
- Trans-resveratrol: synergistic autophagy induction at subthreshold doses in cells, through SIRT1-dependent and SIRT1-independent routes [PMID:21339330] [PMID:20157579].
- Curcumin/Turmeric: curcumin also inhibits EP300 and induces autophagy, so effects may be additive (in vitro) [PMID:25526088].
- Urolithin A: a review describes complementary mitophagy (UroA) and autophagy (spermidine) actions. There are no co-administration trials [PMID:41404767] [PMID:42588134].
- Fisetin and Berberine: reviewed as complementary to spermidine for functional-food combinations. Direct evidence for combined use is limited [PMID:42588134].
- Nicotinamide-containing combinations: spermidine plus nicotinamide, PEA and OEA products have been tested in small trials, without spermidine-alone arms [PMID:39549554] [PMID:41720867] [PMID:36678330]. No co-administration data were found for NMN or Nicotinamide Riboside. Both NAD+ precursors and spermidine are under separate anti-aging trials [PMID:38181790].
*** Conditions
- Wheat allergy/coeliac disease, cancer, CKD: see Contraindications.
- History of colorectal adenoma: above-median dietary polyamine intake was associated with adenoma recurrence (OR 1.39) [PMID:22648715]. A WHI analysis found no increase in CRC [PMID:26135350].
- Hypertension: microbial spermidine synthase genes were more abundant in normotensive people [PMID:41413233].
*** Lab-test interference
- Plasma/serum polyamine assays: 15 mg/d raised plasma spermine without changing spermidine [PMID:37111071]. Research biomarkers based on circulating spermidine (MCI/AD, stroke) could be confounded by supplementation [PMID:35487023] [PMID:36321615] [PMID:33439737].
- No interference with routine clinical chemistry was reported at 40 mg/d [PMID:39405978].

** Genotype notes
- ODC1 +316 G>A polymorphism:
  - ODC1 GG carriers with above-median polyamine intake had higher adenoma odds (OR 1.59, CI 1.00-2.53) [PMID:22648715].
  - The genotype modified the response to eflornithine/sulindac (P interaction .038) [PMID:20798393].
- Erythrocyte spermidine is 82% heritable in twins [PMID:39243176].
- No genotype-guided spermidine supplementation data exist.

** Latest research
- [2026-10] SmartAge exploratory MRI (n=75): precuneus CBF +4.9 ml/100 g/min and preserved cingulum fractional anisotropy vs placebo. Brain volume was unchanged; the authors call the findings preliminary [PMID:42858321].
- [2026-08] Review of spermidine, fisetin, berberine and urolithin A: complementary pathways, limited combined-use evidence [PMID:42588134].
- [2026-07] PLCO (n=54,517): dietary spermidine not associated with prostate cancer incidence (RR 1.00) or prostate cancer mortality [PMID:42591195].
- [2026-07] PREDIMED-Plus: a rising 1-year spermidine intake was associated with better liver indices and HbA1c [PMID:42455181].
- [2026-07] Cell Stress: spermine chelates Fe2+ and inhibits ferroptosis, which complements spermidine-driven autophagy [PMID:42444672].
- [2026-06] Vaccine pilot RCT (n=40, 6 mg/d): reversed immune-senescence markers and raised spike IgG in non-responders [PMID:42169618].
- [2026-06] POLYCAD baseline: plasma spermidine reflects dietary intake only weakly; muscle levels are unrelated to intake [PMID:42000692].
- [2026-06] Alzheimer's review: human evidence is limited and inconclusive, and insufficient to support use [PMID:42358231].
- [2026-04] Brain-aging review: human evidence is encouraging but limited [PMID:42012729].
- [2026-03] Scoping review: spermidine-eIF5A-TFEB axis during starvation [PMID:41874700].
- [2026-02] Mimio RCT (n=42): a multi-ingredient spermidine product lowered LDL and fasting glucose [PMID:41720867].
- [2025-12] Review contrasting urolithin A (mitophagy) with spermidine (autophagy) [PMID:41404767].
- [2025-10] POLYCAD protocol: 24 mg/d vs placebo for 48 weeks in 187 patients with CAD [PMID:41168834].
- [2025-10] Rice germ pilot (n=12): Beclin-1 and ULK1 up, hs-CRP down at 3.3 mg/d [PMID:40862848].
- [2025-09] Mendelian randomization: lower genetically predicted spermidine was associated with CHD [PMID:41013818].
- [2025-09] Rat study: a bovine thymus fraction raised blood spermidine more than a wheat germ supplement [PMID:40924744].
- [2025-08] Lubiprostone CKD RCT: raised spermidine through the gut agmatine pathway, with preserved eGFR [PMID:40880481].
- [2025-07] NHANES (n=2674): higher intake associated with better cognition [PMID:40180050].
- [2025-01] CATIS: high plasma polyamines predicted post-stroke cognitive impairment [PMID:39817544].
- [2024-12] 40 mg/d high-purity spermidine RCT: safe, with minimal change in circulating polyamines [PMID:39405978].
- [2024-12] UK Biobank: nonlinear inverse association of polyamine intake with mortality and CVD [PMID:39770955].
- [2024-12] Autophagy: a spermidine surge is required for rapamycin-induced autophagy [PMID:39212197].

** Open questions
- Does oral spermidine raise tissue spermidine in humans at any practical dose, given that plasma spermidine does not rise? [PMID:37111071] [PMID:39405978]
- Are the mortality/CVD associations causal, or do they reflect healthy diets and residual confounding? [PMID:42591195] [PMID:37964604]
- Will higher-dose RCTs (e.g. POLYCAD, 24 mg/d) show clinical benefit? [PMID:41168834]
- Long-term cancer safety, especially in people with existing neoplasia or at high risk.
- Autophagy-synergy claims with rapamycin, resveratrol and urolithin A have not been tested in humans.
- Independent replication of the industry-linked hair and memory findings.
