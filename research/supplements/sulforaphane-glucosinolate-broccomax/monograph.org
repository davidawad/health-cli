** Identity & forms
- *Product.* BroccoMax (Jarrow Formulas) is a broccoli seed extract. The current NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (DSLD) entry lists 35 mg "Sulforaphane Glucosinolate" (glucoraphanin) per 2 delayed-release veggie capsules, "in a proprietary blend containing myrosinase enzyme". Other ingredients include calcium ascorbate. The suggested use is 2 capsules once daily for adults [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/321953]. The label calls it a "Sulforaphane Generator 35 mg" and says the active myrosinase converts SGS to sulforaphane in the small intestine. An earlier, now off-market DSLD version listed SGS "standardized to 3.5%" with no myrosinase noted [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/321953]. 35 mg of glucoraphanin is roughly 80 µmol. That figure is derived from molecular weight (~437 g/mol) and is not stated on the label.
- *Glucoraphanin vs sulforaphane.* Glucoraphanin (GR) is a water-soluble glucosinolate that is relatively inert and stable. Sulforaphane (SFN) is the reactive isothiocyanate formed when myrosinase hydrolyses GR. Free SFN is hard to deliver in a stable, enriched form [PMID:26524341]. The products used in trials fall into four groups:
  1. GR-only extracts, which have no active myrosinase and depend on gut-microbial conversion.
  2. GR plus active myrosinase products. These include BroccoMax, Avmacol [PMID:39832347], sprout or seed powders, and "activated" extracts.
  3. Pre-hydrolysed SFN-rich beverages [PMID:22045030].
  4. Stabilised SFN. Examples are the alpha-cyclodextrin complex SFX-01 (Sulforadex) [PMID:39520658] and a "stabilized free sulforaphane" at 60 mg/day [PMID:25968598].
- *Conversion and bioavailability.*
  - Sprouts or seeds with intact plant myrosinase gave 3- to 4-fold more bioavailable SFN than GR without active myrosinase [PMID:26524341].
  - In a 2026 randomised crossover (n=16), adding mustard-seed myrosinase to broccoli seed GR roughly doubled SFN bioavailability (39.8% vs 18.6%). Conversion in the first 8 h rose from 8.0% to 25.4% [PMID:41692762].
  - Broccoli supplements without myrosinase gave "dramatically lower" SFN and erucin bioavailability than fresh sprouts, and peaks came later [PMID:21816223].
  - In 6 women, an activated (myrosinase) extract gave a higher AUC than a non-activated one (3559 vs 2172 nM) [PMID:33409874].
  - Gastric acidity appears to reduce myrosinase-dependent conversion. Conversion was higher on omeprazole and with enteric coating, and greater body mass correlated with lower conversion [PMID:31261930].
  - Daily broccoli intake interacted with BMI to change SFN absorption [PMID:30499426].
  - In the Qidong trials, interindividual differences in bioavailability were "striking" [PMID:16284385].
- *Gut microbiome.*
  - In a 2025 prediabetes RCT, people who responded had more of a Bacteroides-encoded regulator needed for GR-to-SFN conversion, and its abundance correlated with serum SFN [PMID:39929977].
  - Four bacterial GR-converting genes correlated with conversion [PMID:41692762].
  - A 2026 exploratory analysis (n=38) found that diet and microbiome "types" shaped how much SFN versus inert SFN-nitrile was produced [PMID:42564592].

** Mechanisms
- *Nrf2/Keap1 activation and phase II induction.*
  - SFN induces glutathione S-transferases and other cytoprotective enzymes [PMID:24913818].
  - In humans, oral SFN from broccoli sprout homogenate raised GSTM1, GSTP1, NQO1 and HO-1 mRNA in nasal lavage cells in a dose-dependent way [PMID:19028145].
  - Oral GR (450 mg/day) raised NQO1 mRNA in skin biopsies 3.1-fold [PMID:40559384].
  - In non-dialysis CKD, 400 µg/day SFN raised NRF2 and NQO1 mRNA [PMID:38772511].
  - A systematic review of human trials still judged the evidence that phytochemicals (including SFN) activate Nrf2 in humans as limited, with many studies at high or unclear risk of bias [PMID:33515348].
  - A 2026 pilot found no change in Nrf2-target mRNA in circulating extracellular vesicles after GR-rich soup [PMID:41603376].
- *Hepatic gluconeogenesis.* SFN suppressed hepatic glucose production via NRF2 nuclear translocation and lower gluconeogenic enzyme expression. This was shown in cells and diabetic animals (mechanistic/animal evidence) [PMID:28615356].
- *Effects on xenobiotic metabolism.*
  - In primary human hepatocytes (in vitro), SFN induced GSTA1/2, decreased CYP3A4 mRNA and activity, and left CYP1A2 unchanged [PMID:9288764].
  - In Caco-2 cells (in vitro), SFN induced UGT1A1/1A8/1A10 [PMID:22559738].
  - Human data are summarised under Interactions.
- *Other proposed mechanisms.*
  - HDAC inhibition and antioxidant/anti-inflammatory actions have been cited as rationales in schizophrenia and autism trials [PMID:39832347][PMID:25313065].
  - In Hi-Myc mice, SFN lowered fatty acid synthesis enzymes (ACC1, FASN) in prostate tumours (animal) [PMID:41738426].
- *Nrf2 in established cancer.* Reviewers caution that Nrf2 activation in cancer cells may cause chemoresistance. Whether it helps or harms depends on context [PMID:28675319].

** Dosing in trials
- *BroccoMax label:* 35 mg SGS with myrosinase per 2 delayed-release capsules, once daily [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/321953]. The 2026 BroccoMax prostate trial gave 4 capsules twice daily for 4 weeks [PMID:41738426].
- *Autism:* 50-150 µmol SFN/day for 18 weeks in ages 13-27 [PMID:25313065]; 50 µmol (≤45 kg) or 100 µmol (>45 kg) with risperidone [PMID:32347624].
- *Pollutant detoxification:* 600 µmol GR + 40 µmol SFN daily for 12 weeks [PMID:24913818]. The benefit appeared at the dose that produced ~25 µmol/day of urinary SFN metabolites [PMID:31268126]. A diesel-exhaust challenge study used 100 µmol SFN/day for 4 days [PMID:24287881].
- *Cancer:*
  - Recurrent prostate cancer: 200 µmol/day SFN-rich extract [PMID:25431127].
  - Pre-biopsy prostate: 200 µmol/day BSE [PMID:31155953].
  - Prostate biochemical recurrence: 60 mg/day stabilised SFN for 6 months [PMID:25968598].
  - Former smokers (lung): ~95 µmol/day for 12 months [PMID:40041932].
- *Schizophrenia:* Avmacol Extra Strength 1,700 mg/day for 24 weeks [PMID:39832347].
- *ADHD:* 30 mg/day with methylphenidate [PMID:41604557].
- *Cognition/MCI:* GR 30 mg/day for 42 months [PMID:41669080].
- *Skin NQO1:* GR 450 mg (1 mmol)/day [PMID:40559384].
- *CKD:* 400 µg/day SFN [PMID:38772511].
- *SFX-01 (stabilised synthetic SFN):* 300-600 mg/day (46.2-92.4 mg SFN) in phase 1 [PMID:39520658]; 300 mg twice daily for 28 days after SAH [PMID:39028412].
- Doses are reported in incompatible units (mg GR, µmol GR, µmol SFN, mg product) and conversion efficiency varies widely. Nominal doses therefore do not translate directly into SFN exposure [PMID:41692762][PMID:21816223].

** Safety & adverse effects
- *Gastrointestinal effects are the most consistent adverse effect.*
  - Prediabetes RCT: GI side effects, no severe adverse events [PMID:39929977].
  - SFX-01 phase 1: treatment-emergent AEs in 94% of participants, mostly mild treatment-related GI events [PMID:39520658].
  - SAH RCT: nausea 16.7% vs 2.0% on placebo [PMID:39028412].
- *Paediatric autism trials:* SFN was well tolerated. Side effects were rare insomnia, irritability and intolerance of taste/smell, and clinical laboratory tests confirmed safety [PMID:34034808]. A 108-child multicentre RCT reported SFN as safe and well tolerated [PMID:36427174].
- *Across cancer RCTs:* no significant AE differences from controls [PMID:38074675]. There were no grade 3 events at 200 µmol/day [PMID:25431127] and no severe AEs over 12 months [PMID:40041932].
- *Schizophrenia RCTs:* discontinuation was lower with SFN than placebo (RR 0.68) [PMID:41184790].
- *Thyroid/goitrogen concern.*
  - In a 12-week RCT of an SFN+GR-rich beverage (45 women), TSH, free T4, thyroglobulin and thyroid autoimmunity were unchanged [PMID:30735751].
  - A 2024 systematic review of 123 studies concluded that brassica intake, particularly with adequate iodine, poses no adverse thyroid effects in humans [PMID:38612798].
  - Goitrin (from progoitrin) and thiocyanate (from indole glucosinolates), rather than SFN, are the goitrogenic species. Broccoli contains little goitrin [PMID:26946249].
  - Data on long-term, high-dose concentrated extracts in people with iodine deficiency or existing thyroid disease are lacking.
- *Long-term data:* the longest controlled exposures are 12 months [PMID:40041932] and 42 months in a small pilot [PMID:41669080]. Both reported no severe AEs, but the samples are small.

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindication was identified in the cited sources.
- *Label cautions:* consult a professional before use if pregnant, nursing, under 18, taking medication, or with a medical condition [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/321953].
- *Pregnancy:* data are limited to a small bioavailability and dose-escalation study in pregnancy hypertension [PMID:33409874].
- *Active cancer treatment:* use is theoretically uncertain because Nrf2 activation may promote chemoresistance [PMID:28675319]. Conversely, SFN is being studied as a cardioprotectant during doxorubicin therapy [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03934905].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- *CYP1A2 substrates (e.g. caffeine, theophylline):*
  - 500 g/day of dietary broccoli for 12 days raised the caffeine metabolic ratio (a CYP1A2 marker) by 19%. This was whole broccoli, not an SFN supplement [PMID:8625493].
  - In human hepatocytes, SFN itself did not affect CYP1A2 [PMID:9288764].
  - Clinical relevance at supplement doses is unknown.
- *CYP3A4 substrates:*
  - SFN decreased CYP3A4 in human hepatocytes in vitro [PMID:9288764].
  - In a human crossover RCT (n=24), 450 µmol SFN did not change midazolam (CYP3A) clearance overall. CYP3A4 activity fell only in the subset with the highest baseline activity. SFN did not block rifampicin induction [PMID:23153560].
  - Overall: low likelihood of a clinically important effect.
- *Drugs cleared by glucuronidation (UGT1A substrates):* SFN induced UGT1A1/1A8/1A10 in Caco-2 cells (in vitro only) [PMID:22559738]. There are no human data.
- *Antineoplastic chemotherapy:* Nrf2 activation may contribute to chemoresistance in tumour cells. This is a theoretical, preclinical concern [PMID:28675319]. Trials testing SFN alongside doxorubicin are ongoing [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03934905].
- *Glucose-lowering agents (metformin, acarbose, others):*
  - SFN lowered fasting glucose and HbA1c in T2D [PMID:28615356] and modestly lowered fasting glucose in prediabetes [PMID:39929977]. It could theoretically add to glucose-lowering drugs.
  - In diabetic animals, the effect was similar in magnitude to metformin [PMID:28615356].
  - No human data on the combination were found.
- *Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole):* in a pilot study, conversion of GR to SFN with co-delivered myrosinase was higher on omeprazole, which suggests a pharmacokinetic effect on the supplement [PMID:31261930].
- *Antipsychotics:* SFN has been added to antipsychotics in schizophrenia RCTs with lower discontinuation and favourable lipid changes [PMID:39832347][PMID:40133020][PMID:41184790]. It has also been added to risperidone in ASD [PMID:32347624]. No adverse pharmacokinetic interaction was reported.
- *Methylphenidate:* combined use as an adjunct in ADHD, with side-effect frequencies comparable to placebo [PMID:41604557].

*** Other supplements
- *Vitamin C:* BroccoMax contains calcium ascorbate [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/321953]. Ascorbic acid was given with GR in both arms of the 2026 myrosinase trial [PMID:41692762]. The cited sources do not quantify its separate contribution to conversion.
- *Curcumin:* combining GR and curcumin induced skin NQO1 3.6-fold, versus 3.1-fold for GR alone and 3.3-fold for curcumin alone (n=6 per arm) [PMID:40559384].
- *Myrosinase sources (mustard seed powder, raw cruciferous sprouts):* these increase SFN yield from GR-only extracts [PMID:41692762][PMID:26524341].
- No cited interaction with other supplements in this batch (Acarbose aside, see Drugs).

*** Conditions
- *Hypothyroidism / iodine deficiency:* reassuring human data (see Safety) [PMID:30735751][PMID:38612798][PMID:26946249].
- *Obesity / higher BMI:* reduced and less predictable conversion of GR to SFN [PMID:31261930][PMID:30499426].
- *Diabetes / prediabetes:* glucose-lowering effects warrant glucose monitoring when combined with therapy [PMID:28615356][PMID:39929977].
- *Pregnancy:* limited data [PMID:33409874].
- *Paediatric use:* label caution [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/321953]. Paediatric ASD RCTs reported good tolerability [PMID:34034808][PMID:36427174].

*** Lab-test interference
- No cited assay interference was found.
- SFN may genuinely change measured glucose/HbA1c [PMID:28615356] and lipids [PMID:41184790]. These are physiological effects, not assay artefacts.

** Genotype notes
- *GSTM1:* in a randomised 3-phase crossover, GSTM1-null subjects had slightly higher plasma SFN-metabolite AUC and faster early urinary excretion than GSTM1-positive subjects [PMID:16332662]. In a 12-month broccoli-diet RCT, gene expression in the prostate differed by GSTM1 genotype [PMID:18596959]. Daily broccoli consumption interacted with BMI but not with GSTM1 genotype in isothiocyanate absorption [PMID:30499426].
- *GSTT1:* plasma SFN was higher in GSTT1-null patients given SFX-01 [PMID:39028412]. In the Qidong trial, benzene-mercapturic acid excretion was higher in GSTT1-positive than null participants, irrespective of study arm [PMID:24913818].
- *GSTP1:* polymorphisms did not affect SFN or erucin metabolism [PMID:21816223].
- *Microbiome "genotype":* the abundance of Bacteroides GR-conversion genes predicted SFN levels and glycaemic response [PMID:39929977][PMID:41692762].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Nrf2-activator schizophrenia MA (16 RCTs): SFN lowered discontinuation and TC/LDL/TG, with no other significant efficacy outcomes [PMID:42154264].
- [2026-08] Exploratory analysis (n=38): pre-intervention diet and gut microbiome community type shaped SFN versus inert SFN-nitrile production after broccoli sprouts [PMID:42564592].
- [2026-06] RCT in women with NAFLD (n=32): broccoli sprout extract 1 g/day with or without Pilates for 8 weeks did not change liver enzymes [PMID:42228225].
- [2026-02] Randomised crossover (n=16): mustard-seed myrosinase doubled SFN bioavailability from broccoli seed GR (39.8% vs 18.6%) [PMID:41692762].
- [2026-02] BroccoMax prostate cancer RCT (n=41, 4 weeks): lower tumour c-Myc, ACC1, FASN and Ki-67, but no change in acetyl-CoA or free fatty acids [PMID:41738426].
- [2026-01] 42-month pilot RCT (n=26): GR 30 mg/day improved memory performance index over time (P=0.012) in people at risk of AD/MCI, with a marginal endpoint result [PMID:41669080].
- [2026-01] Crossover pilot (n=9): GR-rich soup did not change Nrf2-target mRNA in circulating extracellular vesicles [PMID:41603376].
- [2025-11] Schizophrenia SR/MA (4 RCTs, n=369): modest negative-symptom benefit at 12 weeks, no cognitive benefit [PMID:41184790].
- [2025-11] ADHD RCT (n=70): SFN 30 mg/day added to methylphenidate improved ADHD-RS with large effect sizes [PMID:41604557].
- [2025-08] SAH RCT (SFX-01, n=105): no effect on vasospasm or outcome; nausea more frequent [PMID:39028412].
- [2025-06] Phase II RCT in former smokers (n=43, 12 months): bronchial Ki-67 index fell (-20% vs +65% on placebo), histopathology unchanged [PMID:40041932].
- [2025-05] Skin NQO1 RCT (n=18): oral GR and/or curcumin induced NQO1 roughly 3-fold in skin biopsies [PMID:40559384].
- [2025-04] ASD SR/MA (6 RCTs): total-symptom SMD -0.27; adverse events similar to placebo [PMID:40458076].
- [2025-04] RCT in chronic schizophrenia (24 weeks): reduced PANSS negative symptoms, no MCCB cognitive effect [PMID:40133020].
- [2025-03] Prediabetes RCT (n=74): fasting glucose -0.2 mmol/L, primary endpoint missed; microbiome-defined responders [PMID:39929977].
- [2025-01] Schizophrenia RCT (n=77 analysed): Avmacol 1,700 mg/day reduced PANSS negative symptoms (d=0.8 at 24 weeks) [PMID:39832347].
- [2025-01] SFX-01 phase 1: GI AEs common but mild; urinary recovery <1-41% [PMID:39520658].
- [2024-08] CKD RCT: SFN 400 µg/day raised NRF2/NQO1 mRNA and improved glucose, phosphate and triglycerides [PMID:38772511].
- [2024-06] SR of air-pollution supplement trials: SFN showed potential benefit, but trials are few [PMID:38870164].
- [2024-05] Broccoli and cancer SR/MA: cohort RR 0.89 [PMID:38892516].
- [2024-04] Brassica and thyroid SR: no adverse thyroid effect in humans with adequate iodine [PMID:38612798].
- [2024-02] ASD multicentre RCT (n=108): clinician-rated scales improved, caregiver-rated scales did not [PMID:36427174].

** Open questions
- Which product type gives reliable SFN exposure? GR-only, GR plus myrosinase (as in BroccoMax), and stabilised SFN differ several-fold, and how the microbiome and BMI modify conversion is unresolved [PMID:41692762][PMID:39929977].
- No pharmacokinetic study of BroccoMax at its labelled dose was found. Its 35 mg (~80 µmol) GR dose is below many trial doses, but its myrosinase content may raise conversion.
- Do the autism and schizophrenia signals hold up in larger, longer, adequately powered trials with consistent outcome scales? Several are registered or ongoing [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03932136][URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07652866].
- Do the pollutant-detoxification biomarker gains translate into clinical outcomes?
- Is long-term Nrf2 activation safe in people with existing or treated cancer [PMID:28675319]?
- Are there clinically relevant CYP/UGT interactions at chronic high doses? Human data are limited to CYP3A (null) [PMID:23153560] and dietary broccoli on CYP1A2 [PMID:8625493].
