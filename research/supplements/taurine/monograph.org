** Identity & forms
- 2-aminoethanesulfonic acid is a sulfur-containing beta-amino (amino sulfonic) acid. It is not built into proteins and is described as conditionally essential [PMID:42201902] [PMID:42300946]. It is abundant in heart, brain, retina and skeletal muscle [PMID:34443494].
- Taurine is approved in Japan for treating congestive heart failure [PMID:29631391] [PMID:35160118]. Elsewhere it is sold as an OTC supplement (capsules, powder) and as an energy-drink ingredient [PMID:35855073].
- Diet and status: taurine comes from animal foods. Vegan diets supply negligible taurine [PMID:3354491]. Vegans had lower plasma taurine (45 vs 58 umol/L) and much lower urinary excretion than omnivores [PMID:3354491]. Plasma taurine correlated only weakly with dietary intake (n=3693), which suggests endogenous synthesis and metabolism dominate [PMID:41250277].
- Pharmacokinetics: after a 4 g oral dose, Cmax was 86.1 mg/L at 1.5 h, with a plasma half-life of about 1.0 h (n=8) [PMID:22331997].
- Cellular uptake is via the Na+/Cl- dependent transporter TauT (SLC6A6). TauT also binds beta-alanine and GABA [PMID:30612046] [PMID:40789850].
- Infant nutrition: taurine is routinely added to preterm formula and parenteral nutrition. In the Cochrane review it increased fat absorption, but no growth benefit was shown [PMID:17943882].

** Mechanisms
- Calcium handling and vascular tone: in prehypertension, taurine raised plasma H2S and inhibited TRPC3-mediated calcium influx in resistance arteries [PMID:26781281]. In type 2 diabetes it inhibited TRPC6-linked platelet calcium influx [PMID:40546935].
- Mitochondria: taurine is required for the taurine modification of the wobble base of mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR). This modification is deficient in MELAS m.3243A>G, and taurine supplementation restored it in leukocytes [PMID:29666206] [PMID:23257519]. Antioxidant and mitochondria-protective roles are reviewed in [PMID:34443494].
- Neuromodulation: taurine is a GABA-A receptor agonist [PMID:30892104] and a full or partial agonist of human glycine receptors (EC50 about 10 times that of glycine) [PMID:11559772]. It hyperpolarizes neurons [PMID:23410665].
- Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant: lowers MDA and CRP in controlled trials [PMID:34584225]. In T2D trials it lowered the glycation markers pentosidine and methylglyoxal [PMID:32861603].
- Platelets: inhibits platelet activation and aggregation in animal and human studies (review) [PMID:35160118].
- Aging biology (mouse/monkey): supplementation reduced senescence, mitochondrial dysfunction, DNA damage and inflammaging, and extended mouse lifespan [PMID:37289866]. Human relevance is disputed (see Latest research) [PMID:40472098] [PMID:41061678].

** Dosing in trials
- Range in cardiometabolic RCTs: 0.5-6 g/d for 5-365 days [PMID:38755142]. One meta-analysis found 1.5-3.0 g/d most effective. Courses of 8 weeks or more favored glucose and lipid effects; under 8 weeks favored BP and inflammation [PMID:41275513].
- Blood pressure: 1.6 g/d for 12 weeks in prehypertension [PMID:26781281]; 2.4 g/d for 12 weeks in T2D [PMID:40546935]; 1-6 g/d across a BP meta-analysis [PMID:30006901].
- Type 2 diabetes: 3 g/d for 8 weeks [PMID:32472292] [PMID:32861603]. In obesity, 3 g/d gave a larger HbA1c effect than under 3 g/d [PMID:39796489]. Heart-failure studies used 500 mg-6 g/d for 2-48 weeks [PMID:35855073].
- Exercise: acute 1-6 g with no dose-response, possibly best about 1 h before exercise [PMID:40852891] [PMID:29546641]. The 50 mg/kg dose has been combined with caffeine [PMID:39948152].
- Liver: 2 g/d for cramps [PMID:30136291]; 1 g/d for fatigue [PMID:42043864]; 2 g/d for 30 days after liver transplant [PMID:41605371].
- MELAS: 9 or 12 g/d for 52 weeks [PMID:29666206]; 0.25 g/kg/day in early case reports [PMID:23257519].
- Psychiatry: 4 g/d for 12 weeks as an adjunct in first-episode psychosis [PMID:27835719].
- Pediatric epilepsy: 0.05-0.3 g/kg/day (case series) [PMID:7039391].
- Ongoing: 3 g/d for 6 months in a phase II trial with HbA1c and PhenoAge outcomes [PMID:42201902].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Observed Safe Level (OSL): the evidence for no adverse effects is strong up to 3 g/d of supplemental taurine. Higher intakes have been tested without harm but are not enough to support long-term safety [PMID:18325648].
- Meta-analyses found no significant adverse effects versus control (20 and 25 RCTs) [PMID:39148075] [PMID:38755142]. A 2026 review concluded that 1-6 g/d was used in human trials without adverse effects [PMID:42300946].
- High dose: 9-12 g/d for 52 weeks in MELAS gave no severe adverse events [PMID:29666206].
- Cirrhosis: mild adverse events in 11.8% on 1 g/d [PMID:42043864]. None were reported with 2 g/d for cramps [PMID:30136291].
- Pediatric epilepsy series: drowsiness and ataxia in 4 of 25 children on 0.05-0.3 g/kg plus anticonvulsants [PMID:7039391].
- Energy drinks: a single high-volume energy drink raised SBP and prolonged QTc in young adults. Caffeine, but not taurine or glucuronolactone, raised BP, and the effects could not be attributed to taurine alone [PMID:30805607]. In isolated rabbit hearts, taurine shortened repolarization and increased ventricular arrhythmias. This is preclinical only [PMID:35304782]. The ISSN credits caffeine and carbohydrate, not taurine, as the main ergogenic ingredients [PMID:36862943].
- Evidence-quality caveat: a 2017 heart-failure RCT (Ahmadian et al.) was retracted in 2026 and should not be counted [PMID:28118062].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindications were identified in the sources reviewed.
- Advanced chronic kidney disease: the kidney regulates taurine body pool and excretion [PMID:20804616] [PMID:22580723]. Safety data above 3 g/d are limited [PMID:18325648]. This is a precautionary caution rather than a proven harm.
- Children and adolescents taking high-dose energy drinks: the concern is caffeine-driven BP/QTc effects of the drinks, not taurine per se [PMID:30805607].
- Seizure disorders on sedating anticonvulsants: drowsiness and ataxia were reported in children [PMID:7039391].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Antihypertensives: taurine lowers BP by about 3-4 mmHg systolic in meta-analyses [PMID:39148075] [PMID:41275513], so effects may be additive. No direct drug-interaction trial exists.
- Antidiabetics (including metformin): taurine modestly lowers FBG, HbA1c and HOMA-IR [PMID:41275513] [PMID:39796489]. In a diabetic mouse model, taurine improved metformin plus DPP4-inhibitor therapy [PMID:40410431]. Monitor glucose; no human interaction trial.
- Vigabatrin: lowered plasma taurine by 67% in animals, and taurine was low or undetectable in 5 of 6 treated infants. Taurine prevented the drug's retinal phototoxicity in rodents [PMID:19194884]. Case reports describe partial ERG reversal with taurine [PMID:27104484].
- Anticonvulsants and sedatives: GABA-A and glycine-receptor agonism [PMID:30892104] [PMID:11559772]. Drowsiness and ataxia were reported in children on combined therapy [PMID:7039391].
- Antipsychotics: adjunctive 4 g/d improved BPRS in first-episode psychosis with no safety signal [PMID:27835719].
- Antiplatelet drugs (aspirin): taurine inhibits platelet aggregation (review) [PMID:35160118]. Additive bleeding risk is theoretical and not studied clinically.
- Oral iron (ferrous sulfate): adding 1 g/d taurine gave additive Hb and ferritin gains in iron-deficiency anaemia (RCT, n=51) [PMID:12431243].
- Lithium: no PubMed-indexed human or animal study of a taurine-lithium clearance interaction was found. This claim is unverified.
*** Other supplements
- Beta Alanine: it competes with taurine at TauT [PMID:30612046] [PMID:40789850] and lowered muscle taurine in mice [PMID:27444300]. In humans, 6.4 g/d for 24 weeks did not change muscle taurine [PMID:30552505], and a meta-analysis found no effect on muscle taurine [PMID:30980076].
- Caffeine: often co-ingested in energy drinks. In a network meta-analysis, caffeine plus taurine improved anaerobic capacity (g 0.46) and reaction time (g 0.75) more than either alone [PMID:41032459]. Caffeine plus taurine pills lowered heart rate and raised mean arterial pressure [PMID:16699827]. A review argues taurine may blunt caffeine's cardiovascular effects [PMID:24615238].
- Creatine: in a small crossover trial in heat (n=12), taurine plus creatine improved time to exhaustion vs placebo but not repeated sprints [PMID:39972259].
- Magnesium: taurine 3 g/d or magnesium 340 mg/d each improved endothelial progenitor cell function and oxidative stress in healthy men [PMID:27412799].
- N-Acetyl-L-Cysteine (NAC): NAC plus taurine has been used in CKD (open-label RCT, n=69; retrospective study, n=450). The retrospective study reported lower uACR [PMID:35598122] [PMID:42730465].
- B Complex (B6/B9/B12): in astrocytes, taurine raised glutathione only when B9 was adequate. A taurine plus B-vitamin blend improved motivated behavior in an RCT (n=44) [PMID:41889717].
- Vitamin E: a taurine/diltiazem/vitamin E formula slowed visual-field loss in retinitis pigmentosa (n=62) [PMID:12322788].
- CoQ10: compared head-to-head (not combined) in adolescent athletes. CoQ10 lowered MDA and cortisol more, and taurine raised IL-10 more [PMID:42514298].
- Glycine: both are agonists at glycine receptors [PMID:11559772]. Additive inhibitory effects are theoretical.
*** Conditions
- MELAS (m.3243A>G): high-dose taurine is supported by an open-label phase III trial and a Japanese clinical review [PMID:29666206] [PMID:39370837]. The 2026 international consensus nonetheless judged supplement efficacy unproven [PMID:41999163].
- Heart failure: may improve NYHA class and LVEF as an adjunct. The evidence is low quality [PMID:39148075] [PMID:35855073].
- Cirrhosis: reduces cramps [PMID:30136291]. It did not improve fatigue overall [PMID:42043864].
- Chronic kidney disease: low urine taurine marks kidney-allograft injury [PMID:39167563]. A mitochondrial-nephropathy dialysis case improved on taurine [PMID:40237914]. Dosing in advanced CKD is unstudied [PMID:22580723].
- Vegan diet: lower plasma taurine [PMID:3354491].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was identified. Supplementation raises plasma taurine itself [PMID:26781281] [PMID:30136291], and levels peak about 1.5 h after a dose [PMID:22331997]. Timing relative to the last dose therefore matters when taurine is measured as a putative aging biomarker [PMID:40472098].
- Physiological shifts that may show on routine labs are lower glucose/HbA1c, lipids, AST/ALT and CRP [PMID:41275513].

** Genotype notes
- SLC6A6 (TauT) biallelic loss-of-function variants cause early-onset retinal dystrophy with low plasma taurine (-31.7 umol/L vs carriers). Affected patients may be candidates for investigational oral taurine [PMID:41343195].
- A 2026 GWAS meta-analysis (n=36,490) found 8 loci for circulating taurine. Mendelian randomization suggested links to hypertension, cancers and brain phenotypes [PMID:42834262].
- SLC6A6 lies at a dilated-cardiomyopathy GWAS locus [PMID:35855073].
- MELAS m.3243A>G (mitochondrial DNA): see Conditions [PMID:29666206].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Cardiometabolic MA (34 RCTs): FBG -5.90 mg/dL, HbA1c -0.21%, SBP -4.38 mmHg, CRP SMD -1.26; 1.5-3 g/d appeared best [PMID:41275513].
- [2026-10] GWAS meta-analysis of circulating taurine (n=36,490): 8 loci; phenome-wide MR signals [PMID:42834262].
- [2026-08] Retrospective NAC plus taurine study in CKD (n=450): uACR -8.6% vs +11.3% with standard care [PMID:42730465].
- [2026-07] Food Funct review: 1-6 g/d used without adverse effects; calls taurine a healthspan modulator, which is not yet proven in humans [PMID:42300946].
- [2026-07] RCT in obese men (n=44): exercise plus 3 g/d taurine gave the largest falls in asprosin and body fat [PMID:42514394].
- [2026-05] Phase II protocol: 3 g/d for 6 months with HbA1c, PhenoAge and BP outcomes (n=80 planned) [PMID:42201902].
- [2026-05] Open-label RCT in decompensated cirrhosis (n=220): no overall effect on fatigue [PMID:42043864].
- [2026-04] International MELAS consensus: L-taurine efficacy remains unproven [PMID:41999163].
- [2026-04] Liver-transplant RCT (n=169): 2 g/d improved AST, bilirubin, INR and length of stay [PMID:41605371].
- [2026-03] Long-COVID SR/MA (27 trials, n=1030): taurine improved metabolic and inflammatory markers; plasma taurine lower in PASC (SMD -0.35) [PMID:41803812].
- [2026-03] Taurine plus B6/B9/B12 RCT (n=44): improved effortful motivation [PMID:41889717].
- [2026-01] SLC6A6 retinopathy series: loss of taurine transport, low plasma taurine [PMID:41343195].
- [2025-11] Malmo cohort (n=27,786): no link between taurine intake or plasma level and dementia [PMID:41250277].
- [2025-10] Aging Cell (n=137 men, aged 20-93): circulating taurine was not associated with age, muscle or mitochondrial function [PMID:41061678].
- [2025-10] Caffeine plus taurine network MA: combination best for anaerobic capacity and reaction time [PMID:41032459].
- [2025-09] Acute-dose exercise MA (23 RCTs): g 0.25, GRADE low to very low [PMID:40852891].
- [2025-08] Cryo-EM structures of TauT with taurine, beta-alanine and GABA [PMID:40789850].
- [2025-06] Science (Fernandez et al.): taurine rose or stayed stable with age in 3 human cohorts, monkeys and mice, so it is not a universal aging biomarker [PMID:40472098].
- [2025-06] Cognition MA (7 RCTs): no effect [PMID:40320621].
- [2025-05] T2D RCT (n=165, 2.4 g/d): lower SBP, better vasodilation and PWV [PMID:40546935].
- [2024-12] Obesity MA (9 RCTs): lower TG, TC and insulin; HbA1c effect only in obesity [PMID:39796489].
- [2024-11] SR of cirrhosis-cramp RCTs ranks taurine among effective options [PMID:39548657].
- [2024-10] Review: high-dose taurine for MELAS [PMID:39370837].

** Open questions
- Does taurine supplementation slow any aging outcome in humans? The Singh 2023 premise of age-related decline was not replicated in human cohorts [PMID:37289866] [PMID:40472098] [PMID:41061678].
- Hard outcomes (CV events, mortality) are untested. Most RCTs are small, short and use surrogate markers [PMID:35855073] [PMID:41275513].
- Long-term safety above 3 g/d in healthy adults [PMID:18325648].
- MELAS efficacy needs a controlled trial [PMID:41999163].
- Clinical relevance of beta-alanine competition at TauT in humans [PMID:30980076].
- Dosing in CKD, and whether interactions with lithium or antihypertensives are real (no direct data found).
