** Identity & forms
- Eurycoma longifolia Jack (Simaroubaceae) is a shrubby tree native to Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Cambodia, Myanmar, Laos and Thailand. The root is used mainly for sexual dysfunction, aging, fatigue and fever [PMID:26978330] [PMID:20434529]. LiverTox lists "Long Jack" as a synonym and notes it is promoted in the US to raise testosterone and athletic performance [PMID:39527684].
- Bioactives: quassinoids make up most of the root phytochemicals (eurycomanone, 13,21-dihydroeurycomanone, eurycomanol, longilactone). Other classes are canthin-6-one and beta-carboline alkaloids, tirucallane triterpenes, squalene derivatives and bioactive steroids [PMID:26978330] [PMID:36687023]. Eurycomanone was the most abundant quassinoid in all supplement tablets/capsules tested by LC-MS/MS [PMID:25914245].
- Standardized extracts used in trials:
  - Physta: a freeze-dried, standardized hot-water root extract. It was used at 100-300 mg/d in men [PMID:23243445] [PMID:34262417] and 50-100 mg/d in women [PMID:41283187].
  - The same aqueous extract (EU novel-food dossier) contains 40-65% glycosaponins and 0.8-1.5% eurycomanone, with canthin-6-one alkaloids and isoscopoletin, at a proposed 200 mg/d [PMID:34987621].
  - Malaysian Standard MS 2409 water extract (pharmacokinetic work) [PMID:29997335]. Quassinoid-rich TAF273 extract (rat toxicology) [PMID:24318772].
  - China: a 2024 TCM-properties review proposes 2-10 g/d of root as a decoction [PMID:39701690].
- Product quality varies widely:
  - DNA barcoding: only 37% of Malaysian herbal medicinal products were authentic, and 27% were adulterated [PMID:30058427].
  - HPLC: only 5 of 8 randomly purchased products contained detectable E. longifolia compounds [PMID:29154863].
  - UHPLC-MS: commercial products clustered apart from authentic root [PMID:39369924].
  - NIR screening flagged a sildenafil analogue in 2 internet-bought products [PMID:24731345].

** Mechanisms
- Steroidogenesis: in rat Leydig cells, eurycomanone raised testosterone dose-dependently (0.1-10 uM). The authors attribute this to aromatase inhibition and, at high concentrations, phosphodiesterase inhibition [PMID:23810842].
- Hormonal axes: in young men, 600 mg/d for 2 weeks raised total and free testosterone and estradiol without changing LH, FSH or SHBG. The authors suggest an adrenal (HPA) rather than gonadal (HPG) route [PMID:33559971]. In older women, the rise in free testosterone was attributed to lower SHBG [PMID:23754792].
- Erectile tissue (preclinical):
  - Canthin-6-one alkaloids inhibit PDE-5 (IC50 2.86-4.66 uM) [PMID:35652145].
  - 9-hydroxycanthin-6-one relaxes rat corpus cavernosum by interfering with Ca2+ mobilization, independent of NO/cGMP [PMID:21569213].
  - An aqueous extract raised brain cortical/hippocampal dopamine and serum testosterone in rats [PMID:31275418].
- Vascular: a dichloromethane subfraction relaxed rat aorta through ACE inhibition and AT1-receptor antagonism [PMID:27800486].
- Bone (rat orchidectomy/castration models): preserved bone calcium, cortical thickness and biomechanics, in some measures comparably to testosterone [PMID:20874437] [PMID:29689767] [PMID:22844328].
- Glucose: antihyperglycemic in db/db mice through PDX1-linked beta-cell expansion [PMID:32708678]. Lowered glucose in STZ-hyperglycemic rats only at 150 mg/kg, with no effect in normoglycemic rats [PMID:15507337].
- Prostate cells: a quassinoid composition (SQ40) inhibited LNCaP growth and androgen-receptor nuclear translocation and lowered PSA secretion [PMID:25826409]. Isolated quassinoids reduced prostate weight in a testosterone-induced BPH rat model [PMID:36931528].
- Sleep/wake (mice): wake consolidation in the active period and more REM/NREM consolidation at rest, with circadian tau prolonged [PMID:37968729] [PMID:39055967]. No human sleep trial was found.
- Pharmacokinetics: oral eurycomanone has low bioavailability, 10.5% in rats [PMID:16206032], 11.8% in rats and 54.9% in mice, with t1/2 about 0.30 h [PMID:29997335]. Human PK data are lacking.

** Dosing in trials
- Testosterone/aging male: 100-200 mg/d Physta for 12 weeks [PMID:34262417]. 200 mg/d standardized water extract for 1 month in LOH [PMID:21671978]. 200 mg/d for 6 months with concurrent training [PMID:33541567].
- Young men: 600 mg/d for 2 weeks [PMID:33559971]. 200 mg/d for 8 weeks with resistance training [PMID:31367282]. 400 mg/d (4 x 100 mg) for 7 days in rugby players [PMID:37398958].
- Sexual well-being: 300 mg/d Physta for 12 weeks [PMID:23243445].
- Infertility: 200 mg/d for up to 9 months [PMID:20348942].
- Stress: standardized hot-water root extract for 4 weeks [PMID:23705671].
- Seniors: 400 mg/d for 5 weeks [PMID:23754792].
- Women: 50-100 mg/d Physta for 12 weeks [PMID:41283187].
- EU novel-food application: up to 200 mg/d in adults, excluding pregnancy and lactation [PMID:34987621].
- A rat-derived acceptable daily intake of up to 1.2 g/adult/day was proposed for powdered root [PMID:24062779].

** Safety & adverse effects
- RCTs (4-24 weeks) report safety labs (blood count, lipids, liver and renal function) comparable to placebo [PMID:34262417] [PMID:23243445] [PMID:24550993] [PMID:41283187]. Adverse events were similar between arms (7 vs 6) [PMID:30574050]. Trials are small and many are manufacturer-linked, so rare harms cannot be excluded.
- Hepatotoxicity:
  - First published case (2024): a 47-year-old man developed jaundice and raised liver enzymes soon after starting tongkat ali, and improved after stopping [PMID:38646387].
  - LiverTox (updated 2024) notes recent reports of clinically apparent liver injury in body builders [PMID:39527684].
- Cardiac: probable case of new-onset atrial flutter in a 71-year-old after starting the supplement (2025) [PMID:41080360].
- Genotoxicity (EFSA 2021): the aqueous root extract was positive in an in vitro chromosome aberration test and in an in vivo comet assay at 2000 mg/kg (stomach, duodenum). EFSA concluded it "has the potential to induce DNA damage" and that its safety "has not been established under any condition of use" [PMID:34987621]. An earlier study of powdered root found no mutagenicity or clastogenicity [PMID:24062779].
- Animal toxicology:
  - Powdered root: LD50 >6 g/kg. No adverse effects at up to 2 g/kg/day for 13 weeks, though prothrombin time and partial thromboplastin time were reduced [PMID:24062779].
  - Quassinoid-rich extract TAF273: LD50 1293 mg/kg (female) and >2000 mg/kg (male). Reproductive/teratology NOAEL 100 mg/kg/day; no fetal toxicity [PMID:24318772].
  - Tongkat ali coffee: NOAEL 1 g/kg. Lower platelets in chronic-dosed males and some renal/liver marker changes [PMID:33066137].
  - Tongkat ali + Labisia combination: NOAEL >500 but not more than 2000 mg/kg [PMID:33579048].
  - Eurycomanone was the most toxic quassinoid in toxicity-guided fractionation [PMID:12143009].
- Heavy metals (Malaysia):
  - 36 of 100 tongkat ali products had 0.52-5.30 ppm mercury, above the 0.5 ppm limit; 5 of them were registered products [PMID:15162849].
  - 26 of 100 "tongkat ali hitam" products had 0.53-2.35 ppm mercury [PMID:16567029].
  - Arsenic and lead were not detected in two root samples [PMID:22468741].
- Adulteration: undeclared sildenafil analogues [PMID:24731345] and non-authentic material [PMID:30058427] add risk that is independent of the plant itself.
- Sperm in vitro: no harm at therapeutic concentrations (<2.5 ug/mL). Possible harm at very high concentrations [PMID:22332826].

** Contraindications
- Pregnancy and lactation: excluded from the EU novel-food target population, and EFSA did not establish safety [PMID:34987621]. No human data. Rat teratology was reassuring at <=100 mg/kg [PMID:24318772].
- Prostate cancer or other androgen-sensitive malignancy: raises testosterone [PMID:36013514]. A review notes prostate cancer is a debated absolute contraindication for testosterone therapy [PMID:24386995]. Anti-proliferative LNCaP data are in vitro only [PMID:25826409].
- Liver disease or prior herb-induced liver injury: case report and LiverTox entry [PMID:38646387] [PMID:39527684].
- Children/adolescents: no trial data. The EU application targeted adults only [PMID:34987621].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Propranolol: crossover RCT in 14 healthy men. 200 mg aqueous extract reduced propranolol AUC by 29% and Cmax by 42%, and prolonged Tmax by about 86%, through reduced absorption. Pharmacodynamics were unchanged in healthy volunteers, but the authors advise caution [PMID:21054461]. Other orally absorbed beta-blockers are not studied.
- CYP-metabolized drugs (in vitro only):
  - Methanolic root extract weakly inhibited CYP1A2, CYP2A6 and CYP2C19 (IC50 324.9, 797.1 and 562.9 ug/mL) [PMID:26240600].
  - Eurycomanone did not potently inhibit CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2E1 or 3A4 (IC50 >250 ug/mL) [PMID:23881640].
  - Extract inhibited CYP2C8 (amodiaquine N-desethylation) with a Ki 4 times that of quercetin [PMID:27695271].
  - No human CYP probe study exists.
- Testosterone/androgen therapy: additive androgen exposure is plausible [PMID:36013514] [PMID:34262417]. In orchidectomized rats, EL plus low-dose testosterone improved bone strain [PMID:22924057]. No human combination study.
- Estrogen-modulating drugs (aromatase inhibitors, estrogens): eurycomanone enhanced formestane-type aromatase inhibition in vitro [PMID:23810842]. In young men, estradiol rose [PMID:33559971]. The net direction is unclear.
- PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil etc.): canthin-6-ones inhibit PDE-5 in vitro [PMID:35652145], and some products are adulterated with sildenafil analogues [PMID:24731345]. Hidden additive exposure is possible.
- Antihypertensives: ACE inhibition and vasorelaxation in rat aorta [PMID:27800486]. Mechanistic only.
- Antidiabetics: glucose lowering in diabetic rodents [PMID:32708678] [PMID:15507337]. Mechanistic only. HbA1c was a secondary endpoint in one RCT, with no reported effect [PMID:34262417].
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets: rats showed shortened PT/aPTT [PMID:24062779], and lower platelets with tongkat ali coffee [PMID:33066137]. No human data. The clinical direction is unclear.
- Hepatotoxic drugs: additive liver risk is unstudied. Liver injury has been reported [PMID:38646387].
*** Other supplements
- Ashwagandha: both are classed as "possibly effective" testosterone boosters in healthy men [PMID:37697053] and as modest testosterone raisers in a 2026 review [PMID:42575271]. Additive androgen effect is a mechanistic inference.
- DHEA: Physta raised DHEA within-group [PMID:34262417]. Combined androgen-precursor load is a mechanistic inference; no interaction study.
- Labisia pumila (kacip fatimah): combination product tested for menopause [PMID:33061884] and in rat toxicology [PMID:33579048]. Labisia also inhibits CYP2C8, more potently than EL [PMID:27695271].
- Polygonum minus: combination RCT for sexual performance [PMID:24550993].
- Zinc, magnesium: in the testosterone-booster SR, most such agents did not raise testosterone. No interaction data with tongkat ali [PMID:37697053].
*** Conditions
- Androgen-sensitive cancers (prostate, breast), pregnancy/lactation, liver disease: see Contraindications.
- Cardiac arrhythmia: a single probable atrial flutter case [PMID:41080360].
- Sports anti-doping: in 8 weeks at 200 mg/d, the urinary testosterone:epitestosterone ratio was unchanged [PMID:31367282]. Contamination or adulteration of products remains a separate risk [PMID:30058427].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. True physiological shifts reported:
  - Total and free testosterone up [PMID:34262417] [PMID:33559971].
  - Estradiol up in young men [PMID:33559971].
  - SHBG down in older women [PMID:23754792].
  - DHEA up and cortisol down [PMID:34262417] [PMID:23705671].
  - T-cell counts up [PMID:26816234].
- Liver enzymes and bilirubin should be checked if symptoms of liver injury appear [PMID:38646387].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Structured critical review of androgen-modulating botanicals: standardized E. longifolia has limited-to-moderate evidence for modest testosterone increases in selected men (low baseline T, aging, infertility, stress). Durable functional benefit is inconsistent and cannot be generalized across extracts [PMID:42575271].
- [2025-11] RCT in 138 menopausal women: Physta 100 mg lowered MENQOL by 33.9% (P=0.049), with no change in reproductive hormones or safety labs [PMID:41283187].
- [2025-09] Probable tongkat ali-induced atrial flutter in a 71-year-old man [PMID:41080360].
- [2025-02] Indonesian SR plus network pharmacology: stigmasterol, eurycomanone and eurycomalactone are predicted to target 13 erectile-dysfunction genes. This is in silico [PMID:40007786].
- [2025-01] Metabolomics: commercial products differ chemically from authentic root. Root extract protected against 5-FU testicular toxicity in rats [PMID:39369924].
- [2024-10] LiverTox entry updated: liver injury attributed to tongkat ali in body builders [PMID:39527684].
- [2024-09] Chinese TCM-properties review proposes introducing the root as a new medicinal (2-10 g/d decoction) [PMID:39701690].
- [2024-06] SR of 27 testosterone boosters: E. longifolia is "possibly effective" for LOH and healthy men. Most other boosters fail [PMID:37697053].
- Registered trials (ClinicalTrials.gov): NCT06100432 (E. longifolia DLBS5055 + antioxidant vitamins in infertile men; active, not recruiting) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06100432]. NCT04584918 (Euryco-10 in senior men; status unknown) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04584918]. NCT05347329 (Tongkat Ali Maca Plus; completed, results not found in PubMed) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05347329].

** Open questions
- Size and durability of the testosterone effect in independent, preregistered trials. The 2022 MA pooled only 5 small RCTs with high heterogeneity, and many trials used one manufacturer's extract.
- Whether hormonal changes translate into clinical outcomes: sexual function, fertility (live birth), muscle, bone.
- Genotoxicity: the EFSA comet-assay signal at first-contact tissues [PMID:34987621] has not been resolved by newer data.
- Incidence of liver injury. Only isolated case reports exist [PMID:38646387].
- Human pharmacokinetics of eurycomanone and other markers. Human CYP/transporter interaction studies beyond the single propranolol study.
- Long-term safety in men with prostate disease and in androgen-sensitive conditions.
- Contamination (mercury) and adulteration (PDE-5 analogues): no recent surveys outside Malaysia were found.
- Effects of eurypeptides and other non-quassinoid fractions: no cited human data were found.
