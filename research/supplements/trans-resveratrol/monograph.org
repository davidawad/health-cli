** Identity & forms
- Trans-resveratrol is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: resveratrol, trans-3,5,4'-trihydroxystilbene. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [PMID:38255828, PMID:39288907].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [PMID:38255828, PMID:39288907].

- Systematic review catalogued 194 clinical studies but found sparse interaction testing and substantial heterogeneity in doses, populations and endpoints [PMID:38255828].
- Metabolic/cardiovascular surrogate findings are mixed; no robust evidence for lifespan extension or prevention of clinical events. Certainty low/very low due to heterogeneity [PMID:38255828].

** Dosing in trials
- Trials range from tens of mg to multi-gram daily, commonly 150–1,000 mg/day for weeks/months; higher doses increase GI effects [PMID:38255828].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI symptoms (nausea, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort) are the most frequent reported adverse effects; long-term high-dose safety remains uncertain [PMID:38255828].

** Contraindications
- Caution in pregnancy/lactation due to inadequate supplement trial data [PMID:38255828].

** Interactions
- Possible antiplatelet effects and CYP3A4/2E1 modulation are mainly mechanistic; oral anticoagulant interaction evidence is generally low quality [PMID:38255828, PMID:39288907].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2024 systematic review emphasizes gaps in dose-response, safety and interaction characterization across human studies [PMID:38255828].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
