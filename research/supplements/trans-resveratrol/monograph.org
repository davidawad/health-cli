** Identity & forms
- Trans-resveratrol (trans-3,5,4'-trihydroxystilbene) is a stilbene polyphenol found in grapes and red wine. It is sold as a purified supplement. About 200 clinical studies across at least 24 indications have used purified resveratrol, but there is no consensus regimen for any condition [PMID:38255828].
- Oral absorption is high but systemic bioavailability is very low:
  - In a 14C study, at least 70% of a 25 mg dose was absorbed. Only trace unchanged resveratrol (<5 ng/mL) appeared in plasma, because the intestine and liver rapidly sulfate- and glucuronide-conjugate it. Gut microflora also hydrogenate it to dihydroresveratrol [PMID:15333514].
  - A single 5 g dose gave a peak of 539 ng/mL (2.4 umol/L). Sulfate and glucuronide metabolite AUCs were up to 23-fold higher than the parent compound [PMID:17548692].
  - A 2025 meta-analysis of 84 oral administrations (25-5000 mg) found free resveratrol exposure rises linearly with dose. Mean Cmax was about 31 ng/mL, and heterogeneity between studies was substantial [PMID:39557444].
- Micronized resveratrol (SRT501, 5 g/d) gave about 3.6-fold higher plasma levels than equivalent non-micronized doses (mean 1942 ng/mL), and the drug was detectable in liver tissue [PMID:21680702].
- Formulation, dose and population differ widely between trials, which limits comparisons across them [PMID:38255828] [PMID:39557444].

** Mechanisms
- SIRT1 controversy:
  - Resveratrol's apparent activation of SIRT1/Sir2 in vitro depended on the non-physiological Fluor de Lys fluorophore. It had no effect on Sir2 activity or yeast life span in vivo [PMID:15684413].
  - Two independent groups found that resveratrol does not activate SIRT1 with native peptide or full-length protein substrates (p53, acetyl-CoA synthetase, PGC-1alpha). It has multiple off-target activities [PMID:20061378] [PMID:19843076].
  - Later work showed allosteric SIRT1 activation that requires hydrophobic substrate motifs and SIRT1 Glu230. This restored a direct, substrate-dependent mechanism [PMID:23471411]. A 2022 review still describes resveratrol as an allosteric SIRT1 activator with poor pharmacokinetics [PMID:36522127].
  - In humans, a GRADE-assessed meta-analysis (11 RCTs) found no significant effect on SIRT1 gene expression (SMD 0.05, CI -0.24 to 0.34), protein expression or serum levels [PMID:40158656].
- Calorie-restriction mimicry: in 11 obese men, 150 mg/d for 30 days activated muscle AMPK, raised SIRT1/PGC-1alpha protein and lowered liver fat, glucose and systolic BP [PMID:22055504]. A 4-week high-dose trial in 24 obese men found no effect on insulin sensitivity (clamp), BP, energy expenditure, fat depots or inflammatory markers [PMID:23193181].
- Lifespan: in the NIA Interventions Testing Program, resveratrol at 300 or 1200 ppm in food did not extend life span in genetically heterogeneous mice. Rapamycin did [PMID:20974732].
- Estrogen receptor agonism: at about 3-10 uM resveratrol binds ER and activates estrogen-responsive transcription. It acted as a superagonist in MCF-7 cells and stimulated proliferation of estrogen-dependent T47D cells [PMID:9391166]. It is a type I ER agonist with additional ER-independent growth-inhibitory pathways [PMID:12594813]. Typical plasma levels after oral dosing are far lower than these concentrations [PMID:17548692].
- Platelets: inhibits arachidonic-acid-induced aggregation at the COX-1 level [PMID:19652885]. It reduces collagen-induced thrombus formation and TXA2 release in vitro, including in platelets from people with T2D [PMID:35458194].
- Drug metabolism: 1 g/d for 4 weeks inhibited CYP3A4, CYP2D6 and CYP2C9 and induced CYP1A2 in healthy volunteers [PMID:20716633].

** Dosing in trials
- Supplement-type doses: 75 mg twice daily for 12 months (postmenopausal bone/cognition) [PMID:32564438]; 150 mg/d (obese men) [PMID:22055504]; 250 mg/d with exercise training [PMID:23878368]; 500 mg/d (knee OA, exercise) [PMID:41037125] [PMID:28505227].
- High doses: 1 g/d for 4 weeks (CYP phenotyping) [PMID:20716633]; 500 mg/d escalated to 1 g twice daily over 52 weeks in Alzheimer disease [PMID:26362286]; single doses up to 5 g [PMID:17548692]; micronized SRT501 5 g/d [PMID:21680702].
- Dose dependence:
  - BP effects were seen mainly at >=300 mg/d [PMID:29359958].
  - CRP fell more with >=500 mg/d for >=10 weeks [PMID:34472150].
  - Bone formation marker (BAP) rose dose-dependently with 150 vs 1000 mg/d [PMID:25322274].
- Reviews conclude resveratrol is generally well tolerated up to 1 g/d [PMID:38255828]. A bioavailability MA suggests 100-500 mg doses give exposures similar to the mean across all doses [PMID:39557444].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Common: GI effects. Nausea, diarrhea and weight loss were the most frequent AEs at up to 2 g/d in the Alzheimer trial [PMID:26362286]. Single doses up to 5 g caused no serious AEs [PMID:17548692].
- Older adults: a 10-RCT systematic review found no significant AEs. However, high doses raised some CVD-risk biomarkers in overweight people [PMID:38433010].
- Liver: ALT/AST rose in the resveratrol arm of an 8-week NAFLD RCT [PMID:24582567]. A NAFLD MA also found a significant ALT increase [PMID:32823621]. One serious AE (fever, bicytopenia) occurred in a high-dose NAFLD RCT [PMID:26784973].
- Nephrotoxicity in multiple myeloma (SRT501):
  - A phase 2 trial of SRT501 5 g/d, alone or with bortezomib, in relapsed/refractory myeloma (n=24) was terminated [PMID:23205612] [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00920556].
  - A 2026 review reports that it was stopped early for unexpected cast nephropathy/renal failure and that the cause of the toxicity is unclear [PMID:42542881].
  - In rats, 3000 mg/kg/d caused nephropathy and raised BUN/creatinine. The NOAEL was 300 mg/kg/d [PMID:15329443].
  - In contrast, a 31-RCT MA in general populations found no renal harm; BUN fell slightly [PMID:40580309].
- Exercise: 250 mg/d blunted training-induced gains in VO2max, BP and lipids in aged men [PMID:23878368]. It also blunted training-induced falls in muscle protein carbonylation and TNF-alpha mRNA [PMID:24514907].
- Brain volume: the Alzheimer RCT reported greater brain-volume loss with resveratrol. Its interpretation is unclear [PMID:26362286].

** Contraindications
- Multiple myeloma or other high renal-risk states with high-dose (gram-level) resveratrol: SRT501 trial terminated for renal failure [PMID:23205612] [PMID:42542881].
- Estrogen-receptor-positive cancers or other estrogen-sensitive conditions: relative caution, because resveratrol is an ER agonist that stimulated ER+ breast cancer cell growth in vitro. Clinical relevance at achievable plasma levels is unproven [PMID:9391166] [PMID:12594813].
- Pregnancy and lactation: no human safety data were identified in the sources retrieved for this monograph.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- CYP3A4 substrates (buspirone was the probe; sirolimus/Rapamycin is one such substrate): 1 g/d inhibited CYP3A4 activity in healthy volunteers [PMID:20716633]. Intestinal CYP inhibition may cut first-pass metabolism and raise exposure to co-administered substrates, especially at high doses [PMID:22788578]. No direct sirolimus study was found.
- CYP2C9 substrates (losartan was the probe; S-warfarin is one such substrate): CYP2C9 inhibition was shown at 1 g/d [PMID:20716633]. With warfarin, this adds to antiplatelet effects [PMID:35458194]. Monitor INR. No clinical warfarin study was found.
- CYP2D6 substrates (dextromethorphan probe): inhibited at 1 g/d [PMID:20716633].
- CYP1A2 substrates (caffeine was the probe): induced at 1 g/d, so exposure may fall [PMID:20716633].
- Antiplatelets/anticoagulants: resveratrol inhibited collagen- and epinephrine-induced aggregation of platelets from aspirin-resistant patients [PMID:16954814]. It also reduced TXA2 and thrombus formation in vitro [PMID:35458194]. All of this is ex vivo/in vitro, so bleeding risk is theoretical.
- Bortezomib (multiple myeloma): renal failure in the SRT501 combination trial [PMID:23205612] [PMID:42542881].
- Glucose-lowering drugs: modest glucose/HbA1c effects in diabetes [PMID:24695890] [PMID:34320173]. Additive hypoglycemia is unlikely but possible.
- Metformin: a 6-month RCT in PCOS combined resveratrol with metformin. The combination lowered fasting glucose more than resveratrol alone, and no unexpected toxicity was reported [PMID:40888876]. Preclinical data suggest additive AMPK/SIRT1 effects [PMID:32168855].
*** Other supplements
- Aspirin (corpus item), quercetin: resveratrol and quercetin both inhibit platelet COX-1-dependent aggregation. A polyphenol mixture potentiated sub-inhibitory aspirin [PMID:19652885] [PMID:16954814].
- NMN / nicotinamide riboside: combining NAD+ precursors with SIRT1-activating nutraceuticals such as resveratrol is proposed as complementary [PMID:36522127]. Evidence is limited to a rat diabetic-wound study where NR plus resveratrol outperformed either alone [PMID:38583241]. No human combination trial was found, and human SIRT1 is not measurably changed by resveratrol [PMID:40158656].
- Caffeine: CYP1A2 induction may speed caffeine clearance [PMID:20716633].
*** Conditions
- Multiple myeloma / renal impairment, estrogen-sensitive cancers: see Contraindications.
- NAFLD: ALT may rise rather than fall [PMID:24582567] [PMID:32823621].
- People doing endurance or high-intensity training: possible blunting of cardiovascular adaptations [PMID:23878368] [PMID:24514907]. One trial found enhanced muscle adaptations instead [PMID:28505227].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytical assay interference was found. Physiological changes to note:
  - ALT/AST may rise [PMID:24582567].
  - Bone alkaline phosphatase rises at 1 g/d [PMID:25322274].
  - CRP falls slightly [PMID:34472150].
  - Drug levels measured for CYP substrates may change [PMID:20716633].

** Latest research
- [2026-07] Nanoformulation review: the SRT501 plus bortezomib myeloma trial was terminated early for unexpected cast nephropathy/renal failure of ambiguous cause [PMID:42542881].
- [2026-06] T2D umbrella review/MA (10 MAs): no significant effect on FBG, HbA1c, HOMA-IR or most lipids; LDL-C reduced only in SMD analysis [PMID:42268476].
- [2026-04] Umbrella review (45 SRs, 129 associations): 4 high-certainty findings: WC -0.80 cm, TC -0.19 mmol/L in overweight adults, and SBP -7.97 / DBP -3.55 mmHg in T2D [PMID:41987155].
- [2026-03] MA of 40 RCTs (n=2551) in obesity-related disease: reductions in HOMA-IR, lipids, BP, hs-CRP, TNF-alpha and IL-6; no effect on liver enzymes or liver fat [PMID:41760304].
- [2026-03] Overweight/obesity MA (23 RCTs): only waist circumference fell (-1.93 cm) [PMID:41455817].
- [2026-01] PCOS RCT: resveratrol plus metformin vs resveratrol alone; the combination improved fasting glucose and MDA [PMID:40888876].
- [2025-12] Renal-function dose-response MA (31 RCTs): BUN -1.20 mg/dL; no harm signal [PMID:40580309].
- [2025-12] Bayesian NMA in T2D (17 studies): resveratrol ranked highly for HOMA-IR, lipids and BP vs curcumin, silymarin and berberine [PMID:40439602].
- [2025-11] Knee OA RCT (n=137): 500 mg/d improved pain, function and plasma SIRT1 [PMID:41037125].
- [2025-09] GRADE MA of 11 RCTs: no effect of resveratrol on human SIRT1 gene expression, protein or serum level [PMID:40158656].
- [2025-08] Crossover RCT in pre-/stage-1 hypertension: 1 g/d for 4 weeks raised NO but did not lower BP [PMID:40854984].
- [2025-07] Postmenopausal MA (10 RCTs): lower pain and CTX; no effect on cognition, metabolic markers or BP [PMID:40771919].
- [2025-05] Alzheimer RCT CSF sub-analysis: lower TREM2, MMP-9 and neuronal-damage markers [PMID:40507855].
- [2025-01] Bioavailability MA (84 administrations): linear dose-exposure; mean Cmax about 31 ng/mL [PMID:39557444].
- [2024-12] Lipid umbrella MA: TC and TG reduced by SMD only; judged clinically unimportant [PMID:39255906].
- [2024-11] MASLD MA: resveratrol improved steatosis (OR 3.18) but not ALT [PMID:39723101].
- [2024-10] CAD MA: TNF-alpha reduced in primary prevention; IL-6 inconclusive [PMID:38958883].

** Open questions
- Whether any human benefit is mediated by SIRT1, given negative native-substrate assays and null human SIRT1 MA [PMID:20061378] [PMID:40158656].
- Whether conjugated metabolites (sulfates/glucuronides), rather than the parent compound, carry activity [PMID:17548692] [PMID:15333514].
- Mechanism and dose threshold of the myeloma renal failure. Safety of gram-level doses in people with reduced renal reserve.
- Clinical significance of CYP3A4/2C9 inhibition at common supplement doses (<=500 mg/d). Only 1 g/d has been phenotyped [PMID:20716633].
- Whether resveratrol blunts or enhances exercise adaptations, and in whom [PMID:23878368] [PMID:28505227].
- Long-term (>1 y) outcome trials. Existing trials use surrogate endpoints, are small and heterogeneous [PMID:38255828] [PMID:31978258].
- Clinical relevance of ER agonism at achievable plasma levels.
