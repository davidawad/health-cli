** Identity & forms
- "Tributryn" is the corpus spelling of tributyrin. Tributyrin is a triglyceride with three butyrate molecules esterified to glycerol at positions 1, 2 and 3 [PMID:9533530]. It is a neutral SCFA triglyceride that occurs naturally in butter [PMID:41271518].
- It is a butyrate prodrug. It resists gastric acid, and pancreatic lipases convert it to butyrate [PMID:41473189] [PMID:42817105].
  - Free butyric acid has an unpleasant odor and taste, poor GI stability and rapid systemic metabolism. Prodrugs such as tributyrin are designed to get around these problems [PMID:42817105].
  - In a sponsor-funded in vitro upper-GI simulation of CoreBiome, 40.9% (capsule) or 48.7% (softgel) was hydrolyzed in the small intestine. The remaining 59.1% and 51.3% stayed intact and could reach the colon [PMID:41473189].
- Branded forms in the literature:
  - CoreBiome (capsule/softgel): in vitro data only [PMID:41473189].
  - ButyraGen: a "tributyrin complex" also containing partially hydrolyzed guar fiber and acacia fiber. Used in a 2024 healthy-adult pilot [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005] [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05601635].
  - Pharmaceutical-grade oral tributyrin: used in phase I oncology trials [PMID:9533530] [PMID:12736763].
- Category choice: classed as probiotic-prebiotic (gut-microbiome/"postbiotic" group) to match the corpus entry ProButyrate. Butyrate and other SCFAs are described as postbiotics, i.e. microbial fermentation metabolites [PMID:42817105]. Tributyrin is not a live organism or a fermentable fiber.
- Related butyrate forms often used in human trials, labelled as such below: sodium butyrate (often microencapsulated or in a triglyceride matrix), calcium butyrate, and butyrylated starch (HAMSB). Results from these salts and esters cannot be assumed to transfer to tributyrin [PMID:42817105] [PMID:40804708].

** Mechanisms
- Butyrate is a short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) made by colonic bacterial fermentation of dietary fiber. It has three main modes of action: energy substrate, histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor, and ligand for G-protein-coupled receptors [PMID:27259147] [PMID:36502573].
- Colonocyte fuel: butyrate is the main energy source for colonic epithelial cells [PMID:36502573] [PMID:10795763]. MCT1 and SMCT1 transporters take it up [PMID:30915065].
- Receptors: GPR41 (FFAR3), GPR43 (FFAR2) and GPR109A (HCAR2) mediate immune and barrier signaling. Transgenic mouse models support a role for these receptors in intestinal inflammation [PMID:30915065].
- HDAC inhibition and epigenetics drive differentiation and apoptosis in cancer cells in vitro. This was the rationale for the oncology trials [PMID:12736763] [PMID:29438462].
  - In Caco-2 cells, tributyrin was more potent than butyrate. It upregulated the vitamin D receptor and acted synergistically with calcitriol [PMID:11385076].
- Systemic availability is limited because colonocytes metabolize oral butyrate rapidly [PMID:41271518].
  - Oral tributyrin gave peak plasma butyrate of 0-0.45 mM within 0.25-3 h. Butyrate was gone by 5 h [PMID:9533530].
  - Three-times-daily dosing gave a median of 52 uM, with wide variation between patients [PMID:12736763].
  - In rodents, the plasma butyrate AUC rose more than proportionally with tributyrin dose (non-linear PK) [PMID:10321503].
  - Oral SCFA supplements (not tributyrin) peaked in serum within 60 min and returned to baseline by 120 min. Acid-resistant coating delayed and blunted the peak. One week of twice-daily dosing did not raise baseline serum SCFA [PMID:39498577].
- Target engagement: in an open-label PD study, [11C]butyrate PET showed organ-specific changes in butyrate uptake (brain, liver, heart, GI) after 30 days of tributyrin [PMID:41271518].
- Immune and inflammatory effects (preclinical and ex vivo):
  - Tributyrin and sodium butyrate reduced LPS-induced cytokines in explants of human visceral fat [PMID:38987636].
  - In mice, oral tributyrin expanded regulatory T cells and prolonged skin and heart allograft survival [PMID:41554342].
  - Tributyrin extended lifespan in mice with mitochondrial deficiency (TFAM knockout) and restored intestinal histone acylation [PMID:41826362].
- Microbiome (in vitro SHIME): 3 weeks of tributyrin increased butyrate, Bifidobacterium spp. and Akkermansia muciniphila, and protected barrier function in Caco-2/THP-1 co-culture. The study was sponsor-funded [PMID:41473189].
- Endogenous production as an alternative route: fiber RCTs raise faecal butyrate [PMID:29757343]. Inulin raised colonic butyrate production by 44% in a tracer RCT [PMID:40274191].

** Dosing in trials
- Tributyrin, oncology:
  - 50-400 mg/kg/day once daily (fasting), 3 weeks on, 1 week off [PMID:9533530].
  - 150-200 mg/kg three times daily. Escalation stopped at 200 mg/kg TID because of the number of capsules needed [PMID:12736763].
- Tributyrin, nutrition and neurology:
  - 100 or 200 mg tributyrin per capsule (ButyraGen, 1-2 capsules/d) for 21 days [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05601635] [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].
  - 500 mg three times daily for 30 days in PD [PMID:41271518].
  - 4 g/day for 8 weeks in the planned depression pilot RCT [PMID:41248397].
- Sodium butyrate RCTs:
  - 300 mg/d (150 mg twice daily) in IBS and pediatric IBD [PMID:35371361] [PMID:36014789].
  - 600 mg/d in obesity and T2D [PMID:39448815] [PMID:32315958].
  - 1.5-1.875 g/d in T2D and obesity [PMID:41974937] [PMID:41880673].
  - 4 g/d in T1D and UC [PMID:31915895] [PMID:10795763].
  - 20 mg/kg/d in children [PMID:36469320].
- Calcium butyrate: 500 mg/d (pediatric IBS); 1000 mg/d (MASLD) [PMID:40635319] [PMID:40565024].
- PK implication: plasma butyrate is short-lived after each dose, so trials used divided dosing [PMID:9533530] [PMID:12736763] [PMID:39498577].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Phase I tributyrin, 50-400 mg/kg/day (n=13) [PMID:9533530]:
  - Grade 3: nausea, vomiting, myalgia.
  - Grade 1-2: diarrhoea, headache, abdominal cramping, anemia, constipation, azotemia, lightheadedness, fatigue, rash, alopecia, odor, dysphoria and clumsiness.
- Three-times-daily tributyrin up to 200 mg/kg (n=20): no dose-limiting toxicity [PMID:12736763].
- Nutritional doses:
  - ButyraGen 200-400 mg/d was well tolerated, with GI discomfort in under 10%. Triglycerides rose at 400 mg/d [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].
  - 1.5 g/d tributyrin for 30 days in PD showed a "reassuring safety profile" and high adherence [PMID:41271518].
- Sodium and calcium butyrate RCTs:
  - Usually no or mild adverse events [PMID:36014789] [PMID:10795763] [PMID:39125336] [PMID:31915895].
  - Transient mild nausea and headache in 2 children [PMID:36469320].
  - Butyrylated starch added to peanut OIT did not raise the adverse-event rate [PMID:40804708].
- Blood pressure: oral sodium butyrate raised daytime ambulatory SBP by 9.63 mmHg over 4 weeks in untreated hypertensive patients. The authors advise caution in future butyrate trials [PMID:39034917].
- Odor and taste: free butyric acid is unpleasant to smell and taste. Odor was a grade 1-2 event with high-dose tributyrin [PMID:42817105] [PMID:41974937] [PMID:9533530].
- Context: butyrate has been called a "double-edged sword", e.g. its role in obesity is contested [PMID:29438462].
- Toxicology: very high oral tributyrin doses (10.3 g/kg) killed about 10% of mice acutely [PMID:10321503]. These doses are far above human supplement doses.
- No long-term (>6 months) human safety data on tributyrin were found.

** Contraindications
- No formal contraindications are established. Evidence-based cautions:
  - Hypertension, especially when untreated: BP rose with oral butyrate in an RCT [PMID:39034917].
  - Pregnancy, lactation and children: no tributyrin safety data were found. Pediatric data exist only for sodium/calcium butyrate [PMID:36469320] [PMID:40635319].
  - Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency: conversion to butyrate depends on pancreatic lipase, so the product may not work as intended. This is mechanistic only [PMID:41473189] [PMID:42817105].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Antihypertensives: butyrate raised BP in hypertensive patients off medication [PMID:39034917]. This could oppose BP control. No combined study exists.
- Glucose-lowering drugs (Metformin ER, Acarbose, others):
  - Sodium butyrate improved some glycemic markers in small T2D RCTs [PMID:41880673] [PMID:41974937] [PMID:39448815]. Any additive effect is likely small.
  - Acarbose raises colonic butyrate production [PMID:9164992] and serum butyrate [PMID:10961161].
  - Metformin raised serum butyrate at 6 months but lowered Roseburia [PMID:34006565].
  - Combining these with tributyrin increases total butyrate exposure. This has not been studied.
- Mesalazine: in a pilot RCT, adding sodium butyrate 4 g/d to oral mesalazine gave a larger improvement from baseline. The between-arm difference was not significant [PMID:10795763].
- Immunosuppressants and transplant drugs: tributyrin promoted Treg-mediated tolerance in mouse allograft models [PMID:41554342]. Oral butyrate did not change human immune phenotype in T1D [PMID:31915895]. Clinical relevance is unknown.
- Antidepressants: a tributyrin add-on pilot RCT is under way (protocol only). No interaction data exist [PMID:41248397].
*** Other supplements
- ProButyrate (corpus item, sustained-release butyric acid): duplicates butyrate exposure. Dose-related GI effects and the BP signal argue against stacking two butyrate sources without a reason [PMID:9533530] [PMID:39034917] [PMID:42817105].
- Inulin: raises endogenous colonic butyrate production (+44%) and plasma butyrate in older adults [PMID:40274191]. In T2D, butyrate plus inulin lowered fasting glucose within group, which neither did alone [PMID:28962046] [PMID:32315958].
- GOS: fiber RCTs increase faecal butyrate (SMD 0.24, 95% CI 0.00-0.47) and fructans/GOS increase Bifidobacterium [PMID:29757343]. However, GOS 16 g/d lowered Ruminococcus (a butyrate producer) and raised fasting glucose in a 14-day crossover [PMID:28924143].
- Vitamin D-3: tributyrin upregulated the vitamin D receptor and acted synergistically with calcitriol in colon cancer cells (in vitro only) [PMID:11385076]. A calcium butyrate + zinc + D3 formula improved fatty liver index in MASLD [PMID:39125336].
*** Conditions
- Hypertension: see Contraindications [PMID:39034917].
- IBD: adjunct evidence is mixed. Benefit was reported in adult CD and UC, but no effect in pediatric IBD [PMID:41354580] [PMID:36014789] [PMID:42646603].
- Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency: possible reduced activation [PMID:41473189].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference is known. Exogenous butyrate raises plasma/serum butyrate [PMID:39034917] [PMID:41880673] [PMID:39498577]. This confounds serum SCFA measured as a microbiome biomarker.
- Serum triglycerides rose with ButyraGen 400 mg/d [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005]. They fell with sodium butyrate in T2D [PMID:41880673].
- Faecal calprotectin fell with sodium butyrate in UC and CD [PMID:39003477] [PMID:41354580].

** Genotype notes
No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source found. In two RCTs, baseline microbiome signatures (not host genotype) predicted response [PMID:36469320] [PMID:41354580].

** Open questions
- No placebo-controlled efficacy RCT of tributyrin itself was found for any clinical outcome. Most human efficacy data come from sodium/calcium butyrate, which differ in release site and PK.
- Several tributyrin trials are registered or under way:
  - Phase 2 in PD with cognitive impairment [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07154511]
  - Glycemic control in T2D [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07503548]
  - Mild Alzheimer disease [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06797817]
  - Acute pancreatitis prophylaxis [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06147635]
  - A completed PK comparison of three butyrate products [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06700785]
- Whether the BP rise seen with sodium butyrate also occurs with tributyrin [PMID:39034917].
- How much tributyrin reaches the colon in humans. Current data come from in vitro simulation (sponsor-funded) [PMID:41473189].
- Long-term safety and lipid effects (TG rise in the pilot) [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].
- Whether taking tributyrin offers anything over raising endogenous butyrate with fiber/prebiotics [PMID:40274191] [PMID:29757343].
