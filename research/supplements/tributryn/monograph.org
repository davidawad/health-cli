** Identity & forms
- Tributryn is represented by these commonly studied or marketed forms: glyceryl tributyrate, tributyrate. Clinical products and formulations can differ in composition and bioavailability; this limits cross-trial comparisons [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005, NCT05601635].

** Mechanisms
- Proposed mechanisms are outcome- and formulation-dependent; mechanistic plausibility does not establish clinical efficacy. Human evidence is summarized below [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005, NCT05601635].

- 2024 pilot human study evaluated tolerability and exploratory biological effects of a tributyrin-containing formulation; small sample and short duration cannot establish efficacy [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].
- No established health outcome efficacy; proposed butyrate delivery is mechanistic, with human evidence limited to pilot tolerability/exploration [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].

** Dosing in trials
- Single pilot used a commercial tributyrin-containing supplement over a short intervention; no validated dose range or standardized pure-tributyrin regimen established [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Pilot reported tolerability observations but is too small and short to characterize uncommon or long-term harms [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].

** Contraindications
- Insufficient evidence for pregnancy/lactation, children, or significant gastrointestinal disease [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].

** Interactions
- No clinical interaction studies identified; no validated laboratory interference known [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].
- No genotype-specific clinical claim is made because no qualifying pharmacogenomic source was identified for this research set.

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source identified for a clinically useful genotype-specific note.

** Latest research
- 2024–2026: 2024 pilot study (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05601635) is the directly relevant recent human evidence; larger controlled trials needed [DOI:10.3233/NHA-240005].

** Open questions
- Larger, preregistered, adequately powered human trials with standardized, chemically characterized products and longer follow-up are needed to establish clinically important benefits and characterize uncommon harms.
