** Identity & forms
- Trimethylglycine (betaine) is a dietary methyl donor found in foods such as beets and grains; it is distinct from betaine hydrochloride, a gastric acidifier. Supplemental research generally uses anhydrous betaine. [PMID:3610948]

** Mechanisms
- Betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase transfers a methyl group from betaine to homocysteine, regenerating methionine, predominantly in liver and kidney. [PMID:3610948]

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plasma homocysteine | Meta-analysis of randomized trials | decrease | Pooled reduction in healthy adults; dose dependent | moderate | PMID:3610948 |
| Lipids | Meta-analysis of RCTs | increase in LDL/total cholesterol | Several mg/dL increase in total cholesterol; potential offset to homocysteine change | low | PMID:3610948 |
| Homocysteine in active adults | Randomized crossover trial | decrease | 17.04 to 15.44 µmol/L; TMAO change nonsignificant | low | PMID:38876040 |
| Clinical cardiovascular events | No outcome RCT evidence established | unknown | No demonstrated patient-important outcome benefit | very low | PMID:3610948 |

** Dosing in trials
- Trials commonly gave 1.5–6 g/day; one recent crossover study tested betaine in healthy active males (dose per report). A 12-week Chinese trial combined 1 g/day betaine with B vitamins; this does not isolate TMG. [PMID:36717385, PMID:38876040]

** Safety & adverse effects
- Can increase total/LDL cholesterol; nausea, diarrhea and fishy body odor are reported with high doses. TMAO may rise in some participants, with uncertain clinical meaning. [PMID:3610948, PMID:38876040]

** Contraindications
- Use caution in pre-existing hypercholesterolemia and renal/hepatic disorders because outcomes and handling in these groups are not established. Do not confuse TMG with betaine HCl. [PMID:3610948]

** Interactions
- Folate, B12 and B6 participate in parallel homocysteine pathways; combination trials cannot attribute changes to betaine alone. Check lipids if using sustained gram doses; no validated assay interference established. [PMID:36717385, PMID:38876040]

** Genotype notes
- A 2024 randomized crossover study reported betaine concentrations increased more among MTHFR C677T T-allele carriers than CC participants; this biomarker subgroup finding does not establish genotype-guided clinical benefit. [PMID:38876040]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- 2024 randomized crossover: betaine lowered homocysteine in healthy active men, while TMAO rose nonsignificantly; betaine response differed by MTHFR C677T allele. [PMID:38876040]

** Open questions
- Do homocysteine changes translate to clinical benefit? Clarify long-term lipid and TMAO effects and which baseline phenotypes benefit.
