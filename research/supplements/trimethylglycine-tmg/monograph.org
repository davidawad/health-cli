** Identity & forms
- N,N,N-trimethylglycine (glycine betaine) is an N-methylated amino acid. It acts both as an osmolyte and as a methyl donor in the methionine cycle, mainly in liver and kidney [PMID:15321791] [PMID:20446114].
- Dietary sources: richest are wheat bran/germ, spinach and marine invertebrates [PMID:15321791]. Cereal foods are the main source in Western diets [PMID:24128557]. Mean intake across 28 observational studies was about 182 mg/d [PMID:39270852].
- Endogenous betaine comes from choline oxidation. Supplemental choline (1 g/d) raises plasma betaine [PMID:22172554] [PMID:41687879].
- Forms: OTC supplements are betaine anhydrous powder or capsules sold as "TMG". The prescription form, betaine anhydrous for oral solution (Cystadane), is FDA-approved to lower homocysteine in homocystinuria (CBS deficiency, MTHFR deficiency, cobalamin cofactor defects) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559].
- EU: authorized as a novel food. EFSA set a safe added intake of 6 mg/kg bw/day (about 400 mg/d for adults) on top of diet [PMID:32625349] [PMID:32626284].
- Not the same as betaine hydrochloride, a gastric acidifier that transiently lowers gastric pH [PMID:23980906].

** Mechanisms
- BHMT pathway: betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase transfers one methyl group from betaine to homocysteine, forming methionine and dimethylglycine (DMG) [PMID:16197300] [PMID:24022817]. BHMT handles up to 50% of hepatic homocysteine metabolism [PMID:21093336]. It runs parallel to folate/B12-dependent methionine synthase, and the two pathways are interlinked [PMID:15941890] [PMID:40806080].
- Betaine is a stronger determinant of the post-methionine-load homocysteine rise than folate or B6. The association is strongest when folate status is low [PMID:15550695] [PMID:14699020] [PMID:16197300].
- Supplementation raises plasma betaine, DMG and methionine [PMID:33764214]. DMG rose 16.5-fold in prediabetes, consistent with DMG dehydrogenase being rate limiting [PMID:29860335]. Supplementation also shifts other one-carbon metabolites, e.g. sarcosine, SAH and MTA [PMID:40944155].
- Osmolyte role: taken up via the betaine/GABA transporter BGT1. It protects renal medullary, hepatic and brain cells against hypertonic stress [PMID:24795654].
- Preclinical: exercise raises betaine, which binds TBK1 and has geroprotective effects in mice [PMID:40570836]. Proposed anti-steatotic, mitochondrial and anti-inflammatory actions come mostly from animal work [PMID:33818349] [PMID:42792248] [PMID:39647584].
- Lipids: the mechanism of the LDL rise in humans is unclear [PMID:15916468].

** Dosing in trials
- Homocysteine in healthy adults: 1.5-6 g/d for 6 wk lowers fasting tHcy dose-dependently [PMID:14652361]. MAs pooled >=4 g/d trials [PMID:23997720]. Doses <4 g/d may lower Hcy without the lipid rise [PMID:33764214].
- Sport: 2.5-5 g/d, usually split doses, for 1-14 weeks [PMID:37409757] [PMID:30064450] [PMID:33663545] [PMID:20642826]. 3 g/d for 2 weeks in cyclists [PMID:40944155].
- Pharmacokinetics: absorption is rapid (Tmax ~0.9 h). Elimination half-life is ~14 h after a single dose and ~41 h with repeated dosing; only ~4% is excreted in urine [PMID:12534635]. Serum betaine returns to baseline within about 4 days of stopping [PMID:35276860].
- NASH/MASLD: 20 g/d for 12 months [PMID:19824078]; 1-8 g/d in pilots [PMID:40758380].
- Homocystinuria (label): 6 g/d (3 g twice daily) for ages >=3 years. Under 3 years: start at 100 mg/kg/day, titrated weekly [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559]. 6 g/d was the classic regimen in pyridoxine non-responders [PMID:6877313]. 250 mg/kg/day gave no extra tHcy lowering over 100 mg/kg/day in CBS/cblC patients [PMID:36376887]. Registry doses commonly exceed 6 g/d [PMID:35358327].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Healthy-population trials report mostly mild GI symptoms. About 35% had mild, transient GI symptoms in the MASLD pilots [PMID:40758380]. No liver or kidney function changes were seen at 4 g/d for 6 months [PMID:20978525].
- Lipids: at >=4-6 g/d, LDL and total cholesterol rise, which could offset any homocysteine benefit [PMID:15916468] [PMID:33764214] [PMID:12399266] [PMID:29860335]. Long-term 4 g/d in young healthy adults showed no net lipid change [PMID:20978525]. EFSA found LDL increases at 4 g/d in overweight subjects with metabolic syndrome [PMID:32625349].
- Hypermethioninemia and cerebral edema in CBS deficiency: the Cystadane label warns that betaine may worsen hypermethioninemia, and cerebral edema has been reported. Advice: monitor methionine and keep it below 1,000 umol/L [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559]. Postmarketing cases occurred 2 weeks to 6 months after starting, with methionine of 1,000-3,000 uM, and resolved on discontinuation [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559].
  - Case and review evidence: a child with dietary non-adherence reached methionine ~3,000 umol/L and developed massive edema [PMID:11857551]. A review of 19 patients implicates methionine rather than betaine itself; no edema was seen below 559 umol/L [PMID:32154053].
- Rx safety registries: 130 patients, median 6.8 years of use, no new risk even above 6 g/d [PMID:35358327]. RoCH (n=125): drug-related AEs were bad taste, headache and one interstitial lung disease [PMID:30871635]. Label postmarketing AEs include body odor, GI upset, glossitis, hair loss and behavioural changes [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559].
- TMAO: betaine is a TMAO precursor. Supplementation produced a non-significant TMAO rise (P=.053) [PMID:38876040]. Betaine and TMAO predicted CVD risk in a metabolomics cohort [PMID:21475195].
- Pregnancy/lactation: limited data. The label reports no identified drug-associated risk in published cases [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559]. Lactation trials are ongoing [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04633044] [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07243678].

** Contraindications
- The label lists none [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559].
- CBS deficiency (classical homocystinuria): use only under specialist care with dietary methionine restriction and methionine monitoring [PMID:32154053] [PMID:27778219]. Betaine is an adjunct to diet, not a replacement [PMID:11857551]. Doses >100 mg/kg/day may harm patients with pre-existing hypermethioninemia [PMID:36376887].
- Hypercholesterolemia or high ASCVD risk: use caution with >=4 g/d because of the LDL rise [PMID:33764214] [PMID:15916468].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Metformin (incl. ER): lowers B12, and long-term use raises B12-deficiency risk [PMID:20488910] [PMID:26900641]. It raises Hcy (+2.02 umol/L) when B vitamins are not co-supplemented [PMID:27941660]. No study tested betaine with metformin; any offset is theoretical.
- Fibrates (bezafibrate): cause abnormal urinary betaine loss linked to higher homocysteine [PMID:19653086].
- Lipid-lowering therapy: betaine's LDL increase at >=4 g/d may partly counter statin goals (inferred) [PMID:33764214].
- Hydroxocobalamin/pyridoxine/folate in inborn errors: betaine is combined with these under guidelines, e.g. first-line in MTHFR deficiency [PMID:27905001] [PMID:42231716].
*** Other supplements
- Folate (incl. 5-MTHF): parallel remethylation pathway. Folic acid raises plasma betaine by up to 15% [PMID:15941890]. Betaine-Hcy associations are strongest at low folate status [PMID:15550695]. Folic acid lowered fasting Hcy more than betaine and did not raise lipids [PMID:12730412] [PMID:15916468].
- Vitamin B12: methionine synthase cofactor. Betaine is used with hydroxocobalamin in cbl defects [PMID:27905001]. A 12-week RCT of low-dose folate/B6/B12 plus 1 g betaine lowered Hcy by 10.1% [PMID:36717385].
- Vitamin B6: required for transsulfuration (CBS), whereas betaine works through remethylation. B6 deficiency raises post-load Hcy [PMID:8690790]. Betaine added to folate+B6 cut post-load Hcy a further 18% in CKD [PMID:11849459].
- B Complex: combined regimens lower Hcy [PMID:36717385] [PMID:11849459]. Methyl donors (betaine HCl + B vitamins + folate) prevented the GAA-induced Hcy rise [PMID:23351309].
- Choline (incl. Alpha-GPC, citicoline): choline is the betaine precursor. 2.6 g/d choline as PC lowered Hcy 18% but raised TG [PMID:16002808] [PMID:15916468]. 1 g/d choline raised betaine [PMID:22172554]. Both are TMAO precursors [PMID:21475195].
- Creatine: betaine did not raise muscle PCr or add to creatine's strength effect [PMID:21744011]. Creatine lowers GAA but not Hcy [PMID:26311810].
*** Conditions
- CBS deficiency: see Safety and Contraindications.
- MTHFR deficiency and cblC: betaine is guideline-recommended [PMID:27905001] [PMID:42231716].
- Chronic kidney disease/dialysis: betaine did not lower fasting Hcy beyond folate [PMID:10081520] [PMID:11849459]. It is mainly cleared by metabolism [PMID:12534635]. DMG accumulation predicts mortality in CHD (association only) [PMID:24671904].
- NAFLD/MASLD: an RCT was null on histology [PMID:19824078]. An uncontrolled pilot showed ALT falls [PMID:40758380]. Reviewers have called for re-evaluation [PMID:33132631]. A placebo-controlled MASH trial is planned [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07276204].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact was found. Betaine causes true physiological changes: Hcy falls [PMID:23997720]; methionine and DMG rise [PMID:33764214] [PMID:29860335]; plasma betaine rises about 3.5-fold [PMID:38876040]; LDL/TC rise [PMID:31809615].
- The label notes betaine concentrations are not correlated with homocysteine response [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31a684d0-cd98-4e46-b43a-ccbd17c41559].

** Genotype notes
- MTHFR C677T (rs1801133): in a 2024 crossover RCT, T-allele carriers had larger serum betaine increases than CC [PMID:38876040]. A CrossFit RCT found no genotype x dose interaction for performance [PMID:37409757]. No genotype-guided clinical benefit is established.
- BHMT: upstream/intronic SNPs (rs41272270, rs16876512, rs6875201) correlate with hepatic BHMT activity [PMID:21093336]. Coding variants (G199S, Q239R, Q406H) did not alter Hcy [PMID:11073719]. G742A was not associated with CAD in an MA [PMID:22339686].
- BHMT rs3733890 reduced responsiveness to phosphatidylcholine [PMID:41415850].
- An exploratory GWAS linked BHMT, DMGDH and SLC6A12 to serum betaine/DMG [PMID:40871658].
- Severe MTHFR deficiency (inborn error, distinct from C677T) is a labelled Rx indication [PMID:27905001].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] 8-week RCT, 3 g/d in overweight premenopausal women: higher PBMC citrate synthase and plasma catalase activity [PMID:42841999].
- [2026-10] Bayesian NMA of 119 trials: betaine CMJ +3.72 cm; most comparisons very low certainty [PMID:42821324].
- [2026-09] MASLD review: preclinical effects are consistent, but clinical efficacy is not established [PMID:42792248].
- [2026-07] Revised remethylation-disorder guideline: betaine is first-line in MTHFR deficiency [PMID:42231716].
- [2026-03] PREDIMED-Plus cohort (n=6610): dietary choline/betaine and 2-year cognitive change [PMID:41825530].
- [2026-02] Controlled feeding RCT: plasma betaine tracks dietary choline intake [PMID:41687879].
- [2025-12] Choline-form response depends on CHDH/BHMT/PEMT variants [PMID:41415850].
- [2025-08] MASLD pilots (Hepatology): ALT/AST and CK18 fell at 2-8 g/d; LDL unchanged; no placebo arm [PMID:40758380].
- [2025-08] Cyclists' crossover RCT (3 g/d): 60 km TT 1.4 min faster [PMID:40944155].
- [2025-08] GWAS of choline/betaine/DMG [PMID:40871658].
- [2025-07] Review of folate-choline methyl shuttling [PMID:40806080].
- [2025-06] Cell: betaine as an exercise mimetic inhibiting TBK1 (mouse/human omics) [PMID:40570836].
- [2025-01] E-HOD registry (n=311): tHcy was similar at high vs low betaine doses; poor adherence was common [PMID:40095936].
- [2024-12] Aging review [PMID:39647584].
- [2024-11] Exercise MA: strength SMD 0.47 [PMID:39514262].
- [2024-11] Critical review: marketing outpaces RCT evidence [PMID:39270852].

** Open questions
- Whether betaine's Hcy lowering produces any clinical benefit, given that B-vitamin Hcy-lowering trials largely failed [PMID:28816346], and whether the LDL rise offsets it [PMID:15916468].
- Whether dose-dependent lipid effects persist below 4 g/d long term [PMID:33764214] [PMID:20978525].
- Whether the ALT improvements in MASLD hold up under placebo control and translate into histology [PMID:40758380] [PMID:19824078].
- Clinical relevance of TMAO and DMG increases [PMID:38876040] [PMID:24671904].
- Observational betaine intake-mortality data are inconsistent [PMID:31915809].
- Translation of mouse geroprotection to humans [PMID:40570836].
