** Identity & forms
- Turmeric rhizome extracts contain curcuminoids, including curcumin; formulations differ markedly in bioavailability [PMID:33511486].
** Mechanisms
- Curcuminoids have anti-inflammatory and antioxidant actions in experimental systems; clinical effect depends on formulation [PMID:33511486].

** Dosing in trials
- Trials used varied extracts and enhanced-bioavailability formulations, commonly hundreds to ~1500 mg/day for up to 16 weeks; not interchangeable [PMID:33511486].
** Safety & adverse effects
- GI symptoms; rare clinically important liver injury reported with supplements, especially enhanced absorption products [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].
** Contraindications
- Biliary obstruction/gallstones warrant caution; avoid high-bioavailability products with prior supplement-related liver injury.
** Interactions
- Possible bleeding interaction with anticoagulants/antiplatelets is biologically plausible but clinical evidence limited; piperine changes drug metabolism.
- Structured high-priority interactions are represented in interactions.json; absence of an edge is not proof of no interaction.
** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic/nutrigenomic source was identified for a supplement-specific genotype effect; no genotype claim made.
** Latest research
- 2021-02: Recent clinical evidence remains formulation-dependent; opened systematic review included short-term heterogeneous OA trials and found no disease-modifying imaging/biochemical signal [PMID:33511486].
** Open questions
- Which formulations, populations, and durations produce clinically meaningful outcomes rather than biomarker-only changes?
- Are there long-term benefits or harms beyond the relatively short human trials available?
