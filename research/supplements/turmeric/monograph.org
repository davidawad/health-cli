** Identity & forms
- Curcuma longa L. (ginger family). The rhizome is used as a spice, dye and traditional medicine. Its yellow color and most proposed health effects come from curcuminoids, mainly curcumin [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric] [PMID:31643876].
- Scope: whole rhizome powder and Curcuma longa extracts. Purified or enhanced curcumin has its own "Curcumin" monograph. Most RCTs test curcuminoid extracts rather than culinary powder, so the form is stated wherever the source gives it.
- Main forms: Rhizome powder. A 6 g dose used in a human study contained only 2% curcumin [PMID:28370178]. Standardized extracts, and curcuminoids plus piperine. In humans, 20 mg piperine raised the bioavailability of 2 g curcumin by 2000% [PMID:9619120]. Enhanced formulations: phospholipid (Meriva), nano-curcumin and water-dispersible forms. Theracurmin's AUC was 27-fold that of curcumin powder [PMID:25308211].
- Product content varies: curcumin ranged from 0.03 to 37.6 g/100 g across 30 capsule products [PMID:39614547]. Products often add piperine [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].
- Regulatory status: Curcumin (E 100) has an EU food-additive ADI of 3 mg/kg bw/day [PMID:42087949]. EFSA rejected a joint-function health claim [PMID:32625480]. Turmeric is GRAS as a US food ingredient [PMID:30000906].

** Mechanisms
- Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant: pooled RCTs show lower CRP, TNF-alpha, IL-6 and MDA, and higher TAC and SOD [PMID:36804260]. Hepatic NF-kB inhibition predicted NASH resolution in the Meriva trial (AUC 0.90) [PMID:38809154].
- Iron handling: Curcumin binds ferric iron in the gut and caused iron deficiency in mice [PMID:30899609]. 6 g turmeric lowered serum hepcidin by up to 19% in healthy men [PMID:28370178]. 0.5 g with a meal did not inhibit iron absorption in a stable-isotope study [PMID:17116705].
- Platelets and coagulation: Preclinical antiplatelet, anticoagulant and fibrinolytic activity [PMID:29052850] [PMID:25839871]. In rats, curcumin raised warfarin and clopidogrel AUC but did not change PT or platelet aggregation [PMID:23807811].
- Enzymes and transporters: In vitro, curcumin inhibits CYP1A2, 3A4, 2D6, 2C9 (IC50 4.3 uM) and 2B6 [PMID:17433521]. It inhibits intestinal BCRP in humans and monkeys [PMID:22300367] [PMID:29358184]. Its effects on P-gp substrates are variable [PMID:22725663] [PMID:17468862]. Piperine inhibits glucuronidation and CYP3A4 [PMID:9619120] [PMID:27776366].
- Cholekinetic: 20, 40 and 80 mg curcumin reduced gallbladder volume by 34%, 51% and 72% at 2 h [PMID:12495265].

** Dosing in trials
- Knee OA: Extracts were given for up to 16 weeks [PMID:33511486]. <1000 and >=1000 mg/d curcuminoids gave similar relief [PMID:34537344]. More than 12 weeks is suggested [PMID:34017975]. Theracurmin 180 mg/d curcumin was used for 8 weeks [PMID:25308211].
- Arthritis overall: 120-1500 mg/d for 4-36 weeks [PMID:35935936].
- Glycemia and MASLD: >=1 g/d was more effective [PMID:42005325]. 1500 mg/d curcumin was used for 12 months [PMID:40573083] [PMID:41096556].
- NASH: Meriva 2 g/d for 72 weeks [PMID:38809154]. Nano-curcumin 80 mg/d for 16 weeks improved enzymes but not fibrosis [PMID:41168313].
- Dyspepsia: C. longa capsules for 4 weeks [PMID:34652861]. Culinary exposure is about 0.5 g per meal [PMID:17116705].
- Safety benchmarks: Curcumin was safe at 6 g/day for 4-7 weeks [PMID:29480523]. Conventional turmeric is likely safe for 2-3 months at recommended amounts [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects are common: nausea, diarrhea, reflux [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric] [PMID:30000906]. Yellow stool occurred in 11% in one RCT [PMID:40759997]. OA trial AE rates were similar to placebo and lower than NSAIDs [PMID:33511486] [PMID:38036015].
- Hepatotoxicity (idiosyncratic, rare, increasing): US DILIN: 10 cases, 9 hepatocellular, latency 1-4 months; 5 hospitalized, 1 death. Three of 7 tested products contained piperine [PMID:36252717]. Italy (Tuscany phytovigilance): 7 cholestatic hepatitis cases, all with high-bioavailability, high-dose products [PMID:32656820]. Recent turmeric + black pepper cases [PMID:39995754] [PMID:41255460], including recurrent autoimmune-like hepatitis on rechallenge [PMID:42160492]. LiverTox: several dozen clinically apparent cases [PMID:31643876]. Turmeric was the most-used of 6 potentially hepatotoxic botanicals in NHANES [PMID:39102266]. It is listed among newer HDS hepatotoxins [PMID:42092331].
- Lead (lead chromate adulteration of loose spice): South Asia: 14% of 356 samples had detectable lead, some >1000 ug/g [PMID:39053552]. Eastern India: 30% of 503 samples exceeded 10 ug/g; maximum 6416 ug/g [PMID:42091913]. Bangladesh: adulteration in 7 of 9 producing districts [PMID:31550596]. US: pediatric lead poisoning from home-prepared spices [PMID:36660959]. Capsule products tested had low lead (<=0.619 mg/kg) [PMID:39614547].
- Oxalate and kidney stones: 91% of turmeric oxalate is water-soluble, and supplemental doses raised urinary oxalate [PMID:18469248]. 8.6 mg oxalate per OTC supplement dose [PMID:41460700]. Oxalate nephropathy case with chronic use [PMID:38427579].
- Iron deficiency anemia: one case on 6 x 538 mg/d extract, resolved after stopping [PMID:30899609].
- Pregnancy and lactation: Supplements may be unsafe in pregnancy [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric]. Human data are lacking [PMID:29480523]. There are no milk-excretion data. Curcumin suppresses milk production in vitro [PMID:30000906].
- Toxicology review (2026): low acute toxicity, but hepatic and genomic risks at high or prolonged doses [PMID:42488578].

** Contraindications
- Prior turmeric or curcumin liver injury: do not rechallenge, as reinjury was more severe [PMID:42160492] [PMID:36252717].
- Active liver disease or raised transaminases: avoid enhanced or piperine-containing products [PMID:32656820] [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].
- Biliary obstruction or gallstones: precaution based on mechanism, because curcumin contracts the gallbladder [PMID:12495265].
- Calcium-oxalate stones or hyperoxaluria [PMID:18469248] [PMID:38427579].
- Pregnancy at supplemental doses [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric].
- Iron-deficiency anemia [PMID:30899609].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Anticoagulants and antiplatelets: INR rose with fluindione plus turmeric [PMID:25230280]. Bleeding risk is reported with anticoagulants [PMID:36304597] [PMID:30000906]. In rats there was a PK change but no PD change [PMID:23807811].
- Tacrolimus: Case of nephrotoxicity with a trough of 29.9 ng/mL after high-dose dietary turmeric [PMID:28104136]. A review lists turmeric as a CYP3A4 inhibitor [PMID:36297591]. A 10 g/day challenge in another patient did not change levels [PMID:37586787].
- Sulfasalazine and other BCRP substrates: curcumin raised sulfasalazine AUC 3.2-fold in humans [PMID:22300367]. In monkeys it raised rosuvastatin exposure [PMID:29358184].
- Talinolol (P-gp): AUC fell with 300 mg/d curcumin [PMID:17468862] but rose 67-81% with 1000 mg/d [PMID:22725663].
- Tamoxifen: endoxifen AUC fell 7.7% with curcumin and 12.4% with curcumin plus piperine [PMID:30909366]. Curcuma longa-cancer drug PK interactions are documented clinically [PMID:36707434].
- Glucose-lowering drugs: additive lowering with glyburide, without hypoglycemia [PMID:25044423]. Metformin plus nano-curcumin beat either alone in PCOS [PMID:39461199].
- CYP3A4 drugs with piperine products: piperine raised carbamazepine Cmax by 68.7% [PMID:27776366], and also raised propranolol and theophylline exposure [PMID:1815977].
- Other hepatotoxic drugs: most published turmeric-DILI cases involved co-medication [PMID:32656820].
*** Other supplements
- Curcumin: stacking raises total curcuminoid exposure. DILI clusters with high-dose, high-bioavailability products [PMID:32656820] [PMID:36252717].
- Black pepper/piperine: raised curcumin bioavailability by 2000% in humans [PMID:9619120]. It is often co-implicated in DILI [PMID:36252717] [PMID:42160492].
- Heme Iron (Proferrin) and other iron: one deficiency case while on iron supplements [PMID:30899609]. Meal-level turmeric did not reduce iron absorption [PMID:17116705]. Separate the doses as a precaution.
- Garlic, Ginger Root, Yeast Rice + Garlic: possible additive bleeding risk. Garlic is linked to surgical bleeding, and ginger and turmeric are antiplatelet in vitro [PMID:36304597] [PMID:25839871]. Ginger and turmeric are both listed as tacrolimus interactants [PMID:36297591].
- Ashwagandha and red yeast rice: stacking hepatotoxic botanicals adds liver-injury exposure [PMID:39102266] [PMID:42504634].
- EPA/DHA/DPA: in the SPORT trial neither fish oil nor turmeric lowered LDL-C [PMID:36351465]. No interaction data were found.
*** Conditions
- See Contraindications. In diabetes treated with insulin or sulfonylureas, monitor glucose [PMID:42005325] [PMID:25044423].
- Planned surgery: stop nonessential supplements 2 weeks before [PMID:36304597].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference was identified. True changes can occur in glucose/HbA1c [PMID:42005325], lipids [PMID:40044446], CRP [PMID:36804260], AST [PMID:37918958], hepcidin [PMID:28370178], INR on VKAs [PMID:25230280] and blood lead with adulterated spice [PMID:39053552].

** Genotype notes
- HLA-B*35:01: 7 of 10 DILIN turmeric-DILI cases carried the allele (allele frequency 0.450 vs 0.056-0.069 in controls) [PMID:36252717]. A 2024 Spanish case was also a carrier [PMID:39297606]. The same allele is a risk factor for green tea extract liver injury [PMID:42092331].
- ABCB1 C3435T: curcumin's effect on talinolol exposure was largest in TT subjects [PMID:22725663].
- CYP2D6: the fall in endoxifen was greater in extensive metabolizers [PMID:30909366].

** Open questions
- Does whole rhizome powder act like standardized or enhanced extracts? The efficacy of crude powder in diabetes "remains unexplored" [PMID:41319069].
- What is the true incidence of turmeric DILI, and how much do piperine and enhanced bioavailability contribute? Is HLA-B*35:01 screening useful [PMID:36252717]?
- Heterogeneity is very high (I2 often >85%). Independent, preregistered, longer trials are needed [PMID:33511486] [PMID:40044446].
- Are the in vitro CYP and transporter findings clinically relevant beyond sulfasalazine, talinolol and tamoxifen?
- How much lead do people actually get from imported loose turmeric [PMID:39053552]?
