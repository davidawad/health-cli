** Identity & forms
- Urolithin A (UA) is a dibenzo-alpha-pyrone that gut bacteria make from dietary ellagitannins and ellagic acid. Food sources of the precursors include pomegranate, walnuts and other nuts, and berries [PMID:27400265] [PMID:34030963]. UA itself is not present in food in free form [PMID:42468665].
- 13 urolithins and their glucuronide/sulfate conjugates have been described in human urine, blood, feces, breast milk and prostate, colon and breast tissue [PMID:35118817].
- Supplement form: synthetic, high-purity UA (Mitopure, Amazentis SA). It was used in the sponsor's trials at 500-1,000 mg/d [PMID:35584623] [PMID:41174221].
- US regulatory status: GRAS notice GRN No. 791 (Amazentis SA; filed 2018-06-15). FDA closed it on 2018-12-20 with a "no questions" letter. The notice covers use in protein shakes, meal-replacement beverages, oatmeal, nutrition bars and yogurts at 250-500 mg/serving, up to a maximum of 500 mg or 1,000 mg/serving [https://www.cfsanappsexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?set=GRASNotices&id=791].
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - Oral synthetic UA was bioavailable in plasma at all doses tested in the first-in-human study [PMID:32694802].
  - In rats, glucuronide and sulfate conjugates were the main metabolites after oral and i.v. dosing [PMID:28757461]. In humans, circulating UA is also mostly phase II conjugates rather than free aglycone [PMID:42468665].
  - After pomegranate juice, UA-glucuronide appeared in urine in 11/18 subjects on the day of intake and 16/18 the day after. Excretion therefore lags precursor intake by about 1 day [PMID:16988113].
  - UA was drastically unstable during simulated colonic fermentation in vitro [PMID:26439723].
- Formulation research: nanoparticle and liposome formulations raised systemic bioavailability several-fold over unformulated UA in preclinical work [PMID:41866331]. UA conjugated to NSAIDs (diclofenac, aspirin esters) suppressed UA glucuronidation in Caco-2 cells. These are research compounds, not marketed products [PMID:38000358].

** Mechanisms
- Mitophagy induction: UA was identified as a first-in-class natural mitophagy inducer. It prevented accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria and extended lifespan in C. elegans, and improved exercise capacity in aged mice and young rats [PMID:27400265]. Reviews describe PINK1/Parkin-dependent mitophagy [PMID:41404767] [PMID:37925671].
- Human target engagement:
  - Raised skeletal-muscle mitochondrial gene expression and modulated plasma acylcarnitines after 4 weeks at 500-1,000 mg/d [PMID:32694802].
  - Raised muscle mitophagy and mitochondrial-metabolism proteins after 4 months [PMID:35584623].
  - Lowered plasma acylcarnitines, ceramides and CRP [PMID:35050355] [PMID:40034121].
  - Did not change maximal ATP production, mitochondrial biogenesis or dynamics in the 2024 systematic review [PMID:39002645].
- Immune aging: in mice UA expands T memory stem cells and naive T cells. In humans, 4 weeks at 1,000 mg/d expanded naive-like CD8+ cells and raised CD8+ fatty-acid oxidation capacity [PMID:41174221].
- Inflammaging: in old mice, UA-induced mitophagy reduced cytosolic mtDNA-driven cGAS/STING activation and improved neurological function [PMID:38280852].
- Gut barrier and aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR):
  - UA strengthens tight junctions through AhR-Nrf2 and attenuates colitis in mice [PMID:30626868].
  - It activates an epithelial AhR-NLRP6-IL-18-IL-22 axis, also in IBD patient biopsies ex vivo [PMID:42336837].
  - Its colitis protection requires CYP1A1 [PMID:36159798].
  - In a human reporter system UA acted as a selective AhR antagonist and blunted TCDD-induced CYP1A1 [PMID:30501068].
- Estrogen receptor: UA binds ER-alpha/ER-beta (IC50 0.4/0.75 uM). It is weakly estrogenic and antiestrogenic in MCF-7 cells [PMID:16506809] and showed SERM-like activity in breast-cancer models [PMID:37345359].
- Platelets: in human whole blood in vitro, UA inhibited arachidonic-acid-induced aggregation (IC50 3.7 uM) and aggregation from several other agonists. UA-glucuronide did not [PMID:42812264].
- Neuro: UA and NAD+ supplementation both stimulated mitophagy and reversed memory deficits in AD models [PMID:30742114]. Long-term UA improved cognition and lysosomal function in 3 AD mouse models [PMID:38753870]. In aged mice it prevented, but did not reverse, cognitive decline [PMID:41786716]. Brain exposure in humans is unproven and no CNS-endpoint trial has been completed [PMID:42468665].
- Joints: UA improved chondrocyte mitophagy and reduced cartilage degeneration and pain in a mouse OA model [PMID:35778837].

** Dosing in trials
- Range: 10-1,000 mg/d for 28 days to 4 months in the 2024 systematic review [PMID:39002645].
- 500 mg/d and 1,000 mg/d for 4 weeks (first-in-human study, older sedentary adults) [PMID:32694802].
- 500 and 1,000 mg/d for 4 months (middle-aged adults) [PMID:35584623]. 1,000 mg/d for 4 months (adults 65-90) [PMID:35050355].
- 1,000 mg/d for 4 weeks in the immune trial [PMID:41174221] and the endothelial trial [PMID:42853569]. 1,000 mg/d for 4-8 weeks in athletes [PMID:40839339] [PMID:41245402] [PMID:39487653].
- 500 mg twice daily for 4 weeks in HFrEF [PMID:38415449].
- Direct UA (500 mg) gave >6-fold higher plasma UA exposure than a glass of pomegranate juice [PMID:34117375].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Toxicology (rat, synthetic UA):
  - Not genotoxic in a standard battery.
  - 28-day and 90-day dietary studies at up to 5% of diet found no target organs or changes in clinical chemistry or hematology.
  - NOAEL was the highest dose tested: 3,451 mg/kg/d (males) and 3,826 mg/kg/d (females) [PMID:28757461].
- Human trials:
  - The first-in-human study met its primary safety outcome [PMID:32694802].
  - The 4-month RCT in older adults found no between-group difference in adverse events [PMID:35050355].
  - The SR reported only mild or moderate AEs, judged unrelated to UA [PMID:39002645].
  - The 2026 endothelial RCT judged UA well tolerated [PMID:42853569].
- Kidney stones (2026): a reference lab identified UA in urinary stones from 8 patients aged 59-87 (UA 5-80% of stone mass, mixed with calcium oxalate/phosphate; 7 of 8 female). The 6 patients with a dietary history ate walnuts daily or nearly daily, often in smoothies with berries. None was taking UA supplements. The authors attribute such stones to poorly soluble compounds crystallizing in the urinary tract [PMID:42058113]. Whether high-dose supplements carry a similar risk is unstudied.
- Hemostasis: UA inhibits human platelet aggregation in vitro (IC50 3.7 uM) and potentiated aspirin's effect. In vivo relevance is unconfirmed, and the main circulating form (glucuronide) was inactive [PMID:42812264].
- Endocrine: ER binding and weak estrogenic/antiestrogenic activity were seen in vitro [PMID:16506809]. In an animal hollow-fiber model UA did not induce uterine proliferation [PMID:37345359].
- Trial base: all RCTs are small (5 RCTs, n=236 in total in the 2026 MA) and short (4 months or less). Most were sponsored by the manufacturer [PMID:35584623] [PMID:41174221] [PMID:42559151].

** Contraindications
- No established contraindications. Trials enrolled healthy middle-aged or older adults or athletes. Pregnancy, lactation, children and organ impairment have not been studied [PMID:39002645] [PMID:42559151].
- History of calcium-oxalate or unexplained kidney stones: use caution, given the UA-containing stones (diet-derived) reported in 2026 [PMID:42058113].
- Hormone-sensitive cancers: caution is reasonable given ER binding and SERM-like activity in vitro [PMID:16506809] [PMID:37345359]. No clinical harm has been reported.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Antiplatelets/anticoagulants (aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors, warfarin): UA inhibited platelet aggregation ex vivo and potentiated acetylsalicylic acid [PMID:42812264]. These are in vitro data only, so the risk is theoretical; watch for bleeding signs.
- Warfarin and CYP3A4/CYP2C9 substrates:
  - The evidence concerns pomegranate juice, the ellagitannin source, not purified UA. A possible pomegranate-juice/warfarin INR interaction was reported in one case [PMID:19637955].
  - Pomegranate constituents inhibit CYP3A4/CYP2C9 in vitro and in animals, but human relevance is not established [PMID:36903363] [PMID:23673492].
  - No study has tested purified UA.
- CYP1A1/AhR-pathway drugs: UA modulates AhR (antagonist in human reporter cells; agonist-like in gut epithelium) and CYP1A1 [PMID:30501068] [PMID:19469472] [PMID:42336837]. Clinical significance for drug metabolism is unknown.
- Oncology (all preclinical):
  - Bortezomib: synergistic in multiple myeloma cells, including resistant cells [PMID:40022244].
  - 5-fluorouracil: chemosensitized resistant colon cancer by downregulating MDR1/BCRP/MRP2/MRP7 transporters [PMID:35910798].
  - Bicalutamide: additive antiproliferative effect but attenuated pro-apoptotic effect in prostate cancer cells; the authors advise caution [PMID:30097116].
- Cyclosporine A: combination outperformed either agent in murine lupus nephritis [PMID:42130005]. This is preclinical only.
*** Other supplements
- Spermidine: complementary mechanisms (UA targets PINK1/Parkin mitophagy; spermidine targets AMPK/SIRT1 autophagy). This is a review-level rationale with no combination trial [PMID:41404767].
- NMN / NAD+ precursors: NAD+ supplementation and UA both reversed memory deficits through mitophagy in AD models [PMID:30742114]. The PROMETHEUS feasibility protocol assigns UA and/or NMN by gerotype, but has no outcomes yet [PMID:42393428].
- Pomegranate extract plus probiotics: a 24-strain synbiotic with 400 mg pomegranate extract raised urinary UA 49-fold at day 91. 100% of participants became UA producers vs 44.4% on placebo [PMID:40944126].
- Fructooligosaccharides plus ellagic acid: synergistically raised UA biosynthesis and muscle endurance in animals [PMID:41506451].
*** Conditions
- Urolithin metabotype 0 (non-producers): these people make no UA from dietary ellagitannins. Direct supplementation bypasses this [PMID:24976365] [PMID:34117375].
- Nephrolithiasis: see Safety [PMID:42058113].
- Hormone-sensitive cancers: see Contraindications [PMID:16506809].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay interference was found. Physiological changes in trials include lower CRP, acylcarnitines and ceramides [PMID:35584623] [PMID:40034121] and a higher HDL-C in HFrEF [PMID:38415449]. Kidney-stone IR spectra can be misread because UA is missing from standard IR libraries [PMID:42058113].

** Genotype notes
- No cited pharmacogenomic source was found for UA itself.
- The relevant host variable is microbial: the urolithin metabotype.
  - UM-A (UA conjugates only): 25-80% of volunteers. UM-B (also isourolithin A/urolithin B): 10-50%. UM-0 (none): 5-25% [PMID:24976365].
  - About 10% of people are non-producers, independent of age [PMID:41866331].
  - Only 12% of adults had detectable baseline UA, and about 40% converted pomegranate-juice precursors [PMID:34117375].
- Metabotype distribution was unaffected by sex, BMI or age in one 127-person cohort [PMID:37803793].
- UM-B is linked with higher cholesterol and Coriobacteriaceae abundance [PMID:30471188], and with overweight in children (alongside a 24-SNP host panel) [PMID:32398726].
- UA production depends on Enterocloster species carrying the ucd (urolithin C dehydroxylase) operon [PMID:39856097] and on cooperating gut strains [PMID:36840624].
- Gut dysbiosis in older people may add variability to polyphenol/urolithin effects on muscle (review) [PMID:37242251].
- Ellagitannin responses differ by metabotype: pomegranate lowered LDL-C only in UM-B [PMID:27879044], and TMAO fell mainly in UM-A [PMID:41642121].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] JAMA Netw Open RCT (n=50, obesity, 1,000 mg/d x 4 wk): no improvement in brachial FMD; exploratory increase in cerebrovascular task responses [PMID:42853569].
- [2026-10] WAHA walnut trial MRI substudy (n=104): rising urinary UA was associated with preserved cortical thickness (beta 0.023 per SD, CI 0.003-0.043) [PMID:42841852].
- [2026-10] PROMETHEUS precision-geromedicine protocol assigns UA/NMN by gerotype [PMID:42393428].
- [2026-09] UA inhibits human platelet aggregation in vitro (IC50 3.7 uM) and potentiates aspirin; its glucuronide is inactive [PMID:42812264].
- [2026-07] SR/MA of 5 RCTs (n=236): 6MWT MD +17.03 m (CI -5.33 to 39.40); GRADE low; other outcomes exploratory [PMID:42559151].
- [2026-07] Critical review: no completed human CNS-endpoint trial; circulating UA is mostly conjugated [PMID:42468665].
- [2026-06] First human UA-containing kidney stones (8 patients, all diet-related) [PMID:42058113].
- [2026-06] UA activates an AhR-NLRP6-IL-18-IL-22 gut-barrier pathway, including in IBD biopsies [PMID:42336837].
- [2026-06] PolyPAUSE RCT: an ellagitannin-containing polyphenol mix raised LDL-C but lowered oxLDL, depending on metabotype [PMID:42371155].
- [2026-04] UA plus cyclosporine A outperformed monotherapy in murine lupus nephritis [PMID:42130005].
- [2026-03] Early, but not late, UA treatment prevented age-related cognitive decline in mice [PMID:41786716].
- [2026-03] Bioavailability review: about 10% non-producers; nano/liposomal forms raise exposure in preclinical work [PMID:41866331].
- [2026-02] Postmenopausal crossover RCT: polyphenol-induced TMAO reduction was metabotype-dependent (UM-A) [PMID:41642121].
- [2025-12] Review contrasts UA (mitophagy) with spermidine (autophagy) [PMID:41404767].
- [2025-11] Nature Aging RCT (n=50): UA expanded naive-like CD8+ T cells and raised FAO capacity [PMID:41174221].
- [2025-10] Pilot RCT in academy soccer players: Yo-Yo IR1 +239 m (CI 20-454) [PMID:41245402].
- [2025-08] Elite runners RCT (n=42): no time-trial gain; lower CK and RPE [PMID:40839339].
- [2025-08] Synbiotic with pomegranate extract made 100% of participants UA producers [PMID:40944126].
- [2025-01] Enterocloster ucd operon identified as the UA-producing dehydroxylase [PMID:39856097].
- [2024-11] Resistance-trained athletes RCT (n=20): MVIC and reps-to-failure improved; CRP lower [PMID:39487653].

** Open questions
- Whether surrogate mitochondrial and immune changes translate into fewer falls, less disability or less infection. All trials last 4 months or less, and most are sponsor-funded.
- Long-term (>1 year) safety, including kidney-stone risk at supplemental doses given the poorly soluble aglycone [PMID:42058113].
- In vivo antiplatelet relevance and interactions with anticoagulants/antiplatelets.
- Human CYP/UGT/transporter interaction data for purified UA. Current signals come from pomegranate juice and in vitro work.
- Brain penetration and cognitive efficacy in humans.
- Whether benefit differs by urolithin metabotype or baseline mitochondrial status, and whether producers gain anything from supplementation.
- Endocrine safety in hormone-sensitive cancers.
