** Identity & forms
- Thiamine is an essential water-soluble B vitamin. Adult RDAs are 1.2 mg/d for men and 1.1 mg/d for women, and no tolerable upper intake level was set [PMID:23193625].
- Thiamine hydrochloride and thiamine mononitrate are the water-soluble salts used in supplements, fortification and injections [PMID:22305197] [PMID:35734514]. Oral thiamine HCl is absorbed by an active, saturable carrier and by passive diffusion. High blood levels are reached quickly at 100-1500 mg doses, with exposure rising less than proportionally to dose [PMID:22305197].
- Benfotiamine (S-benzoylthiamine O-monophosphate) is a lipid-soluble S-acyl prodrug. Compared with thiamine HCl, it gave about 11-fold higher plasma thiamine bioavailability and about 2-fold higher erythrocyte TDP in humans [PMID:24399744]. In mice it raised blood and liver thiamine but not brain thiamine [PMID:18549472].
- Sulbutiamine (isobutyryl thiamine disulfide) is a lipophilic disulfide developed in Japan for beriberi. It crosses the blood-brain barrier and is marketed for asthenia and as a "nootropic" [PMID:32399290] [PMID:28755683].
- Allithiamine is a disulfide thiamine formed enzymatically in crushed garlic and other Allium bulbs. Fursultiamine (thiamine tetrahydrofurfuryl disulfide, TTFD) is its synthetic counterpart [PMID:15328496].

** Mechanisms
- Thiamine is converted in cells to thiamine diphosphate (TDP). TDP is the cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, branched-chain keto-acid dehydrogenase and transketolase, which link glucose metabolism to ATP production and the pentose phosphate pathway [PMID:40647310] [PMID:33354793].
- Deficiency causes beriberi (cardiac "wet" and neuropathic "dry" forms), Wernicke encephalopathy and Korsakoff syndrome. In regions that rely on milled rice, infantile beriberi is a public-health problem [PMID:30151974] [PMID:41684754].
- Transport: SLC19A2 (THTR-1) and SLC19A3 (THTR-2) carry thiamine into cells. THTR-2 is the main intestinal transporter [PMID:26528626] [PMID:39738067]. OCT1 (SLC22A1) is a high-capacity hepatic thiamine transporter, and metformin competitively inhibits it [PMID:24961373].
- Benfotiamine activates transketolase, which diverts glycolytic intermediates into the pentose phosphate pathway. This was proposed to protect against hyperglycemic microvascular damage [PMID:41571333].
- In sepsis and shock, thiamine is proposed to restore pyruvate dehydrogenase flux and lower lactate. Pooled RCTs show only a small lactate reduction (MD -0.34 mmol/L) [PMID:39307094].
- Magnesium is a cofactor for thiamine-dependent enzymes. Low magnesium was linked to a delayed transketolase and clinical response to thiamine in Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome [PMID:4420329].

** Dosing in trials
- Wernicke encephalopathy: guidelines recommend 200 mg IV three times daily, given before any carbohydrate [PMID:20642790]. Case reports often use 500 mg IV three times daily [PMID:38306946]. An RCT found no advantage of 500 mg three times daily over 100 mg three times daily [PMID:35428992].
- Prevention in at-risk alcohol misuse: 100 mg once daily to 300 mg three times daily parenterally for 3 days performed similarly [PMID:35428992].
- Sepsis trials: 100 mg IV every 6 h (VICTAS) or 200 mg IV every 12 h (VITAMINS), combined with vitamin C and hydrocortisone [PMID:33620405] [PMID:31950979].
- Heart failure: 200 mg/d IV for 1 week, then 200 mg/d oral for 6 weeks [PMID:7733128]. 500 mg/d thiamine mononitrate for 90 days (THIAMINE-HF) [PMID:35734514]. 25-50 mg/d in children taking diuretics [PMID:41872295].
- Benfotiamine: 300-600 mg/d for diabetic neuropathy [PMID:18473286] [PMID:41571333]. 900 mg/d was used for nephropathy [PMID:20413516]. The Alzheimer phase 2 trial tests 600 or 1200 mg/d for 72 weeks [PMID:38809849] [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06223360].
- Sulbutiamine: 400 mg/d in an open MS-fatigue study [PMID:28755683].
- High-dose oral thiamine: 600-1800 mg/d for IBD fatigue [PMID:33210299]. 300 mg/d for diabetic microalbuminuria [PMID:19057893]. 90 mg/d plus folic acid for 96 weeks in hemodialysis [PMID:42059045].
- Refeeding risk: thiamine plus multivitamin for all at-risk patients [PMID:40090863] [PMID:32115791].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Oral thiamine has very low toxicity, and no UL has been set [PMID:23193625]. High oral doses (up to 1800 mg/d) caused only mild side effects in RCTs [PMID:33210299]. Benfotiamine 600 mg/d for 12 months had AE rates similar to placebo [PMID:41571333].
- Parenteral thiamine: In a prospective series of 989 patients given a 100 mg IV bolus, 1.1% had reactions. These were almost all local irritation, with 1 case of generalized pruritus [PMID:2757284]. In 8606 IV push doses, there was no anaphylaxis and 0.30% had injection-site reactions [PMID:31932197]. In 463 doses of 500 mg IV push, there was no anaphylaxis [PMID:35491726]. Anaphylactic shock with cardiac arrest after a thiamine infusion has been reported, so resuscitation facilities should be available [PMID:23853017].
- Sulbutiamine: a case report described escalating, dependence-like use that disrupted bipolar disorder care [PMID:16861144]. Its RCT evidence base is sparse [PMID:32399290].
- Combining vitamin C with thiamine in sepsis was associated with higher mortality in an older, comorbid cluster in an observational analysis (hypothesis-generating) [PMID:39614357].

** Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to thiamine, especially for IV use [PMID:23853017] [PMID:2757284].
- No absolute oral contraindication was identified. Withholding thiamine from at-risk patients is the greater documented hazard [PMID:20642790] [PMID:41684754].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Loop diuretics (furosemide, torasemide): these increase urinary thiamine loss and may inhibit cellular uptake [PMID:26899752]. Furosemide was the drug most often reported in FAERS thiamine-deficiency cases [PMID:41864862]. Furosemide dose predicted worsening TPP effect in children [PMID:41872295]. A stable outpatient cohort had a deficiency prevalence below 11.6% [PMID:26923517].
- Metformin: it is a substrate and inhibitor of THTR-2 and competitively inhibits OCT1-mediated thiamine uptake [PMID:26528626] [PMID:24961373]. It is predicted to reach intestinal concentrations high enough to inhibit THTR-2 [PMID:31764942]. It ranked second among drugs in FAERS thiamine-deficiency reports [PMID:41864862]. Human clinical depletion data are limited.
- Fedratinib (JAK2 inhibitor): it potently inhibits THTR-2. Wernicke encephalopathy halted its early development [PMID:25063672] [PMID:39738067]. It shows a FAERS signal [PMID:41864862].
- 5-fluorouracil: Wernicke encephalopathy after 5-FU chemotherapy has been reported [PMID:19654964]. Low thiamine predicted neurological symptoms in patients on GI-cancer chemotherapy [PMID:31657715]. 5-FU is among the top five FAERS signals [PMID:41864862].
- IV glucose/dextrose: EFNS advises giving thiamine before carbohydrate [PMID:20642790]. The evidence that a single glucose load precipitates Wernicke encephalopathy is limited to case reports; prolonged glucose without thiamine is the risk [PMID:22104258]. In 120 encounters of intoxicated veterans given dextrose before thiamine, there were no cases [PMID:40873301]. Treatment of hypoglycemia should not be delayed [PMID:22104258].
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide): 6 published cases of Wernicke encephalopathy followed prolonged vomiting and rapid weight loss [PMID:42399213].
*** Other supplements
- Green Tea Extract and tea/coffee polyphenols: tannic acid inactivates thiamine in vitro, and ascorbic acid blocks this reaction [PMID:910744]. Polyphenols alter intestinal thiamine transport in experimental models [PMID:20392307]. Clinical relevance at usual intakes is unquantified.
- Magnesium: hypomagnesemia may cause thiamine-refractory Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome [PMID:4420329]. Trialists recommend correcting magnesium as well [PMID:35428992].
- Vitamin C: no benefit when combined with thiamine in sepsis RCTs [PMID:33620405] [PMID:31950979]. It protects thiamine from tannin inactivation in vitro [PMID:910744].
- B Complex: combination B-vitamin regimens appeared more effective than single agents for diabetic neuropathy, though not significantly in most interaction tests [PMID:42452619].
*** Conditions
- Alcohol use disorder: this is the main cause of Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome [PMID:35428992] [PMID:23818100]. Guidelines advise parenteral thiamine for all at-risk patients admitted to emergency departments [PMID:20642790].
- Bariatric and other GI surgery: Pooled deficiency prevalence is 27% after surgery [PMID:35776243]. Wernicke encephalopathy typically appears 4-12 weeks post-op with vomiting [PMID:17353468]. Mortality ranged from 0.3% in bariatric cohorts to 40% in oncologic cohorts [PMID:42453365]. EFNS advises monitoring status for at least 6 months [PMID:20642790].
- Refeeding syndrome: thiamine deficiency is part of the ASPEN severe-RS definition [PMID:32115791]. AuSPEN recommends thiamine for all at-risk patients [PMID:40090863].
- Diabetes: lower circulating thiamine than controls (SMD -0.97) [PMID:37094704].
- Pediatric Wernicke encephalopathy: 37% of cases had received parenteral nutrition without thiamine [PMID:39997622].
- Heart failure on diuretics: see Drugs above.
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact is known. Whole-blood TDP by HPLC is the preferred status test; about 90% of blood thiamine is TDP [PMID:18356241] [PMID:42778271]. Plasma thiamine reflects recent intake only [PMID:42778271].
- Draw blood before giving thiamine, because supplementation raises all thiamine analytes [PMID:20642790] [PMID:41571333].
- The erythrocyte transketolase activity coefficient (ETKAC) is a functional marker, but cut-offs lack harmonization [PMID:33354793].
- TDP expressed in nmol/L can mislead in anemia or polycythemia. A hemoglobin-normalized reference interval is 249-531 ng/g Hb [PMID:42833437].
- No biomarker reliably predicted individual clinical response in infants [PMID:38974350].

** Genotype notes
- SLC19A2 (THTR-1): biallelic mutations cause thiamine-responsive megaloblastic anemia with diabetes and deafness (Rogers syndrome) [PMID:10391221].
- SLC19A3 (THTR-2): mutations cause biotin-thiamine-responsive basal ganglia disease. Crises recurred on biotin alone but not after thiamine was added [PMID:23742248]. Common SLC19A3 variants were associated with protection from severe retinopathy/nephropathy in type 1 diabetes [PMID:26718501]. SLC19A3 haplotypes were associated with glycemic indices in type 2 diabetes and altered the in vitro response to metformin [PMID:40243602].
- Cryo-EM structures show metformin and fedratinib binding THTR-1/THTR-2. These are the structural basis of drug-nutrient interactions [PMID:39738067].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Reference interval for hemoglobin-normalized whole-blood TDP: 249-531 ng/g Hb. Using it reclassified 11.3% of routine samples [PMID:42833437].
- [2026-07] SR of 6 cases of Wernicke encephalopathy after semaglutide for obesity, several with poor outcomes [PMID:42399213].
- [2026-07] B-vitamin MA for diabetic neuropathy (13 RCTs, n=834): MNSI improvements below the MCID; no effect on generic pain scores [PMID:42452619].
- [2026-07] Methods review: whole-blood TDP and ETKAC are the reliable status markers; plasma thiamine is not [PMID:42778271].
- [2026-06] Critical-care review (18 RCTs, 9 MAs since 2023): very-high-dose micronutrients without prior deficiency are not beneficial [PMID:42273945].
- [2026-06] SR of Wernicke encephalopathy after GI procedures (13 studies, n=1036): vomiting is the main trigger, and atypical presentations are common [PMID:42453365].
- [2026-04] Hemodialysis RCT (n=215, 96 wk): thiamine 90 mg plus folic acid improved ADAS-Cog [PMID:42059045].
- [2026-03] FAERS 2004-2024: 1046 drug-related thiamine-deficiency cases. Top signals were furosemide, metformin, fedratinib, metronidazole and 5-FU [PMID:41864862].
- [2026-03] Pediatric RCT (n=45) in children on diuretics: 25-50 mg/d did not change TPP effect or LVEF [PMID:41872295].
- [2026-03] Sepsis MA (9 RCTs, n=520): less RRT, no short-term mortality effect [PMID:41863966].
- [2026-02] Clinical review of thiamine indications in hospitalized patients [PMID:41684754].
- [2026-01] BOND RCT: benfotiamine 600 mg/d for 12 months gave no benefit on DSPN morphometric or clinical measures [PMID:41571333].
- [2025-11] Septic shock MA (6 RCTs): in-hospital mortality RR 0.80, RRT RR 0.48 [PMID:41277404].
- [2025-08] VA cohort: no Wernicke encephalopathy after dextrose-before-thiamine in intoxicated patients [PMID:40873301].
- [2025-05] RCT (n=43): B1+B2 100 mg each reduced perceived stress and improved sleep, but not anxiety [PMID:40507089].
- [2025-03] AuSPEN refeeding consensus: thiamine for all at-risk patients [PMID:40090863].
- [2025-03] Cardiac surgery RCT (n=64): vitamin C plus B1 lowered myocardial injury markers vs vitamin C alone [PMID:40290059].
- [2025-03] SLC19A3 haplotypes associated with metabolic traits and metformin response [PMID:40243602].
- [2024-12] Cryo-EM structures of SLC19A2/A3 with metformin and fedratinib [PMID:39738067].
- [2024-12] Maternal thiamine 10 mg/d improved infant language processing in Cambodia (RCT, n=335) [PMID:39699595].
- [2024-11] Septic cluster analysis: thiamine alone was associated with lower mortality in severe clusters; C+B1 may harm older patients [PMID:39614357].

** Open questions
- What are the optimal dose, route and duration for Wernicke encephalopathy treatment and prophylaxis? RCT evidence remains insufficient [PMID:23818100] [PMID:35428992].
- Does thiamine benefit septic patients with documented deficiency? Exploratory subgroups suggest it might [PMID:41863966] [PMID:42273945].
- Does high-dose thiamine help heart-failure patients with confirmed deficiency or heavy diuretic use? A larger trial was recommended [PMID:35734514] [PMID:38940395].
- Is benfotiamine effective in early Alzheimer disease? BenfoTeam has an estimated completion of 2027 [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06223360] [PMID:33074237].
- Does long-term metformin cause clinically meaningful thiamine depletion in humans [PMID:31764942]?
- What are harmonized ETKAC and TDP cut-offs [PMID:33354793] [PMID:42833437]?
- What is the efficacy and abuse liability of sulbutiamine in RCTs [PMID:32399290] [PMID:16861144]?
