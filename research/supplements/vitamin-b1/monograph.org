** Identity & forms
- Thiamin (vitamin B1) is water soluble; common supplements include thiamin hydrochloride, thiamin mononitrate, and lipid-soluble benfotiamine. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/]

** Mechanisms
- Thiamin diphosphate is a cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase and transketolase, linking carbohydrate metabolism and energy production. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/]

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thiamin deficiency / beriberi | Clinical treatment evidence | benefit | Replacement treats deficiency; untreated deficiency can cause neurologic/cardiac disease | high | URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/ |
| Critical illness mortality | Systematic review of RCTs | uncertain | Meta-analysis does not establish consistent mortality benefit | low | PMID:39307094 |
| Diabetic neuropathy (benfotiamine) | Short RCTs | possible symptom benefit | Short-term symptom scores improved in some trials; long-term outcomes unclear | low | PMID:17383958 |

** Dosing in trials
- RDA is 1.1 mg/day women and 1.2 mg/day men; trials in critical illness use IV regimens, while benfotiamine neuropathy studies often use 300–600 mg/day. Dose depends strongly on deficiency/clinical indication. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/]

** Safety & adverse effects
- No established tolerable upper intake level due to low toxicity; rare allergic reactions reported with parenteral administration. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/]

** Contraindications
- No common oral contraindication identified; parenteral thiamin requires clinical administration because hypersensitivity can occur. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- Loop diuretics and chronic alcohol use can contribute to thiamin depletion; thiamin status can be affected by severe malnutrition. No common direct drug interaction or lab assay interference established. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of IV thiamine RCTs in critically ill patients reflects continued uncertainty on patient-important outcomes. [PMID:39307094]

** Open questions
- Define which critical-illness subgroups benefit and whether oral benfotiamine changes long-term neuropathy outcomes.
