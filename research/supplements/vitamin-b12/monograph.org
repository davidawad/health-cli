** Identity & forms
- Cobalamin forms include cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, adenosylcobalamin and hydroxocobalamin. Supplements and fortified foods are important sources for people with low animal-food intake or impaired absorption. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/]

** Mechanisms
- B12 is a cofactor for methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase, supporting DNA synthesis, red-cell formation and neurologic function. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/]

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed deficiency | Clinical replacement evidence | benefit | Prevents/treats megaloblastic anemia and neurologic manifestations | high | URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/ |

** Dosing in trials
- RDA is 2.4 µg/day in adults. Deficiency treatment regimens commonly use high oral doses (e.g. 1,000–2,000 µg/day) or intramuscular therapy depending on cause/severity. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/]

** Safety & adverse effects
- No UL established; generally low toxicity. Rare acneiform eruptions and hypersensitivity are reported. Check methylmalonic acid when serum B12 borderline and interpretation is needed. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/]

** Contraindications
- Caution in cobalt allergy; cyanocobalamin-specific considerations may apply in rare cyanide metabolism disorders. Treat suspected neurologic deficiency promptly. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- Metformin and proton-pump inhibitors/H2 blockers can impair B12 status with long-term use; nitrous oxide can inactivate B12. Chloramphenicol may blunt hematologic response. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- 2025 meta-analysis of older-adult RCTs found a very small high-certainty pooled effect of B vitamins B6/B9/B12 on global cognition; it does not establish isolated B12 benefit. [PMID:40966571]

** Open questions
- Define screening and treatment strategies for subclinical deficiency and separate benefits of repletion from supplementation in replete people.
