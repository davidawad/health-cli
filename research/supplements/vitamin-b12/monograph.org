** Identity & forms
- Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is a cobalt-containing corrinoid (cyanocobalamin injection is 4.34% cobalt). It is found only in animal-source foods; with normal absorption, dietary deficiency has been reported only in strict vegetarians [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
- Cyanocobalamin: the stable synthetic form. It is used in most OTC tablets, US injections and the large homocysteine trials [PMID:20424250] [PMID:25411293].
- Hydroxocobalamin: the usual UK intramuscular (IM) form. After loading, many patients with malabsorption are maintained on 1000 ug IM every 2 months, but up to half need more frequent dosing [PMID:38987879]. High-dose IV hydroxocobalamin is a cyanide antidote [PMID:33444605].
- Methylcobalamin (mecobalamin) and adenosylcobalamin are the two intracellular coenzyme forms [PMID:33881359]. NICE accepts oral cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin or adenosylcobalamin for dietary deficiency [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations]. The corpus has a separate "B12 methylcobalamin" item; this monograph covers B12 generally. No head-to-head RCT showing that one oral form is clinically superior was retrieved.
- Routes: oral, sublingual, IM and nasal. A 2025 meta-analysis (16 studies, n=6098) found no difference between oral, sublingual and IM routes in serum B12 rise (pooled +402.6 pg/mL) or homocysteine fall (-4.83 umol/L). I2 was >80% [PMID:41487531].

** Mechanisms
- B12 is the cofactor for two enzymes [PMID:33881359] [PMID:39367523]: methionine synthase (methylcobalamin), which remethylates homocysteine using 5-methyl-THF; methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (adenosylcobalamin). Deficiency therefore raises total homocysteine (tHcy) and methylmalonic acid (MMA).
- Absorption: food B12 is released by acid/pepsin, then binds haptocorrin and intrinsic factor (IF), and is absorbed in the ileum by a calcium-dependent IF receptor (cubilin). In blood it circulates on transcobalamin (holoTC, "active B12") and on haptocorrin (holoHC, taken up only by liver) [PMID:33881359] [PMID:29040465] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
- High oral doses bypass IF through passive absorption. In older adults with mild deficiency, 647-1032 ug/day oral cyanocobalamin was needed for 80-90% of the maximal MMA reduction [PMID:15911731]. After injection, 50-98% of a 100-1000 ug dose appears in urine within 48 h [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
- Folate interaction: folic acid >0.1 mg/day can correct the anemia of B12 deficiency but not the neurologic damage [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190]. In NHANES, high folate with low B12 was linked to anemia, cognitive impairment (OR 2.6) and higher tHcy/MMA [PMID:17209196] [PMID:18056804]. Mechanisms are reviewed in [PMID:40315282].
- Nitrous oxide oxidizes cobalamin and inactivates methionine synthase. Functional deficiency occurs even when serum B12 is normal [PMID:37371921].

** Dosing in trials
- Reference intake in pregnancy and lactation is 4 ug/day (FNB value cited on the label) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
- Malabsorption: NICE advises at least 1 mg/day orally. IM is considered for neurological features or rapidly deteriorating conditions [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations]. A US review agrees: oral is non-inferior, and IM is for severe or neurologic cases [PMID:40961307]. Oral-vs-IM RCTs used 1000-2000 ug/day [PMID:29543316] [PMID:9694707].
- After total gastrectomy: oral mecobalamin 500-1500 ug/day normalized serum B12 in most patients, but studies were short and mostly non-randomized [PMID:42287473].
- Homocysteine trials: VITACOG: B12 0.5 mg + folic acid 0.8 mg + B6 20 mg [PMID:20838622]. B-PROOF: B12 500 ug + folic acid 400 ug [PMID:25411293]. DIVINe: cyanocobalamin 1 mg + folic acid 2.5 mg + B6 25 mg [PMID:20424250].
- Across trials, regimens ranged from 0.02-1 mg/day orally to 1-5 mg/week IM, for about 4 weeks to 7 years [PMID:42197057].
- Vegans have lower serum B12/holoTC and higher tHcy than omnivores [PMID:39373282]. In pregnant vegetarians, 250 vs 50 ug/day methylcobalamin gave a slightly higher infant mental DQ [PMID:41850742].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Oral B12 has a wide margin of safety: very low-quality evidence suggests oral is as safe as IM [PMID:29543316]. A 2026 review found no consistent benefit in people without deficiency and advises against routine testing in people without risk factors [PMID:42197057].
- Hypersensitivity: the label reports anaphylaxis and deaths after parenteral B12 [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190]. In a UK series (n=29) there were 8 anaphylaxis cases (4 to hydroxocobalamin, 4 to cyanocobalamin). Some patients tolerated the alternative form, and one reacted to PEG in an oral product [PMID:37898177].
- Label warnings when treating severe megaloblastic anemia [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190]: hypokalemia and sudden death (monitor potassium for 48 h); early pulmonary edema/CHF; peripheral thrombosis and polycythemia vera.
- Acne/rosacea: case reports describe acneiform or rosacea-fulminans eruptions with high-dose B6+B12 that resolved on stopping [PMID:1834437] [PMID:11763399]. A dermatology review lists acne, rosacea and cobalt or injection-site reactions [PMID:25559140]. Paradoxically, an uncontrolled series (n=13) reported that IM hydroxocobalamin acutely reduced rosacea erythema [PMID:35783562].
- Renal impairment: In DIVINe, B vitamins with 1 mg cyanocobalamin sped GFR loss and doubled vascular events [PMID:20424250]. Spence hypothesizes that cyanide/thiocyanate from cyanocobalamin accumulates at GFR <50, and suggests methylcobalamin or hydroxocobalamin instead. This idea is untested in trials [PMID:23449527] [PMID:26597770]. A meta-analysis found cardiovascular benefit from B vitamins only with normal renal function [PMID:37850302].
- Hip fracture (observational, Nurses' Health Study): combined B6 >=35 mg/d plus B12 >=20 ug/d was associated with RR 1.47 (1.15-1.89) [PMID:31074816].
- Cancer: High serum B12 marks cancer, mostly diagnosed within 1 year (SIR 6.27 at >800 pmol/L in people not receiving B12), which suggests reverse causation [PMID:24249744]. Mendelian randomization suggests genetically higher B12 is associated with lung cancer (OR 1.08/SD) and colorectal cancer (OR 1.22/SD) [PMID:30499135] [PMID:37605270]. B-PROOF showed a cancer signal (see table) [PMID:30341095].
- Mortality: each +100 pmol/L serum B12 was associated with HR 1.04 for all-cause mortality in 22 cohorts [PMID:38252787]. The hypervitaminosis-B12 mortality risk was confined to chronically ill and hospitalized groups, and is likely artefactual [PMID:40647287].
- Pregnancy: in a Nepal RCT, maternal B12 had a negative effect on early infant motor scores, more pronounced when mothers were already replete [PMID:41461260].

** Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cobalamin or cobalt [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190] [PMID:37898177].
- Early Leber hereditary optic neuropathy: the label warns of severe, swift optic atrophy with cyanocobalamin. B12 deficiency is also more common in LHON carriers (21% vs 5-7%) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190] [PMID:35945620].
- Diabetic nephropathy or CKD: avoid high-dose cyanocobalamin-containing B-vitamin regimens [PMID:20424250] [PMID:23449527].
- Folic acid alone should not be given for undiagnosed macrocytic anemia, because it masks B12 neuropathy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190] [PMID:17209196].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Metformin (corpus: Metformin ER): RCT (850 mg three times daily for 4.3 y): B12 -19% vs placebo, deficiency +7.2 percentage points, NNH 13.8 [PMID:20488910]. DPPOS: OR 1.13 per year of use [PMID:26900641]. Meta-analyses: deficiency OR 2.45 / RR 2.09 / OR 2.95; dose- and duration-dependent [PMID:25502588] [PMID:30615306] [PMID:36628003]. Children: no significant decline (2026 MA) [PMID:42144864]. Guidance: ADA Standards since 2017 recommend periodic B12 measurement, especially with anemia or neuropathy [PMID:31955662] [PMID:41358900]. MHRA (2022) calls low B12 a common side effect, advises testing if symptomatic and periodic monitoring if risk factors are present [URL:https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/metformin-and-reduced-vitamin-b12-levels-new-advice-for-monitoring-patients-at-risk].
- PPIs and H2RAs: Case-control study (25,956 cases): >=2 years of PPI use OR 1.65 (1.58-1.73); H2RA OR 1.25 (1.17-1.34) [PMID:24327038]. Meta-analysis OR 1.42 (1.16-1.73) [PMID:37060552]. A 2025 meta-analysis of 6 studies found no serum B12 difference [PMID:40823471]. NICE lists both classes as risk factors [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations].
- Nitrous oxide: In recreational users, total B12 was low in only 42%, but tHcy was raised in about 80% [PMID:37371921]. Adolescent myeloneuropathy series and review [PMID:42474763]. US poison-center cases rose 1332% from 2003 to 2024 [PMID:41396044]. NICE: test tHcy or MMA first [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations].
- Colchicine causes B12 malabsorption [PMID:5677718]. The label also lists para-aminosalicylic acid and heavy alcohol use [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].
- NICE also lists phenobarbital, primidone, topiramate and pregabalin as risk-factor medicines [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations].
- Capecitabine: methylcobalamin halved grade >=2 hand-foot syndrome. Capecitabine dose reductions were 7.7% vs 13.7% (not significant) [PMID:40935571].
*** Other supplements
- B12 methylcobalamin and B Complex: these duplicate cobalamin dosing. High combined B6+B12 intake is associated with hip fracture and with acneiform/rosacea eruptions [PMID:31074816] [PMID:1834437] [PMID:11763399].
- Vitamin B6: co-component of VITACOG and DIVINe [PMID:20838622] [PMID:20424250]. High-dose combinations carry the fracture and skin signals above [PMID:31074816].
- Quatrefolic (5-MTHF)/folate: Additive lowering of tHcy [PMID:24965307]. High folate can mask or worsen B12 deficiency [PMID:17209196] [PMID:18056804] [PMID:40315282]. TCN2 776GG carriers with folate >800 ug/d had higher odds of neuropathy [PMID:27733392].
- EPA/DHA/DPA: in VITACOG, B vitamins slowed cognitive decline only when omega-3 status was good [PMID:26757190].
- Trimethylglycine (TMG): betaine remethylates homocysteine by a B12-independent route; >=4 g/d lowered tHcy by 1.23 umol/L (MA) [PMID:23997720].
- Vitamin C: the 1974 claim that ascorbate destroys food B12 [PMID:4479087] was not reproduced with standard assays [PMID:1274888].
*** Conditions
- Autoimmune gastritis/pernicious anemia: lifelong replacement is needed; oral >=1 mg/day is acceptable [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations]. 10-year incidence of gastric NET was 18% and of adenocarcinoma 9% [PMID:41101382].
- Gastrectomy/bariatric surgery [PMID:42287473]; vegan diet [PMID:39373282]; CKD/diabetic nephropathy [PMID:20424250]; LHON [PMID:35945620].
*** Lab-test interference
- Recent B12 intake raises serum B12. Draw samples before treatment and ask about OTC B12 use [URL:https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations].
- IF antibody radioassay: falsely positive for 24 h after a 1 mg injection, and unreliable when serum B12 >3500 pg/mL [PMID:6465096].
- Anti-IF antibodies can make competitive-binding B12 assays falsely normal while MMA/tHcy are raised [PMID:37987415].
- No test is a gold standard. Total B12 includes inactive holoHC; holoTC, MMA and tHcy each have pitfalls [PMID:39367523] [PMID:24942828].
- Hydroxocobalamin at antidote levels interferes with 27 of 77 analytes [PMID:33444605].
- Label: most antibiotics, methotrexate and pyrimethamine invalidate folate/B12 diagnostic assays [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cdd4fd12-e214-43a6-b560-d56fc0d87190].

** Genotype notes
- FUT2 (rs601338 non-secretor): GWAS association with serum B12 [PMID:19303062]. The effect acts through holoHC, not holoTC, so it mainly shifts the test value rather than function [PMID:29040465].
- CUBN rs1801222: AA genotype OR 2.43 for metformin-induced B12 deficiency, replicated in DPPOS [PMID:41537778].
- TCN2 776C>G: GG genotype neuropathy OR 3.33, rising to 6.9 with high folate intake [PMID:27733392].
- MTR/MTRR (with MTHFR): methylfolate + P5P + methylcobalamin lowered tHcy by 30% (48% in homozygotes) in a small RCT (n=54) [PMID:38892484]. No genotype-guided B12 dosing data were found.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Gastrectomy scoping review: oral mecobalamin is biochemically non-inferior to parenteral [PMID:42287473].
- [2026-07] Adolescent N2O myeloneuropathy: 92% had raised tHcy/MMA, but only 45% had low B12 [PMID:42474763].
- [2026-06] Cochrane review in children: less deficiency, no cognition or growth effect [PMID:42227307].
- [2026-05] Review: no consistent benefit without deficiency [PMID:42197057]. Pediatric metformin MA: no significant decline [PMID:42144864].
- [2026-04] CUBN rs1801222 identified as a risk factor for metformin-induced deficiency [PMID:41537778]. US N2O misuse up 1332% [PMID:41396044].
- [2026-03] MATCOBIND pregnancy RCT [PMID:41850742].
- [2026-02] Nepal RCT: negative infant motor effect [PMID:41461260].
- [2026-01] ADA Standards of Care 2026, section 9 [PMID:41358900].
- [2025-12] Atrophic gastritis/pernicious anemia cohort: 10-year incidence of gastric NET 18%, adenocarcinoma 9% [PMID:41101382].
- [2025-12] Route MA: no difference [PMID:41487531]. Cognition MA: g 0.42, very low certainty [PMID:40966571]. CHD MA: no effect on MACE [PMID:41615824].
- [2025-09] Methylcobalamin prevented capecitabine hand-foot syndrome (BMJ) [PMID:40935571]. AFP review [PMID:40961307].
- [2025-08] PPI MA: null [PMID:40823471].

- [2024-11] MA: no effect on memory or depression [PMID:39655146].

** Open questions
- Whether methyl-, hydroxo- or adenosylcobalamin beats cyanocobalamin clinically, including in renal impairment.
- Whether high B12 causes cancer or only marks it (MR and B-PROOF signals).
- The optimal metformin screening interval, and whether supplementation prevents neuropathy.
- Whether VITACOG-type benefit in MCI with high tHcy reduces dementia incidence.
- Long-term oral maintenance in pernicious anemia, given OB12's 52-week shortfall.
