** Identity & forms
- Riboflavin is a water-soluble, yellow, naturally fluorescent B vitamin. It is the precursor of the coenzymes flavin mononucleotide (FMN, also called riboflavin-5'-phosphate) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/] [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/7].
- Supplement forms: Free riboflavin is the form used in the migraine and MTHFR trials [PMID:9484373] [PMID:23608654]. Riboflavin-5'-phosphate sodium (FMN sodium) is the more water-soluble salt. EFSA re-evaluated it as food additive E 101(ii) together with riboflavin E 101(i) [DOI:10.2903/j.efsa.2013.3357]. Free riboflavin is about as bioavailable as dietary FAD and FMN, which are hydrolysed to riboflavin before absorption [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. No head-to-head human trial showing clinical superiority of oral R5P over riboflavin was found.
- Topical riboflavin 5'-phosphate ophthalmic solutions are FDA-approved photoenhancers for corneal cross-linking: Photrexa (epithelium-off) and Epioxa (epithelium-on) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f82cfe92-1a24-11e3-8ffd-0800200c9a66] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13cefc46-337c-41a3-b113-106bfe553bbb].
- Reference intakes: US RDA: 1.3 mg/day for men and 1.1 mg/day for women; 1.4 mg in pregnancy and 1.6 mg in lactation [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. EFSA published its own Dietary Reference Values in 2017 [DOI:10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4919]. US mean intake is about 2.5 mg/day in men and 1.8 mg/day in women [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Absorption is saturable. In a human PK study, the most absorbed from a single oral dose was 27 mg (absorption half-life 1.1 h), and urinary excretion accounted for about half of plasma clearance [PMID:8604671].
- Status markers: The erythrocyte glutathione reductase activation coefficient (EGRAC) is the standard marker. Common cut-offs are <=1.2 adequate, 1.2-1.4 marginal and >1.4 deficient [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. Results are sensitive to how the assay is run. Methodological changes may explain a jump in UK deficiency prevalence from 2% to 46% in men between survey rounds [PMID:19102813]. Erythrocyte pyridoxamine phosphate oxidase (PPO) activity is an alternative marker that shows a dose response [PMID:19740970].

** Mechanisms
- Flavoproteins: FAD and FMN are cofactors for redox enzymes in energy production, glutathione recycling, and fat, drug and steroid metabolism [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/] [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin]. Poor status reduces metabolism of folate and vitamin B6 [PMID:12791609].
- Migraine rationale: migraine brains are proposed to have a mitochondrial energy deficit, and riboflavin was trialled to increase mitochondrial energy efficiency [PMID:7828189] [PMID:9484373].
- One-carbon metabolism: FAD is the cofactor of MTHFR. The 677TT variant has reduced MTHFR activity, and riboflavin status appears to be the environmental factor modifying its BP and homocysteine phenotype [PMID:27720779] [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin]. FMN/FAD-dependent methionine synthase reductase regenerates methylcobalamin, linking riboflavin to vitamin B12 function [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Vitamin B6 activation: pyridoxine/pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate oxidase (PNPO/PPO) is FMN-dependent [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin]. Plasma PLP falls stepwise as riboflavin status worsens, independent of B6 intake [PMID:32805038] [PMID:36264281].
- Niacin: tryptophan-to-NAD conversion needs the FAD-dependent kynurenine 3-monooxygenase [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Iron: riboflavin deficiency is linked to impaired iron handling, including release of iron from ferritin for red-cell production [PMID:39540254] [PMID:21525198].
- Transport: three transporters (RFVT1-3, encoded by SLC52A1-3) mediate absorption and tissue distribution. Cryo-EM structures of RFVT2 and RFVT3 were solved in 2025 [PMID:40307217].
- Ferroptosis (preclinical): FAD binding stabilises the ferroptosis suppressor FSP1, linking riboflavin metabolism to resistance to lipid peroxidation [PMID:41826699].
- Corneal cross-linking: UV-A light excites riboflavin in the corneal stroma, producing reactive oxygen species that cross-link collagen [PMID:39681212].

** Dosing in trials
- Migraine prophylaxis: 400 mg/day once daily for at least 3 months is the standard regimen [PMID:9484373] [PMID:7828189]. Benefit was most pronounced in the third month [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin]. Dose-response meta-analysis found a linear benefit up to 400 mg/day [PMID:41769676]. Children: 200-400 mg/day in observational use [PMID:19649688]. A 200 mg/day RCT was null [PMID:18984840]. Combination product: riboflavin with magnesium and Q10 for 3 months [PMID:25916335].
- MTHFR 677TT blood pressure and homocysteine: 1.6 mg/day for 12-16 weeks, a physiological dose [PMID:23608654] [PMID:19952781] [PMID:16380544].
- Anemia: 2 or 4 mg/day for 8 weeks in UK women [PMID:21525198]. 1.0 mg/day added to 60 mg iron + 400 ug folic acid in pregnancy [PMID:18806105].
- Inherited metabolic disease: pharmacological high doses, started immediately when riboflavin transporter deficiency is suspected, without waiting for genetic confirmation [PMID:26973221] [PMID:42046426].
- Above about 27 mg per dose, absorption saturates, so split dosing is a pharmacokinetic rationale. This has not been tested clinically [PMID:8604671].

** Safety & adverse effects
- No UL was set because no adverse effects from food or supplements have been reported [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/7] [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. The IOM noted that high-dose studies were not designed to assess harm systematically [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/7]. No short-term side effects were seen with 400 mg/day for at least 3 months [PMID:7828189].
- In the 400 mg RCT, AEs were minor: diarrhoea and polyuria in 2 patients [PMID:9484373]. The Cochrane BP review found little to no difference in adverse events (low certainty) [PMID:41123035]. AEs in inherited metabolic disease were infrequent and mild [PMID:42046426].
- Urine discoloration: high doses turn urine bright yellow (flavinuria). This is harmless [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin]. The Canadian Headache Society judged side effects, such as discoloured urine, to be minimal [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/].
- Light sensitivity: riboflavin is photolabile. Prolonged neonatal phototherapy can deplete it [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/], and also PLP [PMID:4040702].
- Possible harm signals: Riboflavin/niacin increased posterior subcapsular cataract in the Linxian trial [PMID:8363468]. In cell culture, excess riboflavin increased DNA strand breaks with chromium(VI); no human data exist [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Topical CXL is a separate risk profile: ulcerative keratitis, haze and herpetic keratitis per the labels. These risks do not apply to oral use [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f82cfe92-1a24-11e3-8ffd-0800200c9a66] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13cefc46-337c-41a3-b113-106bfe553bbb].

** Contraindications
- Oral riboflavin has no established contraindications beyond hypersensitivity. No UL exists [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/7].
- Pediatric migraine: evidence is inconsistent, and 2024 reviews do not consider pediatric efficacy proven [PMID:39853578] [PMID:18984840].
- Epioxa (topical) is contraindicated in aphakic eyes and in pseudophakic eyes without a UV-blocking IOL, and with hypersensitivity to benzalkonium chloride [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=13cefc46-337c-41a3-b113-106bfe553bbb].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- ODS states that riboflavin "is not known to have any clinically relevant interactions with medications" [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. The interactions below are mostly drug effects on riboflavin status, based on animal or in-vitro data.
- Phenothiazines (chlorpromazine) and tricyclic antidepressants (imipramine, amitriptyline): In rat tissues they inhibit flavokinase and block conversion of riboflavin to FAD [PMID:6262379]. Chlorpromazine doubled urinary riboflavin loss and sped up FAD depletion in rats on a riboflavin-deficient diet [PMID:6626265]. In rabbit renal tubules, chlorpromazine inhibited tubular riboflavin transport [PMID:11078692]. LPI also lists quinacrine and doxorubicin (adriamycin) as inhibitors of flavocoenzyme formation [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Probenecid: a human study specifically examined probenecid's effect on renal riboflavin clearance [PMID:5440673]. In isolated renal tubules, probenecid inhibited riboflavin secretion, which runs through the organic-anion pathway [PMID:11078692].
- Phenobarbital: long-term use may increase hepatic breakdown of riboflavin [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Other drugs that promote renal riboflavin excretion: boric acid and some antimalarial, antimicrobial, anticancer, tricyclic and antipsychotic agents (review) [PMID:3319474].
- Oral contraceptives: an apparent lowering of riboflavin status disappeared after adjustment for diet [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Tetracyclines/doxycycline: no cited source found. ODS (2022 update) lists no clinically relevant drug interactions, and no PubMed evidence of a riboflavin-tetracycline absorption interaction was found.
- Antihypertensives: in MTHFR 677TT patients already on treatment, riboflavin lowered BP further [PMID:23608654] [PMID:22277556].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin B6: FMN-dependent PNPO is needed to form PLP. Low riboflavin status predicts lower plasma PLP, most markedly in older adults and in MTHFR 677TT carriers [PMID:32805038] [PMID:36264281] [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Folate / 5-MTHF (Quatrefolic): Riboflavin acts upstream of 5-MTHF as the MTHFR cofactor [PMID:27720779]. L-methylfolate lowered homocysteine more in MTHFR 677TT than in CC/CT adults [PMID:38056998]. In elderly people with low status, folic acid, not riboflavin, lowered homocysteine [PMID:12209373].
- Vitamin B12: methionine synthase reductase needs FMN and FAD to regenerate methylcobalamin [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Iron (including heme iron): Correcting marginal riboflavin deficiency raised hemoglobin without changing iron intake or absorption [PMID:21525198]. Adding riboflavin with retinol to iron-folate reduced anemia in pregnancy [PMID:18806105]. Correcting riboflavin deficiency improves the response of iron-deficiency anemia to iron [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- Magnesium and CoQ10 (migraine co-therapy): The Canadian Headache Society strongly recommends riboflavin, CoQ10 and magnesium citrate individually [PMID:22683887]. AAN/AHS rated riboflavin and magnesium Level B and CoQ10 Level C [PMID:22529203]. A fixed combination reduced migraine burden but not migraine days [PMID:25916335]. Riboflavin 400 mg + magnesium + feverfew did no better than riboflavin 25 mg [PMID:15447697]. Single-agent evidence: CoQ10 RCT [PMID:15728298], CoQ10 MA [PMID:33402403], magnesium MA [PMID:26752497]. A 2026 network MA found CoQ10 and vitamins reduced frequency, while magnesium did not reach significance for frequency but did lower severity [PMID:42707444].
- B complex / multivitamins: riboflavin is a common component, so totals add up. A B1+B2 100 mg/100 mg product raised urinary B2 about 20-fold [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin] [PMID:40507089].
*** Conditions
- Riboflavin transporter deficiency (formerly BVVL/Fazio-Londe; SLC52A2, SLC52A3): High-dose riboflavin is lifesaving and should start empirically [PMID:26973221] [PMID:23107375] [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. The biochemical profile can mimic mild MADD [PMID:21110228]. Adult-onset disease can mimic ALS or distal hereditary motor neuropathy (56% of adult cases), and 86% improved on riboflavin [PMID:33087424].
- MADD (ETFDH, FLAD1), ACAD9 complex I deficiency and FAD transporter deficiency: these are riboflavin-responsive [PMID:17584774] [PMID:38228875] [PMID:20929961] [PMID:42046426]. Riboflavin-responsive MADD is associated with hyperhomocysteinemia [PMID:37606529] and can involve the spinal cord in adults [PMID:41615582]. Unresponsive MADD presents earlier and more severely [PMID:31331668].
- ALS: no trial evidence supports riboflavin. Riboflavin intake was often inadequate in an ALS cohort [PMID:37367854]. The key clinical point is to exclude treatable transporter deficiency in ALS-like presentations [PMID:33087424].
- Pregnancy: riboflavin deficiency is linked to preeclampsia and anemia [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin] [PMID:39540254]. A personalised BP strategy in pregnancy has been proposed [PMID:41768514].
- Higher risk of deficiency: neonatal phototherapy, vegetarian athletes, pregnant or lactating women with low dairy and meat intake, older adults, adolescents and chronic alcohol use [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/] [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin] [PMID:12791609].
*** Lab-test interference
- Fluorescence immunoassays: 200-400 mg doses caused background-fluorescence errors (MX BKG) on Abbott TDx urine drugs-of-abuse assays within hours. These cleared about 60 h after stopping, with no false negatives [PMID:9846401].
- Urine fluorescence spectroscopy: B2 supplements were the only tested substance that altered the urine fluorescence spectrum. Authors advise avoiding them before such testing [PMID:38605494].
- Urinalysis colour: high doses intensify urine colour to bright yellow (flavinuria). This is harmless, but it should be expected when reading urine colour [URL:https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/vitamins/riboflavin].
- EGRAC status assay: invalid in G6PD deficiency [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]. Results vary with methodology [PMID:19102813].
- Homocysteine and PLP: these are real physiological changes, not artefacts. Homocysteine falls in MTHFR 677TT [PMID:16380544], and PLP is lower when riboflavin status is poorer [PMID:32805038].

** Genotype notes
- MTHFR C677T TT homozygotes (about 12% of adults in the JINGO cohort): TT carries an OR of 1.42 for hypertension, and 3.00 when combined with deficient riboflavin status [PMID:33172445]. A case-control study in older Chinese adults confirmed the genotype-riboflavin interaction [PMID:40738729]. Riboflavin 1.6 mg/day lowered BP only in TT, not in CT or CC [PMID:19952781] [PMID:23608654] [PMID:22277556]. It lowered homocysteine only in TT [PMID:16380544].
  -
  - The Cochrane review (all genotypes) judged BP evidence very uncertain [PMID:41123035]. Precision approaches are still advocated [PMID:42067935].
- SLC52A2 / SLC52A3 biallelic variants cause riboflavin transporter deficiency types 2 and 3. Treatment response depends on how early riboflavin starts after onset; presymptomatic siblings found by cascade screening stayed asymptomatic [PMID:42759448] [PMID:40168907] [PMID:26973221].
- ETFDH variants cause riboflavin-responsive MADD [PMID:17584774]. Single heterozygous carriers may develop the disease when riboflavin deficiency and a high-fat diet are added (cell and mouse data) [PMID:42231330].
- FLAD1 (FAD synthase) and ACAD9 variants are also riboflavin-responsive [PMID:38228875] [PMID:20929961].
- G6PD deficiency: the EGRAC assay cannot be used [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Turkish SLC52A2 p.Pro134Leu kindred followed 5 years: full recovery when treated 8 months after onset; only stabilisation when treated after 13 years; presymptomatic siblings stayed normal [PMID:42759448].
- [2026-08] Network MA of 14 supplement RCTs (n=791): vitamins MD -1.85 attacks/month and CoQ10 -1.91; magnesium did not reach significance for frequency but lowered severity [PMID:42707444].
- [2026-06] ETFDH single-heterozygous carriers: riboflavin deficiency plus high-fat diet triggered the RR-MADD phenotype (mechanistic) [PMID:42231330].
- [2026-05] SR of riboflavin in 33 inherited metabolic diseases: effective in MADD type 3, RTD2/3, ACAD9 and FAD transporter deficiency; not effective in MADD type 1 [PMID:42046426].
- [2026-03] CRISPR screen identifies riboflavin/FAD as required for FSP1 stability and ferroptosis resistance (preclinical) [PMID:41826699].
- [2026-01] Dose-response MA (12 trials, n=749): migraine frequency WMD -1.39 and duration -1.36, linear up to 400 mg/d; I2 >90% [PMID:41769676].
- [2026-01] Spinal cord involvement described in adult-onset RR-MADD [PMID:41615582].
- [2025-10] Cochrane review (4 RCTs): BP effect very uncertain; 3 of 4 RCTs at high risk of bias [PMID:41123035].
- [2025-09] Proposal for riboflavin as a personalised BP strategy in pregnancy [PMID:41768514]. The OPTIMUM pregnancy-hypertension trial (n=2250) is active [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04723836].
- [2025-08] Chinese case-control study: MTHFR TT with low riboflavin status gave the highest hypertension prevalence [PMID:40738729].
- [2025-05] Pediatric SR (17 studies): riboflavin reduced frequency in 7 of 10 comparisons; mixed findings on other outcomes [PMID:40411246].
- [2025-05] B1+B2 RCT (n=43): lower perceived stress; no anxiety effect [PMID:40507089].
- [2025-05] Cryo-EM structures of RFVT2/RFVT3 [PMID:40307217].
- [2025-01] Headache nutraceutical review: riboflavin can be recommended in adults; pediatric efficacy not proven [PMID:39853578].
- [2024-11] Review of riboflavin deficiency as an overlooked contributor to anemia in pregnancy [PMID:39540254].
- [2024-10] Supplement dose-response MA (22 RCTs): riboflavin frequency MD -1.34 [PMID:39404918]. Pediatric NMA: riboflavin RoM 0.50, based on a single study [PMID:39388181].

** Open questions
- Large, low-bias migraine RCTs comparing 400 mg/day with active preventives are lacking. Heterogeneity in MAs is high (I2 about 90%) [PMID:41769676] [PMID:33779525].
- It is unknown whether riboflavin lowers BP in unselected populations or only in MTHFR 677TT, and whether genotype-guided supplementation reduces CVD events [PMID:41123035] [PMID:23608654]. The RIBOGENE trial has unknown status [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02463513].
- Whether riboflavin-5'-phosphate offers any clinical advantage over riboflavin at doses above the absorption ceiling [PMID:8604671].
- The best EGRAC thresholds, and whether functional deficiency should be screened for in pregnancy and in older adults [PMID:19102813] [PMID:39540254].
- Whether riboflavin-tricyclic or riboflavin-phenothiazine interactions shown in animals matter clinically in humans [PMID:6262379].
- Pediatric migraine efficacy and the right dose [PMID:18984840] [PMID:29629048].
