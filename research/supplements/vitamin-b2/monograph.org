** Identity & forms
- Riboflavin (vitamin B2) is water soluble; active coenzyme forms are flavin mononucleotide (FMN) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD). [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]

** Mechanisms
- FMN and FAD are cofactors for redox enzymes, energy metabolism and conversion of other B vitamins. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Migraine frequency/duration | Systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of 12 trials | decrease | WMD frequency −1.39 and duration −1.36; high heterogeneity | moderate | PMID:41769676 |
| Migraine prevention | Meta-analysis of RCTs | benefit | 400 mg/day for about 3 months improved pooled migraine measures | moderate | PMID:33779525 |
| Cardiovascular disease prevention | No outcome evidence | unknown | Insufficient evidence | very low | URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/ |

** Dosing in trials
- Dietary RDA is 1.1 mg/day for women and 1.3 mg/day for men; migraine trials commonly used 400 mg/day for 3 months. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/, PMID:33779525]

** Safety & adverse effects
- High oral doses can cause bright yellow urine; no UL established and toxicity is considered low because absorption is limited. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]

** Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity is the only routine contraindication; pregnancy/lactation requirements differ from high-dose migraine trial use. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]

** Interactions
- Phenobarbital and probenecid may alter riboflavin status; chlorpromazine and tricyclic agents may interfere with conversion/handling. Supplementation can affect some urine fluorescence-based lab methods; confirm assay specifics. [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/]

** Latest research (last 24 months)
- 2025-search dose-response meta-analysis (published 2026) pooled 12 trials and found reductions in migraine frequency and duration, but heterogeneity was high. [PMID:41769676]

** Open questions
- Larger low-bias trials should establish dose, responder characteristics, and whether benefit generalizes beyond migraine.
