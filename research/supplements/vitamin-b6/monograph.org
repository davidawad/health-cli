** Identity & forms
- Vitamin B6 is six interconvertible vitamers: pyridoxine (PN), pyridoxal (PL), pyridoxamine (PM) and their 5'-phosphates (PNP, PLP, PMP). Plant foods supply mostly PN/PNP, sometimes as a glucoside; animal tissues mostly PLP/PMP. The main urinary catabolite is 4-pyridoxic acid [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/9].
- PLP is the coenzyme for more than 100-160 enzymes in amino acid, carbohydrate, lipid and neurotransmitter metabolism [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/9] [PMID:38187791].
- Pyridoxine HCl vs P5P (PLP):
  - Pyridoxine hydrochloride is the usual supplement form and the form in the prescription doxylamine-pyridoxine product [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8bde08f6-e8bd-4e90-baa7-82e01876a86a].
  - PN has to be phosphorylated by pyridoxal kinase (PDXK) and oxidised by the FMN-dependent PNPO enzyme to become PLP [PMID:33912895] [PMID:32805038].
  - In neuronal cell culture only PN, not PL, PM, PLP or PMP, caused concentration-dependent cell death. PN also competitively inhibited PLP-dependent enzymes ("vitamin B6 paradox") [PMID:28716455]. This is in vitro evidence. No human trial compares neuropathy risk between PN and PLP supplements.
  - PLP is not authorised as a medicinal product in Europe. Patients with PNPO deficiency therefore rely on less-regulated PLP food supplements [PMID:40751583].
- Reference intakes (US IOM 1998):
  - RDA is 1.3 mg/day at ages 19-50, 1.7 mg/day for men over 50 and 1.5 mg/day for women over 50.
  - RDA is 1.9 mg/day in pregnancy and 2.0 mg/day in lactation.
  - The RDA is based on keeping plasma PLP at or above 20 nmol/L [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/9].
- Upper limits:
  - US IOM UL: 100 mg/day for adults, derived from a NOAEL of 200 mg/day divided by an uncertainty factor of 2. The critical effect is sensory neuropathy [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/9].
  - EFSA 2023 UL: 12 mg/day for adults, including pregnancy and lactation. EFSA used a reference point of 50 mg/day with an uncertainty factor of 4 (giving 12.5 mg/day), checked it against a dog LOAEL (giving 11.7 mg/day), and rounded down. Children's ULs range from 2.2-2.5 mg/day (4-11 months) to 6.1-10.7 mg/day (7-17 years) [PMID:37207271] [DOI:10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8006].

** Mechanisms
- PLP-dependent enzymes include:
  - delta-aminolevulinate synthase (ALAS2; heme synthesis) [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/9] [PMID:40195342];
  - cystathionine beta-synthase and cystathionase (transsulfuration of homocysteine) [URL:https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6015/chapter/9] [PMID:27778219];
  - aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (dopa decarboxylase) [PMID:15965967];
  - glutamate decarboxylase (GABA synthesis) [PMID:33912895] [PMID:35851507].
- About a third of mammalian PLP-dependent enzymes are mitochondrial. SLC25A38 is required for PLP to accumulate in mitochondria [PMID:39856062].
- Riboflavin link: PNPO needs FMN. Poorer riboflavin status (higher EGRac) predicts lower plasma PLP in healthy adults (n=407) and stepwise lower PLP across riboflavin categories (n=5612) [PMID:32805038] [PMID:36264281] [PMID:37603429].
- Neurotoxicity hypothesis: excess PN inhibits PDXK, causing functional PLP deficiency in peripheral sensory neurons and disturbed GABA signalling [PMID:33912895] [PMID:28716455]. This is consistent with axonal neuropathy in hereditary PDXK deficiency, which responds to PLP [PMID:31187503].
- Plasma PLP is lowered by inflammation independently of intake. Hypoalbuminaemia, alkaline phosphatase activity and renal function also confound it, so combined or ratio biomarkers (PAr, HK:XA) are proposed [PMID:25974692] [PMID:22623384].

** Dosing in trials
- NVP:
  - Pyridoxine alone: 30 mg/day for 5 days [PMID:7573262].
  - Doxylamine 10 mg + pyridoxine 10 mg delayed release: 2 tablets at bedtime, up to 4 tablets/day (40 mg pyridoxine) [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8bde08f6-e8bd-4e90-baa7-82e01876a86a] [PMID:20843504].
  - Doxylamine 12.5 mg + pyridoxine 25 mg in the ondansetron comparison [PMID:25198265].
- PMS: up to 100 mg/day [PMID:10334745].
- Homocysteine/CVD trials (combinations):
  - HOPE-2: B6 50 mg + folic acid 2.5 mg + B12 1 mg [PMID:16450017] [PMID:19228852].
  - NORVIT/WENBIT: B6 40 mg, alone or with folic acid 0.8 mg + B12 0.4 mg [PMID:16531614] [PMID:19920236].
  - VITATOPS: B6 25 mg + folic acid 2 mg + B12 0.5 mg [PMID:20688574].
- Mood/sensory trials: 100 mg/day pyridoxine for 1 month [PMID:35851507] [PMID:42569537] [PMID:39180365].
- Magnesium combination: 30 mg B6 with 300 mg magnesium for 8 weeks [PMID:30562392].
- PDE-ALDH7A1 (guideline): newborns 100 mg/day; infants 30 mg/kg/day (max 300 mg); older patients 30 mg/kg/day (max 500 mg) [PMID:20301659] [PMID:33200442].
- Oral-contraceptive users with low B6: 10 mg/day for 28 days raised PLP from 25.8 to 143 nmol/L [PMID:26201817].
- Theophylline-induced low PLP: 10 mg/day for 1 week corrected it [PMID:2535870] [PMID:2231024].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Sensory neuropathy is the defining toxicity:
  - First described in 7 adults on megadoses with ataxia and sensory loss. All improved after withdrawal [PMID:6308447].
  - It is a predominantly sensory axonal neuropathy that improves after stopping [PMID:37447150] [PMID:33912895].
- Netherlands (Lareb pharmacovigilance):
  - 2016: 53 reports, including products within the then 25 mg/day limit [URL:https://www.lareb.nl/en/news/over-50-reports-of-vitamin-b6-in-multi-vitamin-preparations-and-neuropathy/].
  - 2018 case series: 90 reports, products contained 1.4-100 mg/tablet, serum B6 88-4338 nmol/L. Causality was plausible, including at doses below 50 mg/day [PMID:29737502] [URL:https://www.lareb.nl/en/news/vitamin-b6-in-health-supplements-and-neuropathy].
  - The Dutch maximum fell to 21 mg/day in October 2018. Of 224 reports up to 2023, only one exceeded 21 mg afterwards, but low-dose cases continue. Dose explained only 15% of the variance in plasma levels [PMID:39888171].
- Australia (TGA):
  - Since 1 March 2023, a neuropathy label warning is required above 10 mg/day (previously above 50 mg/day).
  - The maximum adult daily dose in listed products was cut from 200 to 100 mg.
  - The review of more than 30 reports found neuropathy below 50 mg/day and with stacked products, with no clear minimum dose or duration.
  - These points come from the TGA safety alert [URL:https://www.tga.gov.au/news/safety-alerts/health-supplements-containing-vitamin-b6-can-cause-peripheral-neuropathy].
  - A TGA scheduling decision (final 25 Nov 2025, effective 1 June 2027) keeps oral products at 50 mg/day or less unscheduled. Products above 50 and up to 200 mg/day become pharmacist-only, and products above 200 mg/day prescription-only [URL:https://www.tga.gov.au/news/safety-updates/medicines-containing-vitamin-b6-pyridoxine-pyridoxal-or-pyridoxamine].
  - Australian clinical reviews and case series (including lung-transplant recipients) document toxicity from OTC products [PMID:40754968] [PMID:41532169].
- EFSA 2023 set the adult UL at 12 mg/day on the basis of neuropathy [PMID:37207271].
- Low-dose cases:
  - Neuropathy with serum B6 259.9 nmol/L while taking only a 6 mg multivitamin [PMID:38098895].
  - Three cases from B-complex use, one with CNS symptoms and complement abnormalities that resolved [PMID:40418248].
- Singapore's HSA issued a high-dose B6 neuropathy alert in May 2023. Hospital deprescribing rose from 15.2% to 100% [PMID:40441734].
- Elite athletes: 38.7% of 654 PLP measurements were above 125 nmol/L, plausibly from supplements [PMID:42730844].
- Pyridoxine challenge in a neonate with CBS deficiency caused respiratory failure and rhabdomyolysis [PMID:32820583].
- High-dose PLP in PNPO deficiency: liver toxicity in 20.4% of patients [PMID:40751583], and hepatocellular carcinoma in 2 patients on long-term PLP [PMID:40349651].
- Observational harm signals (not proven causal): lung cancer in men taking more than 20 mg/day [PMID:28829668]; hip fracture at 35 mg/day or more, especially with high B12 [PMID:31074816]. In NORVIT, B6 alone showed RR 1.14 (0.98-1.32) for CV events [PMID:16531614].
- Pregnancy (Diclegis label):
  - Epidemiology, including a meta-analysis of 27 studies, showed no increase in malformations.
  - Somnolence was 14.3% vs 11.7% on placebo [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8bde08f6-e8bd-4e90-baa7-82e01876a86a].
  - A factorial RCT reported more small-for-gestational-age births with doxylamine-pyridoxine (OR 3.8, CI 1.0-14.1) [PMID:37335994].
  - Large cohorts found no neurodevelopmental or malformation signal for pyridoxine or doxylamine [PMID:41327197] [PMID:41980593].

** Contraindications
- Existing B6-toxicity neuropathy, or unexplained sensory neuropathy with raised B6: stop all B6 sources [PMID:37447150] [PMID:29737502].
- Levodopa monotherapy without a decarboxylase inhibitor: 10-25 mg pyridoxine can reverse levodopa's effect [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9b17b028-964a-473c-823d-81423535bd66].
- Diclegis label contraindications are concurrent MAO inhibitors and hypersensitivity to ethanolamine antihistamines or pyridoxine [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8bde08f6-e8bd-4e90-baa7-82e01876a86a].
- Hypophosphatasia: plasma PLP is already high [PMID:34547524]. Neuropathic pain in HPP has been linked to B6 metabolism (speculative) [PMID:40636590].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Levodopa (without carbidopa): pyridoxine speeds peripheral decarboxylation and reduces the effect [PMID:6087425] [PMID:4827061]. Carbidopa blocks this, so carbidopa-levodopa can be given with B6 [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9b17b028-964a-473c-823d-81423535bd66].
- Carbidopa-levodopa causing B6 depletion:
  - Carbidopa binds PLP. Human studies show lower B6 biomarkers mainly at high carbidopa exposure, but no threshold or prophylactic dose has been set [PMID:42703450].
  - Neuropathy has been reported with foslevodopa/foscarbidopa infusion together with B6/B12 deficiency [PMID:41942316] [PMID:35802009].
- Phenytoin and phenobarbital: pyridoxine lowered their serum concentrations [PMID:55569]. These anti-seizure drugs (with carbamazepine and valproate) can also lower plasma PLP [PMID:35737815].
- Isoniazid: INH antagonises PLP and causes neuropathy. Pyridoxine prophylaxis is recommended for high-risk groups (e.g. HIV co-infection) [PMID:13334809] [PMID:6269259] [PMID:21477422] [PMID:30285383].
- Theophylline: noncompetitive pyridoxal kinase inhibitor (Ki 1.28e-5 M) that lowers plasma and red-cell PLP. 10 mg/day pyridoxine corrects this [PMID:2535870] [PMID:2231024] [PMID:22879864].
- Hydralazine: pyridoxine-deficiency neuropathy has been reported [PMID:5847557].
- Penicillamine: blocks the tryptophan-niacin pathway through B6 deficiency in Wilson disease [PMID:5262012]. Many drugs inactivate PLP by forming Schiff-base complexes with it [PMID:6342968].
- Oral contraceptives: low-dose pills only transiently lowered PLP [PMID:2527729]. A subset of users have PLP below 30 nmol/L with functional metabolic changes that 10 mg/day reverses [PMID:26201817].
- Doxylamine: fixed combination for NVP. CNS depressants and alcohol add to sedation [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8bde08f6-e8bd-4e90-baa7-82e01876a86a].
*** Other supplements
- Vitamin B2 (riboflavin): FMN is needed by PNPO. Low riboflavin status lowers PLP [PMID:32805038] [PMID:36264281] [PMID:37603429].
- Quatrefolic (5-MTHF) and folate: the homocysteine-lowering combination trials used folic acid with B6/B12, which lowered homocysteine but not MI. Stroke fell modestly [PMID:28816346] [PMID:16531613]. Folic acid + B12 (not B6) was linked to more cancer in NORVIT/WENBIT [PMID:19920236].
- Vitamin B12: high combined B6+B12 intake was associated with hip fracture [PMID:31074816] and, from single-nutrient supplements, lung cancer in men [PMID:28829668]. NORVIT combined arm RR 1.22 [PMID:16531614].
- B Complex and multivitamins: stacking products drives cumulative exposure. Cases occur at 6 mg/day and with B-complex use [PMID:38098895] [PMID:40418248] [URL:https://www.lareb.nl/en/news/over-50-reports-of-vitamin-b6-in-multi-vitamin-preparations-and-neuropathy/].
- Magnesium: Mg+B6 was not superior to Mg alone for stress overall [PMID:30562392]. TGA advises checking for hidden B6 in magnesium and zinc products [URL:https://www.tga.gov.au/news/safety-alerts/health-supplements-containing-vitamin-b6-can-cause-peripheral-neuropathy].
- Trimethylglycine (TMG, betaine): in CBS deficiency, pyridoxine responders are treated with B6, while non-responders need betaine and diet [PMID:27778219] [PMID:40095936].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy: NVP is an established use (see above). EFSA's 12 mg/day UL also applies in pregnancy [PMID:37207271].
- Pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy, PNPO deficiency, CBS deficiency, X-linked sideroblastic anemia and primary hyperoxaluria type 1 (G170R) are pharmacological B6 indications under specialist care [PMID:33200442] [PMID:40751583] [PMID:27778219] [PMID:40195342] [PMID:24797341].
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis: B6 deficiency (PLP <20 nmol/L) in 25-50% of patients predicted worse transplant-free survival [PMID:42002000].
- Hypophosphatasia: diagnostic PLP elevation, so do not supplement on the basis of the B6 level [PMID:40047955] [PMID:34547524].
*** Lab-test interference
- Supplements raise plasma B6/PLP well above reference. In Lareb cases the mean was 907 nmol/L against a 51-183 nmol/L reference range [URL:https://www.lareb.nl/en/news/vitamin-b6-in-health-supplements-and-neuropathy] [PMID:29737502].
- High PLP is a diagnostic marker of hypophosphatasia (low ALP, high PLP, high PEA) [PMID:30049651] [PMID:40047955].
  - Supplement use confounds this marker. In fibromyalgia patients with low ALP, 31% had high PLP but none had ALPL variants [PMID:41037513].
  - PLP correlates inversely with ALP [PMID:30049651] and falls during pregnancy in carriers as placental ALP rises [PMID:7706447].
- Low PLP can reflect inflammation rather than intake (PLP 61 vs 80 nmol/L across inflammation tertiles) [PMID:22623384]. Interpret alongside CRP, albumin, ALP and renal function [PMID:25974692].
- Theophylline lowers erythrocyte ALT/AST activity by 70%/50% through PLP depletion [PMID:2231024].

** Genotype notes
- ALPL: GWAS identified rs4654748 (p=8.3e-18) as the main locus for serum B6, probably through catabolism [PMID:19303062]. Pathogenic ALPL variants cause hypophosphatasia with high PLP [PMID:34547524].
- ALDH7A1 (PDE), PNPO and PDXK: inborn errors needing lifelong PN or PLP [PMID:20301659] [PMID:24645144] [PMID:31187503].
- ALAS2: variant-dependent pyridoxine responsiveness in X-linked sideroblastic anemia [PMID:27247955] [PMID:39281190].
- CBS: variant determines pyridoxine responsiveness [PMID:40095936]. AGXT G170R homozygotes with PH1 respond to pyridoxine [PMID:24797341].
- MTHFR: the relation between riboflavin status and PLP was examined by MTHFR genotype [PMID:36264281].
- COMT: the low-activity allele predicted motor response to high-dose pyridoxine in Parkinson's disease on levodopa (small study) [PMID:15965967].

** Open questions
- The true dose threshold for neuropathy. Cases occur well below 50 mg/day, and plasma levels correlate poorly with dose [PMID:39888171] [PMID:38098895].
- Whether PLP or PL supplements are less neurotoxic than pyridoxine in humans. The evidence is only in vitro [PMID:28716455].
- Whether the efficacy of doxylamine-pyridoxine is clinically meaningful [PMID:29342163] [PMID:24798673].
- Whether the observational lung cancer and hip fracture signals at high doses are causal [PMID:28829668] [PMID:31074816].
- Whether B6 lowers stroke risk independently of folic acid and B12 [PMID:28816346] [PMID:32503038].
- Monitoring and prophylaxis for B6 depletion with carbidopa-containing infusions [PMID:42703450].
