** Identity & forms
- Water-soluble essential vitamin (L-ascorbic acid). The adult RDA is 90 mg/day for men and 75 mg/day for women. People who smoke need 35 mg/day more. The adult UL is 2,000 mg/day (ages 19+, including pregnancy and lactation), and it does not apply to medically supervised treatment [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- Supplement forms: ascorbic acid (most common; bioavailability equivalent to food ascorbate), sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate and other mineral ascorbates, ascorbic acid with bioflavonoids, and combinations such as Ester-C (calcium ascorbate plus dehydroascorbate, threonate and other metabolites). Head-to-head data on these forms are sparse. One study found no plasma difference between ascorbic acid, Ester-C and ascorbic acid with bioflavonoids. Another found higher leukocyte but equal plasma levels with Ester-C. ODS considers plain ascorbic acid the preferred source [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- Alternative oral forms: a 2025 systematic review (13 studies, healthy adults) found more favourable plasma concentrations with alternative forms in 7 studies and better GI tolerability for calcium ascorbate-EC. No study measured tissue retention [PMID:39861409]. In a single-dose crossover study, liposomal vitamin C was 1.77 times more bioavailable (AUC/Cmax) than non-liposomal vitamin C [PMID:32901526]. This is small, short-term pharmacokinetic evidence with no clinical-outcome data.
- Intravenous (IV) vitamin C is a prescription product (e.g. Ascor). Its label lists a maximum recommended duration of one week and adult nutritional doses of 200 mg/day [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306]. "Pharmacological ascorbate" in oncology and sepsis trials uses grams per dose (see Dosing).

** Mechanisms
- Absorption is saturable. About 70-90% is absorbed at 30-180 mg/day, but less than 50% above 1 g/day. Oral 1.25 g/day gives a mean peak plasma level of about 135 micromol/L. Modelling predicts only about 220 micromol/L even with 3 g every 4 hours [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- IV administration can reach plasma concentrations up to about 26,000 micromol/L. These levels are selectively cytotoxic to tumour cells in vitro, possibly via a pro-oxidant (H2O2-generating) mechanism. This is mechanistic and animal evidence [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- Enhances non-heme iron absorption (reduction and chelation of Fe3+). In iron-depleted women, 80 mg ascorbic acid increased fractional absorption of a 100 mg iron dose by 30%, and 500 mg gave no further gain [PMID:37357807].
- It is a reducing agent, so it interferes with redox-based assays (glucose-oxidase/peroxidase strips and meters, urine dipsticks, guaiac) [PMID:29479748][PMID:1200528][URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Ascorbate is partly metabolised to oxalate. Urinary acidification may precipitate oxalate, urate or cystine stones [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Proposed anti-inflammatory effects in sepsis were not seen in biomarker data from the LOVIT trial (no discernible anti-inflammatory effect) [PMID:39774855].

** Dosing in trials
- Common cold prevention and duration: regular supplementation of at least 0.2 g/day in Cochrane-eligible trials, and at least 1 g/day for severity effects. Two therapeutic trials found 6-8 g/day roughly twice as effective as 3-4 g/day for duration [PMID:23440782][PMID:38082300][PMID:39803741].
- Blood pressure: median 500 mg/day for a median of 8 weeks (2012 MA). Median 757.5 mg/day for 6 weeks (2023 MA) [PMID:22492364][PMID:37791386].
- Endothelial function: 500-2,000 mg/day orally in longer trials [PMID:25919436].
- Uric acid: 600 mg/day for 2 weeks [PMID:41739218].
- Iron co-administration: 80 mg ascorbic acid was as effective as 500 mg [PMID:37357807].
- Sepsis (IV): 50 mg/kg every 6 h for up to 96 h (LOVIT) [PMID:35704292]. 1.5 g every 6 h for 4 days (C-EASIE) [PMID:40269974]. The umbrella review's monotherapy signal was at 25-100 mg/kg/day, started within 24 h, for 3-4 days [PMID:42384633].
- Oncology (IV): 75 g three times weekly (pancreatic) [PMID:39369582]. 1 g/kg (prostate) [PMID:39076107].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Oral: low toxicity. High doses cause diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal cramps and other GI upset. The adult UL is 2,000 mg/day [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- Kidney stones (oral): in three US cohorts (156,735 women, 40,536 men), total vitamin C of at least 1,000 mg/day was associated with stones in men (HR 1.43, 1.15-1.79). Supplemental intake of at least 1,000 mg/day also carried risk in men (HR 1.19, 1.01-1.40). Neither was associated with stones in women [PMID:26463139]. A meta-analysis of 4 studies likewise found an association in men but not women [PMID:30178451].
- Oxalate nephropathy: in a single-centre biopsy series, supplement-associated oxalate nephropathy rose from 0% to 44% of cases during the COVID-19 pandemic, linked to vitamin C and other supplements. Many patients had pre-existing CKD (49%) or diabetes (57%) [PMID:36120391]. The IV label warns of acute and chronic oxalate nephropathy with prolonged high doses [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Hemolysis in G6PD deficiency: a meta-summary of 14 case reports found 71.4% had G6PD deficiency. Doses were 1-200 g/day and 57.1% were IV, with AKI, DIC and methemoglobinemia also reported [PMID:35712748]. The label recommends a reduced dose and monitoring of hemoglobin [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- IV high-dose harms: a scoping review of 74 studies (2,801 recipients) found no consistent excess harm vs placebo in double-blind RCTs. It did report cases of oxalate nephropathy, hypernatremia, hemolysis in G6PD deficiency and glucometer error [PMID:32404636]. LOVIT reported one severe hypoglycaemic episode and one anaphylaxis event in the vitamin C arm [PMID:35704292]. A 2026 systematic review names oxalate nephropathy and G6PD hemolysis as the major risks and suggests pre-screening [PMID:41815850].
- Iron overload: in hereditary hemochromatosis, chronic high doses may exacerbate iron overload [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].

** Contraindications
- G6PD deficiency: avoid high-dose (especially IV) vitamin C; risk of severe hemolysis [PMID:35712748][URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Hereditary hemochromatosis / iron overload: avoid chronic high doses [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- Renal impairment, history of oxalate stones or hyperoxaluria: higher risk of oxalate nephropathy [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306][URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/][PMID:36120391].
- Septic shock on vasopressors: IV vitamin C monotherapy is not supported (LOVIT harm signal) [PMID:35704292][PMID:42384633].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Statins plus niacin (HATS): an antioxidant cocktail (vitamins E and C, beta-carotene, selenium) blunted the HDL2 response to simvastatin-niacin (HDL2-C +42% vs 0%) and attenuated angiographic benefit [PMID:11757504][PMID:11498460]. ODS advises lipid monitoring [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- Chemotherapy and radiotherapy: controversial. Antioxidants might protect tumour cells from radiation and agents such as cyclophosphamide, chlorambucil, carmustine, busulfan, thiotepa and doxorubicin, but this evidence is contested [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/]. Bleomycin is inactivated by ascorbic acid in vitro [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306]. By contrast, IV ascorbate has been studied as an intended chemotherapy adjunct (see Evidence) [PMID:39369582][PMID:39076107].
- Warfarin: case reports describe warfarin resistance (subtherapeutic INR) that resolved after ascorbic acid was stopped [PMID:23592361][PMID:38738175]. The IV label notes no effect at up to 1,000 mg/day for 2 weeks and advises standard INR monitoring [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Amphetamine, fluphenazine and other urine-pH-sensitive drugs: urinary acidification may lower serum levels [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Certain antibiotics (erythromycin, kanamycin, streptomycin, doxycycline, lincomycin): the IV label states decreased activity [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Levothyroxine: a pilot RCT (n=11) of 1 g/day in hypothyroid patients needing high-dose levothyroxine found a signal of TSH and symptom improvement. It had severe baseline imbalance, so treat as hypothesis-generating [PMID:41210509].
- Aluminium antacids and estrogens (ethinylestradiol): older reports exist, but no abstract or full text could be opened, so no claim is made.

*** Other supplements
- Iron: synergy. It increases non-heme iron absorption, and 80 mg is enough [PMID:37357807][PMID:2321571]. This is undesirable in iron overload (see Conditions).
- Antioxidant combinations: combining vitamins C and E abolished the endothelial-function benefit seen with either alone (SMD 0.12, NS) [PMID:25919436]. Antioxidant mixtures blunted HDL response to niacin [PMID:11498460].
- Niacin: see HATS above [PMID:11757504].

*** Conditions
- Hemochromatosis / iron overload: avoid high doses [URL:https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/].
- G6PD deficiency: hemolysis with high-dose or IV use [PMID:35712748][PMID:32404636].
- Kidney stones (especially men) and hyperoxaluria; chronic kidney disease: oxalate risk [PMID:26463139][PMID:30178451][PMID:36120391].
- Sepsis/septic shock: IV monotherapy is not recommended outside trials [PMID:35704292][PMID:42384633][PMID:40269974].
- Gout/hyperuricemia: a modest SUA-lowering effect may be relevant [PMID:34280483][PMID:41739218].

*** Lab-test interference
- Point-of-care glucose meters: during and after IV vitamin C (66 mg/kg/h in burn patients), all 7 patients had falsely elevated POC glucose, with discrepancies of 10-200 mg/dL. The authors advise avoiding POC glucose testing during and after infusion [PMID:25127026]. Susceptibility varies by meter [PMID:36332153]. The label says to delay redox-based tests until 24 h after infusion [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=388aad52-fc01-4784-9791-1dbc80c69306].
- Urine dipsticks: ascorbic acid causes false-negative glucose, hemoglobin (blood), nitrite and bilirubin results. The size of the interference varies between dipstick brands [PMID:29479748].
- Guaiac fecal occult blood test: ascorbic acid inhibits the pseudoperoxidase reaction, giving false negatives [PMID:1200528]. A systematic review advises withholding it before guaiac FOBT [PMID:22972722].

** Genotype notes
- SLC23A1 (SVCT1) rs33972313: each minor allele was associated with about 6 micromol/L lower circulating ascorbate (-5.98; 95% CI -8.23 to -3.73) in pooled data from more than 15,000 participants [PMID:20519558]. No genotype-guided dosing evidence exists.
- G6PD deficiency (an inherited enzymopathy) raises hemolysis risk with high doses [PMID:35712748].

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Updated meta-analysis (14 RCTs, n=1,958) of IV vitamin C in sepsis: 28-day mortality RR 0.70 (0.53-0.93). The benefit was driven by single-centre trials and absent in multicentre low-risk-of-bias trials [PMID:42761050].
- [2026-07] Umbrella review of 31 SRs: HAT and vitamin C plus thiamine combinations do not reduce mortality. There is a possible monotherapy signal (started within 24 h, 25-100 mg/kg/day), but routine use is not supported [PMID:42384633].
- [2026-02] HesperidrinC crossover RCT (n=40): 600 mg/day vitamin C, but not hesperidin, lowered serum uric acid without changing urinary excretion [PMID:41739218].
- [2026-01] Systematic review of high-dose IV vitamin C: no support for routine use in sepsis; the oncology role is exploratory. It highlights oxalate nephropathy and G6PD hemolysis [PMID:41815850].
- [2025-10] Pilot RCT (n=11) in hypothyroid patients on high-dose levothyroxine: possible symptom and TSH benefit; very small and imbalanced [PMID:41210509].
- [2025-04] C-EASIE RCT (n=292): early ED IV vitamin C did not significantly reduce organ dysfunction in sepsis [PMID:40269974].
- [2025-01] LOVIT biomarker sub-study: no sepsis subtype benefited, harm varied by subtype, and there was no discernible anti-inflammatory effect [PMID:39774855].
- [2025-01] Hemilä review: 6-8 g/day therapeutic dosing may outperform 3-4 g/day for cold duration (2 trials) [PMID:39803741].
- [2025-01] SR of alternative oral forms (liposomal, calcium ascorbate-EC): some PK advantages, limited outcome data [PMID:39861409].
- [2024-11] Randomised phase II trial in metastatic pancreatic cancer: IV ascorbate plus gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel gave OS of 16 vs 8.3 months [PMID:39369582].
- [2024-08] Placebo-controlled phase II in mCRPC: IV vitamin C plus docetaxel was futile, with a numerically worse OS [PMID:39076107].
- [2024-04] Case report of warfarin resistance attributed to ascorbic acid [PMID:38738175].

** Open questions
- Whether IV vitamin C monotherapy benefits any sepsis subgroup (early, non-shock, deficient) given LOVIT harm and single-centre-driven benefit.
- Whether the pancreatic cancer phase II survival signal replicates in adequately powered phase III trials.
- Long-term effects of oral supplementation on blood pressure and cardiovascular events (trials are short).
- Clinical relevance of liposomal and other enhanced forms beyond plasma pharmacokinetics.
- Stone risk threshold in men and the role of baseline oxalate handling.
- Aluminium-antacid and estrogen interactions remain unverified here (sources not accessible).
