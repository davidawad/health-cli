** Identity & forms
- "Vitamin E" covers eight fat-soluble compounds: four tocopherols and four tocotrienols (alpha, beta, gamma, delta) [PMID:34785321] [PMID:25225959].
- Only alpha-tocopherol meets the human requirement. The hepatic alpha-tocopherol transfer protein (alpha-TTP, gene TTPA) selects it and secretes it into lipoproteins. Non-alpha forms are preferentially broken down [PMID:34699937] [PMID:34563650].
- Natural vs synthetic:
  - Natural-source vitamin E is RRR-alpha-tocopherol ("d-alpha"). Synthetic all-rac-alpha-tocopherol ("dl-alpha") is an equal mix of 8 stereoisomers.
  - In a deuterium-labelled head-to-head study, natural vitamin E had roughly twice the availability of synthetic vitamin E. The plasma RRR:all-rac ratio was about 2:1, higher than the officially accepted 1.36:1 [PMID:9537614].
  - Trials used different forms. SELECT used all-rac-alpha-tocopheryl acetate [PMID:21990298]. HOPE and the Women's Health Study used natural-source vitamin E [PMID:10639540] [PMID:15998891].
- Mixed tocopherols and tocotrienols: supplements may add gamma-/delta-tocopherol or a palm-derived tocotrienol-rich fraction (TRF). Gamma- and delta-tocopherol and the tocotrienols are metabolized more extensively than alpha-tocopherol [PMID:34785321].
- Reference intakes (US IOM 2000):
  - RDA 15 mg/day alpha-tocopherol for adult men and women. The RDA counts only the 2R-stereoisomeric forms (RRR, and the RSR, RRS and RSS isomers in synthetic products). Other tocopherols and the tocotrienols do not count.
  - UL 1,000 mg/day of any form of supplemental alpha-tocopherol, based on an increased tendency to hemorrhage [URL:https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9810/chapter/8].
- Deficiency is rare. It is mainly seen in fat malabsorption and in familial ataxia with vitamin E deficiency (AVED), which is caused by TTPA mutations [PMID:34563650] [PMID:34699937].

** Mechanisms
- Chain-breaking lipid-soluble antioxidant in membranes and lipoproteins [PMID:34699937].
- Platelet inhibition: oral alpha-tocopherol at 400-1200 IU/d raised platelet alpha-tocopherol content. This correlated with inhibition of PMA-induced aggregation through protein kinase C, an effect not reproduced by another lipophilic antioxidant (BHT) [PMID:8921785].
- Vitamin K antagonism: 1000 IU/d RRR-alpha-tocopherol for 12 weeks raised PIVKA-II (under-carboxylated prothrombin), a marker of poor vitamin K status, in adults not taking anticoagulants [PMID:15213041]. Proposed bleeding mechanisms combine this effect with platelet inhibition [PMID:25225959].
- Catabolism: CYP4F2 omega-hydroxylation starts vitamin E breakdown to carboxychromanols and CEHC. The long-chain metabolites (13'-COOH) inhibit COX-1/-2 and 5-lipoxygenase in experimental models [PMID:34785321] [PMID:20861217].
- Pharmacogenomic hypothesis: haptoglobin 2-2 protein binds free hemoglobin poorly, which increases oxidative stress. This is the proposed basis for a vitamin E benefit restricted to diabetic Hp 2-2 carriers [PMID:22939808] [PMID:39474248].

** Dosing in trials
- Cardiovascular/cancer prevention: 400 IU/d natural source (HOPE, 4.5-7 y) [PMID:10639540] [PMID:15769967]; 600 IU natural source on alternate days (WHS, 10.1 y) [PMID:15998891]; 400 IU every other day (PHS II, 8 y) [PMID:18997197]; 50 mg/d (ATBC) [PMID:8127329].
- SELECT: 400 IU/d all-rac-alpha-tocopheryl acetate, planned for 7-12 years [PMID:19066370].
- MASH: 800 IU/d for 96 weeks (PIVENS, TONIC) [PMID:20427778] [PMID:21521847]; 300 mg/d in a Chinese RCT [PMID:39970876]. Cochrane-included trials used 298-1000 IU/d for up to 24 months [PMID:39412049]. A cohort used 800 IU/d for >=2 years [PMID:30506586].
- Alzheimer's disease: 2000 IU/d (TEAM-AD, Sano trial) [PMID:24381967] [PMID:9110909]. MCI: 2000 IU/d for 3 years [PMID:15829527].
- AREDS: 400 IU/d with vitamin C 500 mg, beta-carotene 15 mg, and zinc 80 mg with copper 2 mg [PMID:11594942].
- Chemotherapy neuropathy: 600 mg/d [PMID:34148036]. Tocotrienols: TRF 250-400 mg/d for type 2 diabetes [PMID:37321474]; >=200 mg/d for HDL effects [PMID:32951713].
- Dose-response: Miller's MA linked doses above 150 IU/d with higher mortality [PMID:15537682]. Abner's MA found no dose relationship up to 5,500 IU/d [PMID:21235492].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Bleeding is the defining toxicity and is the basis of the UL [URL:https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9810/chapter/8].
  - Hemorrhagic stroke risk rises by 22% in RCT MAs [PMID:21051774], with HR 1.74 in PHS II [PMID:18997197] and excess hemorrhagic-stroke deaths in ATBC [PMID:8127329]. USPSTF lists hemorrhagic stroke as a possible serious harm [PMID:35727272].
  - Gingival bleeding was highest with alpha-tocopherol plus aspirin: 33.4% vs 25.8% of probed sites (ATBC substudy) [PMID:9920356].
  - Occult GI bleeding during MASLD treatment: in a prospective series on 200-800 IU/d, iron deficiency developed in 11 of 20 patients (55%) after a median 11 weeks and anemia in 6. A GI bleeding source was found in 75% of fully investigated patients, and diabetes increased the risk [PMID:39960325].
  - Coagulopathy: a 2025 case had prolonged INR and APTT after vitamin E overdose in a patient with prior biliopancreatic diversion. It reversed after stopping vitamin E and giving vitamin K [PMID:40499949].
- Heart failure: risk rose in high-risk patients (HOPE-TOO) [PMID:15769967]. No rise was seen in healthy women (WHS) [PMID:22438520].
- Prostate cancer: 17% relative increase in SELECT [PMID:21990298].
- Mortality: the Cochrane review judged vitamin E to increase mortality in low-bias trials (RR 1.03) [PMID:22419320]. Other pooled analyses found no effect [PMID:21235492] [PMID:35727272]. USPSTF recommends against vitamin E for CVD or cancer prevention (grade D) [PMID:35727271].
- Pregnancy: combined antioxidant regimens raised term prelabour rupture of membranes and self-reported abdominal pain [PMID:26343254].
- Inhalation: vitamin E acetate was found in BAL fluid of 48 of 51 (94%) EVALI patients, mostly linked to THC vaping products, and in no healthy comparators [PMID:31860793]. This is a non-oral hazard.
- Short-term trials at 800-2000 IU/d reported no excess of serious adverse events [PMID:20427778] [PMID:24381967] [PMID:28418065].

** Contraindications
- History of hemorrhagic stroke or intracranial bleeding, and active bleeding: high-dose vitamin E raises hemorrhagic stroke risk [PMID:21051774] [PMID:18997197].
- Bleeding disorders, vitamin K deficiency, and fat malabsorption or bariatric diversion (impaired vitamin K): vitamin E can unmask coagulopathy [PMID:40499949] [PMID:15213041].
- Heart failure or high-risk vascular disease/diabetes: HOPE-TOO heart-failure signal [PMID:15769967].
- Men at risk of prostate cancer: high-dose supplementation should be avoided, especially with low selenium status [PMID:21990298] [PMID:24563519].
- Inhalation of vitamin E acetate (vaping products) [PMID:31860793].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Warfarin and other vitamin K antagonists: evidence is mixed.
  - A small RCT (n=21) found no INR change [PMID:8629604].
  - High-dose vitamin E antagonizes vitamin K (PIVKA-II rise) [PMID:15213041], and reviews list warfarin among plausible interactions at >=300 mg/d [PMID:25225959] [PMID:40065887].
  - Check INR when starting or stopping high doses.
- Aspirin and antiplatelet agents: additive bleeding. Gingival bleeding was highest with alpha-tocopherol plus aspirin [PMID:9920356], and alpha-tocopherol inhibits platelet aggregation [PMID:8921785].
- Simvastatin plus niacin (HATS):
  - An antioxidant cocktail of vitamins E and C, beta-carotene and selenium blunted the HDL2 rise (42% vs 0%) and weakened the angiographic benefit [PMID:11757504] [PMID:11498460].
  - However, 1200 IU/d RRR-alpha-tocopherol alone for 2 years did not change HDL subfractions in statin-treated patients with CAD [PMID:17234730]. The interaction is therefore attributed to the cocktail, not clearly to vitamin E.
- Cyclosporine, tamoxifen: high-dose vitamin E may alter their activity. This comes from a mechanistic and clinical review; data are limited [PMID:25225959].
- Orlistat: reduced acute alpha-tocopherol absorption in adolescents, though serum levels were maintained with a multivitamin [PMID:12126214].
*** Other supplements
- Selenium: in SELECT the combination did not raise prostate cancer risk significantly (HR 1.05) [PMID:21990298]. Vitamin E alone raised risk mainly in men with low selenium status [PMID:24563519].
- Vitamin C and other antioxidants:
  - The E plus antioxidant combinations were the ones linked to hemorrhagic stroke in a 2024 MA [PMID:37698992].
  - The antioxidant cocktail blunted simvastatin-niacin HDL effects [PMID:11498460].
  - E plus C in pregnancy raised term PROM [PMID:26343254].
- K1: high-dose vitamin E antagonizes vitamin K [PMID:15213041], and vitamin K reversed vitamin E-associated coagulopathy [PMID:40499949].
- Zinc (AREDS formula): combined antioxidants plus zinc slowed AMD progression, which was not shown for vitamin E alone [PMID:11594942] [PMID:37702300].
- Other bleeding-prone supplements (e.g. fish oil, garlic, ginkgo): no retrieved clinical interaction study. Stacking is a theoretical concern from vitamin E's platelet and vitamin K effects [PMID:25225959].
*** Conditions
- Hemorrhagic stroke history, bleeding disorders, malabsorption, heart failure, prostate cancer risk: see Contraindications.
- MASLD/MASH treated with vitamin E: monitor ferritin and hemoglobin in the first months, especially with diabetes [PMID:39960325].
- Type 2 diabetes with Hp 2-2 genotype: possible CV benefit (see Genotype notes) [PMID:29731659].
*** Lab-test interference
- No analytic assay artifact was found. True physiological effects include a raised PIVKA-II [PMID:15213041] and, with excess intake or low vitamin K, prolonged PT/INR and APTT [PMID:40499949].
- Ferritin, MCV and hemoglobin may fall from occult GI blood loss [PMID:39960325]. Serum ALT/AST fall in MASLD [PMID:39412049].

** Genotype notes
- Haptoglobin (HP) 1/2 polymorphism:
  - In diabetic Hp 2-2 carriers, vitamin E was associated with fewer CV events in MAs of trial subgroups (OR 0.66, 0.48-0.90; and OR 0.66, 0.45-0.95). No benefit was seen in other Hp types [PMID:22939808] [PMID:29731659].
  - A 2024 SR (5 RCTs) found no HDL-level change but improved HDL function only in Hp 2-2 diabetes [PMID:39474248].
  - In the LURIC cohort, plasma gamma-tocopherol interacted with HP genotype for mortality in diabetes (P interaction 0.021) [PMID:39882297].
  - These data are post hoc or observational and have not been tested in a prospective genotype-stratified trial.
- CYP4F2:
  - In vitro, W12G raised and V433M (rs2108622) lowered tocopherol omega-hydroxylase activity [PMID:20861217].
  - In ATBC, rs2108622 and two other vitamin E-related SNPs did not modify the serum alpha-tocopherol-prostate cancer association [PMID:35197557].
- TTPA mutations cause AVED, a monogenic deficiency state [PMID:34563650].

** Latest research
- [2026-01] Diabetic neuropathy MA (5 RCTs, n=660): tocotrienol-rich vitamin E raised sural NCV by 1.77 m/s, with no change in amplitude or HbA1c [PMID:41528693].
- [2025-11] Pediatric MASLD MA (11 RCTs, n=665): ALT -5.23 U/L, AST -3.00 U/L, HOMA-IR -0.82 [PMID:41199412].
- [2025-11] NHANES/UK Biobank cohorts: highest tertile of dietary vitamin E intake had lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.68 NHANES; 0.93 UK Biobank). The relationship was non-linear, and these are dietary, observational data [PMID:41706672].
- [2025-07] MASH MA (3 RCTs): ALT MD -12.27 U/L; fibrosis improvement OR 1.96 (1.25-3.09); MASH resolution not significant (OR 1.71) [PMID:40895855].
- [2025-06] Case report: reversible coagulopathy (raised INR/APTT) from vitamin E excess after bariatric diversion [PMID:40499949].
- [2025-02] Multicentre Chinese RCT (n=124, non-diabetic MASH): 300 mg/d improved histology (29.3% vs 14.1%), including fibrosis stage [PMID:39970876].
- [2025-02] Prospective MASLD series: 55% developed iron deficiency on vitamin E, with a GI bleeding source in 75% of those fully investigated [PMID:39960325].
- [2025-02] Narrative review of high-dose supplementation risks: bleeding with warfarin/aspirin, hemorrhagic stroke, mortality [PMID:40065887].
- [2024-12] Adult MASLD MA (7 RCTs): MASH resolution RR 1.9; no fibrosis effect (MD -0.23) [PMID:39150005].
- [2024-10] Cochrane NAFLD review (16 RCTs, n=1066): ALT -9.29 U/L (moderate certainty); mortality and liver outcomes very uncertain or unreported [PMID:39412049].
- [2024-10] SR of Hp genotype-stratified RCTs: vitamin E improved HDL function only in Hp 2-2 diabetes [PMID:39474248].
- [2024-10] LURIC cohort: HP genotype x gamma-tocopherol interaction for mortality in diabetes [PMID:39882297].

** Open questions
- Whether the MASH histology benefit translates into fewer liver events or deaths. The only outcome data are observational [PMID:30506586] [PMID:39412049]. The 2024 EASL-EASD-EASO guideline does not list vitamin E among recommended MASH-targeted drugs [PMID:38851997].
- Whether lower doses (300 mg/d) keep the efficacy while reducing bleeding and prostate risk [PMID:39970876].
- Whether natural RRR vs synthetic all-rac forms, or mixed tocopherols, change harm signals. SELECT used all-rac acetate, while HOPE used a natural source [PMID:9537614] [PMID:21990298].
- Prospective genotype-guided (Hp 2-2) trials [PMID:29731659].
- Whether the TEAM-AD functional benefit replicates. Cochrane evidence rests on single trials [PMID:28418065].
- The clinical significance of the warfarin interaction at common supplement doses [PMID:8629604] [PMID:15213041].
- Tocotrienol efficacy beyond small, short, often industry-linked trials [PMID:37321474] [PMID:41528693].
