** Identity & forms
- Branded oral hair supplement (Lifes2good) built around a proprietary marine protein/collagen complex, "AminoMar" ("AminoMar C" in older formulas). It is sold for temporary or self-perceived hair thinning [PMID:25883641] [PMID:26355631].
- The original 1990s Finnish formulation was described as "special marine extracts and a silica compound" [PMID:1286738]. The marine complex is described as a proprietary blend of proteins, lipids and glycosaminoglycans from marine sources [PMID:26355631]. In vitro work names its raw components as shark and oyster extracts [PMID:37156114].
- Formulas differ by product and year. NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (DSLD) entries list the following per tablet (taken twice daily):
  - Viviscal Advanced Hair Health (2025 entry): AminoMar 450 mg (collagen type II, oyster extract), vitamin C 59 mg, niacin 12 mg NE, biotin 120 mcg, calcium 60 mg, iron 10 mg, zinc 11 mg, horsetail extract 36.7 mg, millet seed extract 7.5 mg. Allergen statement: "Contains: Fish (shark); Mollusks" [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690].
  - Viviscal Man (2025 entry): AminoMar 452.9 mg, zinc 8 mg, vitamin C 30 mg, calcium 61 mg, horsetail 24.5 mg, flax seed extract 50 mg [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331691].
  - Viviscal Professional (2020 entry): AminoMar 475 mg (shark cartilage, oyster extract), biotin 100 mcg, vitamin C 20 mg (acerola), calcium 60 mg, apple extract 40 mg, L-methionine 12.5 mg [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/240219].
  - Viviscal Extra Strength (2017 entry): AminoMar C 600 mg, vitamin C 60 mg, acerola 240 mg, horsetail 60 mg [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/70690].
- At the labelled dose (2 tablets/day), the current women's formula supplies about 240 mcg biotin, 20 mg iron, 22 mg zinc, 118 mg vitamin C and 120 mg calcium [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690]. The tested trial products were not always the current retail formula. One trial named "Viviscal Extra Strength" [PMID:25883641], one "Viviscal Man" [PMID:27506633], and the 2015 extension trial a "new oral supplement" [PMID:26705444].
- Other marine-protein hair products (e.g., Hairgain, marine protein plus minerals and vitamins) have their own RCTs [PMID:11277343]. Their results should not be pooled with Viviscal's.

** Mechanisms
- In immortalized and primary human dermal papilla cells, Viviscal and its raw components (AminoMar C, shark and oyster extract) were not cytotoxic. They increased proliferation, alkaline phosphatase and glycosaminoglycan production, and the expression of hair-cycle genes. This was interpreted as anagen activation. It is in vitro only [PMID:37156114].
- Proposed: supply of amino acids, glycosaminoglycans and micronutrients to the follicle; manufacturer-linked reviews present this without human mechanistic data [PMID:26355631] [PMID:28921826]. Shark-cartilage chondroitin sulfate is partly absorbed orally in humans: plasma CS rose >120% after 4 g, with a peak at 8.7 h [PMID:12801483]. Whether the much smaller amounts in Viviscal reach the follicle is unknown.
- Components (correcting deficiency, not proven to boost hair in replete people):
  - Iron, zinc, biotin and vitamins are needed for follicle cycling. Supplementing them is thought to help mainly when a deficiency exists [PMID:30547302] [PMID:41692085] [PMID:39440586].
  - Vitamin C is the most efficient enhancer of non-heme iron absorption [PMID:15743017]; calcium and polyphenols inhibit it [PMID:35755397].
  - Horsetail (Equisetum arvense) is a silicon source. Silicon from horsetail tea is absorbed, with serum Cmax about 2.5-2.9 mg/L, and does not accumulate [PMID:34706374]. A hair-growth role for silicon is unproven.

** Dosing in trials
- All Viviscal RCTs used 1 tablet twice daily for 90-180 days [PMID:25883641] [PMID:27506633] [PMID:26705444] [PMID:23198010]; the 1992 trial used 2 tablets once daily for 6 months [PMID:1286738]. The label directs 1 tablet twice daily with food for 3-6 months [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690].
- Registered trials (all completed, sponsor- or investigator-site-run): NCT02288858 (n=96, Lifes2good), NCT02297360 (n=60) and NCT02302053 (n=40, Viviscal Professional) [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02297360] [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02302053].
- Bayesian NMA data suggest regrowth with Viviscal attenuates after about 12 weeks, so longer use may not add benefit [PMID:36755885]. For context, zinc gluconate 50 mg/day was used in zinc-deficient AA [PMID:20523772], and interference studies used biotin 10 mg/day [PMID:34042535], about 40 times Viviscal's daily biotin.

** Safety & adverse effects
- Viviscal RCTs reported no treatment-emergent adverse events [PMID:25883641] [PMID:27506633] [PMID:26705444]. Pooled reviews of hair supplements describe AEs as rare and mild [PMID:36449274] [PMID:41954038]. These trials were small and short, and most were run by or funded through the manufacturer or an investigator with disclosed Viviscal ties [PMID:34741573] [PMID:34053595].
- Allergy (fish and mollusc ingredients):
  - Labels declare fish (shark) and mollusc (oyster) content [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690].
  - Fish collagen is a clinically relevant allergen. 21% of 101 fish-allergic patients were sensitized to fish collagen [PMID:32389794]. Fish collagen in gummy tablets caused anaphylaxis in a child [PMID:33178560].
  - Fish gelatin IgE was found in 3/10 fish-allergic children [PMID:10984381], but a DBPCFC of fish gelatin in 30 fish-allergic patients produced only mild subjective reactions [PMID:15500940]. Shark (cartilaginous fish) causes fewer reactions than bony fish because α-parvalbumin cross-reacts weakly with β-parvalbumin. Tolerance cannot be assumed, since some patients reacted to both [PMID:28117510] [PMID:27236375].
  - Oyster tropomyosin (Cra g 1) is the main mollusc allergen [PMID:18291306]. Shellfish allergy affects about 0.5-2.5% of people [PMID:27404324]. Fish allergy prevalence is 0-7% depending on diagnostic method [PMID:27613460]; in Europe, self-reported lifetime prevalence is fish 1.4% and shellfish 0.4% [PMID:37405695].
- Iron:
  - The label carries the standard warning that accidental iron overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6 [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690].
  - Iron is a leading cause of poisoning deaths in young children, and risk rises around a sibling's birth [PMID:12796332]. There is insufficient evidence to recommend iron in hair loss without proven deficiency [PMID:16635664].
- Zinc (22 mg/day in the women's formula): below the US UL of 40 mg/day but close to the EFSA UL of 25 mg/day. Excess zinc can cause anemia, neutropenia and copper deficiency [PMID:38999082]. Zinc-induced copper deficiency is often missed (7/14 confirmed cases were previously undiagnosed) [PMID:37070154].
- Biotin is generally nontoxic but interferes with blood tests, which complicates cancer monitoring [PMID:40362760]. Horsetail: at 900 mg/day extract, a diuretic effect equal to hydrochlorothiazide without electrolyte loss [PMID:24723963]. Another trial found BP lowering of -12.6/-8.1 mmHg [PMID:35168030]. Viviscal contains only 25-73 mg/day, so the clinical relevance is likely small.

** Contraindications
- Fish or mollusc/shellfish allergy: the label declares shark and oyster ingredients [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690] [PMID:32389794] [PMID:18291306].
- Pregnancy, breastfeeding and under-18s: the label states it is not intended for these groups [URL:https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/331690]. No safety data exist.
- Iron overload (hereditary hemochromatosis): management aims to keep ferritin ≤50 µg/L by phlebotomy [PMID:9867745]. Supplemental iron with vitamin C, which enhances absorption [PMID:15743017], runs counter to this goal.

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Levothyroxine:
  - Ferrous sulfate reduced levothyroxine absorption and caused hypothyroidism, which reversed on stopping iron [PMID:9191742]. A 2026 case showed refractory hypothyroidism despite a ≥4 h separation [PMID:42438581].
  - Calcium carbonate with levothyroxine raised TSH from 1.6 to 2.7 mIU/L [PMID:10838651]. All calcium salts reduced absorption by about 20-25% [PMID:21595516]. Iron and calcium are both listed as absorption inhibitors [PMID:19942153]; vitamin C may increase absorption [PMID:28153426]. Viviscal's iron (20 mg/day) and calcium (120 mg/day) are lower than in these studies.
- Fluoroquinolones: calcium, iron and probably zinc chelate quinolones and reduce their absorption [PMID:2686430].
- Tetracyclines: iron and calcium salts can reduce absorption by 50-90%. A 3-hour separation prevents this [PMID:946598].
- Drugs monitored by immunoassay (thyroid replacement, thyroid cancer follow-up): biotin can cause falsely low TSH and thyroglobulin, which matters when titrating levothyroxine [PMID:34042535].
- Diuretics/antihypertensives: horsetail is diuretic [PMID:24723963] and BP-lowering [PMID:35168030] at 900 mg/day; an additive effect at Viviscal's dose is theoretical. Hair-loss drugs (minoxidil, finasteride): no interaction studies. Guidelines base AGA treatment on licensed drugs, not supplements [PMID:29178529] [PMID:31677111].
*** Other supplements
- Biotin-containing products (B complex, "hair/skin/nail" products): these add to Viviscal's 240 mcg/day. Interference is dose-dependent and shown at 10 mg/day [PMID:34042535] and in high-dose users [PMID:27362288] [PMID:29856977].
- Zinc: stacking 22 mg/day from Viviscal with a separate zinc product can exceed the UL. Risk of copper deficiency [PMID:38999082] [PMID:37070154].
- Iron (including heme iron): cumulative iron load. Avoid without documented deficiency [PMID:16635664] [PMID:35755397].
- Vitamin C enhances non-heme iron absorption in single meals [PMID:15743017], less so over a whole diet [PMID:11124756]. Green tea extract: phenolic-rich green tea extract cut non-heme iron absorption from 12.1% to 8.9% [PMID:11237939].
- Collagen: duplicates the marine collagen. Fish-skin collagen peptides raised hair diameter in one 24-week RCT (n=114) [PMID:41788055], and fish collagen is an allergen [PMID:32389794].
*** Conditions
- Fish/shellfish allergy, iron overload, pregnancy/lactation: see Contraindications.
- Hair loss with an underlying cause: work-up before supplementing is advised.
  - Telogen effluvium is associated with low ferritin (SMD -0.57) and low vitamin D (SMD -0.87) [PMID:42077991]. A 2024 case-control study found low zinc [PMID:39107936].
  - TE is often triggered by physiological stress and usually remits [PMID:33541773] [PMID:31686886]. After bariatric surgery, hair loss (57%) is linked to low zinc, folate and ferritin [PMID:33675022]. GLP-1 RA users have reported hair loss [PMID:41111833].
- Biotin deficiency: reported in 38% of women complaining of hair loss in one series [PMID:27601860]. Biotin levels did not differ between TE cases and controls in another study [PMID:32308796].
- Zinc deficiency: oral zinc improved zinc-deficiency TE in 5 patients [PMID:22741940]. Low serum zinc correlated with alopecia areata severity [PMID:37787421].
- Iron deficiency: the evidence remains contested, and no RCT of iron for hair loss in iron-deficient women exists [PMID:20888064].
*** Lab-test interference
- Biotin (240 mcg/day in the women's formula, 200 mcg/day in Professional) is a component-level concern:
  - Mechanism: biotin causes falsely low results in sandwich assays and falsely high results in competitive assays. This can mimic hyperthyroidism (low TSH with high FT4/FT3) [PMID:32567529].
  - Other affected assays include troponin I/T, ferritin, vitamin D, B12, PTH, PSA, LH/FSH, estradiol, testosterone and NT-proBNP [PMID:32567529] [PMID:30296442].
  - Factitious Graves' disease has been reported [PMID:27362288] [PMID:29856977]; the 2026 ETA guideline lists biotin among pharmaceutical interferents [PMID:42466561].
- Troponin:
  - FDA (2017, updated 2019) reported a death after a falsely low troponin in a patient taking high-dose biotin [URL:https://www.tctmd.com/news/fda-reminds-clinicians-about-biotin-interference-troponin-tests].
  - The Roche TnT Gen 5 threshold is >20 ng/mL, with a modelled false-negative AMI risk of 0.026% [PMID:32804676]. The Ortho troponin I threshold is only 2.5 µg/L, which 0.8% of UK samples exceeded [PMID:32936669].
- Dose context: thyroid assay interference was shown at 10 mg/day, with a peak 2 h post-dose [PMID:34042535]. Biotin is absorbed and excreted rapidly [PMID:26699811]. Newer assays tolerate up to 1200 ng/mL [PMID:32324155] or 1000-3000 ng/mL [PMID:40417451].
- No study measured serum biotin at Viviscal doses. Interference from Viviscal alone is probably unlikely except on the most sensitive assays. Whether it matters for a given test depends on the assay and the total biotin intake from all products. Tell the laboratory about biotin use.

** Genotype notes
No cited pharmacogenomic or nutrigenomic source found for Viviscal or its marine complex. HFE hemochromatosis is relevant only through the iron content (see Contraindications) [PMID:9867745].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] ETA guideline on thyroid immunoassay interference: biotin is listed among pharmaceutical interferents. Exclude interference before diagnosing rare disorders [PMID:42466561].
- [2026-07] Case report: iron-levothyroxine interaction persisted despite ≥4 h dose separation [PMID:42438581].
- [2026-04] SR/MA of 14 commercial-supplement studies (n=967, incl. Viviscal): better anagen/telogen measures, total hair count not significant. Calls for independent research [PMID:41954038].
- [2026-03] JAAD clinical review: some adjuncts show modest benefit, but trials are small, heterogeneous and often industry-funded. Independently funded RCTs are needed [PMID:41887578].
- [2026-03] TE trace-element MA: low ferritin and vitamin D in TE [PMID:42077991].
- [2026-02] Review "Vitamins and hair: sham or science?": vitamins help in deficiency; benefit in replete people is unproven [PMID:41692085].
- [2026-01] NMA of 19 AGA supplement RCTs (n=1658): density gains for Nutrafol, apple extract, tocotrienols, pumpkin seed oil and MK-R7; Viviscal is not named in the abstract [PMID:41561175].
- [2025-08] Male AGA OTC NMA: topical minoxidil 5% was most effective [PMID:40869239].
- [2025-05] Biotin interference in routine immunoassays: higher biotin in dialysis/ICU patients; newer reagents more tolerant [PMID:40417451].
- [2025-01] Trace-element MA in alopecia areata: lower zinc (SMD -0.69) and vitamin D [PMID:39739356].
- [2024-08] Biotin evidence review: no high-quality support for biotin as a hair supplement [PMID:39148962].
- No independent Viviscal RCT published in the last 24 months was found in PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov.

** Open questions
- No independent, adequately powered RCT of Viviscal exists. All product RCTs are small and manufacturer-linked or from sites with disclosed Viviscal ties [PMID:34741573] [PMID:34053595]. Some reported effects (doubling of terminal hairs, 95% "cure") are implausibly large [PMID:23198010] [PMID:1286738].
- Which component, if any, drives the effect. The marine complex's activity is shown only in vitro [PMID:37156114], and the micronutrients mainly help deficient users [PMID:41692085].
- Whether benefit persists after stopping, or continues beyond about 12 weeks [PMID:36755885]. Serum biotin levels at 200-240 mcg/day and the real-world risk of assay interference are also unknown.
- Allergenicity of the processed shark/oyster complex in fish- or mollusc-allergic people. No challenge data exist for Viviscal itself.
- Comparative efficacy vs minoxidil, and value as an adjunct to it [PMID:40869239] [PMID:29178529].
