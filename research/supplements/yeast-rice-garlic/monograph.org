** Identity & forms
- This is a combination product: red yeast rice (RYR; rice fermented with Monascus spp.) plus an odor-free (deodorized or aged) garlic (Allium sativum) preparation. No randomized trial of this exact two-ingredient combination was found. The evidence below is therefore per ingredient.
- RYR contains monacolins. Monacolin K in its lactone form is identical to lovastatin [PMID:32626016] [PMID:39511729]. RYR also contains pigments, sterols, polyphenols and sometimes the mycotoxin citrinin [PMID:40927271] [PMID:25168220].
- Product content varies widely. For monacolin K, 12 US products labeled "600 mg/capsule" contained 0.10-10.09 mg monacolin K per capsule, and 4 of 12 had elevated citrinin [PMID:20975018]. A separate analysis found monacolin K varied more than 40-fold in 600 mg products (0.03-2.18 mg) and the monacolin ratios differed from authentic RYR [PMID:25168220]. NCCIH notes that labels usually do not state monacolin K content [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/red-yeast-rice].
- Standardized Chinese preparations are distinct from retail supplements: Xuezhikang (XZK), a partially purified RYR extract, was used in the CCSPS outcome trial [PMID:18549841]. Zhibitai and Zhibituo are polyherbal products that contain RYR [PMID:39108747].
- Multi-ingredient nutraceuticals (e.g., Armolipid Plus: RYR + berberine + policosanol) dominate the European trial literature [PMID:33669333] [PMID:27131395].
- Garlic forms differ in sulfur chemistry and dose: powder, oil and aged garlic extract (AGE, the usual "odor-free" form) [PMID:40628369] [PMID:39113105]. AGE contains S-allyl-L-cysteine and related compounds [PMID:16484563].

** Mechanisms
- Monacolin K reversibly inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, which lowers LDL-C, non-HDL-C, apoB and hs-CRP [PMID:33538260]. In vitro, the RYR phytocomplex inhibited HMG-CoA reductase more strongly than isolated lovastatin, which suggests secondary monacolins contribute [PMID:39511729].
- Lovastatin is activated to lovastatin acid. It is cleared by CYP3A4 and taken up into the liver by OATP1B1, the basis of its drug and gene interactions [PMID:8689812] [PMID:26020121]. RYR extracts inhibited CYP1A2, CYP2C19 and P-gp in vitro. In volunteers, one RYR capsule did not change nifedipine pharmacokinetics, but gemfibrozil raised lovastatin acid levels [PMID:23227093].
- RYR depleted hepatic and cardiac CoQ10 in mice in a dose-dependent way (mechanistic, animal data) [PMID:15705235].
- Garlic antiplatelet effect: AGE inhibits platelet aggregation in vitro by several mechanisms, including blunting the rise in intraplatelet Ca2+ [PMID:16484563]. In RCTs, results are inconsistent: 6 of 12 trials showed reduced aggregation [PMID:36222178]. Dietary raw garlic (4.2 g) did not impair platelet function in volunteers [PMID:17959943].
- Garlic affects drug pharmacokinetics: garlic caplets lowered saquinavir AUC by 51% [PMID:11740713].

** Dosing in trials
- RYR trials: typical daily doses were 200-4800 mg of RYR, over 4-24 weeks [PMID:35111069] [PMID:38794691]. The JACC seminar describes 3-10 mg/day monacolin K, giving 15-25% LDL-C reduction within 6-8 weeks [PMID:33538260]. Statin-intolerance RCTs used 1800 mg twice daily for 24 weeks [PMID:19528562] and 2400 mg twice daily for 12 weeks [PMID:20102918].
- Other RYR regimens. Natto RYR: 1950 mg/d for 3 months [PMID:42212982]. RYR + CoQ10: 10 mg monacolins + 30 mg CoQ10 for 6 months [PMID:27055107]. Monacolin 4.5%-standardized ingredient with gamma-oryzanol/GABA: LDL-C -19.3% at 3 months [PMID:39275298].
- EU dose limit: since Regulation (EU) 2022/860, each daily portion must provide less than 3 mg monacolins [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860]. In one case series, a combination product reformulated to 2.99 mg monacolin K kept its LDL-lowering effect [PMID:39614370]. A retrospective study used 3 mg/d monacolin K after sleeve gastrectomy [PMID:41754164].
- Monacolin dose and safety: in a safety MA, higher monacolin K doses were not linked to more musculoskeletal AEs (subgroups <=3, 3.1-5, >5 mg/d) [PMID:30844537].
- Garlic: MAs pooled heterogeneous powder, oil and AGE doses [PMID:40580481] [PMID:39113105]. Lipid effects needed more than 2 months of use [PMID:23590705]. The AGE-warfarin RCT used 5 mL twice daily for 12 weeks [PMID:16484565].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Muscle (RYR): RCT pooled data (53 RCTs, n=8535): no excess musculoskeletal disorders (OR 0.94, CI 0.53-1.65). Non-musculoskeletal AEs and serious AEs were fewer than in controls [PMID:30844537].
- Spontaneous reports show statin-like muscle signals. Italy: 55 reactions including myalgia/CK rise (19), rhabdomyolysis (1) and liver injury (10). 13 required hospitalization [PMID:28093797]. US CAERS: rhabdomyolysis/myopathy ROR 8.44 [PMID:29582393]. Case reports describe severe rhabdomyolysis with CK 112,985 U/L, AKI and respiratory failure after 2 g/d for 1 month [PMID:40909457], and fulminant rhabdomyolysis 2 days after starting [PMID:40264460].
- Liver: FAERS hepatic disorders ROR 13.71 [PMID:29582393]. One biopsy-proven acute hepatitis occurred after an RYR product in a patient who had prior lovastatin hepatotoxicity [PMID:19398239]. An umbrella review found raised ALT, AST and uric acid among 61 outcome associations, with low overall credibility [PMID:38413255].
- Industry nutrivigilance: reporting rates were 0.02% for all AEs and 0.0003% for serious AEs across 608,815 exposed subject-periods [PMID:42514371]. Another dataset reported 0.049% AEs across 3.9 million consumers [PMID:40741249]. Both are manufacturer-run, and spontaneous reporting undercounts events. A narrative review of 2020-2025 case reports found Fanconi syndrome and kidney dysfunction were the main AEs, mostly from Japan [PMID:40927271].
- Citrinin is a nephrotoxic mycotoxin found in some products [PMID:20975018]. NCCIH reports a 2021 analysis in which only 1 of 37 products met the EU citrinin limit [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/red-yeast-rice]. A Taiwanese probabilistic assessment derived an RfD of 0.08 ug/kg bw/day and flagged regular consumers as a risk subgroup [PMID:41360537].
- 2024 Kobayashi "Beni-koji CholesteHelp" outbreak (Japan). Cause: specific lots were contaminated with puberulic acid, made by Penicillium adametzioides growing in the factory [PMID:40159186]. Citrinin was not produced by the implicated strains [PMID:39708997].
- Beni-koji outbreak scale: by November 2024, 2628 people had sought care [PMID:41732753]. One report cites 76 possibly related deaths and 492 hospitalizations [PMID:39424254]. In a nationwide survey (n=192): 94% had eGFR <60, with Fanconi syndrome and tubulointerstitial nephritis or tubular necrosis on biopsy. CK was normal. 87% still had eGFR <60 at last follow-up [PMID:39708997].
- Beni-koji outbreak mechanism: puberulic acid reproduced proximal tubular mitochondrial injury in cells, organoids and mice [PMID:41732753] [PMID:39978331]. It was mutagenic in Ames tests but not in vivo [PMID:41641978]. Novel lovastatin analogs Y/Z were also present in the contaminated lots [PMID:42477306].
- Beni-koji outbreak outcomes: case reports describe partial or full recovery after stopping, sometimes with steroids, and some progression to CKD [PMID:41684976] [PMID:39639196].
- EFSA 2018: monacolins are of "significant safety concern" at 10 mg/d, with severe reactions reported at 3 mg/d. No safe intake could be identified [PMID:32626016]. EFSA NDA 2025: reiterated that no safe level below 3 mg/d can be established [PMID:40027377].
- EU Regulation 2022/860 caps monacolins below 3 mg per daily portion and requires these label warnings: not for pregnancy or lactation, under-18s, over-70s, users of cholesterol-lowering medication, or with other RYR products [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860].
- A 2026 draft Commission regulation would move monacolins to Annex III Part A, i.e. a ban. The Standing Committee approved it on 13 May 2026, and full applicability is estimated for late 2027 [URL:https://www.complifegroup.com/2026/04/14/project-for-a-european-commission-regulation-monacolins-towards-a-definitive-ban/].
- FDA: products with added or enhanced lovastatin are unapproved new drugs that cannot be sold as dietary supplements. FDA has issued warning letters [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/red-yeast-rice].
- Garlic: common effects are breath and body odor and GI upset. Allergy, contact dermatitis, altered platelet function/coagulation and burns from topical raw garlic are reported [PMID:17918162] [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]. Excess surgical bleeding occurred with about 12 g/d garlic [PMID:27782907]. In a hypertension MA, adverse reactions were slightly but not significantly more frequent with garlic, mainly GI discomfort and bad breath [PMID:41393949].

** Contraindications
- Pregnancy and lactation: there are no safety studies, and NCCIH advises against use. The EU-mandated label warning also excludes pregnant and lactating women [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/red-yeast-rice] [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860].
- Concurrent statin or other cholesterol-lowering prescription, or other RYR products: this duplicates monacolin K/lovastatin exposure, and the EU label prohibits it [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860] [PMID:32626016].
- Prior statin-associated hepatotoxicity or rhabdomyolysis: RYR can act as a re-challenge [PMID:19398239] [PMID:28093797].
- Active liver disease, and kidney disease or transplant: RYR carries hepatic and muscle signals [PMID:29582393], citrinin is nephrotoxic [PMID:20975018], and rhabdomyolysis occurred in a cyclosporine-treated renal transplant recipient [PMID:12438974].
- Children under 18 and adults over 70 are excluded by EU labeling [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860]. A small pediatric Armolipid cohort reported benefit without safety signals, but it is not a basis for general use [PMID:39766315].
- Garlic: stop 1-2 weeks before elective surgery, as advised by ASA/AANA for herbal medicines [PMID:38613937]. Avoid with garlic allergy [PMID:17918162].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Statins: RYR adds lovastatin-equivalent exposure. The EU label prohibits combined use [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860]. Chinese RYR polyherbal products combined with statins improved lipids in low-quality RCTs [PMID:39108747]. Adding natto RYR to simvastatin gave no significant extra LDL-C lowering at 3 months [PMID:42212982].
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and grapefruit: these sharply raise lovastatin exposure, so the same is expected for monacolin K. Itraconazole raised lovastatin AUC more than 20-fold [PMID:8689812]. Clarithromycin is expected to raise lovastatin AUC more than 5-fold. Statin-clarithromycin rhabdomyolysis cases involved CYP3A4-metabolized statins [PMID:31628882]. Grapefruit juice raised lovastatin AUC 15-fold [PMID:9585793]. Direct RYR studies are lacking. The FDA cited antibiotics, antifungals, nefazodone and HIV drugs [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/red-yeast-rice].
- Fibrates (gemfibrozil): co-administration raised lovastatin acid with an RYR product [PMID:23227093]. FDA reports describe severe myopathy with the lovastatin-gemfibrozil combination (median CK 15,250 U/L) [PMID:2355431].
- Cyclosporine: case of RYR-induced rhabdomyolysis in a renal transplant recipient, attributed to CYP450 interaction [PMID:12438974].
- Warfarin/anticoagulants (garlic component): case reports suggest garlic may enhance anticoagulants [PMID:17918162]. However, a 12-week RCT (n=48) of aged garlic extract with warfarin found no increase in bleeding [PMID:16484565], and an older review found weak evidence for a warfarin-garlic interaction [PMID:11144706]. Monitor INR.
- Antiplatelet agents: garlic has antiplatelet activity, though RCT evidence is inconsistent [PMID:36222178] [PMID:16484563]. The additive effect with platelet-inhibiting drugs is unstudied [PMID:17959943].
- HIV protease inhibitors: garlic lowered saquinavir AUC by 51% [PMID:11740713].
- Antihypertensives: garlic lowers BP by about 4/3 mmHg in hypertension, so a small additive effect is possible [PMID:41393949].
*** Other supplements
- Berberine: frequently co-formulated with RYR. The combination lowered LDL-C (MD -18.79 mg/dL) [PMID:37183391] [PMID:27131395]. This is an intended additive lipid effect.
- CoQ10: RYR depletes tissue CoQ10 in mice [PMID:15705235]. RYR + CoQ10 has been tested together in an RCT [PMID:27055107]. Benefit of CoQ10 for RYR myalgia is not established.
- Garlic and Garlic (Kyolic): this product already contains garlic. Stacking other garlic supplements duplicates the antiplatelet and BP effects [PMID:40628369] [PMID:17918162].
- EPA/DHA/DPA, Ginger Root, Ginseng (Panax), Green Tea Extract: bleeding risks are reported for each (as for garlic), so additive bleeding risk with garlic is plausible [PMID:26949700] [PMID:11144706].
- Aspirin: NCCIH advises discussing garlic use with aspirin. Garlic's antiplatelet effect could add to aspirin's, but this is unquantified [URL:https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic] [PMID:36222178].
- Turmeric/Curcumin and Green Tea Extract: like RYR, these are among the 6 botanicals most often reported as hepatotoxic. Stacking adds liver-injury exposure [PMID:39102266].
- Other monacolin- or RYR-containing products: duplicate exposure [URL:https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32022R0860].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy and lactation, liver disease, kidney disease or transplant, prior statin myopathy, upcoming surgery: see Contraindications.
- Older adults (>=70): 58% of nutrivigilance reports came from people aged >=70, and both serious AEs occurred in this group [PMID:42514371].
- Diabetes: in a claims cohort, incident diabetes was lower with RYR than with lovastatin (HR 0.46). This is observational and confounded [PMID:32021355].
*** Lab-test interference
- No assay artifact found. The observed changes are true physiological effects: LDL-C, TC and apoB fall [PMID:33538260]. CK and ALT/AST may rise with myotoxicity or hepatotoxicity [PMID:28093797] [PMID:38413255]. A pharmacovigilance study advises clinicians to monitor muscle and liver safety [PMID:29582393]. With contaminated beni-koji, eGFR fell and Fanconi markers appeared (hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia, hypouricemia, glycosuria) [PMID:39708997].
- Garlic may alter INR and platelet-function tests [PMID:17918162] [PMID:36222178].

** Genotype notes
- SLCO1B1 (OATP1B1) variants affect lovastatin, the drug identical to monacolin K: SLCO1B1*5/*15 or *15/*15 carriers had lovastatin acid AUC 286% of reference [PMID:26020121]. The SLCO1B1 rs4149056 C allele raised simvastatin myopathy risk (OR 4.5 per allele) [PMID:18650507]. CPIC gives SLCO1B1-based lovastatin recommendations for statin-associated musculoskeletal symptoms [PMID:35152405].
- None of these studies used RYR itself, so extrapolating to monacolin K is an inference.

** Open questions
- No RCT has tested the RYR + garlic combination as sold. Whether garlic adds clinically to RYR's lipid effect is unknown, and garlic's LDL-C effect is only about -6 mg/dL [PMID:40580481].
- Efficacy at the EU-compliant <3 mg/d monacolin dose lacks large placebo-controlled RCTs [PMID:39614370] [PMID:40027377].
- The only hard-outcome trial (CCSPS) used Xuezhikang. Applicability to retail products of unknown monacolin content is uncertain [PMID:18549841] [PMID:20975018].
- Muscle safety: RCT data show no excess myopathy [PMID:30844537], while pharmacovigilance shows signals [PMID:29582393]. How to reconcile these is unresolved.
- Long-term kidney outcomes after puberulic acid exposure, and how to control contamination in manufacturing [PMID:39708997] [PMID:40159186].
- Whether garlic meaningfully increases bleeding with modern antithrombotics [PMID:36222178] [PMID:16484565].
