** Identity & forms
- Lutein and zeaxanthin are non-provitamin-A xanthophyll carotenoids. Mammals cannot make them and get them from leafy greens, broccoli, pumpkin, spinach and egg yolk [PMID:39304612]. Category "other" is used because they are carotenoids, not vitamins.
- With meso-zeaxanthin they form the retina's macular pigment [PMID:27420092]. Lutein is the main carotenoid of the infant brain and retina [PMID:24899160].
- Supplements are mostly marigold extracts, sold as free lutein or lutein esters [PMID:38794653]. The standard dose is the AREDS2 dose: lutein 10 mg + zeaxanthin 2 mg/day [PMID:22840421].
- AREDS2 formula: vitamin C 500 mg, vitamin E 400 IU, copper 2 mg and zinc 80 mg (25 mg in the low-zinc arm), with L/Z replacing beta-carotene [PMID:22840421] [PMID:35653117].
- Free vs ester forms: free lutein gave a 17% larger serum AUC over 4 weeks [PMID:21677121]. A 2024 crossover study (6 mg/day) found no difference between forms [PMID:38794653].
- Formulation matters: an oil-based lutein raised AUC0-72 by 39% over a reference product [PMID:42449082].

** Mechanisms
- Blue-light filtering and antioxidant activity at the fovea. MPOD correlates with contrast sensitivity (r 0.31-0.37), photostress recovery and glare disability [PMID:32792595].
- Intestinal uptake is partly carrier-mediated:
  - SR-BI: blocking it cut lutein transport 30-57% in Caco-2 cells, and beta-carotene (not lycopene) cut it about 20% [PMID:15554873].
  - NPC1L1: ezetimibe inhibited up to 40% of lutein uptake [PMID:22579005].
- Distribution: HDL is the main carrier [PMID:22375926].
  - Adipose tissue competes with the retina: obese people have about 21% less macular pigment [PMID:11773011].
  - MPOD tracks serum L/Z more closely than diet. Waist size and diabetes predict lower MPOD [PMID:17093164].
- Anti-inflammatory: L + meso-Z + Z lowered IL-1beta, TNF-alpha and oxidized LDL in an RCT [PMID:38890092].
- Carotenoids interact through competition for micelles, lipoprotein exchange and inhibition of beta-carotene cleavage. Human study results are inconsistent [PMID:10047699].

** Dosing in trials
- AMD: L 10 mg + Z 2 mg daily on top of the AREDS formula for 5 years, plus 5 years of follow-up [PMID:22840421] [PMID:35653117].
- MPOD response: serum lutein plateaus in proportion to dose (5/10/20 mg), and MPOD rises linearly with dose [PMID:20599660]. Serum L/Z rises within 2 weeks [PMID:27426932]. The lowest baseline MPOD gives the largest gains (r = -0.71) [PMID:27420092].
- Visual and cognitive RCTs: 10 mg L + 2 mg Z (or 12 mg L+Z) for 6-12 months [PMID:25468896] [PMID:28824416] [PMID:42413424].
- Other doses: lutein 20 mg/d in drivers [PMID:23360692]; L 10 + meso-Z 10 + Z 2 mg in CREST-type products [PMID:27367585] [PMID:38890092]; 12 mg/d in RP [PMID:20385935]; 6-12 mg/d in lactation [PMID:24899160]; 10/2 mg in pregnancy [PMID:39071916].
- Take with a fat-containing meal. Plasma lutein-ester response was 207% with 36 g fat vs 88% with 3 g fat [PMID:10799382].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Mortality: AREDS2 data pooled by Cochrane show little or no effect of L/Z (HR 1.06, 0.87-1.31; very low certainty) [PMID:37702300].
- RCTs in children, teenagers and screen users report good tolerability. Liver, kidney, lipid and blood-count labs were unchanged [PMID:38363462] [PMID:42413424] [PMID:39963662].
- Observed Safe Level for lutein is 20 mg/day. Long-term safety above this is not established [PMID:16814439].
- Beta-carotene and lung cancer (the reason AREDS2 swapped beta-carotene for L/Z):
  - ATBC: beta-carotene raised lung cancer by 18% (3-36%) and total mortality by 8% in smokers [PMID:8127329].
  - CARET (beta-carotene + retinol): lung cancer RR 1.28 (1.04-1.57) [PMID:8602180].
  - Meta-analyses of RCTs: lung cancer RR 1.16-1.19 [PMID:35405977] [PMID:36715090].
  - AREDS2: lung cancer 2.0% with beta-carotene vs 0.9% without, mostly in former smokers [PMID:23644932]. At 10 years: OR 1.82 (1.06-3.12) for beta-carotene vs 1.15 (0.79-1.66) for L/Z [PMID:35653117].
- Lutein and lung cancer (observational): in the VITAL cohort, more than 4 years of individual lutein supplement use was associated with lung cancer (HR 2.02, 1.28-3.17). Confounding is possible [PMID:19208726].
- Carotenodermia (yellowing, mainly palms and soles) is benign and follows prolonged high carotenoid intake [PMID:29460797].
  - In volunteers, 15 mg/day lutein raised serum lutein 5-fold and caused carotenodermia. Levels returned to baseline within 3 months of stopping [PMID:9924266].
  - A case of lutein-rich egg-diet carotenodermia was mistaken for jaundice [PMID:10748650].
- Crystalline maculopathy: one case of foveal crystals after 8 years of 20 mg/day lutein plus a very high-lutein diet. Vision was unaffected and the crystals partly resolved after stopping [PMID:27787539].
- Zinc component: GI symptoms. AREDS showed more genitourinary problems in men, with no difference between 80 and 25 mg in AREDS2 [PMID:37702300].

** Contraindications
- No absolute contraindication was found [PMID:16814439] [PMID:37702300].
- Smokers and former smokers should use the L/Z (AREDS2) formula, not beta-carotene formulas [PMID:23644932] [PMID:35653117].
- Avoid chronic intake far above 20 mg/day [PMID:16814439] [PMID:27787539].
- Lung cancer history or heavy smoking: there is an unconfirmed observational lutein signal and no proven non-ocular benefit to offset it [PMID:19208726].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Orlistat: cut absorption of a beta-carotene dose by about one-third [PMID:8852391]. Over 4.5 months it lowered alpha- and beta-carotene by 45% and 32%, but not L/Z (n=6) [PMID:17707976].
  - The label advises a multivitamin taken at least 2 h before or after orlistat [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6240792b-9224-2d10-e053-2a91aa0a2c3e].
- Bile-acid sequestrants: cholestyramine 12 g cut the rise in serum vitamin A by 59.5% [PMID:4706914]. There are no direct L/Z data.
  - Labels warn of reduced fat-soluble vitamin absorption. Take other drugs 1 h before or 4-6 h after cholestyramine, and vitamins at least 4 h before colesevelam [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=214ea05f-de81-4026-9df9-4ce3820668ee] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5ca9a9b7-5efd-09bf-e063-6294a90a681d].
- Ezetimibe: inhibited lutein uptake up to 40% via NPC1L1 in Caco-2 cells. No human data [PMID:22579005].
- Statins: atorvastatin (not rosuvastatin) lowered MPOD in a small study, and MPOD fell with longer statin use [PMID:22375926]. 44% of AREDS2 participants took statins [PMID:22840421].
*** Other supplements
- Zinc (AREDS2 zinc arm): lowering zinc from 80 to 25 mg did not change AMD progression at 5 years [PMID:23644932] or 10 years (HR 1.04, 0.94-1.14) [PMID:35653117].
  - Zinc alone helped in AREDS/Cochrane (OR 0.83) [PMID:37702300] [PMID:11594942].
  - Genotype did not modify the zinc response [PMID:31358387].
- Vitamin C, Vitamin E: co-components of the AREDS2 formula [PMID:22840421]. Vitamin E alone did not prevent AMD (RR 0.97) [PMID:28756617].
- Beta-carotene (not on list): competes with L/Z for absorption and lowered retinal L/Z in chicks [PMID:15554873] [PMID:20082082]. L/Z replaced it in AREDS2 [PMID:24310343].
- Astaxanthin: 6 mg plus 10 mg L + 2 mg Z still raised MPOD over 8 weeks [PMID:36986186]. Single high-dose xanthophylls lowered other xanthophylls in non-retinal tissues in chicks [PMID:20082082].
- Lycopene: competes with lutein for incorporation into chylomicrons after a meal, but 3 weeks of co-supplementation did not lower plasma status [PMID:11864859]. It did not reduce lutein uptake in Caco-2 cells [PMID:15554873].
- EPA/DHA/DPA: AREDS2 found no interaction and no added AMD or cognitive benefit [PMID:23644932] [PMID:26305649].
- Plant sterol/stanol esters (food): lowered plasma lutein by 14% [PMID:15145979]. Serum L/Z fell over 18 months but MPOD did not [PMID:18986598].
*** Conditions
- Intermediate AMD: benefit is shown for the AREDS formula plus L/Z, not for L/Z alone [PMID:37702300] [PMID:35653117].
- Smokers and former smokers: prefer L/Z over beta-carotene [PMID:35653117] [PMID:8127329].
- Obesity and diabetes: associated with lower MPOD [PMID:11773011] [PMID:17093164]. Hypothyroidism and diabetes predispose to carotenaemia [PMID:29460797].
- Pregnancy: L-ZIP (10/2 mg) raised maternal MP and 5-fold cord-blood L/Z [PMID:37247819] [PMID:39071916].
- Lactation: 6-12 mg/d raised breast-milk lutein 140-250% [PMID:24899160].
- Preterm infants: lower risk of severe ROP [PMID:40292760].
*** Lab-test interference
- Jaundice work-up: carotenodermia can mimic jaundice. It reflects carotenaemia, not bilirubin [PMID:10748650] [PMID:29460797].
- Skin carotenoid scores (Raman or reflection spectroscopy, a fruit/vegetable biomarker) rise with supplements [PMID:23823930] [PMID:37247819] [PMID:39964702].
- MPOD did not correlate with serum or skin carotenoids in AREDS2, so serum is a poor proxy for retinal status [PMID:22879423].

** Genotype notes
- TwinsUK (n=310): SCARB1 rs11057841, RPE65 rs4926339, ABCA1 rs1929841 and FADS1 rs174534 were associated with MPOD response [PMID:23891863].
- CFH/ARMS2 risk alleles did not modify response to L/Z vs beta-carotene, zinc dose or omega-3 in AREDS2 [PMID:31358387].
- APOE e4: higher plasma L/Z was associated with slower cognitive decline in carriers (MIND trial cohort, observational) [PMID:40876538].

** Latest research
- [2026-10] Oil-based lutein: 32% higher Cmax and 39% higher AUC0-72 than a reference [PMID:42449082].
- [2026-08] Teen RCT (n=82, 10/2 mg, 6 months): MPOD, attention and processing speed improved. Memory and sleep did not [PMID:42413424].
- [2026-04] MA of 9 AMD RCTs: MPOD g -0.59, BCVA g -0.83, mostly in early AMD [PMID:42028334].
- [2025-12] Myopia SR: no consistent slowing of progression [PMID:41515122]. Lutein esters reduced choroidal thinning in children [PMID:41342624].
- [2025-11] MIND cohort: L/Z linked to slower cognitive decline only in APOE e4 carriers [PMID:40876538].
- [2025-04] Cochrane: L/Z probably reduces ROP stage >=3 in preterm infants (RR 0.49) [PMID:40292760].
- [2025-03] RCT in central obesity: lower LDL-C, apoB and AGEs [PMID:39964702].
- [2025-02] Screen-user RCT: better Schirmer, TBUT and photostress recovery. Self-reports unchanged [PMID:39963662].
- [2025-01] AREDS/AREDS2: L/Z slowed GA progression toward the fovea [PMID:39025435].
- [2024-12] Dry-eye SR (3-20 mg/d, 4-12 wk): heterogeneous, possible TBUT/OSDI benefit [PMID:40134251].
- [2024-12] Narrative review of L/Z health effects calls for larger trials [PMID:39304612].

** Open questions
- Whether L/Z alone prevents AMD. No primary-prevention RCT exists [PMID:28756617] [PMID:21899805].
- Whether gains in MPOD and lab vision measures matter in daily life [PMID:32792595] [PMID:38582248].
- Cognition: small RCTs are positive, but AREDS2 and a meta-analysis are null [PMID:26305649] [PMID:34641336] [PMID:35752349].
- Long-term safety above 20 mg/day and the observational lutein lung-cancer signal [PMID:16814439] [PMID:19208726].
- Optimal zinc dose and the role of meso-zeaxanthin [PMID:35653117] [PMID:27420092].
- Clinical relevance of interactions with lipid-lowering and fat-blocking drugs [PMID:18986598] [PMID:22375926].
