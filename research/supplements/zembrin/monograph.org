** Identity & forms
- Zembrin is a proprietary standardized extract of Sceletium tortuosum (kanna), a South African succulent; trial extract used a 2:1 plant ratio [PMID:23441963].

** Mechanisms
- Mesembrine alkaloids are reported to inhibit serotonin reuptake and PDE4 in preclinical work; clinical relevance and product-specific exposure are uncertain [PMID:34333104].

** Evidence summary by outcome
| Outcome | Best evidence type | Direction | Effect size | GRADE-style certainty | Citations |

** Dosing in trials
- Human trials used standardized Zembrin 8 or 25 mg once daily for three months, and 25 mg/day for three weeks in crossover study; these do not establish general dose or long-term safety [PMID:23441963; PMID:25389443].

** Safety & adverse effects
- Small trial reported tolerability without significant ECG or laboratory changes; n=37 is inadequate to detect rare events. Serotonergic pharmacology creates an unquantified interaction concern [PMID:23441963; PMID:34333104].

** Contraindications
- Avoid inferring safety in pregnancy, lactation, psychiatric illness, or with serotonergic medicines from small healthy-volunteer trials; these populations were not adequately evaluated [PMID:23441963].

** Interactions
- Drugs, supplements, conditions, and relevant laboratory interference are summarized in the structured interaction object. Potential serotonergic interaction with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tramadol, or other serotonergic agents is mechanistic, not clinically quantified [PMID:34333104]. No genotype-specific claim is made because no directly relevant pharmacogenomic source was identified.

** Genotype notes
- No directly relevant, clinically validated pharmacogenomic/nutrigenomic source was identified in the sources reviewed.

** Latest research
- No high-quality human efficacy trial published in the last 24 months was identified in the searched literature; the principal placebo-controlled safety trial remains small and older [PMID:23441963].

** Open questions
- Independent, adequately powered trials and pharmacokinetic/interactions studies are needed; current human evidence is sparse [PMID:23441963; PMID:34333104].
