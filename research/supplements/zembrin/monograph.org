** Identity & forms
- Zembrin is a proprietary standardized extract of Sceletium tortuosum (L.) N.E.Br. (syn. Mesembryanthemum tortuosum L.; Aizoaceae/Mesembryanthemaceae), a South African succulent known as kanna, channa or kougoed [PMID:34333104] [PMID:35134486].
- Traditional use by San and Khoikhoi peoples: chewed (often after "fermentation" into kougoed), smoked, snuffed, or taken as tea or tincture for mood, sedation, analgesia, and to suppress thirst and hunger [PMID:18761074] [PMID:8691846] [PMID:33924742].
- Zembrin specification: a hydroethanolic extract of a "mak" (mild) variety, standardized to 0.35-0.45% total alkaloids (mesembrenone + mesembrenol >=60%, mesembrine <20%) [PMID:25301237]. The trial extract was a 2:1 plant-to-extract ratio [PMID:23441963].
- Measured Zembrin alkaloid profile: mesembrenone 47.9%, mesembrenol 32%, mesembrine 13.2%, mesembranol 6.8% of total alkaloids [PMID:34454055].
- Other products differ. Trimesemine is a high-mesembrine Sceletium extract [PMID:26615766] [PMID:26608706]. Zembrin-specific data cannot be assumed to apply to them.
- S. tortuosum contains about 25 alkaloids in four structural classes (mesembrine, Sceletium A4, joubertiamine, tortuosamine) [PMID:34333104] [PMID:27939420]. Alkaloid content varies by chemotype and region [PMID:29783186] [PMID:35425789] [PMID:40374049].
- Fermentation converts mesembrine to delta7-mesembrenone (mesembrine fell from 1.33% to 0.05% over 10 days) [PMID:18996176].
- Commercial kanna can contain hordenine [PMID:34750996]. One product was found adulterated with ephedrine [PMID:26821203]. S. tortuosum is on a UNODC list of "plants of concern" [PMID:34750996].
- Analytical methods for identity and quality control: HPLC, CZE, LC-MS and NACE-MS [PMID:21486531] [PMID:18280080] [PMID:26626243] [PMID:22736358].
- Novel delivery forms under development include chewing gum [PMID:33712901] and intranasal delivery (ex vivo mesembrine permeation across sheep nasal tissue) [PMID:37242666].

** Mechanisms
- Serotonin transporter (SERT) inhibition: Zembrin blocked 5-HT transporter binding (IC50 4.3 ug/mL). Mesembrine was the most potent alkaloid (Ki 1.4 nM) [PMID:21798331]. A review describes mesembrine as a more potent SERT inhibitor than fluoxetine in binding assays [PMID:31299396].
- PDE4 inhibition: Zembrin inhibited PDE4 (IC50 8.5 ug/mL) but not other PDEs. Mesembrenone was active on both SERT and PDE4 (IC50 <1 uM) [PMID:21798331] [PMID:27939420].
- Monoamine release: a high-mesembrine extract (Trimesemine) down-regulated SERT expression like citalopram and up-regulated VMAT-2 in cell models, with only mild AChE and MAO-A inhibition. The authors argue that monoamine release is the primary action and SERT inhibition secondary [PMID:26615766].
- Neurochemistry in mice: 35 days of two chemotype extracts raised noradrenaline and lowered GABA in all brain regions studied. A commercial extract did not have this effect [PMID:40374049].
- Rat model: Zembrin and its alkaloids attenuated hippocampal excitability. Mesembrenol and mesembranol reduced AMPA-mediated transmission [PMID:29758341]. The rat EEG profile resembled that of rolipram (a PDE4 inhibitor) and of some antidepressants [PMID:26608705].
- Immune and redox effects (in vitro): cytoprotective effects and IL-10 up-regulation in monocytes [PMID:29253615]. Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects in astrocytes, which differ by extraction method [PMID:29520661].
- Adrenal steroidogenesis (in vitro, H295R cells): a mesembrine-rich extract lowered cortisol, androgens and aldosterone, suggesting inhibition of CYP17, 3betaHSD and 17betaHSD [PMID:26608706].
- Other in vitro targets: sub-fractions inhibited AChE, MAO-B and NMDA receptors in vitro, and docking predicted A2A and CB2 targets [PMID:36006974].
- Behavioural models: anxiolytic-like effects in chicks (75-100 mg/kg i.p.) [PMID:27553978] and zebrafish larvae [PMID:32840669] [PMID:35134486] [PMID:37660956]. In Flinders Sensitive Line rats, Zembrin 50 mg/kg was antidepressant-like and equivalent to escitalopram 5 mg/kg [PMID:34454055].
- Human brain: a single 25 mg dose of Zembrin reduced amygdala reactivity to fearful faces and amygdala-hypothalamus coupling (fMRI, n=16) [PMID:23903032].

** Dosing in trials
- Most Zembrin trials used 25 mg once daily, orally [PMID:23441963] [PMID:25389443] [PMID:32740286] [PMID:32761980] [PMID:23903032].
- Safety trial: 8 or 25 mg/day for 3 months [PMID:23441963].
- Cognition: 25 mg/day for 3 weeks (crossover) [PMID:25389443]. 25 mg/day for 8 days [PMID:32740286].
- Acute studies: single doses of 25 mg [PMID:23903032] [PMID:32761980], and 25 or 50 mg [URL:https://www.scirp.org/html/4-2400309_70480.htm].
- Older adults: 25 or 50 mg/day for 6 weeks [URL:https://www.plthealth.com/resources/effect-zembrin-brain-electrical-activity-60-older-subjects-6-weeks-daily-intake-prospective-randomized-double-blind-placebo-controlled-3-armed-study-parallel-design].
- 25 mg Zembrin is described as equivalent to about 50 mg of dry plant. No trial has established long-term safety beyond 3 months [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf].
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - No human PK data were found.
  - In mice, oral bioavailability of mesembrine and mesembrenone was poor, and plasma levels were below detection after oral dosing [PMID:27526669].
  - Mesembrine permeated porcine intestinal mucosa better than caffeine. Buccal/sublingual uptake may contribute when the plant is chewed [PMID:22105579].
  - Metabolism (rat urine, pooled human liver preparations): O- and N-demethylation, dihydration, hydroxylation, then glucuronide/sulfate conjugation [PMID:25240931].

** Safety & adverse effects
- 3-month RCT (n=37):
  - Both doses were well tolerated.
  - Headache, abdominal pain and URTI were the most common AEs, all more frequent with placebo.
  - No significant laboratory or ECG changes [PMID:23441963].
  - Individual AEs judged possibly related at 25 mg included agitation, depressed mood and irritability [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf].
- 3-week crossover trial: well tolerated [PMID:25389443]. The 8-day trial reported no side effects [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf].
- Rat toxicology (Zembrin): no mortality or treatment-related adverse effects. NOAEL was 5000 mg/kg bw/day (14-day) and 600 mg/kg bw/day (90-day), the highest doses tested [PMID:25301237].
- Other preclinical signals with non-Zembrin Sceletium preparations:
  - Ataxia with an alkaloid fraction in rats. Separately, mesembrine showed no conditioned place preference [PMID:24930358].
  - In a rat stress model: raised IL-1beta, CRP and PGE2, which the author interpreted as possible intolerance [PMID:20816940].
  - The higher Zembrin dose (25 mg/kg) lowered cortical glutathione in stressed rats, suggesting a pro-oxidant effect [PMID:40643548].
- In silico prediction for mesembrine: moderate acute oral toxicity (predicted rat LD50 340-370 mg/kg), low-to-moderate probability of hERG inhibition, and non-mutagenic [PMID:40651754]. These are predictions, not observations.
- Abuse/recreational use:
  - Kanna is sold as a "legal high" [PMID:25240931].
  - Reviews list Sceletium among psychoactive plants used or abused in southern Africa [PMID:38855756] [PMID:24695249].
  - A 1996 review found no evidence that kougoed or Sceletium alkaloids are hallucinogenic [PMID:8691846].
- Product quality: ephedrine adulteration and variable hordenine content have been found in commercial kanna [PMID:26821203] [PMID:34750996].
- A review of herbal sexual enhancers covering Sceletium notes that adverse effects arise mainly with overdose, unstandardized products, or drug combinations [PMID:33066617].
- Evidence gaps: a bibliometric review notes a lack of toxicology assays, clinical assessments and bioavailability studies [PMID:38576783].

** Contraindications
- No formal contraindications are established. The cautions below are based on mechanism or on missing data.
- Concurrent SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs or other serotonergic drugs: SERT inhibition and monoamine release make serotonin toxicity plausible, but it has not been reported [PMID:21798331] [PMID:26615766] [PMID:37309284].
- Pregnancy and lactation: no human data. Trials enrolled only healthy non-pregnant adults [PMID:23441963]. Toxicology is limited [PMID:38576783].
- Diagnosed depression, anxiety or bipolar disorder under treatment: no trials in clinical populations [PMID:34333104] [PMID:41771298].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- SSRIs/SNRIs: serotonin toxicity is theoretical, based on potent SERT inhibition by mesembrine [PMID:21798331] [PMID:31299396]. A review of serotonin syndrome notes that it can arise from interactions between two or more serotonergic agents [PMID:37309284]. The ADDF report advises against combining Zembrin with drugs that alter serotonin uptake or release. It also states that no herb-drug interactions have been reported [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf].
- MAO inhibitors: a Sceletium extract acts as a monoamine-releasing agent with mild MAO-A inhibition [PMID:26615766], and sub-fractions inhibit MAO-B [PMID:36006974]. Combining it with MAOIs is not studied, and serotonin toxicity is theoretical [PMID:37309284].
- Other serotonergic drugs (e.g., tramadol, triptans, linezolid): the same theoretical additive mechanism applies [PMID:37309284] [PMID:21798331].
- PDE4 inhibitors (e.g., roflumilast): Zembrin inhibits PDE4 [PMID:21798331], so effects could be additive. This has not been studied [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf]. Roflumilast's adverse-effect profile includes GI upset, insomnia and psychiatric signals [PMID:41458197].
- CYP-metabolized drugs: no human or in vitro hepatic CYP inhibition study of Zembrin or mesembrine was found. Mesembrine and mesembrenone are oxidatively metabolized (demethylation and hydroxylation) in human liver preparations [PMID:25240931]. In adrenal cells, a high-mesembrine extract inhibited steroidogenic CYP17 [PMID:26608706].
- CNS depressants and alcohol: an alkaloid fraction caused ataxia in rats [PMID:24930358], and traditional tinctures were used for sedation [PMID:8691846]. Additive effects are plausible on mechanistic grounds; there are no human data.
- Cannabis: historical (Paterson 1789) and contemporary user reports describe synergism and potentiation with smoked cannabis [PMID:8691846].
- Stimulants/sympathomimetics: some kanna products contain hordenine [PMID:34750996] or are adulterated with ephedrine [PMID:26821203]. This is a product-quality issue, not a property of the standardized extract.
*** Other supplements
- No cited interaction studies with supplements in this corpus.
- Serotonergic supplements (e.g., 5-HTP, St John's wort): additive serotonergic load is possible (mechanistic inference) [PMID:37309284] [PMID:21798331].
- Rhodiola Rosea: in rats, the EEG profile of Zembrin resembled that of Rhodiola rosea and Ginkgo extracts. This is a pharmacodynamic similarity, not an interaction study [PMID:26608705].
*** Conditions
- Pregnancy and lactation, and treated psychiatric illness: see Contraindications.
- Depression on SSRIs (bone health): in vitro, S. tortuosum extracts promoted osteoblast mineralization, unlike SSRIs. The authors propose it as an adjunct [PMID:42629587]. This is cell data only.
- Epilepsy: preclinical attenuation of AMPA-mediated hippocampal excitability has been proposed as a basis for anticonvulsant drug leads [PMID:29758341]. No clinical data.
*** Lab-test interference
- No immunoassay cross-reactivity data were found.
- Mesembrine and mesembrenone metabolites can be detected by GC-MS/LC-MS urine screening [PMID:25240931]. Forensic NACE-MS methods can identify kanna alkaloids [PMID:22736358].
- In vitro adrenal effects (lower cortisol, androgens and aldosterone) [PMID:26608706] have not been shown in humans. Clinical laboratory panels were unchanged over 3 months [PMID:23441963].

** Latest research
- [2026-08] In vitro: S. tortuosum extracts promoted osteoblast (SaOS-2) mineralization, in contrast to SSRIs. Mesembrine alone was only a weak inducer [PMID:42629587].
- [2026-07] Critical review of animal and clinical studies of M. tortuosum and Zembrin: mixed evidence. Studies are small and in clinically irrelevant populations. Large trials in relevant populations are needed [PMID:41771298].
- [2026-07] Zembrin (12.5 ug/mL) reversed reserpine-induced hypolocomotion and oxidative stress in zebrafish larvae. In silico data suggest stable alkaloid-target complexes [PMID:42451736].
- [2026-01] ADDF Cognitive Vitality report (updated 2026-01-29): Zembrin may improve some complex cognitive functions and mood, but trials are small and short. It is generally well tolerated. No long-term safety data exist beyond 3 months [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf].
- [2025-10] In silico toxicity profile of mesembrine: predicted rat LD50 340-370 mg/kg (GHS category 4), non-mutagenic, low-to-moderate hERG risk [PMID:40651754].
- [2025-09] Zembrin showed neuroprotection, and a methanol extract showed neurorestoration, in a 6-OHDA zebrafish model of Parkinson's disease [PMID:40691887].
- [2025-07] Rat chronic mild stress model: Zembrin 12.5 mg/kg reduced anhedonia- and anxiety-like behavior and PDE4B in males. 25 mg/kg raised IL-10 but lowered cortical glutathione [PMID:40643548].
- [2025-06] In mice, extracts from two wild chemotypes raised brain noradrenaline and lowered GABA; a commercial extract did not [PMID:40374049].
- No new human RCT of Zembrin or kanna published between Oct 2024 and Oct 2026 was found in PubMed.
  - ClinicalTrials.gov lists an 8-week Zembrin RCT (n=120, stress and cognition, Northumbria University) with status "unknown" and no posted results [URL:https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05471804].

** Open questions
- Efficacy in clinical anxiety or depressive disorders. All RCTs to date are in healthy volunteers, with n<=60 and duration <=3 months [PMID:41771298] [PMID:34333104].
- Human pharmacokinetics: alkaloid exposure after 25 mg Zembrin is unknown, and oral bioavailability was poor in mice [PMID:27526669].
- Whether CYP inhibition or induction occurs. No hepatic CYP or transporter studies were found.
- Whether serotonin toxicity occurs with SSRIs or MAOIs in practice. No controlled combination data or case reports were found [PMID:37309284].
- Which alkaloids drive which effects. Mesembrine drove anxiolytic-like effects, but the antidepressant-like effect may need the whole extract [PMID:37660956]. Extract chemotype changes neurochemical effects [PMID:40374049].
- Long-term (>3 months) safety, abuse liability and safety in pregnancy [URL:https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Sceletium_tortuosum_(supplement).pdf] [PMID:38576783].
- Industry links: some trial authors are affiliated with the manufacturer (HG&H Pharmaceuticals) [URL:https://www.scirp.org/html/4-2400309_70480.htm]. Independent, adequately powered trials are lacking [PMID:41771298].
- Regulatory pathways may hinder development of this traditional herbal medicine outside southern Africa [PMID:33588735].
