** Identity & forms
- Zinc is an essential trace element that is a cofactor in over 300 enzymatic reactions and in gene regulation and immune modulation [PMID:38999082]. Supplements are salts, and the dose should be read as elemental zinc, not salt mass [PMID:39770891].
- Gluconate and citrate: fasting fractional absorption (10 mg zinc) was 60.9% for gluconate and 61.3% for citrate, versus 49.9% for oxide. Some people absorbed almost no zinc from oxide [PMID:24259556]. A 2024 review ranks glycinate and gluconate as the best absorbed [PMID:39770891].
- Picolinate: a small 4-week crossover (n=15, 50 mg/d) found that only picolinate raised hair, urine and erythrocyte zinc. This study is old and used indirect markers [PMID:3630857].
- Acetate lozenges: the form used in the strongest cold trials [PMID:27378206] [PMID:28515951]. Zinc acetate (Galzin) is also an FDA-approved prescription drug for Wilson disease [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2c4fb943-4b2f-273d-e063-6394a90abb47].
- Sulfate: used in the leg-ulcer trials [PMID:25202988] and as syrup in paediatric prevention trials [PMID:36994923]. Oxide (80 mg zinc plus 2 mg copper) was used in AREDS [PMID:11594942]. Intranasal gluconate gels are a separate route that carries a risk of anosmia [PMID:16467707].
- Reference values: WHO dietary reference values are 6.7-15 mg/d. The UL is 25 mg/d (EFSA) or 40 mg/d (US) [PMID:38999082].
** Mechanisms
- Immunity: zinc supports T-cell number and function. Deficiency is common in older adults and is linked to immunosenescence, inflammaging and weaker vaccine responses (reviews) [PMID:34915196] [PMID:40390089].
- Antiviral (common cold): lozenges act locally in the oropharynx. Benefit seems to depend on lozenge composition, and doses above about 100 mg/d added nothing [PMID:28515951] [PMID:27378206].
- Copper antagonism: zinc induces enterocyte metallothionein, which binds dietary and secreted copper and carries it out in faeces. This is the therapeutic action in Wilson disease and the cause of zinc-induced copper deficiency [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2c4fb943-4b2f-273d-e063-6394a90abb47] [PMID:42087025].
- Glucose metabolism: zinc is involved in insulin synthesis, storage and secretion. The beta-cell transporter ZnT8 (SLC30A8) affects how insulin secretion responds to zinc [PMID:42594341] [PMID:25348609].
- Ionophores: quercetin and EGCG carried zinc across cell and liposome membranes in vitro [PMID:25050823]. This is a laboratory finding only.
- Gonadal axis: dietary zinc restriction lowered testosterone in young men, and repletion raised it in marginally deficient older men (small studies) [PMID:8875519].
- Depression: blood zinc is about 1.85 umol/L lower in depressed than non-depressed people, with a gradient by severity (observational) [PMID:23806573].
- Bone: 50 mg/d raised total and bone-specific alkaline phosphatase (a zinc metalloenzyme) in healthy men [PMID:11787985].

** Dosing in trials
- Common cold: lozenges giving about 80-92 mg/d of zinc worked as well as 192-207 mg/d, started within 24 h of symptoms [PMID:28515951]. The Cochrane review included gluconate 45-276 mg/d for 4.5-21 days [PMID:38719213].
- AMD: AREDS used 80 mg zinc (as oxide) plus 2 mg copper with antioxidants [PMID:11594942]. AREDS2 tested 25 mg against 80 mg and found no difference in progression; the 10-year HR for low vs high zinc was 1.04 (0.94-1.14) [PMID:23644932] [PMID:35653117].
- Childhood diarrhoea: the WHO-commissioned review found lower-dose zinc caused less vomiting than high-dose (RR 0.80) and favoured reducing the dose [PMID:39641338]. Adherence to 10-day courses pools at 63%, and at 35% for 14 days [PMID:41178278].
- Glycaemia and adult trials: doses are commonly 20-50 mg/d. The 2019 MA found larger fasting-glucose effects with inorganic salts and in diabetes [PMID:31161192].
- Older adults: 30 mg/d for 3 months raised serum zinc, but not to normal in those starting at or below 60 ug/dL [PMID:26817502]. A pilot trial is comparing 30 vs 60 mg/d [PMID:38450236].
- COVID-19: 50 mg/d gluconate for 10 days [PMID:33576820]; 90 mg/d acetate for 14 days [PMID:41076985].
- Wilson disease (prescription): 50 mg three times daily as acetate, taken away from food [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2c4fb943-4b2f-273d-e063-6394a90abb47].

** Safety & adverse effects
- GI effects and taste: cold treatment raised non-serious adverse events (RR 1.34, 1.15-1.55) [PMID:38719213]. An earlier MA found more bad taste (RR 1.65) and nausea (RR 1.64) [PMID:22566526]. Zinc raised vomiting in children, both as treatment (RR 1.57) [PMID:27996088] and as prevention (RR 1.29) [PMID:36994923]. FAZST (30 mg/d) found more abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting [PMID:31910279]. The Galzin label lists gastric irritation and post-marketing gastric ulcers [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2c4fb943-4b2f-273d-e063-6394a90abb47].
- Copper deficiency: a 2026 SR of 37 cases found exposure from supplements, denture creams and coins, at doses of about 50 to over 1500 mg/d.
  - Anaemia was nearly universal, often with neutropenia, and copper was low in every case. Ring sideroblasts were often mistaken for myelodysplasia [PMID:42087025].
  - Blood counts recovered after stopping zinc and giving copper. Neurological deficits recovered slowly or incompletely [PMID:42087025].
  - Case reports: an OTC product caused sideroblastic anaemia with low ceruloplasmin [PMID:38846187]. Denture-cream zinc caused myeloneuropathy that mimicked a paraneoplastic syndrome [PMID:40416147]. 150 mg/d lowered ceruloplasmin ferroxidase and SOD activity in women [PMID:3365292].
- Intranasal zinc: the "squirt, sniff, burn, anosmia" syndrome was described in 17 patients [PMID:16467707]. Smell loss can be long-lasting or permanent [PMID:15283486], and a Bradford Hill analysis supports causation [PMID:20644061]. Benefit is unproven (pooled RR 0.62, 0.18-2.19) [PMID:20690364].
- HDL: in healthy people HDL fell by 0.10 mmol/L (about 7%) [PMID:20034629]. In T2D and CKD it rose instead [PMID:38224402] [PMID:41651464].
- Magnesium: 142 mg/d zinc lowered magnesium absorption and balance [PMID:7836627]. A 2025 case describes refractory hypomagnesaemia [PMID:40270655].
- Other: sperm DNA fragmentation rose by 2.4% with zinc plus folic acid [PMID:31910279]. Body weight rose by 0.68 kg, which is clinically trivial [PMID:41617950].
- Upper limits: 40 mg/d (US) and 25 mg/d (EFSA) [PMID:38999082]. Cold-lozenge and AREDS doses exceed these and are meant for time-limited or supervised use [PMID:28515951] [PMID:11594942].
** Contraindications
- Intranasal zinc products: avoid because of the risk of permanent anosmia [PMID:16467707] [PMID:20644061].
- Existing copper deficiency, unexplained cytopenias or myelopathy: high-dose zinc should not be continued without checking copper status [PMID:42087025] [PMID:40416147].
- Long-term intake above the UL without supervision, especially stacked sources (multivitamin plus lozenges plus denture cream) [PMID:38999082] [PMID:42087025].
- Wilson disease is an indication, not a contraindication. Zinc is used for maintenance under specialist monitoring, but only chelators are recommended for significant liver disease [PMID:40089450] [PMID:31388634].
- Infants under 6 months with diarrhoea: no benefit and more vomiting [PMID:27996088].

** Interactions
*** Drugs
- Quinolones: a zinc-containing multivitamin lowered ciprofloxacin AUC from 14.5 to 11.3 ug.h/mL [PMID:2610494]. The ciprofloxacin label advises taking it 2 h before or 6 h after zinc-containing products [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3b3d22dc-df09-4910-8c9b-1204eeedbd68].
- Tetracyclines: 45 mg zinc as sulfate cut tetracycline absorption by about 30%, with no significant effect on doxycycline [PMID:786686].
- Penicillamine: the label advises taking it at least 1 h apart from zinc- or iron-containing products, and says mineral supplements may block its response [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f658b20a-269f-4e78-a79b-a56a5ed8dc1b]. The Galzin label found no pharmacodynamic interaction in Wilson patients [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2c4fb943-4b2f-273d-e063-6394a90abb47].
- Thiazides: urinary zinc excretion rose by 60% on thiazides [PMID:7001863]. It was also raised with hydrochlorothiazide-amiloride [PMID:3595066]. The effect lowers zinc stores rather than being a dosing hazard [PMID:23279674].
- ACE inhibitors and ARBs: captopril and enalapril lowered monocyte zinc, with more urinary loss on captopril [PMID:9477394] [PMID:2195291]. A systematic review also lists losartan and valsartan [PMID:23279674].
- PPIs: on long-term PPIs, plasma zinc rose only 37% with supplementation versus 126% in controls [PMID:27957023]. Omeprazole also cut absorption of a zinc sulfate dose [PMID:12546170]. However, short-term omeprazole did not reduce zinc absorption from food [PMID:8568113].
- Antidiabetic drugs: zinc's glucose-lowering effect could be additive (FG -14 mg/dL) [PMID:31161192] [PMID:42594341].
*** Other supplements
- Heme Iron (Proferrin): non-heme iron at Fe:Zn 2:1-3:1 inhibited zinc absorption, but heme iron at 3:1 did not [PMID:7223699] [PMID:3522825]. In the reverse direction, zinc inhibited non-heme iron absorption at Zn:Fe molar ratios of 5:1 and above [PMID:17873388]. In children, adding iron blunted zinc's benefits [PMID:36994923].
- Calcium (off-list): 468 mg/d extra calcium cut net zinc absorption by about 2 mg/d. A 600 mg CaCO3 dose with a meal halved zinc absorption [PMID:9174476].
- Magnesium: high-dose zinc (142 mg/d) reduces magnesium absorption [PMID:7836627] [PMID:40270655].
- Quercetin: acts as a zinc ionophore in vitro [PMID:25050823]. No human outcome data were found.
- Copper (off-list): zinc antagonises copper, which is why AREDS added 2 mg copper [PMID:11594942] [PMID:42087025].
- Zeaxanthin + Lutein: the AREDS2 formula with lutein/zeaxanthin replacing beta-carotene is the current standard. Lutein/zeaxanthin vs beta-carotene HR 0.85 for late AMD [PMID:35653117] [PMID:37702300].
- Vitamin C and Vitamin E: the AREDS antioxidant-plus-zinc arm had the strongest effect [PMID:11594942]. Adding vitamin C to zinc gave no benefit in COVID-19 outpatients [PMID:33576820].
- Folic acid plus zinc (FAZST): no fertility benefit [PMID:31910279].
*** Conditions
- Wilson disease (therapy) [PMID:40089450]. Zinc deficiency risks include CKD [PMID:41651464], older age [PMID:40390089] and malnutrition in children [PMID:27996088].
- Phytate-rich diets: low-phytate maize did not improve zinc absorption in children [PMID:16400050]. Phytate showed no detectable effect in young children [PMID:26108545].
- Depression: lower zinc is linked to severity [PMID:23806573].
*** Lab-test interference
- Alkaline phosphatase: raised by zinc [PMID:11787985] [PMID:18813411] [URL:https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2c4fb943-4b2f-273d-e063-6394a90abb47].
- Serum copper and ceruloplasmin: lowered by zinc [PMID:3365292] [PMID:38846187]. Copper status should be checked during long-term high-dose use [PMID:42087025].
- HDL cholesterol: lowered in healthy people [PMID:20034629].
- Serum zinc: rises with supplementation (pooled +2.42 umol/L) [PMID:20034629]. 30 mg/d did not bring all deficient older adults back to normal [PMID:26817502].

** Genotype notes
- SLC30A8 rs13266634 (R325W): after 14 days of zinc acetate, the early insulin response rose 15% in RW/WW carriers, and proinsulin:insulin improved more than in RR homozygotes [PMID:25348609].
- SLC30A2 rs3008217 explained 6.1% of urinary zinc variation in a GWAS. Higher urinary zinc was associated with cardiometabolic risk [PMID:41325371]. No dosing guidance follows from this.

** Latest research
- [2026-09] Diabetes MA (18 RCTs): HOMA-IR -1.10; CRP SMD -0.91; LDL +3.46 mg/dL [PMID:42594341]. Prediabetes NMA (378 RCTs): zinc ranked top for lowering 2-h glucose [PMID:42711998].
- [2026-09] SR of zinc-induced haematological toxicity (37 cases), with copper low in all [PMID:42087025].
- [2026-06] Oral mucositis MA: zinc mouthwash RR 0.16; systemic zinc borderline (RR 0.52, 0.27-1.01) [PMID:42346271]. Anthropometry MA (41 RCTs): weight +0.68 kg [PMID:41617950].
- [2026-02] CKD MA: weight +2.52 kg, HDL +8.05 mg/dL, albumin up [PMID:41651464].
- [2025-12] Inpatient COVID-19 RCT: progression 5.7% vs 23.5% (OR 0.21) [PMID:41076985]. Urinary-zinc GWAS (SLC30A2) [PMID:41325371].
- [2025-11] Quantile reanalysis: bigger absolute lozenge benefit in longer colds [PMID:41286897]. Diarrhoea adherence MA: 63% for 10-day courses [PMID:41178278].
- [2025-10] Infant sepsis RCT (n=3153): no mortality benefit [PMID:41066442].
- [2025-09] Pregnancy MA (77 RCTs): fetal growth restriction RR 0.23 with zinc alone [PMID:40836314]. [2025-08] PMS MA: g -0.38 [PMID:40737185].
- [2025-05] Immunosenescence review [PMID:40390089]. Depression NMA (zinc+ADT SMD 1.59) [PMID:40314175].
- [2025-04/03] Cases: denture-cream myeloneuropathy [PMID:40416147] and zinc-induced hypomagnesaemia [PMID:40270655].
- [2025-02] EASL-ERN Wilson guideline [PMID:40089450]. Hospital-stay MA: -0.43 d [PMID:39435877].
- [2025-01] AREDS/AREDS2 post hoc: antioxidants slowed GA progression toward the fovea [PMID:39025435].
- [2024-12] WHO-commissioned diarrhoea SR: favours lower doses [PMID:39641338]. Zinc-form bioavailability review [PMID:39770891].
- [2024-11] Dysmenorrhoea MA (g -1.54) [PMID:39683510]. T2D lipid MA [PMID:38224402].
- [2024-10] Sleep SR: possible improvement in sleep quality [PMID:39377022]. Critique of the 2024 Cochrane cold review [PMID:39478818].
** Open questions
- Which lozenge formulation (free ionic zinc) works best, and why the Cochrane estimates are so heterogeneous [PMID:38719213] [PMID:39478818]. Whether zinc helps COVID-19 or other respiratory infection in replete adults, since the trials conflict [PMID:33576820] [PMID:36367144].
- Whether zinc causally prevents pneumonia in deficient older adults; a definitive RCT is pending [PMID:38450236]. Long-term glycaemic and hard outcomes, and genotype-guided use [PMID:42594341] [PMID:25348609].
- The lowest dose that works in AMD, given that 25 mg matched 80 mg [PMID:35653117]. Whether HDL changes in healthy users matter clinically [PMID:20034629].
